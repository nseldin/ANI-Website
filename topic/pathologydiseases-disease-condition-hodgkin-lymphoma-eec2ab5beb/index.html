Definition

Hodgkin lymphoma develops when malignant Reed-Sternberg cells shape an inflammatory microenvironment and usually spread predictably through contiguous lymph-node groups. The characteristic pattern is painless cervical or mediastinal adenopathy, pruritus, alcohol-associated node pain, or fever, drenching sweats, and weight loss. Confirm or stage it with excisional lymph-node biopsy with immunophenotyping; distinguish it from infectious mononucleosis, tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and metastatic cancer by tissue architecture and immunophenotype. The priority is to use stage- and response-adapted combination chemotherapy with selective radiation because infection can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma. can follow if the process advances.

Pathophysiology

Malignant cells grow abnormally, invade local tissue, and may spread through lymphatic or bloodstream routes.

Signs Symptoms

  • Fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, fever, bone pain, lymphadenopathy, weight loss, neurologic change, or transfusion symptoms guide hematology/oncology priority.

  • Escalate neutropenic fever, active bleeding, severe anemia symptoms, tumor lysis signs, hypercalcemia crisis, or transfusion reaction.

Diagnostics

  • excisional lymph-node biopsy with immunophenotyping

  • PET-CT and clinical assessment for staging and response

  • CBC, metabolic, viral, fertility, cardiac, and pulmonary baseline evaluation before therapy

  • Distinguish Hodgkin lymphoma from infectious mononucleosis, tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and metastatic cancer by tissue architecture and immunophenotype.

Labs

  • hemoglobin/hematocrit may fall with anemia or bleeding

  • platelets may fall with bleeding risk

  • ANC below 500 means severe neutropenia risk

  • calcium, potassium, phosphate, and uric acid matter in tumor lysis

  • CBC may show anemia, leukopenia/leukocytosis, or thrombocytopenia depending on disease and treatment

  • calcium may rise in selected malignancies

Treatments

  • use stage- and response-adapted combination chemotherapy with selective radiation

  • preserve fertility and reduce late cardiopulmonary and second-cancer risk

  • use salvage systemic therapy and transplant strategies for relapse

Nursing Priorities

  • Assess painless nodes, fever, drenching night sweats, weight loss, pruritus, fatigue, chest symptoms, infection risk, and fertility goals because symptoms, bulk, and baseline priorities shape staging and treatment planning.

  • Coordinate excisional tissue diagnosis, PET or CT staging, blood count, metabolic panel, ESR, pregnancy testing, and cardiopulmonary baseline studies because accurate subtype, extent, and organ reserve prevent over- or undertreatment.

  • Administer prescribed chemotherapy, radiation, growth support, antiemetics, and infection prophylaxis with independent verification because curative regimens require exact dosing while exposing marrow, heart, lungs, nerves, and gonads.

  • Monitor fever, cytopenias, tumor lysis, neuropathy, cough, dyspnea, cardiac symptoms, thyroid function, fertility, and late second-cancer risk because treatment toxicity can emerge during therapy or years afterward.

  • Escalate for neutropenic fever, stridor or superior vena cava symptoms, acute dyspnea, chest pain, neurologic deficit, severe tumor lysis, or sepsis because infection, compression, thrombosis, and organ toxicity are emergencies.

Complications

  • infection can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • marrow suppression can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • infertility can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • cardiopulmonary toxicity can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • hypothyroidism can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • second malignancy can complicate Hodgkin lymphoma.

Red Flags

  • Fever during neutropenia, rigors, hypotension, confusion, or suspected sepsis

  • Stridor, facial swelling, venous distention, rapidly worsening dyspnea, or hypoxemia

  • Chest pain, new dysrhythmia, syncope, severe cough, or treatment-related lung decline

  • Rising potassium, phosphate or uric acid with oliguria, weakness, or rhythm change

Patient Education

  • Keep all laboratory and survivorship visits even after cure because heart, lung, thyroid, fertility, and second-cancer effects may appear years later.

  • Call immediately for fever, breathing change, facial swelling, chest pain, or new neurologic symptoms because infection or a compression syndrome can progress rapidly.

Nclex Traps

  • A needle sample may miss the tissue architecture needed for diagnosis

  • B symptoms affect staging and prognosis but are not required
