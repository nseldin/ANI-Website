Used To Treat

Adjunct to a thiazide or other kaliuretic diuretic in heart failure or hypertension to restore or prevent hypokalemia under current labeling. Off-label high-yield uses include lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and Liddle syndrome.

Mechanism

Amiloride is secreted into the tubular lumen and binds the ENaC pore on principal cells. Less sodium enters the cell, basolateral sodium-potassium-ATPase has less substrate, and the lumen becomes less negative. Potassium secretion through ROMK or BK and hydrogen secretion by intercalated cells therefore fall, explaining hyperkalemia and normal-anion-gap acidosis. The action is independent of aldosterone, so amiloride can work when ENaC is constitutively active in Liddle syndrome. Lithium uses ENaC as an entry pathway; intracellular lithium inhibits glycogen-synthase-kinase-related signaling, reduces aquaporin-2 expression, and makes the collecting duct vasopressin resistant. Amiloride reduces entry rather than chelating lithium or treating acute systemic toxicity. In severe renal impairment, less potassium can be excreted and drug clearance falls, turning intended potassium conservation into a rapid danger.

Contraindications

  • Hyperkalemia, anuria, acute or severe chronic renal impairment, another potassium-sparing drug, and routine potassium supplementation are central contraindication patterns.

  • A diabetic patient or older adult with reduced renal reserve needs especially careful potassium and kidney assessment.

  • The current label contraindicates potassium above the upper limit of normal and concurrent potassium-conserving agents.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check potassium, bicarbonate, creatinine/eGFR, and blood pressure before therapy, early after a start or dose change, and during dehydration or acute illness.

  • Teach avoidance of unreviewed potassium salt substitutes or supplements and urgent reporting of weakness, palpitations, or reduced urine.

  • For lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, continue serum lithium, sodium, osmolality, kidney, thyroid, urine-volume, and hydration monitoring. Amiloride does not treat acute lithium poisoning.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, MRAs, potassium products, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, and heparin magnify hyperkalemia.

  • Other diuretics may be intentionally combined for potassium balance, but chemistry and kidney function determine whether the combination remains safe.

  • Lithium is the causal target in one off-label use, but amiloride does not eliminate lithium's narrow therapeutic index or toxicity risk.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, bicarbonate, sodium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, blood pressure, volume, and ECG when hyperkalemia is meaningful.

  • Trend after acute illness or interaction changes even when the chronic dose was previously stable.

  • For nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, track serum sodium and osmolality, urine volume and osmolality, lithium concentration, kidney function, and thirst or nocturia.

Nclex Traps

  • ENaC blockers act directly on a luminal channel and do not require aldosterone receptor blockade.

  • Their weak natriuresis does not imply weak hyperkalemia risk.

  • Amiloride helps lithium-induced nephrogenic DI by blocking lithium entry through ENaC; it is not an antidote for an elevated lithium level.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Amiloride can cause fatal hyperkalemia. Used without a kaliuretic diuretic, hyperkalemia occurs more often; risk is higher with renal impairment, diabetes, older age, acute illness, potassium supplements or salt substitutes, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, MRAs, trimethoprim, or NSAIDs. Check potassium and kidney function before and early during therapy.

Class Pathway

  • Late distal tubule and collecting duct

  • Direct luminal ENaC blockade

  • Reduced sodium uptake, potassium secretion, and lithium entry

Description

Amiloride, historically branded Midamor, directly blocks ENaC in the late distal nephron. It is not an aldosterone antagonist and does not need genomic signaling to work. Because this segment reabsorbs little total sodium, amiloride is a weak diuretic alone; its labeled role is usually to prevent potassium loss caused by a thiazide or other kaliuretic drug. Its most distinctive mechanistic use is lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus: lithium enters principal cells through ENaC and disrupts aquaporin-2 water handling, so closing ENaC reduces ongoing intracellular lithium exposure and can lessen polyuria over time. It also directly treats ENaC overactivity in Liddle syndrome. The same channel block that saves potassium can cause fatal hyperkalemia, especially when amiloride is used alone, renal function is reduced, or RAAS blockers and potassium products are present.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyperkalemia, normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis, nausea, diarrhea, headache, and dizziness follow reduced ENaC-mediated sodium entry and potassium or hydrogen secretion.

  • Dangerous hyperkalemia can cause weakness, paresthesia, bradycardia, conduction block, ventricular arrhythmia, or arrest.
