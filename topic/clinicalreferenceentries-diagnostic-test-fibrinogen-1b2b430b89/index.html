Summary

Fibrinogen is a high-yield Hematology diagnostic reference. Clotting factor level important in DIC, bleeding, liver disease, and pregnancy contexts.

Quick Answer

Fibrinogen: Clotting factor level important in DIC, bleeding, liver disease, and pregnancy contexts. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Low

    Meaning

    Low fibrinogen impairs clot formation and can occur in DIC, massive bleeding, liver failure, or obstetric hemorrhage.

  • Label

    High

    Meaning

    High fibrinogen is an acute-phase response and can rise with inflammation, pregnancy, infection, or malignancy.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    In bleeding/DIC/OB emergencies, falling fibrinogen can be a major danger signal.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Clotting factor level important in DIC, bleeding, liver disease, and pregnancy contexts.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
