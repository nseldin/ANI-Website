Definition

Belly-press sign is a bedside resisted-shoulder maneuver. It is a clue to subscapularis dysfunction. A positive finding means the wrist flexes or elbow falls backward to maintain pressure.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Belly-press sign means the wrist flexes or elbow falls backward to maintain pressure.

Why It Matters

A positive Belly-press sign response can signal subscapularis dysfunction. A weak subscapularis is compensated by wrist flexion and shoulder extension. Belly-press sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subscapularis dysfunction

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to press the palm into the abdomen while keeping the elbow forward.

Positive Finding

The wrist flexes or elbow falls backward to maintain pressure.

Mechanism

A weak subscapularis is compensated by wrist flexion and shoulder extension.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Belly-press sign response raises concern for subscapularis dysfunction because a weak subscapularis is compensated by wrist flexion and shoulder extension. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Belly-press sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Belly-press sign to focus evaluation of subscapularis dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: pectoral weakness, wrist weakness, arthritis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to press the palm into the abdomen while keeping the elbow forward. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Belly-press sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain and poor instruction cause compensation. Therefore, Belly-press sign cannot confirm or exclude subscapularis dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pectoral weakness

  • wrist weakness

  • arthritis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from pectoral weakness, wrist weakness, arthritis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Belly-press sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current alternative when lift-off is not possible. Interpret Belly-press sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Pain and poor instruction cause compensation.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Belly-press sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to press the palm into the abdomen while keeping the elbow forward. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Keep resistance gentle and observe compensation rather than pressure alone.
