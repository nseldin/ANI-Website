Used To Treat

Hypertension, selected edema states, prevention of recurrent calcium stones in patients with hypercalciuria, and selected nephrogenic diabetes-insipidus strategies. Metolazone or IV chlorothiazide may be paired with a loop in resistant congestion under close monitoring.

Description

Thiazide and thiazide-like drugs block the sodium-chloride cotransporter NCC in the early distal convoluted tubule. Hydrochlorothiazide and chlorothiazide are benzothiadiazines; chlorthalidone, indapamide, and metolazone are chemically thiazide-like but converge on NCC. They are weaker acute natriuretics than loops because this segment handles less filtered sodium, yet they are foundational antihypertensives because an initial sodium-volume effect is followed by sustained reduction in peripheral vascular resistance. They increase calcium reabsorption and reduce urinary calcium, unlike loops. The same distal sodium shift that supports their benefits also explains hyponatremia, potassium loss, magnesium loss, alkalosis, hyperuricemia, and glucose effects. Long half-life and tissue partitioning make chlorthalidone especially durable; metolazone retains useful synergy at low GFR; IV chlorothiazide supplies a parenteral option when enteral absorption is unreliable.

Mechanism

NCC blockade lowers intracellular sodium in distal convoluted-tubule cells. Basolateral sodium-calcium exchange then pulls more sodium into the cell while moving calcium into blood, lowering intracellular calcium and favoring calcium entry from the lumen through TRPV5; mild volume contraction also increases proximal sodium and calcium reabsorption. Together these effects reduce urinary calcium. More sodium reaches ENaC downstream, making the lumen negative and increasing potassium and hydrogen secretion; volume contraction activates RAAS and aldosterone, producing hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis. Hyponatremia is especially important because thiazides impair urinary dilution while leaving the medullary concentrating mechanism and vasopressin responsiveness relatively intact. In nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, mild volume contraction paradoxically reduces polyuria by increasing proximal sodium-water reabsorption, so less filtrate reaches the collecting duct. Chronic vasodilation involves reduced vascular sodium and calcium reactivity and may outlast the initial plasma-volume change.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Profound thiazide-associated hyponatremia can develop rapidly or after a new stressor, especially in older, lower-body-mass patients with high water intake or interacting drugs. Neurologic symptoms require urgent sodium assessment, not reassurance that the drug is a mild water pill.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, volume depletion, orthostasis, hyperuricemia or gout, photosensitivity, rash, and glucose intolerance are class-pattern effects.

  • Severe outcomes include seizure or cerebral edema from acute hyponatremia, arrhythmia from potassium or magnesium loss, pancreatitis, severe cutaneous reactions, acute angle-closure glaucoma or myopia, and kidney injury from excessive volume loss.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and drug hypersensitivity are common label contraindications. Correct severe electrolyte depletion and clarify symptomatic hyponatremia, hypotension, gout, or evolving kidney injury before continuing.

  • Low GFR reduces conventional thiazide natriuresis, but metolazone and sometimes chlorthalidone can still work. This is a drug- and indication-specific decision, not a blanket rule that every thiazide is useless below one eGFR threshold.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline and follow-up sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid when relevant, pressure, orthostasis, weight, and symptoms. Check earlier after initiation or escalation in high-risk patients.

  • Teach morning dosing when possible, slow position changes, hydration matched to the plan, photosensitivity precautions, and urgent reporting of confusion, severe weakness, cramps, palpitations, eye pain, or sudden visual change.

Interactions

  • Lithium toxicity can rise because sodium depletion increases proximal lithium reabsorption. NSAIDs can blunt antihypertensive or diuretic effects and worsen kidney function.

  • SSRIs, carbamazepine, desmopressin, and high free-water intake amplify hyponatremia; digoxin risk rises with hypokalemia; corticosteroids and laxatives can worsen potassium loss.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, daily weight when used for edema, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid, and urine calcium or stone profile when that is the indication.

  • A low serum sodium after thiazide initiation is mechanistically plausible even when edema improves; do not interpret it as simple sodium deficiency without assessing osmolality, urine studies, water intake, and volume status.

Nclex Traps

  • Thiazides decrease urinary calcium but can increase serum uric acid. Calcium and urate move in different directions for different transport reasons.

  • Thiazide-associated hyponatremia can be more dramatic than loop-associated hyponatremia because the kidney loses its diluting segment while retaining the ability to concentrate urine.
