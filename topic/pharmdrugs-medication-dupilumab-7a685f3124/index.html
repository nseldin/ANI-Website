Used To Treat

Type-2-inflammatory disease including age-approved atopic dermatitis, asthma, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, eosinophilic esophagitis, prurigo nodularis, COPD, chronic spontaneous urticaria, bullous pemphigoid and allergic fungal rhinosinusitis under current indication-specific labeling.

Mechanism

IL-4 binding to IL-4R-alpha/common-gamma receptor and IL-4 or IL-13 signaling through IL-4R-alpha/IL-13R-alpha1 activate JAK-STAT6 transcription. Dupilumab occupies IL-4R-alpha and prevents both receptor systems from propagating those signals. Downstream biomarkers such as IgE, periostin and eotaxin fall. It does not broadly deplete T cells, but eosinophilic conditions can become clinically apparent during steroid reduction and ocular-surface inflammation is a characteristic adverse effect.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Not rescue therapy for acute bronchospasm/status asthmaticus or anaphylaxis. Treat pre-existing helminths, avoid live vaccines, and taper systemic corticosteroids gradually. Assess anaphylaxis/angioedema, conjunctivitis/keratitis/blepharitis, eye pain or vision change, severe arthralgia and eosinophilic pneumonia/vasculitis symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to dupilumab or excipients

  • Treat pre-existing helminth infection and avoid live vaccines

  • Clarify acute asthma symptoms, unexplained eosinophilic systemic illness, active severe ocular disease and abrupt steroid withdrawal

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication, age/weight dose, loading regimen, device, injection technique and storage; the schedule is not universal across diseases.

  • Track disease-specific outcomes plus eyes, eosinophilic symptoms, infections, vaccines and corticosteroid taper.

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines; complete age-appropriate vaccines before therapy when possible.

  • Do not abruptly discontinue inhaled, topical or systemic corticosteroids.

  • Cytokine normalization can theoretically change CYP activity; monitor narrow-index drugs when disease control changes substantially.

Key Labs

  • No therapeutic serum level and no universal routine lab panel

  • CBC/eosinophils when symptoms or indication warrant

  • Disease-specific pulmonary function, skin severity, endoscopy/pathology or ocular examination

Nclex Traps

  • Blocking IL-4R-alpha blocks both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling.

  • Dupilumab is maintenance therapy and does not replace a rescue inhaler.

  • New eye pain or vision change is not just expected injection discomfort.

Description

Dupilumab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that binds the IL-4 receptor-alpha subunit. Because IL-4R-alpha is shared by the type-I IL-4 receptor and the type-II IL-4/IL-13 receptor, one antibody blocks signaling from both IL-4 and IL-13. This is not a generic 'biologic effect': it directly suppresses type-2 programs that drive IgE switching, eosinophilic inflammation, mucus, itch and barrier dysfunction across multiple skin, airway and GI diseases.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reactions, conjunctivitis, blepharitis, keratitis and dry eye

  • Eosinophilia, herpes viral events, arthralgia and upper-respiratory symptoms

  • Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, serum sickness-like reaction and angioedema

  • Indication-specific adverse effects such as oropharyngeal pain or gastritis
