Used To Treat

Serious susceptible lower-respiratory, urinary, bloodstream, skin/soft-tissue, intra-abdominal, gynecologic, bone/joint, and CNS infections, including meningitis. It is a high-yield neonatal alternative to ceftriaxone when a third-generation cephalosporin is indicated because it lacks ceftriaxone's bilirubin-displacement and calcium-precipitation profile.

Mechanism

Cefotaxime acylates essential PBPs and leaves autolysins to weaken the uncross-linked wall. Its active desacetyl metabolite contributes antibacterial activity, while renal elimination of parent and metabolite makes clearance and interval clinically important. Inflamed meninges increase CSF penetration, but meningitis still requires high, protocol-specific exposure because the relevant target is free concentration in CSF relative to MIC. Beta-lactamases such as ESBLs can hydrolyze cefotaxime; AmpC induction and target or permeability changes can also cause failure despite the generation label.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity, CDI, cytopenias, hepatic injury, interstitial nephritis, and concentration-related neurotoxicity can occur. Meningitis deterioration requires immediate reassessment of organism, exposure, resistance, complications, and source.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to cefotaxime, cephalosporins, or a relevant beta-lactam requires phenotype-specific avoidance.

  • Do not use cefotaxime alone when Listeria or Enterococcus requires coverage because cephalosporins do not bind those targets adequately.

Nursing Essentials

  • For meningitis or neonatal sepsis, give the exact protocol dose on time and document first-dose timing because adequate early CSF and serum exposure changes outcome.

  • Trend renal function, CBC, liver tests, mental status, cultures, and diarrhea during prolonged treatment because toxicity and clearance evolve during critical illness.

Interactions

  • Nephrotoxic drugs can reduce clearance and increase beta-lactam exposure; loop diuretics and critical illness add renal vulnerability.

  • Warfarin effect can rise through illness, nutrition, and microbiome change, so monitor INR in high-risk patients.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, CBC, creatinine/eGFR, liver tests, direct Coombs context when hemolysis is suspected, and CSF cell count/protein/glucose/culture for meningitis.

  • A third-generation cephalosporin result does not imply Listeria coverage or stability to ESBL/AmpC enzymes.

Nclex Traps

  • Cefotaxime and ceftriaxone share a generation but are not interchangeable in neonates because ceftriaxone has bilirubin and calcium hazards.

  • Cefotaxime does not provide dependable Pseudomonas coverage; ceftazidime is the classic third-generation antipseudomonal exception.

Description

Cefotaxime is an IV/IM third-generation cephalosporin beta-lactam antibiotic that enters susceptible Gram-negative bacteria, binds PBPs, blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking, and produces bactericidal lysis. It retains useful streptococcal and pneumococcal activity and penetrates inflamed meninges, making it important in severe infection and meningitis. It is not a reliable Pseudomonas, Enterococcus, Listeria, MRSA, ESBL, or carbapenemase drug. In neonates, cefotaxime has historically been selected over ceftriaxone when this spectrum is needed because ceftriaxone can displace bilirubin and form fatal calcium precipitates; cefotaxime does not share those defining neonatal restrictions.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection pain/phlebitis, rash, fever, diarrhea, nausea, candidiasis, liver-test elevation, positive direct Coombs test, and CDI.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, interstitial nephritis, encephalopathy, myoclonus, or seizures at excessive exposure.
