Used To Treat

Prevention, not rescue, of acute and especially delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting as part of a multidrug antiemetic regimen; selected products also prevent postoperative nausea and vomiting.

Description

NK1 antagonists block substance P signaling in brainstem emetic networks and are particularly important for delayed chemotherapy-induced emesis. Fosaprepitant is an IV phosphorylated prodrug converted to aprepitant within about 30 minutes; oral aprepitant is already active. They complement rather than replace 5-HT3 antagonists and dexamethasone.

Mechanism

Aprepitant crosses the blood-brain barrier and occupies high-affinity NK1 receptors, preventing substance P from sustaining the vomiting response. It has little affinity for dopamine or 5-HT3 receptors, explaining why combination blockade covers different emetic pathways and phases. Fosaprepitant's conversion means its interaction profile becomes aprepitant's profile.

Boxed Warning

No current boxed warning. Serious hypersensitivity can occur during or soon after fosaprepitant infusion, and peripheral infusion-site thrombophlebitis or necrosis is a meaningful route-specific risk. CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 effects alter dexamethasone, contraindicated pimozide exposure, warfarin INR, and hormonal-contraceptive efficacy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give prophylactically on the regimen schedule, verify companion antiemetics and dexamethasone adjustment, inspect the IV site continuously, and stop fosaprepitant for infusion hypersensitivity.

  • Review CYP3A4 inducers/inhibitors, warfarin and hormonal contraception; recommend backup contraception during treatment and for the product-specified interval afterward.

Key Labs

  • Clinical nausea/vomiting control and hydration, infusion site, hypersensitivity, liver function when indicated, and INR after therapy in warfarin-treated patients.

Nclex Traps

  • Fosaprepitant is not a separate receptor mechanism; it is a prodrug of aprepitant.

  • NK1 antagonists prevent emesis and are not reliable single-agent rescue once uncontrolled vomiting is established.
