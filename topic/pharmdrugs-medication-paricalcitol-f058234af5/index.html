Used To Treat

Prevention and treatment of secondary hyperparathyroidism in chronic kidney disease, with oral or IV indication depending on CKD stage and dialysis status.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for paricalcitol in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Paricalcitol activates nuclear vitamin D receptors, changing transcription in parathyroid, intestine, bone, and kidney. In parathyroid tissue it suppresses PTH synthesis and secretion and reduces gland hyperplasia; systemic mineral effects raise calcium and phosphate exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend intact PTH with calcium and phosphorus during initiation/titration; do not chase PTH to zero.

  • Review calcium products, phosphate binders, other vitamin D agents, thiazides, digoxin, and strong CYP3A inhibitors; monitor weakness, confusion, constipation, bone pain, or rhythm changes from hypercalcemia.

Key Labs

  • Corrected/ionized calcium, phosphorus, intact PTH, alkaline phosphatase, vitamin D status, and renal/dialysis trends.

Nclex Traps

  • Paricalcitol lowers PTH but can oversuppress bone turnover. Calcium, phosphorus, and PTH must be interpreted together.

Description

Paricalcitol is an active vitamin D2 analog designed to suppress excess PTH in CKD. It binds vitamin D receptors in the parathyroid gland and reduces PTH gene transcription/secretion; oversuppression can produce adynamic bone, while increased calcium and phosphate absorption can cause hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, vascular calcification, and digoxin-sensitive dysrhythmias.
