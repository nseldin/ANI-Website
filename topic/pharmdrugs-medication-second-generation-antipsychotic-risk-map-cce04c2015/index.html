Used To Treat

Comparison of commonly used second-generation antipsychotics for psychosis, bipolar illness, and product-specific adjunctive indications.

Description

Second-generation antipsychotics are not one interchangeable low-EPS class. Risperidone has prominent prolactin elevation and dose-related EPS. Olanzapine has very high weight, glucose, and lipid burden. Quetiapine is strongly sedating and orthostatic with relatively low EPS at usual doses. Ziprasidone has lower average metabolic burden but the strongest QT and food-absorption teaching in this group. Aripiprazole lowers prolactin and metabolic burden for many patients but commonly causes akathisia and impulse-control problems. Clozapine is uniquely effective for treatment-resistant schizophrenia and suicide-risk reduction but requires ANC and multisystem toxicity surveillance.

Mechanism

Most agents antagonize D2 and 5-HT2A receptors, but differing affinity for H1, M1, alpha-1, alpha-2, 5-HT2C and cardiac ion channels predicts much of the clinical divergence. H1/5-HT2C blockade promotes appetite and weight gain; M1 blockade causes anticholinergic effects; alpha-1 blockade causes orthostasis; sustained D2 blockade raises prolactin and EPS; hERG potassium-channel effects prolong QT. Aripiprazole instead partially activates D2/D3 and 5-HT1A receptors. Clozapine binds D2 more loosely and has broad serotonergic, muscarinic, histaminergic and adrenergic activity, which helps explain low EPS but high autonomic and metabolic burden.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; second-generation antipsychotics is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Match monitoring to the specific drug: metabolic surveillance is universal, but QT, ANC, myocarditis, prolactin and post-injection monitoring are agent/formulation specific.

Adverse Effects

  • Risperidone: prolactin elevation and dose-related EPS

  • Olanzapine/clozapine: greatest metabolic burden; clozapine adds neutropenia, myocarditis, ileus and seizures

  • Quetiapine: sedation, orthostasis and metabolic effects

  • Ziprasidone: QT prolongation; aripiprazole: akathisia and compulsive behaviors

Contraindications

  • Use each product's cardiac, hematologic, hepatic and interaction contraindications

  • Avoid clozapine without baseline ANC and capacity for ongoing monitoring

  • Avoid ziprasidone in congenital long-QT syndrome, recent MI, uncompensated heart failure or prohibited QT-drug combinations

Nursing Essentials

  • Choose monitoring from the drug's receptor fingerprint rather than writing only monitor for EPS.

  • Ask directly about amenorrhea/galactorrhea/sexual dysfunction, appetite, sleep, constipation, palpitations, fainting, fever, chest pain and new compulsive gambling, shopping, eating or sexual behavior.

Interactions

  • CYP2D6/3A4 interactions affect risperidone and aripiprazole; CYP3A4 strongly affects quetiapine and ziprasidone.

  • Smoking induces CYP1A2 and can lower olanzapine/clozapine exposure; abrupt smoking cessation can raise levels.

  • Sedatives, anticholinergics and QT-prolonging drugs add predictable toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Weight/BMI/waist, blood pressure, A1c/glucose and lipids

  • AIMS/EPS and prolactin assessment

  • ECG/electrolytes, ANC, troponin/CRP or drug concentrations when the selected agent requires

Nclex Traps

  • Lower metabolic risk does not erase ziprasidone's QT and meal requirements.

  • Clozapine REMS enrollment ended, but ANC monitoring and neutropenia risk did not.

  • Aripiprazole's partial agonism reduces some D2-blockade effects but can produce severe akathisia.
