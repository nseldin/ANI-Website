Class

Direct ophthalmic muscarinic-M3 agonist miotic/trabecular outflow enhancer

Used To Treat

Product-specific treatment of elevated intraocular pressure, acute angle-closure management after initial pressure reduction, reversal of pharmacologic mydriasis, or adult presbyopia; concentration and schedule differ sharply by indication.

Description

Pilocarpine ophthalmic directly stimulates muscarinic receptors, constricting the pupil and contracting ciliary muscle. Ciliary contraction pulls on the scleral spur and opens trabecular meshwork, improving conventional aqueous outflow. The same mechanism causes brow ache, accommodative spasm/induced myopia and dim vision, especially at night. Miotics can rarely precede retinal tear or detachment in susceptible eyes.

Mechanism

M3-Gq signaling raises intracellular calcium in iris sphincter and ciliary smooth muscle. Miosis moves the peripheral iris away from the angle; ciliary-muscle contraction tensions the scleral spur and trabecular meshwork, lowering outflow resistance. For presbyopia, a smaller pupil creates a pinhole effect that improves near depth of focus rather than restoring a young flexible lens.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Blurred/dim vision can impair night driving. Retinal tear or detachment has been reported; examine retinal risk and urgently evaluate flashes, new floaters or a curtain/field defect. Avoid or use great caution in active iritis because miosis can promote posterior synechiae. Product concentration and indication must be verified.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache/brow ache, miosis, accommodative spasm, induced myopia, blurred or dim vision, conjunctival hyperemia, eye irritation, retinal tear/detachment, iritis/synechiae and systemic cholinergic effects rarely.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity and situations where miosis is undesirable, including active iritis under many labels. Clarify retinal detachment risk, high myopia, prior retinal disease and ability to avoid hazardous low-light activity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and concentration; glaucoma and presbyopia products are not dose-for-dose substitutes. Teach urgent reporting of flashes, floaters, curtain-like vision loss or sudden field change and caution with night driving.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Other cholinergic drugs add muscarinic effects; antimuscarinics oppose the ocular action. Separate other topical drugs and recognize that phenylephrine may dilate against the miotic effect.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure and angle/optic-nerve assessment

  • Retinal examination when risk or symptoms exist

  • Visual acuity/refraction, pupil and inflammation assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Pilocarpine improves trabecular outflow by ciliary contraction; it is not a carbonic-anhydrase inhibitor.

  • Presbyopia benefit is a pinhole/depth-of-focus effect, not lens rejuvenation.

  • Flashes and floaters after a miotic require retinal assessment.
