Definition

Anuria means urine output is absent or nearly absent. A common adult nursing definition is less than 50 milliliters in 24 hours, while twelve hours with no urine is also a severe acute-kidney-injury criterion.

Plain Language

Anuria means the kidneys or urinary drainage system are producing or delivering almost no urine.

Why It Matters

Sudden anuria can signal complete urinary obstruction, profound shock, renal vascular blockage, or severe kidney injury. First rule out a kinked or displaced collection system, then escalate promptly because restoring perfusion or relieving obstruction can be time critical.

Associated Conditions

  • severe acute kidney injury, profound shock or renal hypoperfusion, complete bilateral urinary obstruction or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney, renal vascular occlusion, acute glomerular disease, blocked drainage tubing, or measurement failure

Assessment Method

Immediately verify the collection system, recent voids, intake, catheter position and patency without forceful flushing, and the correct time window; assess bladder distention or scan when indicated, blood pressure and perfusion, edema and lungs, flank pain, trauma, medications, creatinine, potassium, acid-base status, and renal or bladder imaging as ordered.

Positive Finding

Urine production or measured drainage is essentially absent; a common adult nursing definition is less than 50 milliliters in 24 hours, and twelve hours of anuria is a severe acute-kidney-injury criterion.

Mechanism

Near-total loss of glomerular filtration or renal blood flow prevents urine formation, while complete outflow obstruction prevents formed urine from reaching the collection point; a blocked device can mimic either state.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Anuria finding raises concern for severe acute kidney injury, profound shock or renal hypoperfusion, complete bilateral urinary obstruction or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney, renal vascular occlusion, acute glomerular disease, blocked drainage tubing, or measurement failure because near-total loss of glomerular filtration or renal blood flow prevents urine formation, while complete outflow obstruction prevents formed urine from reaching the collection point; a blocked device can mimic either state. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Anuria does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Anuria to focus evaluation of severe acute kidney injury, profound shock or renal hypoperfusion, complete bilateral urinary obstruction or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney, renal vascular occlusion, acute glomerular disease, blocked drainage tubing, or measurement failure and the reviewed alternatives: blocked urinary catheter, complete urinary retention, collection failure, severe dehydration. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Immediately verify the collection system, recent voids, intake, catheter position and patency without forceful flushing, and the correct time window; assess bladder distention or scan when indicated, blood pressure and perfusion, edema and lungs, flank pain, trauma, medications, creatinine, potassium, acid-base status, and renal or bladder imaging as ordered. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Anuria as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

An empty drainage bag can reflect a kink, displacement, closed clamp, leak, unmeasured void, or incorrect time window rather than true anuria; absence of bladder fullness does not exclude upper-tract obstruction or kidney failure. Therefore, Anuria cannot confirm or exclude severe acute kidney injury, profound shock or renal hypoperfusion, complete bilateral urinary obstruction or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney, renal vascular occlusion, acute glomerular disease, blocked drainage tubing, or measurement failure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • blocked urinary catheter

  • complete urinary retention

  • collection failure

  • severe dehydration

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as blocked urinary catheter, complete urinary retention, collection failure, severe dehydration.

False Negative Situations

Anuria may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe acute kidney injury, profound shock or renal hypoperfusion, complete bilateral urinary obstruction or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney, renal vascular occlusion, acute glomerular disease, blocked drainage tubing, or measurement failure.

Modern Status

Current emergency-level urine-output finding; sudden anuria is not simply severe oliguria and should trigger immediate confirmation and evaluation for reversible obstruction, perfusion failure, vascular compromise, or intrinsic kidney injury. Safety Precautions

Observe Anuria with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Immediately verify the collection system, recent voids, intake, catheter position and patency without forceful flushing, and the correct time window; assess bladder distention or scan when indicated, blood pressure and perfusion, edema and lungs, flank pain, trauma, medications, creatinine, potassium, acid-base status, and renal or bladder imaging as ordered. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Treat sudden or persistent anuria as urgent after rapidly checking the equipment; do not repeatedly irrigate or manipulate against resistance, and escalate immediately with hypotension, shock, pulmonary edema, hyperkalemia, severe acidosis, flank or pelvic pain, fever, recent pelvic or urinary procedure, solitary kidney, trauma, or rapidly worsening creatinine because urgent decompression or kidney support may be time critical.
