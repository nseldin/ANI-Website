Class

Topical lincosamide 50S ribosomal inhibitor for acne

Used To Treat

Acne vulgaris; use with benzoyl peroxide rather than prolonged antibiotic monotherapy to reduce C. acnes resistance.

Description

Topical clindamycin is a lincosamide antibiotic applied to acne-prone skin to reduce Cutibacterium acnes and inflammatory lesions. After phosphatases convert clindamycin phosphate to active clindamycin, the drug binds 23S rRNA in the bacterial 50S ribosomal subunit and blocks protein-chain elongation. It is route-specific: this card is not systemic clindamycin for anaerobic infection, but enough drug can still be absorbed through skin to cause life-threatening C. difficile colitis.

Mechanism

Clindamycin occupies the peptidyl-transferase region of 23S rRNA and interferes with peptide elongation, usually producing bacteriostasis. Methylation of the ribosomal site creates macrolide-lincosamide-streptogramin B cross-resistance, and inducible resistance can emerge. Benzoyl peroxide adds nonselective oxidant killing, which reduces resistant-strain selection. The phosphate prodrug improves topical delivery but does not alter the ribosomal target after activation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Systemic absorption occurs. Diarrhea, bloody diarrhea and pseudomembranous/C. difficile colitis have occurred with topical and systemic clindamycin and can begin after therapy. Stop the drug and assess significant diarrhea; antiperistaltic agents can worsen toxin-mediated colitis.

Adverse Effects

  • Dryness, burning, pruritus, erythema, peeling and oily skin

  • Contact dermatitis, eye pain and gram-negative folliculitis

  • Diarrhea, abdominal cramping, bloody stool and C. difficile colitis

Contraindications

  • Clindamycin or lincomycin hypersensitivity

  • History of regional enteritis, ulcerative colitis or antibiotic-associated colitis

  • Clarify active significant diarrhea and concurrent systemic/topical antibiotics

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a thin film to the affected acne field using the exact once- or twice-daily formulation instruction.

  • Ask about bowel history and teach immediate reporting of persistent diarrhea, blood or severe cramps even after treatment ends.

Interactions

  • Erythromycin can antagonize clindamycin at overlapping ribosomal sites and compounds resistance pressure.

  • Benzoyl peroxide is intentionally combined to reduce resistance.

  • Other drying/peeling acne products increase irritation.

Key Labs

  • No routine clindamycin serum level

  • C. difficile stool testing and CBC/metabolic assessment when colitis is suspected

  • Serial acne response and resistance/adherence review

Nclex Traps

  • Topical clindamycin can still cause C. difficile colitis.

  • It is not preferred as chronic monotherapy because resistance develops.

  • A new pustular eruption during therapy can be gram-negative folliculitis rather than worsening ordinary acne.
