Used To Treat

Preventive treatment of migraine in adults by intravenous infusion every 3 months; 100 mg is recommended and some patients may benefit from 300 mg.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current VYEPTI labeling has no boxed warning. Hypersensitivity can occur during infusion and may require discontinuation or treatment. Current labeling also warns about severe constipation, new or worsening hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon. Infusion completion does not end risk because the antibody remains in the body for weeks.

Mechanism

Eptinezumab binds alpha- and beta-CGRP ligand before either can activate the CLR/RAMP1 receptor complex, reducing Gs-adenylyl-cyclase-cAMP signaling in trigeminovascular pain pathways. Proteolytic catabolism and an approximately 27-day half-life explain minimal conventional CYP interaction potential, quarterly dosing, and why exposure persists long after an infusion ends.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify 100 mg or selected 300 mg, dilute in 100 mL 0.9% sodium chloride, use the labeled filter and infusion setup, infuse over about 30 minutes, and observe for hypersensitivity. Do not administer as an undiluted IV push.

  • Record baseline and follow-up migraine days, bowel pattern, blood pressure, digital perfusion, pregnancy plans, infusion reaction, and rescue use. Escalate severe abdominal symptoms or painful color-changing digits after discharge because delayed complications remain possible.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to eptinezumab-jjmr or an excipient. A prior infusion reaction requires careful characterization rather than automatic re-exposure.

Interactions

  • Conventional CYP interactions are unlikely because eptinezumab undergoes proteolytic catabolism.

  • Review other constipating drugs and vascular risk. The 100-mg and 300-mg regimens are selected from product evidence, not adjusted by simply scaling infusion frequency.

Key Labs

  • No routine concentration, CBC, liver panel, TB screen, or hepatitis screen is required solely for eptinezumab. Monitor clinical response, infusion reactions, bowel function, blood pressure, digital perfusion, and hypersensitivity.

Nclex Traps

  • Eptinezumab binds CGRP ligand and is given IV; erenumab targets the receptor and is subcutaneous.

  • The infusion is preventive every 3 months, not an acute migraine rescue infusion.

  • About 30 minutes describes infusion time, not drug half-life or duration of effect.

Description

Eptinezumab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds CGRP ligand and is the IV member of the preventive CGRP-antibody class. It is diluted in 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride and infused over about 30 minutes every 3 months. It is not an IV rescue treatment for an attack already underway; the route provides scheduled preventive exposure and allows direct observation for infusion hypersensitivity.

Adverse Effects

  • Nasopharyngitis and hypersensitivity reactions are the most common adverse reactions.

  • Serious infusion hypersensitivity, severe constipation, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon are the high-consequence warnings.
