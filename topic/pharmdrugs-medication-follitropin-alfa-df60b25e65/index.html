Class

Recombinant human follicle-stimulating hormone and FSH-receptor agonist gonadotropin

Used To Treat

Induction of ovulation in selected oligo-anovulatory infertility not caused by primary ovarian failure, development of multiple follicles during assisted reproductive technology, and induction of spermatogenesis in selected males with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, usually with hCG support.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Follitropin alfa has no current U.S. boxed warning, but labeling requires use by clinicians experienced in infertility with access to appropriate monitoring. It can cause OHSS with pulmonary or vascular complications, ovarian torsion, abnormal enlargement, thrombosis, multiple gestation, ectopic pregnancy, and spontaneous abortion. If ovaries are abnormally enlarged or response is excessive, do not administer hCG because the trigger can intensify OHSS. International Units and injection devices are product-specific; verify the pen concentration, dialed dose, remaining volume, storage, and single- versus multi-dose formulation.

Mechanism

Follitropin alfa binds the Gs-coupled FSH receptor on ovarian granulosa cells and testicular Sertoli cells. Adenylyl cyclase raises cAMP, protein kinase A phosphorylates transcriptional regulators, and granulosa cells increase aromatase, inhibin, growth factors, and follicular survival. Theca-cell androgens are converted to estradiol, which tracks follicular mass. In males, Sertoli signaling increases androgen-binding protein and the cellular environment required for germ-cell maturation, but hCG is usually needed first or concurrently to stimulate Leydig-cell testosterone through LH/CG receptors. When many follicles respond, later hCG exposure drives luteinization and ovarian VEGF, increasing vascular permeability; this explains why hCG may be withheld when the FSH response is excessive.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach and observe one complete injection setup: product, reconstitution if needed, pen priming, International Unit dial, site rotation, storage, sharps disposal, and what to do after a missed dose. Never guess or double.

  • Escalate rapid weight gain, increasing girth, severe pelvic pain, vomiting, dyspnea, oliguria, or unilateral pain because OHSS and torsion can progress after the final FSH dose.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, primary gonadal failure or high FSH showing absent gonadal reserve, uncontrolled non-gonadal endocrine disease, pituitary/hypothalamic tumor, sex-hormone-dependent reproductive tumor, unexplained bleeding, or ovarian cyst/enlargement not due to PCOS.

  • Do not continue an unchanged dose when ultrasound, estradiol, symptoms, or prior response shows excessive or inadequate stimulation; dose is individualized by biologic response.

Interactions

  • Other gonadotropins and hCG have additive ovarian effects and belong only inside the prescribed protocol.

  • Clomiphene or letrozole may be combined in specialist regimens, but response and OHSS risk cannot be inferred from either drug alone.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test, baseline pelvic ultrasound, estradiol and serial follicle measurements; semen analysis and testosterone-related monitoring for male treatment

  • Weight, abdominal girth, urine output, hematocrit, electrolytes, creatinine, liver tests, oxygenation, ascites, thrombosis and torsion symptoms when response is high

Nclex Traps

  • Follitropin alfa is recombinant FSH that grows follicles; hCG is the LH-like trigger that completes maturation and ovulation.

  • OHSS is not simple fluid overload. VEGF-driven capillary leak causes third spacing while intravascular volume and renal perfusion can fall.

Description

Follitropin alfa is laboratory-produced human FSH that directly stimulates FSH receptors. In ovaries it recruits and grows follicles and increases granulosa-cell aromatase and estradiol; in testes it stimulates Sertoli-cell support of spermatogenesis when intratesticular testosterone is provided by hCG/LH signaling. Unlike clomiphene or letrozole, it does not persuade the pituitary to release more FSH: it supplies the hormone itself. That direct potency makes response more predictable but also allows multiple follicles to grow at once. Ultrasound and estradiol monitoring are therefore part of the therapy, because excessive response can lead to OHSS, torsion, thrombosis, ovarian rupture, or multifetal gestation. It is entirely unrelated to andexanet alfa despite the shared alfa suffix.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, abdominal or pelvic pain, nausea, bloating, injection-site bruising, breast tenderness, and ovarian enlargement

  • OHSS with ascites or pleural effusion, hemoconcentration, renal dysfunction, thrombosis, ovarian torsion or rupture, ectopic pregnancy, multifetal gestation, and anaphylaxis
