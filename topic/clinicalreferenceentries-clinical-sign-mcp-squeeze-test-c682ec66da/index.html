Definition

MCP squeeze test is a bedside joint-compression maneuver. It is a clue to metacarpophalangeal synovitis. A positive finding means pain is reproduced across the joints.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive MCP squeeze test means pain is reproduced across the joints.

Why It Matters

A positive MCP squeeze test response can signal metacarpophalangeal synovitis. Compression increases pressure within inflamed joint capsules and adjacent synovium. MCP squeeze test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • metacarpophalangeal synovitis

Assessment Method

Compress the MCP row gently from the sides.

Positive Finding

Pain is reproduced across the joints.

Mechanism

Compression increases pressure within inflamed joint capsules and adjacent synovium.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive MCP squeeze test response raises concern for metacarpophalangeal synovitis because compression increases pressure within inflamed joint capsules and adjacent synovium. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; MCP squeeze test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use MCP squeeze test to focus evaluation of metacarpophalangeal synovitis and the reviewed alternatives: osteoarthritis, fracture, tenosynovitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compress the MCP row gently from the sides. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating MCP squeeze test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain may reflect bone or soft tissue and a negative test does not exclude synovitis. Therefore, MCP squeeze test cannot confirm or exclude metacarpophalangeal synovitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • osteoarthritis

  • fracture

  • tenosynovitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from osteoarthritis, fracture, tenosynovitis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

MCP squeeze test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current screening adjunct with limited specificity. When performed as described, MCP squeeze test can adjust concern for metacarpophalangeal synovitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for MCP squeeze test: Pain may reflect bone or soft tissue and a negative test does not exclude synovitis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing MCP squeeze test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Compress the MCP row gently from the sides. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid in suspected fracture or severe acute inflammation.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact MCP squeeze test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Pain is reproduced across the joints. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Compress the MCP row gently from the sides. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, metacarpophalangeal synovitis, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For MCP squeeze test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Avoid in suspected fracture or severe acute inflammation. Base escalation for MCP squeeze test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
