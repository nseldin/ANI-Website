Definition

Romaña sign is an observed periocular sign. It is a clue to acute Chagas disease. A positive finding means one eye has marked periorbital swelling, sometimes with conjunctivitis.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Romaña sign means one eye has marked periorbital swelling, sometimes with conjunctivitis.

Why It Matters

The observed Romaña sign finding can signal acute Chagas disease. Trypanosoma cruzi enters conjunctiva and produces local inflammation with regional lymphatic response. Romaña sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • acute Chagas disease

One eye has marked periorbital swelling, sometimes with conjunctivitis.

Evidence Limitations

It occurs in a minority and is not specific outside epidemiologic context. Therefore, Romaña sign cannot confirm or exclude acute Chagas disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as insect bite, nephrotic edema, preseptal cellulitis.

False Negative Situations

Romaña sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of acute Chagas disease.
