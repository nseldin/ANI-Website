Class

IV phosphorylated aprepitant prodrug/NK1 receptor antagonist antiemetic

Used To Treat

With other antiemetics, prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting from highly or moderately emetogenic chemotherapy in adults and pediatric patients according to regimen-specific age and dosing instructions.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current EMEND-for-injection labeling has no boxed warning. Serious infusion hypersensitivity, including flushing, erythema, dyspnea and anaphylaxis, can occur during or soon after infusion; stop and do not restart for serious reactions. Infusion-site thrombosis, vasculitis and necrosis are more frequent with higher concentrations or small veins. CYP3A4 interactions can make pimozide dangerous, alter dexamethasone and chemotherapy exposure, reduce warfarin INR and decrease hormonal-contraceptive efficacy.

Mechanism

Tissue phosphatases rapidly convert fosaprepitant to active aprepitant. Aprepitant binds central NK1 receptors with high affinity and prevents substance P from sustaining vomiting-center and nucleus-tractus-solitarius signaling. It has little affinity for 5-HT3 or dopamine receptors, so combining it with a 5-HT3 antagonist and dexamethasone blocks complementary acute and delayed pathways.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify prophylaxis schedule, chemotherapy emetogenicity, companion 5-HT3 antagonist and dexamethasone dose adjustment, venous access, allergy history, warfarin, pimozide/CYP3A drugs and contraception.

  • Inspect the IV site throughout infusion, stop immediately for hypersensitivity or severe pain/swelling, and check delayed-emesis control over subsequent days rather than judging efficacy only in the infusion chair.

Key Labs

  • Clinical nausea/vomiting and hydration, infusion-site and hypersensitivity assessment, liver function when indicated, INR 7 to 10 days after therapy in chronic warfarin use and medication-interaction review.

Nclex Traps

  • Fosaprepitant is converted to aprepitant; its clinical receptor target is NK1, not 5-HT3.

  • Because it prevents emesis, it can mask a toxin-related vomiting signal and drug-induced emesis is unreliable in overdose.

Description

Fosaprepitant is an IV water-soluble phosphorylated prodrug converted to aprepitant within about 30 minutes. Aprepitant crosses the blood-brain barrier and blocks substance-P neurokinin-1 receptors, making the regimen especially effective against delayed chemotherapy-induced emesis. It is preventive combination therapy, not an isolated rescue drug.
