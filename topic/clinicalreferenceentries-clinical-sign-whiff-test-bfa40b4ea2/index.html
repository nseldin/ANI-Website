Definition

Whiff test is a laboratory bedside test. It is a clue to bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines. A positive finding means a fishy amine odor is released.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Whiff test means a fishy amine odor is released.

Why It Matters

The Whiff test laboratory finding can signal bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines. Alkalinization volatilizes amines produced by anaerobic vaginal bacteria. Whiff test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines

Assessment Method

Add validated potassium hydroxide to a vaginal-fluid specimen in a controlled clinical setting.

Positive Finding

A fishy amine odor is released.

Mechanism

Alkalinization volatilizes amines produced by anaerobic vaginal bacteria.

Clinical Interpretation

The Whiff test laboratory finding raises concern for bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines because alkalinization volatilizes amines produced by anaerobic vaginal bacteria. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Whiff test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Whiff test to focus evaluation of bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines and the reviewed alternatives: normal odor, cervicitis, semen. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Add validated potassium hydroxide to a vaginal-fluid specimen in a controlled clinical setting. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Whiff test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Odor perception varies and lubricants or contamination interfere. Therefore, Whiff test cannot confirm or exclude bacterial vaginosis-associated volatile amines by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal odor

  • cervicitis

  • semen

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with normal odor, cervicitis, semen.

False Negative Situations

Whiff test may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.

Modern Status

Current Amsel-criteria component; subjective and not diagnostic alone. Interpret Whiff test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Odor perception varies and lubricants or contamination interfere.

Safety Precautions

For Whiff test, verify the correct patient and the specimen, collection, handling, preparation, and laboratory method used to identify this finding. The reviewed precaution is: Do not intentionally smell directly; waft safely and integrate pH, clue cells, and discharge.
