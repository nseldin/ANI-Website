Definition

Festinating gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to parkinsonism. A positive finding means steps become progressively shorter and faster as the patient chases the center of gravity.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Festinating gait means steps become progressively shorter and faster as the patient chases the center of gravity.

Why It Matters

The observed Festinating gait finding can signal parkinsonism. Impaired basal-ganglia scaling and forward posture move the center of mass ahead of inadequate corrective steps. Festinating gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • parkinsonism

Assessment Method

Observe stride length, cadence, posture, turning, and ability to stop.

Positive Finding

Steps become progressively shorter and faster as the patient chases the center of gravity.

Mechanism

Impaired basal-ganglia scaling and forward posture move the center of mass ahead of inadequate corrective steps.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Festinating gait finding raises concern for parkinsonism because impaired basal-ganglia scaling and forward posture move the center of mass ahead of inadequate corrective steps. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Festinating gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Festinating gait to focus evaluation of parkinsonism and the reviewed alternatives: frontal gait, anxious rushing, orthopedic limitation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe stride length, cadence, posture, turning, and ability to stop. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Festinating gait as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It may be absent early and medication state changes expression. Therefore, Festinating gait cannot confirm or exclude parkinsonism by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • frontal gait

  • anxious rushing

  • orthopedic limitation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as frontal gait, anxious rushing, orthopedic limitation.

False Negative Situations

Festinating gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of parkinsonism.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Provide guarding because rapid propulsion can end in a forward fall.
