Definition

Burton line is an observed gingival sign. It is a clue to chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions. A positive finding means a blue-gray line follows the gum-tooth border.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Burton line means a blue-gray line follows the gum-tooth border.

Why It Matters

The observed Burton line finding can signal chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions. Circulating lead reacts with sulfur produced by oral bacteria and deposits dark lead sulfide in gingiva. Burton line cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions

Assessment Method

Inspect the gingival margin.

Positive Finding

A blue-gray line follows the gum-tooth border.

Mechanism

Circulating lead reacts with sulfur produced by oral bacteria and deposits dark lead sulfide in gingiva.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Burton line finding raises concern for chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions because circulating lead reacts with sulfur produced by oral bacteria and deposits dark lead sulfide in gingiva. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Burton line does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Burton line to focus evaluation of chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions and the reviewed alternatives: amalgam tattoo, cyanosis, gingivitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the gingival margin. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Burton line as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is usually absent with good dental hygiene and is not specific. Therefore, Burton line cannot confirm or exclude chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • amalgam tattoo

  • cyanosis

  • gingivitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as amalgam tattoo, cyanosis, gingivitis.

False Negative Situations

Burton line may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions.

Modern Status

Historical clue; uncommon and insensitive. Burton line remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude chronic lead exposure with poor oral conditions. Apply the reviewed limitation for Burton line: It is usually absent with good dental hygiene and is not specific.

Observe Burton line with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect the gingival margin. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Obtain a venous blood lead level; do not scrape the gum.
