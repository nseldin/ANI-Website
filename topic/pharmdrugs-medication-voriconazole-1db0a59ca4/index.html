Used To Treat

Invasive aspergillosis; serious Scedosporium apiospermum and Fusarium infections when other therapy is unsuitable; selected resistant Candida infections and guideline-directed CNS or deep Aspergillus disease. It does not cover Mucorales.

Mechanism

By coordinating CYP51 heme iron, voriconazole blocks ergosterol synthesis. In adults, saturable metabolism means clearance does not rise proportionally with dose; once enzymes approach capacity, a small dose increase can produce a large concentration rise. Low exposure risks failure, while high exposure correlates with visual, neurologic, and hepatic toxicity, which explains therapeutic drug monitoring in invasive disease. Visual effects likely arise in the retina and usually reverse after stopping. Long-term fluoride exposure can stimulate periosteal bone formation, explaining diffuse bone pain and elevated alkaline phosphatase in periostitis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hepatic toxicity, QT prolongation/arrhythmia, severe skin reactions, embryo-fetal harm, photosensitivity and skin malignancy with long-term exposure, skeletal fluorosis/periostitis, visual toxicity, and extensive CYP interactions require active monitoring.

Contraindications

  • Numerous CYP substrates and strong inducers are contraindicated because voriconazole can either dangerously raise the partner or be reduced to ineffective exposure. Check the current label rather than memorizing a partial list.

  • Correct potassium, magnesium, and calcium before treatment when possible; avoid in a high-risk proarrhythmic setting unless benefit and monitoring justify it.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give oral tablets at least one hour before or after food as labeled. Verify loading doses, trough timing after steady state, liver tests, ECG/electrolytes, visual symptoms, skin protection, and every interacting medicine.

  • For therapy beyond 28 days, monitor visual function as labeled. Escalate new severe bone pain, phototoxic lesions, changing skin lesions, confusion, jaundice, or palpitations.

Interactions

  • CYP2C19/2C9/3A inhibitors or substrates create extensive bidirectional interactions with tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus, warfarin, statins, benzodiazepines, opioids, kinase inhibitors, and antiarrhythmics.

  • Rifampin, carbamazepine, long-acting barbiturates, St. John's wort, and other strong inducers can cause treatment failure and may be contraindicated.

Key Labs

  • Voriconazole trough for invasive/prolonged therapy or suspected failure/toxicity, AST/ALT/alkaline phosphatase/bilirubin, potassium/magnesium/calcium, ECG when indicated, renal function/formulation review, species/susceptibility, and skin/visual assessment.

  • Elevated alkaline phosphatase plus diffuse bone pain during prolonged therapy should prompt periostitis evaluation rather than being assumed to be hepatic alone.

Nclex Traps

  • Voriconazole is strong for Aspergillus but inactive against Mucorales.

  • Transient visual changes are common; persistent optic symptoms or long-term therapy require formal monitoring.

Class Pathway

  • Lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase inhibition

  • Ergosterol depletion

  • Primary mold-active therapy for invasive Aspergillus with nonlinear exposure

Description

Voriconazole is a mold-active triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP51), depletes ergosterol, and destabilizes the cell membrane; it is a principal therapy for invasive aspergillosis. It penetrates tissues including CNS, but adult metabolism is nonlinear and strongly influenced by CYP2C19 plus CYP2C9/3A4, so two patients receiving the same dose can have breakthrough-level or toxic-level exposure. Early transient visual changes are common; prolonged exposure adds photosensitivity, skin-cancer risk, periostitis from fluoride, neuropathy, hepatic injury, and drug interactions. Voriconazole has no meaningful Mucorales activity, so differentiating Aspergillus from mucormycosis is a treatment-critical diagnostic step.

Adverse Effects

  • Visual disturbance, photophobia, color change, hallucinations, confusion, headache, nausea, rash, photosensitivity, and liver-test elevation.

  • Severe hepatotoxicity, QT-mediated dysrhythmia, SJS/TEN/DRESS, skin cancer with chronic phototoxicity, periostitis, neuropathy, and interaction-mediated toxicity.
