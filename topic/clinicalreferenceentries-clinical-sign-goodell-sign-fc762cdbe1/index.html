Definition

Goodell sign is a palpated cervical sign. It is a clue to early pregnancy-related cervical softening. A positive finding means the cervix feels softer than the nonpregnant cervix.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Goodell sign means the cervix feels softer than the nonpregnant cervix.

Why It Matters

The observed Goodell sign finding can signal early pregnancy-related cervical softening. Progesterone, vascularity, and stromal remodeling soften cervical collagen. Goodell sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • early pregnancy-related cervical softening

Assessment Method

During an indicated bimanual examination, assess cervical consistency gently.

Positive Finding

The cervix feels softer than the nonpregnant cervix.

Mechanism

Progesterone, vascularity, and stromal remodeling soften cervical collagen.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Goodell sign finding raises concern for early pregnancy-related cervical softening because progesterone, vascularity, and stromal remodeling soften cervical collagen. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Goodell sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Goodell sign to focus evaluation of early pregnancy-related cervical softening and the reviewed alternatives: infection, prelabor cervical change, medication effect. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During an indicated bimanual examination, assess cervical consistency gently. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Goodell sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Examiner experience and parity affect consistency. Therefore, Goodell sign cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related cervical softening by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • infection

  • prelabor cervical change

  • medication effect

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as infection, prelabor cervical change, medication effect.

False Negative Situations

Goodell sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of early pregnancy-related cervical softening.

Modern Status

Historical presumptive sign; subjective and not diagnostic. Goodell sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related cervical softening. Apply the reviewed limitation for Goodell sign: Examiner experience and parity affect consistency.

Safety Precautions

Observe Goodell sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: During an indicated bimanual examination, assess cervical consistency gently. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Avoid unnecessary repeated cervical examination, especially with bleeding or ruptured membranes.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Goodell sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The cervix feels softer than the nonpregnant cervix. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: During an indicated bimanual examination, assess cervical consistency gently. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, early pregnancy-related cervical softening, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
