Used To Treat

Reduction of cardiovascular events in adults with established cardiovascular disease and additional LDL-C reduction in label-defined adults and pediatric patients with primary hyperlipidemia, heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, or homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia.

Mechanism

Secreted PCSK9 binds the epidermal-growth-factor-like A domain of the LDL receptor. When the complex enters an endosome, PCSK9 prevents the receptor from changing conformation and recycling, so receptor and LDL are degraded together. Evolocumab sequesters free PCSK9 before receptor binding, thereby increasing the number of surface LDL receptors without changing the LDLR gene itself. More receptors accelerate removal of LDL and ApoB-containing remnants, which explains the large LDL fall. Antibody concentration falls over weeks, explaining recurring every-two-week or monthly dosing. In HoFH, response tracks residual receptor function because the antibody cannot preserve a receptor that is not made or cannot function. Antibodies are degraded to peptides and amino acids, so kidney or CYP clearance does not drive ordinary dose adjustment and therefore classic CYP interactions are not expected.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity, including angioedema, can occur. Stop and seek urgent care for facial or airway swelling, generalized hives, breathing difficulty, or systemic reaction. Latex-sensitive patients must check the exact device components in current packaging.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to evolocumab or an excipient is the principal contraindication.

  • Do not use the large monthly volume presentation without following product-specific administration instructions; multiple injections may be needed within a defined time window.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication, dose schedule, last dose, storage, device, expiration, appearance, injection site, complete delivery, and sharps disposal. Warm the device only as labeled and never use external heat.

  • Document baseline and follow-up LDL. A missed dose is handled according to how late it is and the schedule; use the current label rather than restarting by guess.

Interactions

  • No clinically important CYP interactions are expected. Background statin, ezetimibe, or other lipid therapy is usually continued because the pathways are complementary.

  • Immunologic reactions and other biologics still require medication and allergy review even when metabolic interactions are absent.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel and percentage LDL change; no routine serum evolocumab or PCSK9 concentration is needed. Monitor injection and hypersensitivity symptoms.

  • For HoFH, interpret response in the context of receptor function and concurrent apheresis or specialist therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • Evolocumab binds circulating PCSK9 protein; it does not silence PCSK9 mRNA like inclisiran.

  • Monthly dosing is not one ordinary pen unless the specific presentation supplies the full dose. Verify device and volume.

Description

Evolocumab, branded Repatha, is a subcutaneous fully human monoclonal antibody that binds PCSK9 in plasma. PCSK9 normally attaches to hepatic LDL receptors and redirects them from endosomal recycling to lysosomal destruction; evolocumab preserves those receptors, so each receptor can clear many more LDL particles. It therefore produces large additional LDL reductions when added to statin or other therapy and has demonstrated reduction of myocardial infarction, stroke, and coronary revascularization in established cardiovascular disease. Typical schedules are 140 mg every two weeks or 420 mg monthly, with HoFH-specific escalation rules because receptor biology and particle production differ. Its effect depends partly on functional LDL receptors, so receptor-negative HoFH may respond less. Because it is a protein cleared by proteolysis rather than CYP enzymes, classic metabolic interactions are minimal, but injection technique, access, storage, allergy, and schedule remain essential because an incompletely delivered or missed dose cannot preserve receptors.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site erythema, pain, bruising, nasopharyngitis, upper-respiratory symptoms, influenza, back pain, and diabetes-related complaints are commonly reported.

  • Serious hypersensitivity is uncommon. Improper storage, incomplete injection, or missed dosing can produce apparent treatment failure without a pharmacologic adverse event.
