Summary

Complement levels is a high-yield Hematology/Immunology diagnostic reference. C3/C4 and related complement testing used in autoimmune, immune-complex, infection, or hereditary complement disorders.

Quick Answer

Complement levels: C3/C4 and related complement testing used in autoimmune, immune-complex, infection, or hereditary complement disorders. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Low C3/C4/CH50

    Meaning

    Complement consumption or deficiency is suggested, seen in immune-complex disease, lupus activity, infection, hereditary deficiency, or liver/synthesis issues.

  • Label

    Normal

    Meaning

    Complement consumption is not evident at that draw, though autoimmune disease can still be active by other markers.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Pair with lupus symptoms, urinalysis/proteinuria, creatinine, anti-dsDNA, infection pattern, and hereditary infection history.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • C3/C4 and related complement testing used in autoimmune, immune-complex, infection, or hereditary complement disorders.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
