Class

Cytidine-analog DNA/RNA methyltransferase inhibitor and hypomethylating agent

Used To Treat

Injectable azacitidine for myelodysplastic syndromes and selected myeloid disease; oral azacitidine for continued maintenance of first-remission AML after intensive chemotherapy when curative therapy is not completed. Formulations are not interchangeable.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Never substitute oral Onureg for IV/SQ azacitidine or vice versa. Monitor severe anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, infection/bleeding, renal and hepatic toxicity, GI losses, tumor lysis, and embryo-fetal risk; injectable product is contraindicated in advanced malignant hepatic tumors.

Mechanism

After cellular uptake, azacitidine is phosphorylated to nucleotide triphosphates. Most enters RNA, disrupting RNA methylation, stability, and protein synthesis; a converted fraction enters DNA and covalently traps DNA methyltransferases, causing passive hypomethylation during replication, altered gene expression, differentiation, and apoptosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Read the product, route, indication, daily schedule, and cycle length aloud during verification; identical generic names do not mean dose equivalence.

  • Premedicate and monitor nausea/vomiting as ordered, rotate SQ sites, trend marrow nadirs/recovery, and teach that benefit may require several cycles.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential/platelets before and during cycles, creatinine/electrolytes, liver tests, pregnancy testing when applicable, and tumor-lysis labs when indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • Onureg is not an oral replacement for Vidaza and injectable azacitidine is not an IV version of the oral AML-maintenance schedule.

  • Counts can worsen before response; cycle decisions use nadir, recovery, marrow, and protocol rather than one low CBC alone.

Description

Azacitidine is a cytidine analog that enters RNA and DNA, inhibits RNA/DNA methyltransferases, lowers abnormal methylation, and can restore differentiation or tumor-suppressor gene expression while directly killing dividing myeloid cells. The oral and injectable products have radically different exposure, indication, dose, and schedule; substitution can be fatal or ineffective.
