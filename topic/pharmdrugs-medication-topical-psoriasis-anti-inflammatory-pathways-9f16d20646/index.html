Used To Treat

Plaque psoriasis with selection based on site, severity, age, formulation, potency, surface area and need for steroid-sparing maintenance.

Description

Topical psoriasis therapy can slow keratinocyte overproduction, suppress immune transcription, or both. Calcipotriene activates nuclear vitamin-D receptors to normalize keratinocyte differentiation. Triamcinolone activates glucocorticoid receptors and broadly suppresses inflammatory genes but can atrophy skin and suppress the HPA axis. Tapinarof activates AhR, and roflumilast inhibits PDE4. Their mechanisms, anatomic suitability, laboratory risks, and long-term strategies are distinct.

Mechanism

VDR-RXR complexes bind vitamin-D response elements and restrain keratinocyte proliferation while promoting differentiation. Cytosolic glucocorticoid receptors enter the nucleus, transactivate anti-inflammatory genes and transrepress NF-kappa-B/AP-1 signaling. AhR-ARNT signaling modifies barrier and immune genes. PDE4 inhibition preserves cAMP and activates PKA-mediated anti-inflammatory transcription. Combination therapy can improve efficacy while reducing cumulative steroid exposure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Large-area calcipotriene can cause hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria. Potent or occluded topical corticosteroids can cause atrophy, striae, infection masking, glaucoma/cataract exposure, HPA-axis suppression and pediatric growth effects. Roflumilast is contraindicated in Child-Pugh B/C hepatic impairment; formulation and intertriginous/scalp labeling matter.

Adverse Effects

  • Irritation, burning, pruritus and contact dermatitis

  • Calcipotriene: hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria with excessive exposure

  • Corticosteroids: atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, hypopigmentation, infection and HPA suppression

  • Tapinarof folliculitis; roflumilast diarrhea/headache/insomnia

Contraindications

  • Calcipotriene in hypercalcemia or vitamin-D toxicity and avoid excessive weekly dose

  • Avoid topical steroid on untreated infection or under unplanned occlusion

  • Roflumilast in Child-Pugh B/C hepatic impairment; clarify product age/site restrictions

Nursing Essentials

  • Map body site and BSA: eyelids, face, folds, scalp, palms/soles and thick plaques need different vehicles and potency.

  • Teach fingertip-unit quantity, planned duration, steroid-free intervals and the difference between plaque control and cure.

Interactions

  • Calcium/vitamin D supplements or other VDR agonists can add hypercalcemia risk.

  • Salicylic acid can inactivate calcipotriene in some mixtures; use validated combinations.

  • Other steroids increase cumulative HPA/skin toxicity; CYP inhibitors may matter for extensive roflumilast exposure.

Key Labs

  • Calcium/urine calcium when calcipotriene exposure is large or symptoms occur

  • Morning cortisol/ACTH stimulation when extensive potent-steroid suppression is suspected

  • Liver status for roflumilast and serial BSA/severity assessment

Nclex Traps

  • A topical steroid can suppress the systemic HPA axis when potency, area, duration, occlusion and age align.

  • Calcipotriene is a vitamin D analog, not a corticosteroid.

  • Inverse psoriasis treatment should not be copied from a thick extensor-plaque regimen.
