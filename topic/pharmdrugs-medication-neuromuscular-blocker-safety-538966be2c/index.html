Used To Treat

Facilitation of tracheal intubation, surgery, or mechanical ventilation when continuous airway support, sedation, analgesia, and quantitative neuromuscular monitoring are available.

Description

Neuromuscular blockers paralyze skeletal muscle at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors but do not cause unconsciousness, amnesia, or analgesia. Succinylcholine depolarizes the end plate and holds it refractory; nondepolarizing agents competitively prevent acetylcholine from activating the receptor. The safety rule is absolute: paralysis can hide pain, awareness, seizures, respiratory distress and neurologic examination findings.

Mechanism

Depolarizing blockade opens nicotinic channels, produces fasciculation, then prevents repolarized reactivation. Nondepolarizing blockade occupies nicotinic receptor sites without opening the channel. Reversal depends on drug and depth: acetylcholinesterase inhibition raises acetylcholine to compete with residual blocker, while sugammadex encapsulates rocuronium or vecuronium. Neither strategy replaces ventilation until quantitative recovery is adequate.

Boxed Warning

Accidental neuromuscular-blocker administration can be fatal. Store and label these drugs to prevent selection errors; ensure immediate ventilation and intubation capability. Continuous sedation/analgesia and objective train-of-four monitoring are required. Succinylcholine adds malignant-hyperthermia, hyperkalemic-arrest and prolonged-apnea risks; nondepolarizers add anaphylaxis and prolonged weakness.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm sedation and analgesia before and throughout paralysis, document eye protection and repositioning, and reassess whether blockade remains necessary.

  • Use quantitative train-of-four information with airway, ventilation and clinical recovery; movement alone does not prove safe pharyngeal strength.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative train-of-four ratio, continuous ventilation/oxygenation and hemodynamics; potassium and acid-base status when succinylcholine is considered; renal/hepatic function and temperature because clearance and blockade duration can change.

Nclex Traps

  • A paralytic is never a sedative.

  • A patient can be awake while completely unable to move.

  • Four visible twitches do not automatically prove a train-of-four ratio of at least 0.9.
