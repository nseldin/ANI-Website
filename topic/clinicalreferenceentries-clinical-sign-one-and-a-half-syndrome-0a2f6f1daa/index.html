Definition

One-and-a-half syndrome is an ocular motor syndrome-sign. It is a clue to pontine gaze center plus medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion. A positive finding means one eye cannot move horizontally and the other can only abduct.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive One-and-a-half syndrome means one eye cannot move horizontally and the other can only abduct.

Why It Matters

The One-and-a-half syndrome pattern can signal pontine gaze center plus medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion. A lesion combines an ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy with an ipsilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia. One-and-a-half syndrome cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Assessment Method

Assess horizontal gaze in both directions.

One eye cannot move horizontally and the other can only abduct.

Mechanism

A lesion combines an ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy with an ipsilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia.

Evidence Limitations
