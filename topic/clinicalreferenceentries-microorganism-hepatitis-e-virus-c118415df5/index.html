Definition

Hepatitis E virus is a clinically important virus associated with acute hepatitis E, pregnancy-associated severe hepatitis, and chronic hepatitis in immunocompromised hosts. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Safe water, sanitation, hand hygiene, and thoroughly cooked food reduce exposure; vaccine availability and indications are jurisdiction-specific. The reviewed route description is fecal oral transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 14 to 70 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    HEV is usually acquired enterically and causes immune-mediated liver injury; severity rises in some pregnancies and chronic infection can occur with immunosuppression. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Hepatitis E virus is associated with acute hepatitis E, pregnancy-associated severe hepatitis, and chronic hepatitis in immunocompromised hosts. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
