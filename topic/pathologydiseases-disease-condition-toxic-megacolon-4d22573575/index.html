Definition

Toxic megacolon develops when severe colonic inflammation causes nonobstructive dilation, systemic toxicity, and loss of wall integrity. The characteristic pattern is abdominal distention and tenderness with fever, tachycardia, leukocytosis, anemia, dehydration, and radiographic colonic dilation. Confirm or stage it with urgent abdominal radiograph or CT without delaying surgical review; distinguish it from mechanical obstruction, ileus, acute colonic pseudo-obstruction, and uncomplicated severe colitis by transition point, systemic toxicity, and inflammatory context. The priority is to stop antimotility and precipitating medicines because perforation can complicate Toxic megacolon. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Abdominal pain pattern, distention, nausea/vomiting, diarrhea or constipation, stool/emesis blood, jaundice, dysphagia, poor intake, dehydration, or guarding are the useful GI clues.

  • Escalate rigid abdomen, peritonitis signs, GI bleeding, shock, severe dehydration, fever with jaundice, or worsening mental status.

Diagnostics

  • urgent abdominal radiograph or CT without delaying surgical review

  • serial abdominal examination, circumference, CBC, electrolytes, lactate, and inflammatory markers

  • stool testing and disease evaluation for inflammatory bowel disease or infectious colitis

  • Distinguish Toxic megacolon from mechanical obstruction, ileus, acute colonic pseudo-obstruction, and uncomplicated severe colitis by transition point, systemic toxicity, and inflammatory context.

Labs

  • hemoglobin may fall with bleeding

  • AST/ALT and bilirubin may rise with hepatic/biliary injury

  • lipase rises in pancreatitis

  • albumin and INR reflect liver synthetic function

Treatments

  • stop antimotility and precipitating medicines

  • resuscitate, correct electrolytes, rest the bowel, and treat the inflammatory or infectious cause

  • obtain early surgery and proceed to colectomy for perforation, uncontrolled bleeding, or failed intensive therapy

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for peritoneal signs, increasing dilation, shock, rising lactate, severe leukocytosis, or free air because urgent surgical colectomy evaluation may be lifesaving.

  • Stop nonessential antimotility and bowel-slowing medicines as directed because suppressing motility can worsen colonic dilation and toxin retention.

  • Keep the patient nothing by mouth, establish intravenous access, and begin ordered antibiotic and fluid therapy because fulminant colitis causes systemic toxicity and volume loss.

  • Measure abdominal girth and assess distention, tenderness, bowel sounds, stool output, fever, and mental status frequently to detect progression toward perforation.

  • Monitor white blood cell count, hemoglobin, electrolytes, lactate, creatinine, vital signs, and serial abdominal imaging because worsening dilation and organ dysfunction guide surgery timing.

Complications

  • perforation can complicate Toxic megacolon.

  • peritonitis can complicate Toxic megacolon.

  • sepsis can complicate Toxic megacolon.

  • hemorrhage can complicate Toxic megacolon.

  • multiorgan failure can complicate Toxic megacolon.

Red Flags

  • Peritoneal signs or free intraperitoneal air

  • Increasing colonic dilation or rapidly worsening distention

  • Shock, rising lactate, or worsening organ dysfunction

  • Severe leukocytosis with declining clinical status

Patient Education

  • Teach the patient not to self-treat severe infectious diarrhea with antimotility medicine without clinician advice.

  • Explain that increasing distention, severe pain, fever, faintness, or decreasing stool during severe colitis can signal dangerous loss of bowel movement.

Nclex Traps

  • Antidiarrheals and opioids can worsen colonic dilation

  • Temporary symptom improvement does not remove the need for serial imaging and surgical readiness
