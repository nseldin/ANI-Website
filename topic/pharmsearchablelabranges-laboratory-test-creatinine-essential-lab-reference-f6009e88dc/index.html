Name

Creatinine

Range

Adult serum creatinine reference intervals vary by laboratory, assay, muscle mass, age, sex, and population. Healthy pregnancy generally runs lower, so use the printed interval and the patient's baseline rather than a universal normal.

Why

An increased or rising creatinine result signals reduced filtration or altered creatinine handling and raises concern for acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, poor renal perfusion, urinary obstruction, or medication effect. Because creatinine production depends partly on muscle mass and diet and creatinine-based estimates become less reliable when the concentration is changing, a normal-looking value can miss kidney dysfunction in a low-muscle-mass patient, and a rise is not itself the injury mechanism. Promptly assess urine output, fluid and perfusion status, weight, edema and lung sounds, medicines and contrast exposure, potassium and other electrolytes, and obstruction symptoms; cause-directed evaluation can include repeat laboratory testing, eGFR when clinically valid, urinalysis or other urine tests, urine albumin, and ultrasound for suspected obstruction as ordered. Escalate immediately for rapid deterioration with oliguria or anuria, hyperkalemia or ECG changes, pulmonary edema or respiratory distress, altered consciousness, or uremic symptoms. A single creatinine value does not diagnose the cause, distinguish acute from chronic kidney disease, or prove normal kidney function; interpret baseline and trend, urine findings, and clinical context, and recognize that creatinine-based eGFR may not perform reliably when creatinine is unstable, including during acute kidney injury and pregnancy.

High Causes

  • Acute kidney injury

  • Chronic kidney disease

  • Poor kidney perfusion or dehydration

  • Urinary obstruction

  • Medication effect or nephrotoxic exposure

  • Muscle injury or high creatinine generation

Low Causes

  • Low muscle mass

  • Pregnancy

  • Low protein/muscle states

Critical Concerns

  • A rapid rise with oliguria or anuria, hyperkalemia or ECG changes, fluid-overload respiratory distress, altered consciousness, or uremic symptoms requires urgent escalation.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess urine output, perfusion and volume status, weight, edema, lung sounds, blood pressure, nephrotoxic or renally cleared medicines, contrast exposure, electrolytes, and obstruction symptoms.

  • Trend serum creatinine with an appropriate eGFR, urinalysis, urine albumin, and imaging or other cause-directed evaluation as ordered; do not use creatinine alone to declare kidney function normal.

Nclex Traps

  • A creatinine that looks normal in pregnancy, a child, or a low-muscle-mass older adult may still be concerning because the expected baseline is lower.

  • Creatinine-based eGFR is less reliable during rapidly changing kidney function and is not validated for routine pregnancy interpretation.
