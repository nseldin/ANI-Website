Summary

INR (international normalized ratio) is a high-yield Hematology diagnostic reference. Standardized warfarin anticoagulation measure and bleeding risk clue.

Quick Answer

INR (international normalized ratio): Standardized warfarin anticoagulation measure and bleeding risk clue. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Low/subtherapeutic

    Meaning

    Warfarin effect may be inadequate for the ordered goal, raising clot risk for clients who require anticoagulation.

  • Label

    Therapeutic

    Meaning

    Anticoagulation is within the ordered target range for that indication.

  • Label

    High/supratherapeutic

    Meaning

    Bleeding risk rises. Assess bleeding, falls, interacting drugs, diet changes, liver disease, and reversal plan if severe.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Standardized warfarin anticoagulation measure and bleeding risk clue.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
