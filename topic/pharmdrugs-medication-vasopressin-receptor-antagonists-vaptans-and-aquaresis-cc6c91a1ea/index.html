Used To Treat

Tolvaptan as Samsca treats selected clinically significant euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia; tolvaptan as Jynarque is a separate REMS product for adults at risk of rapidly progressing ADPKD. IV conivaptan treats euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia under its label and local availability.

Description

Vaptans remove water more selectively than sodium, a process called aquaresis. Vasopressin normally binds V2 receptors on collecting-duct principal cells, activates Gs-adenylyl cyclase-cAMP-protein kinase A signaling, and inserts aquaporin-2 channels into the apical membrane. Tolvaptan blocks V2; conivaptan blocks both V2 and vascular V1a receptors. Without aquaporin-2 recruitment, the collecting duct becomes less water permeable, dilute urine increases, and serum sodium can rise. This is useful when vasopressin-mediated water retention causes euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia, but it can overcorrect sodium fast enough to cause osmotic demyelination. It is not a replacement for urgent hypertonic-saline treatment when immediate sodium correction is required. Product identity matters: Samsca and Jynarque contain the same molecule but have different indications, doses, safety programs, and duration rules.

Mechanism

V2 blockade interrupts the cAMP signal that phosphorylates aquaporin-2 trafficking proteins, so fewer water channels occupy the luminal membrane. Solute remains relatively preserved while electrolyte-free water clearance rises; thirst and oral water intake become essential defenses against excessive correction and dehydration. Conivaptan's V1a blockade can add vasodilation and infusion-related hypotension. In ADPKD, chronic V2 blockade also lowers cAMP signaling that promotes cyst-cell proliferation and fluid secretion, explaining Jynarque's disease-modifying use. In hyponatremia, however, rapid water loss can raise sodium unpredictably, especially when the underlying vasopressin stimulus suddenly resolves or other therapy is added. That causal chain explains hospital initiation for Samsca, frequent sodium monitoring, avoidance of fluid restriction during the first day, and immediate access to water unless the patient cannot respond to thirst.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - SAMSCA INITIATION AND SODIUM CORRECTION: Initiate and reinitiate Samsca in a hospital where serum sodium can be monitored. Overly rapid correction can cause osmotic demyelination with dysarthria, mutism, dysphagia, weakness, seizures, coma, and death. Patients with severe malnutrition, alcoholism, or advanced liver disease are especially vulnerable. Samsca is not approved for ADPKD; ADPKD treatment uses the separate Jynarque product and REMS because of serious liver-injury risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Thirst, dry mouth, polyuria, nocturia, dehydration, hypernatremia, hypotension, and overly rapid sodium correction follow aquaresis.

  • Tolvaptan can cause serious liver injury in the ADPKD exposure context; conivaptan commonly causes infusion-site phlebitis and can cause hypotension or hypokalemia.

Contraindications

  • Urgent need to raise sodium acutely, inability to sense or respond to thirst, hypovolemic hyponatremia, anuria, and strong CYP3A inhibitors are central product-dependent contraindications.

  • Do not use vaptans simply because sodium is low. Confirm hypotonicity, volume pattern, symptoms, cause, correction target, and whether the patient can drink and be monitored.

Nursing Essentials

  • For Samsca, document sodium, osmolality, volume state, urine studies, neurologic findings, liver context, medications, and the correction plan; initiate or reinitiate in hospital and monitor sodium and volume frequently.

  • Permit access to water and avoid fluid restriction during the first 24 hours unless a specialist protocol says otherwise, because thirst limits excessive water loss. Stop and actively relower or otherwise manage overcorrection according to protocol.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors markedly increase exposure and are contraindicated; inducers can reduce effect. Other sodium-raising treatments, diuretics, or sudden resolution of SIADH can accelerate correction.

  • Tolvaptan can affect P-glycoprotein substrates. Conivaptan is both a CYP3A substrate and inhibitor, creating a substantial interaction burden.

Key Labs

  • Serum sodium at frequent protocol-defined intervals, serum and urine osmolality, urine output, fluid balance, pressure, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, liver tests for relevant tolvaptan use, and neurologic examination.

  • The correction rate matters more than reaching a normal sodium quickly. Trend from the pretreatment value and account for urine output and any concurrent saline or diuretic.

Nclex Traps

  • Samsca is short-term hyponatremia treatment and is limited to 30 days because of liver-injury concern; Jynarque is the separate chronic ADPKD product under a REMS.

  • A vaptan is an aquaretic, not a conventional natriuretic. It can raise sodium by removing water even if total body sodium does not increase.
