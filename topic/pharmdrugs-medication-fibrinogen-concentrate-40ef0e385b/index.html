Class

Plasma-derived human fibrinogen (factor I) replacement concentrate

Used To Treat

Product-specific treatment of acute bleeding in pediatric and adult patients with congenital afibrinogenemia or hypofibrinogenemia; some products have additional perioperative indications. It is not indicated for dysfibrinogenemia unless that exact product label says otherwise.

Description

Human fibrinogen concentrate is purified factor I replacement that supplies the soluble precursor thrombin converts into fibrin. It rapidly raises Clauss functional fibrinogen in congenital deficiency without the large volume and variable fibrinogen content of plasma products. Dose is calculated from measured or estimated fibrinogen and a target based on bleeding severity and product-specific recovery.

Mechanism

Thrombin cleaves fibrinopeptides A and B from fibrinogen, exposing binding sites that let fibrin monomers polymerize. Calcium and factor XIIIa then cross-link the polymer into an elastic, fibrinolysis-resistant mesh that reinforces the platelet plug. Concentrate restores substrate for this final common pathway; it does not supply platelets, thrombin generation or factor XIII.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current RIASTAP, FIBRYGA and FESILTY labeling has no boxed warning. Thrombosis, including pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction and DVT, can occur when replacement overshoots or other risk factors coexist. Stop for anaphylaxis or severe hypersensitivity. As a human-plasma product, manufacturing reduces but cannot absolutely eliminate infectious-agent risk. Dose and target differ by product and bleeding severity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm congenital quantitative fibrinogen deficiency, exact product, actual vial potency, baseline Clauss fibrinogen, target, body weight, bleeding site and thrombosis risk; calculate rather than rounding to a generic number.

  • Recheck functional fibrinogen and clinical hemostasis after infusion, because recovery varies. Persistent bleeding may reflect dysfibrinogenemia, factor XIII deficiency, thrombocytopenia, surgical source or ongoing consumption.

Key Labs

  • Clauss functional fibrinogen before and after dosing, CBC/hemoglobin, PT/INR, aPTT, thromboelastography/ROTEM when used, factor XIII and platelet count when bleeding persists, plus thrombosis surveillance.

Nclex Traps

  • Fibrinogen concentrate supplies factor I; tranexamic acid merely protects formed fibrin from breakdown.

  • An antigenic fibrinogen concentration can look adequate while function is abnormal in dysfibrinogenemia; use functional testing and the correct indication.
