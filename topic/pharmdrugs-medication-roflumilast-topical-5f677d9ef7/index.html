Route Specific Card

true

Class

Topical phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor anti-inflammatory

Used To Treat

Plaque psoriasis including intertriginous areas with 0.3% cream from age 2; mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis with age-specific 0.15% or 0.05% cream; 0.3% foam treats seborrheic dermatitis from age 9 and scalp/body psoriasis from age 12.

Description

Topical roflumilast is a PDE4 inhibitor formulated as Zoryve cream or foam for psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, or seborrheic dermatitis. It is not oral roflumilast for COPD. PDE4 normally degrades cyclic AMP inside inflammatory cells; inhibition preserves cAMP and activates protein kinase A programs that suppress inflammatory mediator production. The strength and vehicle encode the indication and age, and clinically meaningful systemic exposure makes moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment a contraindication.

Mechanism

Roflumilast and its active N-oxide metabolite competitively inhibit PDE4, slowing cAMP hydrolysis to AMP. Higher cAMP activates PKA and CREB-related transcription while reducing NF-kappa-B-driven cytokines in keratinocytes and immune cells. The effect is anti-inflammatory rather than antimicrobial or vasoconstrictive. Topical delivery limits but does not abolish systemic adverse effects such as diarrhea, nausea, headache and insomnia.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C). Match strength and vehicle to age/disease: 0.3%, 0.15% and 0.05% creams are not interchangeable, and foam has separate indications. Monitor diarrhea, vomiting, weight/hydration and insomnia even though the product is topical.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, headache, nausea, vomiting and insomnia

  • Application-site pain or urticaria

  • Upper respiratory infection, rhinitis or conjunctivitis in younger AD populations

  • Rare hypersensitivity

Contraindications

  • Moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment, Child-Pugh B or C

  • Known serious product hypersensitivity

  • Clarify age, disease, strength, vehicle, BSA and liver status

Nursing Essentials

  • Read the tube: cream strength and foam are linked to different diagnoses and ages.

  • Apply once daily as a thin layer, including folds only when the product indication permits, and monitor systemic GI/sleep symptoms.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or dual CYP3A4/CYP1A2 inhibitors can increase exposure.

  • Strong enzyme inducers can reduce efficacy and are not recommended in systemic labeling contexts.

  • Other PDE4 inhibitors add class exposure; reconcile oral roflumilast.

Key Labs

  • Liver function and Child-Pugh assessment before use when hepatic disease is possible

  • Weight, hydration and electrolytes if vomiting/diarrhea is clinically important

  • BSA, lesion severity, itch and sleep

Nclex Traps

  • Topical roflumilast is anti-inflammatory PDE4 inhibition, not bronchodilation.

  • A topical product can still be contraindicated in Child-Pugh B/C liver disease.

  • The 0.05% cream is for AD ages 2-5; the 0.15% cream is for AD age 6 and older.
