Definition

Rooting reflex in adults is a bedside primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. A positive finding means the mouth or head turns toward the stimulus.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rooting reflex in adults means the mouth or head turns toward the stimulus.

Why It Matters

A positive Rooting reflex in adults response can signal frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. Loss of cortical inhibition releases an infant feeding-orientation reflex. Rooting reflex in adults cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.
