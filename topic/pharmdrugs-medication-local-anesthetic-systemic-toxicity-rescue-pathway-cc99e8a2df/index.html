Used To Treat

Recognition and emergency management of seizures, dysrhythmia, or cardiovascular collapse after systemic local-anesthetic exposure.

Description

The local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) rescue pathway interrupts escalating brain and cardiac sodium-channel toxicity after local-anesthetic injection or infusion by stopping exposure, supporting oxygenation and ventilation, suppressing seizures with a benzodiazepine, and delivering 20% intravenous lipid for severe toxicity. Trigger it when perioral numbness, tinnitus, metallic taste, agitation, seizure, conduction delay, ventricular dysrhythmia, hypotension, or collapse follows exposure, and call for the LAST kit and resuscitation team immediately. LAST resuscitation differs from routine ACLS: ASRA advises smaller epinephrine doses at or below 1 microgram/kg and avoidance of vasopressin, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, and additional local anesthetic. The bare word last is not enough to invoke this emergency pathway.

Mechanism

Local anesthetics block voltage-gated sodium channels in the brain and heart; at high free concentrations they also impair calcium handling, potassium channels, contractility, and mitochondrial energy production. Hypoxia and acidemia increase the active ionized fraction and worsen channel binding, so airway control directly reduces toxicity. Lipid partitions lipophilic anesthetic away from target tissue and may improve myocardial energy supply. Because tissue redistribution continues after initial recovery, recurrence remains possible and observation must outlast apparent stabilization.

Administration Timing

  • At the first credible severe signs, stop local anesthetic delivery, activate emergency help, manage airway and ventilation, and treat seizure with benzodiazepines.

  • Use the 20% lipid protocol promptly for severe LAST: commonly 1.5 mL/kg bolus then 0.25 mL/kg/min, adjusted by the ASRA checklist and limited to 12 mL/kg cumulative exposure.

  • Continue monitoring and lipid for at least 15 minutes after stability; move early toward cardiopulmonary bypass or VA-ECMO if shock or arrest remains refractory.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record agent, concentration, total mg, mg/kg, injection site, time, aspiration or test-dose findings, symptoms, and weight because systemic burden and intravascular injection change onset.

  • Use continuous ECG, hemodynamic, capnographic, and oxygenation monitoring; reassess temperature and neurologic status frequently and track cumulative lipid dose because seizure, cardiovascular toxicity, and dose-related complications can evolve separately.

  • Alert laboratory and ECMO teams after lipid because lipemia can invalidate tests and impair circuits.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG, hemodynamic, and oxygenation monitoring plus serial blood gas, lactate, glucose, electrolytes, triglycerides, lipase, temperature, and neurologic reassessment support care; no serum local-anesthetic level should delay treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • The poisoning causes seizure, coma, conduction block, ventricular dysrhythmia, shock, or arrest; rescue lipid can cause fat overload, pancreatitis, lung injury, and assay or circuit interference.

Contraindications

  • Do not delay lifesaving support for a theoretical lipid nutrition contraindication; avoid propofol as a substitute for lipid in an unstable patient because its cardiovascular depression can worsen collapse.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Refractory dysrhythmia, shock, arrest, or recurrent symptoms after lipid requires immediate toxicology, anesthesia, perfusion, and VA-ECMO escalation.

Interactions

  • Large epinephrine doses, vasopressin, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, and more local anesthetic can worsen LAST physiology.

  • Sedatives may suppress seizures but also depress circulation; lipid can alter free concentrations of other lipophilic drugs and distort assays.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Neuraxial and regional procedures increase exposure opportunity; maternal oxygenation and circulation are the fastest way to protect fetal perfusion.

  • Population

    Infants, older adults, or low muscle mass

    Note

    Reduced distribution reserve and clearance can increase free local-anesthetic exposure, so dose ceilings and early symptoms deserve special attention.

  • Population

    Cardiac, hepatic, or renal disease

    Note

    Conduction disease and impaired metabolism or protein binding reduce tolerance and can delay clearance and recurrence.

Evidence Limitations

  • LAST recommendations combine physiologic evidence, animal studies, cases, and expert consensus because randomized resuscitation trials are not feasible.

Nclex Traps

  • LAST-specific resuscitation is not identical to routine ACLS.

  • The bare English word last must not trigger this pathway because the emergency diagnosis requires a compatible local-anesthetic exposure and toxic syndrome.

  • A seizure after a nerve block can precede cardiovascular collapse even when the injection site looks normal.

Regulatory Status

This is a professional-guideline educational pathway. Intravenous lipid is FDA-marketed for nutrition; its LAST rescue use is off-label but recommended by AHA and ASRA emergency guidance.

Boxed Warning

ACTIVE LAST WARNING: seizure, dysrhythmia, or collapse after local anesthetic requires immediate emergency response, ventilation, seizure control, and the ASRA lipid checklist. Do not use routine large epinephrine doses because they can worsen dysrhythmia and acidosis, and do not delay VA-ECMO planning when refractory cardiotoxic shock may require extracorporeal circulation.
