Class

Topical selective histamine-H1 antagonist with mast-cell-stabilizing activity

Used To Treat

Ocular itching from allergic conjunctivitis; it does not treat contact-lens irritation, bacterial infection, acute glaucoma or an undiagnosed painful red eye.

Description

Olopatadine ophthalmic is an antihistamine eye drop that blocks H1-mediated itching and vascular permeability while stabilizing conjunctival mast cells to reduce further mediator release. It is targeted to allergic conjunctivitis, where itching is prominent and discharge is usually watery/stringy. It should not be used as a universal redness drop or to cover pain, photophobia, reduced vision or purulent discharge.

Mechanism

Allergen cross-linking of IgE on conjunctival mast cells releases histamine and other mediators. Olopatadine competitively blocks H1 receptors on sensory nerves and vessels, reducing itch, vasodilation and permeability, and also limits mast-cell degranulation. Low systemic exposure means little sedation compared with oral first-generation antihistamines.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: For topical ocular use only. Do not use to treat contact-lens-related irritation and do not wear lenses while the eye is red. Preservative can be absorbed by soft lenses; remove them and wait the product-specific interval before reinsertion. Pain, photophobia, vision loss or purulent discharge requires another diagnosis.

Adverse Effects

  • Transient burning/stinging, dry eye, blurred vision, headache, bitter taste, pharyngitis, hyperemia and hypersensitivity.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to olopatadine or formulation components. Clarify contact-lens injury/infection and red-flag symptoms inconsistent with uncomplicated allergy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm that itching rather than pain or vision loss is the dominant symptom. Avoid lenses while eyes are red and do not use the medicine to treat lens-related irritation.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Systemic interactions are minimal. Other antihistamines may add dryness. Separate topical eye medicines and follow lens/preservative timing.

Key Labs

  • Symptom pattern and ocular examination

  • Visual acuity/corneal evaluation when red flags occur

  • No routine laboratory monitoring

Nclex Traps

  • Itching is the hallmark symptom that supports allergic conjunctivitis.

  • Olopatadine is not an antibiotic and does not treat contact-lens keratitis.

  • Do not insert lenses into a red inflamed eye.
