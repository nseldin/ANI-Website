Class

FGF23-neutralizing human IgG1 monoclonal antibody

Used To Treat

X-linked hypophosphatemia in adults and children age 6 months and older, and FGF23-related tumor-induced osteomalacia in adults and children age 2 years and older when the causative tumor cannot be localized or curatively treated.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current CRYSVITA labeling has no boxed warning. Concomitant oral phosphate or active vitamin-D analogs are contraindicated because severe hyperphosphatemia and nephrocalcinosis can result. Do not initiate when phosphate is already normal/high or in severe/end-stage renal disease. Monitor hyperphosphatemia, nephrocalcinosis, hypersensitivity, injection reactions and restless legs symptoms.

Mechanism

FGF23 normally binds an FGFR/Klotho receptor complex in renal tubules, internalizes sodium-phosphate cotransporters and suppresses 1-alpha-hydroxylase while increasing 24-hydroxylase. Burosumab binds circulating FGF23 and prevents receptor activation. TmP/GFR and serum phosphate rise, calcitriol production recovers and intestinal calcium/phosphate absorption improves. Overshooting produces hyperphosphatemia and ectopic mineralization risk.

Nursing Essentials

  • Stop oral phosphate and active vitamin D at least one week before initiation, verify fasting age-adjusted phosphate below normal, renal function, calcium, PTH and nephrocalcinosis status.

  • Draw phosphate at the product-specific point in the dosing interval; titrating from a random post-dose value can overestimate baseline and cause unsafe dosing.

Key Labs

  • Fasting serum phosphate, calcium, creatinine/eGFR, alkaline phosphatase, PTH, 25-OH and 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D as directed, TmP/GFR and renal ultrasound for nephrocalcinosis.

Nclex Traps

  • Burosumab raises phosphate by stopping renal wasting; it is not oral phosphate replacement.

  • Serum FGF23 may rise after antibody treatment and is not the dose target; phosphate and clinical mineralization guide care.

Description

Burosumab-twza is a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody that neutralizes excess fibroblast growth factor 23. In XLH and tumor-induced osteomalacia, too much FGF23 wastes phosphate through the kidney and suppresses calcitriol synthesis; blocking it restores tubular phosphate reabsorption and increases 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D so rickets or osteomalacia can mineralize.
