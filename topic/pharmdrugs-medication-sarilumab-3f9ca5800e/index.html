Class

Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody against soluble and membrane IL-6 receptors

Used To Treat

Adults with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis after inadequate response or intolerance to one or more DMARDs, adults with polymyalgia rheumatica after inadequate corticosteroid response or inability to tolerate taper, and label-specified polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis. It is subcutaneous and not interchangeable with tocilizumab dosing or indications.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: risk of serious infections. Bacterial, viral, invasive fungal, TB, and other opportunistic infections may cause hospitalization or death. Test for latent TB and treat before therapy, do not start through an active infection, monitor closely, and interrupt sarilumab until a serious infection is controlled. CRP and fever may be blunted, so focal pain, cough, dyspnea, hypotension, confusion, or wound change requires prompt evaluation even with reassuring inflammatory markers.

Mechanism

IL-6 bound to membrane or soluble IL-6R alpha recruits gp130, activating JAK1/JAK2/TYK2 and STAT3, MAPK, and PI3K pathways. Sarilumab binds IL-6R alpha with high affinity and blocks both classical and trans-signaling. Hepatic acute-phase output falls, hepcidin and inflammatory anemia can improve, and leukocyte recruitment changes. Chronic IL-6 suppresses CYP expression; receptor blockade can restore CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4 activity, increasing clearance of their substrates. IL-6 also contributes to bowel mucosal repair, so diverticulitis, glucocorticoids, and NSAIDs increase concern for GI perforation. Decreased measured neutrophils can reflect altered trafficking, but serious infection remains a clinical warning.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and weight/product criteria, injection technique and rotation, CBC/liver/lipid schedule, infection status, and corticosteroid-taper plan.

  • Treat new abdominal pain as potentially serious and assess infection clinically rather than relying on CRP; monitor mood, glucose, bone, and adrenal risk as steroids taper.

Contraindications

  • Known sarilumab hypersensitivity; product-specific low ANC/platelets or high transaminases preclude initiation, and active serious infection requires delay.

  • Clarify diverticulitis, bowel ulcer, steroid/NSAID use, liver disease, dyslipidemia, TB/hepatitis, vaccination, and narrow-index CYP therapy.

Interactions

  • Do not combine with biologic DMARDs or JAK inhibitors; avoid live vaccines.

  • Restored CYP metabolism can lower warfarin, oral-contraceptive, statin, theophylline, or other substrate exposure; narrow-index drugs need effect or concentration review.

Key Labs

  • CBC with ANC/platelets, ALT/AST, lipids, TB assessment, hepatitis/infection context, and disease activity

  • Abdominal findings, fever-independent infection cues, steroid dose and adrenal symptoms, and narrow-index medication effect

Nclex Traps

  • Sarilumab and tocilizumab share an IL-6R target but are not route-, dose-, or indication-interchangeable.

  • A falling CRP is expected pharmacology and cannot by itself distinguish disease control from masked infection.

Description

Sarilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds soluble and membrane IL-6 receptor alpha, preventing IL-6 from assembling a signaling complex with gp130. It suppresses JAK-STAT3 acute-phase and inflammatory programs, reducing rheumatoid and polymyalgia-rheumatica activity and helping some patients taper corticosteroids. Because IL-6 drives CRP, fibrinogen, fever, hepcidin, platelet and leukocyte behavior, sarilumab can normalize inflammatory markers while also causing neutropenia, liver-test and lipid changes. The drug can make infection appear biochemically quiet and can restore CYP metabolism previously suppressed by inflammation. This explains why a normal CRP and a stable prescription list do not guarantee unchanged infection or drug exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Neutropenia, elevated ALT, injection-site erythema, upper-respiratory infection, urinary infection, hyperlipidemia, and thrombocytopenia

  • Serious/opportunistic infection, TB, GI perforation, anaphylaxis, liver injury, and clinically important CYP-substrate reduction
