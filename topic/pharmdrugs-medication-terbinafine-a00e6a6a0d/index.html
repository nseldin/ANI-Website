Used To Treat

Oral therapy for dermatophyte onychomycosis of toenails or fingernails; topical products treat selected tinea pedis, cruris or corporis and are not equivalent to oral nail therapy.

Mechanism

Fungal squalene epoxidase converts squalene to 2,3-oxidosqualene upstream of lanosterol and ergosterol. Terbinafine inhibits this enzyme noncompetitively, producing membrane sterol deficiency and fungicidal squalene accumulation. This target is not fungal CYP51, so terbinafine's inhibition of human CYP2D6 is an interaction property rather than the antifungal mechanism. Drug persists in keratinized tissue after plasma concentrations fall.

Contraindications

  • Chronic or active liver disease for oral tablets

  • History of serious allergy to oral terbinafine

  • Clarify renal impairment, cytopenia, lupus, depression and major CYP2D6 substrate therapy

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm dermatophyte onychomycosis with KOH, culture or nail pathology before a long systemic course.

  • Teach liver-injury symptoms and specifically ask about taste, smell, appetite, weight and mood rather than waiting for spontaneous reporting.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibition can raise tricyclics, SSRIs, beta blockers, class 1C antiarrhythmics and other CYP2D6 substrates.

  • Rifampin increases terbinafine clearance; cimetidine decreases clearance.

  • Warfarin INR changes and caffeine exposure changes have been reported; monitor clinically.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT before oral therapy and during treatment when clinically indicated

  • CBC for prolonged courses, recurrent infection, fever or bruising

  • KOH/culture/nail pathology and clinical healthy-nail outgrowth

Nclex Traps

  • Terbinafine inhibits squalene epoxidase, not CYP51.

  • A mycologically cured nail remains discolored until healthy keratin grows out.

  • Taste loss can cause malnutrition and depression and may persist after discontinuation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Oral terbinafine is contraindicated in chronic or active liver disease. Liver failure requiring transplant or causing death has occurred; obtain pretreatment transaminases and stop for biochemical or clinical liver injury. Stop and assess new taste/smell disturbance, severe rash, fever, mouth sores, bruising, depression, lupus symptoms or thrombotic microangiopathy signs.

Description

Terbinafine is an allylamine antifungal that kills many dermatophytes by blocking squalene epoxidase, an early enzyme in fungal ergosterol synthesis. Ergosterol depletion destabilizes the membrane while intracellular squalene accumulates to toxic levels. Oral terbinafine concentrates in newly forming skin, hair and nail keratin, making it a major onychomycosis drug, but visible nail normalization lags for months as damaged nail grows out. Oral treatment can cause liver failure and prolonged or permanent taste/smell loss.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, abdominal pain and rash

  • Hepatitis, cholestasis or liver failure

  • Taste or smell disturbance that may be prolonged/permanent with reduced intake, weight loss or depression

  • SJS/TEN, DRESS, cytopenias, lupus, rhabdomyolysis and thrombotic microangiopathy
