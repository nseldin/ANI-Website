Passive-plus-active rabies postexposure immunoprophylaxis map

Used To Treat

Prevention of rabies after a credible exposure. Previously unvaccinated people receive immediate wound care, one HRIG dose infiltrated into and around wounds when anatomically feasible, and vaccine on days 0, 3, 7, and 14; previously vaccinated people receive vaccine on days 0 and 3 without HRIG. Immunocompromised patients need an additional day-28 dose and response assessment.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): rabies is almost uniformly fatal after symptoms begin. Do not delay indicated PEP, and never mix HRIG with vaccine or inject them at the same site because passive antibody can suppress active immunization.

Mechanism

Rabies virus replicates near the inoculation site and then enters peripheral nerves, traveling retrograde toward the central nervous system where circulating antibody has limited access. Thorough washing mechanically reduces viral burden. HRIG placed in and around the wound neutralizes extracellular virus during the interval before active immunity develops. Vaccine antigen stimulates B- and T-cell responses that generate neutralizing antibody and memory. Giving HRIG in the same syringe or site as vaccine can neutralize vaccine antigen, which explains the strict separation and why previously vaccinated patients do not receive HRIG.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask specifically about bats in bedrooms, saliva on mucosa or broken skin, prior vaccine series, immune suppression, and whether the animal can be tested because people may not recognize a small bite.

  • Calculate HRIG from actual weight and divide it among wounds without exceeding the total because local antibody is beneficial but excess can suppress active vaccine response.

  • Use separate syringes and distant anatomic sites for HRIG and vaccine because direct contact can neutralize vaccine antigen.

  • Create a dated follow-up plan before discharge and notify public health because missed doses, wrong sites, or undocumented prior vaccination can leave the patient incompletely protected.

Contraindications

  • There is no contraindication to indicated rabies PEP after a credible exposure because rabies is nearly universally fatal once symptomatic; manage allergy risk with public health and specialists.

  • Do not give HRIG to a previously vaccinated person and do not inject HRIG and vaccine in the same syringe or anatomic site.

Interactions

  • HRIG can neutralize rabies vaccine if mixed, placed in the same site, or given in excessive dose; spatial separation and dose limits preserve active immune priming.

  • Immunosuppressive medicines and illnesses can blunt vaccine response. Chloroquine can reduce response to intradermal regimens used in some settings; U.S. PEP uses approved intramuscular schedules and public-health guidance.

Key Labs

  • Routine baseline laboratory testing is not required to start PEP. Document weight, wound number and anatomy, vaccine history, immune status, product, lot, site, route, and exact administration dates.

  • Rabies virus neutralizing antibody testing is recommended after the series for immunocompromised patients and in other special circumstances directed by public health; wound evaluation should also address tetanus and bacterial infection risk.

Nclex Traps

  • Previously vaccinated patients do not receive HRIG.

  • Put HRIG into and around the wound; put vaccine at a distant site.

  • Pregnancy is not a contraindication to indicated rabies PEP.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Previously vaccinated patients

    Note

    Immune memory changes the regimen: give vaccine on days 0 and 3 but no HRIG, because passive antibody is unnecessary and can interfere with the booster response.

  • Population

    Immunocompromised patients

    Note

    Reduced vaccine response requires a fifth dose on day 28 and antibody confirmation, plus efforts to minimize immunosuppression when medically feasible.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, infants, and children

    Note

    Indicated PEP should not be withheld; children receive the same vaccine schedule with weight-based HRIG, while wound anatomy demands careful infiltration to avoid pressure injury.

Description

Rabies PEP combines two immune strategies because virus may remain in peripheral tissue before entering nerves. HRIG supplies immediate local neutralizing antibody at the exposure site, while vaccine teaches the patient's immune system to produce durable antibody over days. Once clinical rabies begins, prophylaxis cannot reliably rescue the disease, so wound cleansing, public-health risk assessment, and correct first-dose technique are time critical. Pregnancy is not a reason to withhold indicated PEP, and bat exposures may require assessment even without a remembered bite.

Administration Timing

  • Immediately wash and irrigate every wound thoroughly with soap and water and use an appropriate virucidal agent when available; contact public health to assess species, geography, animal availability, behavior, and exposure type.

  • For a previously unvaccinated person, infiltrate the calculated HRIG dose into and around all wounds as anatomically feasible, give any remainder intramuscularly distant from vaccine, and start vaccine at a different site on day 0.

  • Complete vaccine on days 3, 7, and 14; add day 28 and postseries antibody assessment for immunocompromised patients. Previously vaccinated patients receive vaccine on days 0 and 3 and no HRIG.

Adverse Effects

  • HRIG can cause local pain, swelling, headache, fever, and rare hypersensitivity; vaccine can cause injection-site pain, erythema, headache, nausea, myalgia, dizziness, or immune reactions.

  • Incorrect administration can cause functional harm: injection into a tight digit can compromise perfusion, while same-site HRIG and vaccine or excessive HRIG can reduce vaccine effectiveness.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Airway symptoms, anaphylaxis, compromised perfusion after wound infiltration, severe neurologic symptoms, or a substantial administration error requires urgent treatment and immediate public-health consultation.

  • A missed dose, delayed series, uncertain prior vaccine history, immunosuppression, or exposure discovered after initial wound healing requires individualized catch-up guidance rather than restarting or abandoning PEP without consultation.

Evidence Limitations

  • Exposure decisions depend on local rabies epidemiology and animal testing, so public-health assessment is essential; a generic bite category cannot determine PEP alone.

  • Once neurologic rabies begins, PEP is not established treatment. Prevention depends on correct wound care and timely immunoprophylaxis before neural invasion.

Regulatory Status

CDC-recommended postexposure use of FDA-licensed human rabies immune globulin and rabies vaccines according to product labeling and public-health guidance.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
