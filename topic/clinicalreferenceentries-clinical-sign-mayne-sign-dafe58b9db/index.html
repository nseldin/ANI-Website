Definition

Mayne sign is a blood-pressure response sign. It is a clue to aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means diastolic pressure falls noticeably after arm elevation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mayne sign means diastolic pressure falls noticeably after arm elevation.

Why It Matters

A positive Mayne sign response can signal aortic regurgitation. Reduced peripheral resistance and rapid diastolic runoff may accentuate the pressure decline. Mayne sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Measure diastolic pressure before and after raising the arm above the head with correct cuff technique.

Positive Finding

Diastolic pressure falls noticeably after arm elevation.

Mechanism

Reduced peripheral resistance and rapid diastolic runoff may accentuate the pressure decline.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Mayne sign response raises concern for aortic regurgitation because reduced peripheral resistance and rapid diastolic runoff may accentuate the pressure decline. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mayne sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mayne sign to focus evaluation of aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: vasodilation, arrhythmia, improper cuff. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Measure diastolic pressure before and after raising the arm above the head with correct cuff technique. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Mayne sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Thresholds are not consistently validated and measurement noise is substantial. Therefore, Mayne sign cannot confirm or exclude aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • vasodilation

  • arrhythmia

  • improper cuff

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from vasodilation, arrhythmia, improper cuff, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Mayne sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable adjunct. Mayne sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude aortic regurgitation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Mayne sign: Thresholds are not consistently validated and measurement noise is substantial.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Mayne sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Measure diastolic pressure before and after raising the arm above the head with correct cuff technique. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not repeat uncomfortable cuff cycles or use the sign to grade valve disease.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Mayne sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Diastolic pressure falls noticeably after arm elevation. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Measure diastolic pressure before and after raising the arm above the head with correct cuff technique. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, aortic regurgitation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Mayne sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
