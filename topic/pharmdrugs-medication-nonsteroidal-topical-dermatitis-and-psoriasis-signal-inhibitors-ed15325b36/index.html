Used To Treat

Product-, strength-, age-, and surface-area-specific treatment of atopic dermatitis, plaque psoriasis, or nonsegmental vitiligo.

Description

These creams are not interchangeable 'nonsteroid eczema drugs.' Ruxolitinib blocks JAK1/JAK2 cytokine signaling and uniquely carries the JAK-inhibitor boxed warning. Tapinarof activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor, altering barrier and immune gene programs. Roflumilast inhibits PDE4, preserves intracellular cAMP, and reduces inflammatory-cell activation. The correct drug depends on diagnosis, age, strength, body-surface area, liver function, and immunologic risk.

Mechanism

JAK inhibition prevents STAT phosphorylation downstream of multiple cytokine receptors. AhR activation causes receptor translocation, ARNT heterodimerization, and response-element-driven transcription linked to barrier differentiation and inflammatory regulation, although the exact clinical mechanism remains incomplete. PDE4 inhibition slows cAMP degradation; cAMP-dependent protein kinase then shifts transcription away from pro-inflammatory mediator production. Each pathway reaches inflammation through a different intracellular switch.

Boxed Warning

Ruxolitinib cream carries a boxed warning for serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events, and thrombosis based on JAK-inhibitor risk; use is limited by BSA and tube quantity. Tapinarof and roflumilast have no boxed warnings. Roflumilast is contraindicated in moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment; tapinarof commonly causes folliculitis.

Adverse Effects

  • Ruxolitinib: infection, folliculitis/acne, cytopenia, nonmelanoma skin cancer and thrombotic/MACE concern

  • Tapinarof: folliculitis, contact dermatitis, pruritus and headache

  • Roflumilast: diarrhea, headache, nausea, insomnia and application-site pain

Contraindications

  • Roflumilast: Child-Pugh B or C hepatic impairment

  • Avoid ruxolitinib during active serious infection and generally avoid combination with biologics, other JAK inhibitors, or potent immunosuppressants

  • Clarify diagnosis, age, strength, BSA limit, occlusion and product hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • Match the exact formulation and strength to age and disease; oral roflumilast and oral ruxolitinib are not the same card.

  • Document treated BSA and screen infection, malignancy, cardiovascular/thrombotic risk, liver disease and pregnancy where relevant.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase ruxolitinib exposure; extensive topical use increases concern.

  • Systemic CYP3A4/1A2 inhibitors can increase roflumilast exposure; enzyme inducers may reduce it.

  • Combining ruxolitinib with other potent immune therapies is not recommended.

Key Labs

  • CBC when clinically indicated for ruxolitinib

  • Liver function/Child-Pugh assessment for roflumilast

  • BSA, infection/skin-cancer examination and symptom response

Nclex Traps

  • Only ruxolitinib in this group carries the JAK boxed warning.

  • Roflumilast cream has strength- and age-specific indications and is contraindicated in Child-Pugh B/C disease.

  • Tapinarof is an AhR agonist, not a topical steroid or JAK inhibitor.
