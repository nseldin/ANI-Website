Class

CGRP ligand-neutralizing humanized IgG2-delta-a/kappa monoclonal antibody

Used To Treat

Preventive treatment of migraine in adults using monthly or quarterly subcutaneous dosing, and prevention of episodic migraine in patients 6 to 17 years who weigh at least 45 kg using monthly dosing.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current AJOVY labeling has no boxed warning. Serious hypersensitivity can occur within hours or be delayed up to 1 month because the long-lived antibody remains in the body after injection; therefore a late rash, swelling, dyspnea, or anaphylaxis still requires drug review and urgent treatment according to severity. Current labeling also warns about severe constipation, new or worsening hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon. Evaluate progressive abdominal pain, distention, vomiting, inability to pass stool or gas, or painful color-changing digits promptly.

Mechanism

Fremanezumab binds circulating alpha- and beta-CGRP ligand before either can activate the CLR/RAMP1 receptor complex, reducing downstream Gs-adenylyl-cyclase-cAMP signaling in trigeminovascular pain pathways. It does not broadly suppress immune cells. Proteolytic catabolism and an approximately 31-day half-life explain the monthly or quarterly interval, minimal conventional CYP interaction potential, and slow washout after a reaction.

Nursing Essentials

  • For adults verify either 225 mg monthly or 675 mg every 3 months as three consecutive 225-mg injections. For eligible pediatric episodic migraine verify age 6 to 17 years, weight at least 45 kg, and 225 mg monthly.

  • Record baseline headache days, bowel pattern, blood pressure, digital perfusion, hypersensitivity history, pregnancy plans, storage, injection-site rotation, and sharps disposal. If a dose is missed, give it as soon as possible and reset the schedule from that date according to the selected regimen.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to fremanezumab or an excipient. Delayed timing does not rule out the drug because reactions may occur for weeks after injection.

Interactions

  • Conventional CYP interactions are unlikely because fremanezumab is degraded proteolytically rather than metabolized through hepatic CYP pathways.

  • Review other constipating drugs and vascular risk. Do not convert 225-mg monthly and 675-mg quarterly schedules by casual arithmetic; quarterly dosing requires three injections at the same visit and has a different studied exposure pattern.

Key Labs

  • No routine concentration, CBC, liver panel, TB screen, or hepatitis screen is required solely for fremanezumab. Monitor headache burden, bowel function, blood pressure, digital perfusion, injection sites, and hypersensitivity.

Nclex Traps

  • Fremanezumab binds CGRP ligand; erenumab binds the receptor.

  • The 675-mg quarterly adult dose is three injections at one visit, not one 675-mg device.

  • The pediatric indication is episodic migraine in patients 6 to 17 years weighing at least 45 kg and uses monthly dosing.

Description

Fremanezumab is a humanized IgG2-delta-a/kappa monoclonal antibody that binds the CGRP ligand and prevents it from activating the CGRP receptor. It is a long-acting preventive injection, not an acute rescue drug. Adults may receive 225 mg monthly or 675 mg quarterly as three consecutive 225-mg injections; the pediatric episodic-migraine regimen is 225 mg monthly and is not a scaled version of the adult quarterly option.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site pain, induration, and erythema are the most common adverse reactions.

  • Serious or delayed hypersensitivity, severe constipation, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon are the high-consequence warnings.
