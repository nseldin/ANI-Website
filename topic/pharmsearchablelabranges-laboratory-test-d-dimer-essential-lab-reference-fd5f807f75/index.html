Category

Coagulation / clot labs

Name

D-dimer

Range

Use the reporting laboratory's assay, units, and cutoff. Mayo Clinic Laboratories reports 500 ng/mL fibrinogen-equivalent units (FEU) or less for one assay, but D-dimer methods can use FEU or D-dimer units (DDU), and the numbers are not interchangeable. Some validated pathways use an age-adjusted or other context-specific cutoff. A negative result helps exclude venous thromboembolism only when paired with an appropriate low or selected intermediate pretest-probability pathway; there is no universal critical D-dimer cutoff.

Why

D-dimer is produced when plasmin breaks down cross-linked fibrin, so an elevated result signals increased clot formation and breakdown. It raises concern for venous thromboembolism (deep-vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism) or disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), but it is nonspecific and also rises after surgery, trauma, or bleeding and with pregnancy, increasing age, infection or inflammation, cancer, and liver disease. Assess symptoms, vital signs, oxygenation, hemodynamic stability, unilateral limb findings, recent procedures or trauma, pregnancy status, cancer and inflammatory context, and the clinician-selected pretest probability. Cause-directed evaluation may include compression ultrasound for suspected DVT, CT pulmonary angiography or ventilation-perfusion imaging for suspected PE, or platelet count, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, smear, and trend review when DIC is suspected. Escalate immediately for acute dyspnea, hypoxemia, syncope, hypotension, chest pain, hemoptysis, signs of shock, or rapidly progressive bleeding or thrombosis. A positive D-dimer does not diagnose a clot or identify its location, and the amount of elevation does not reliably grade disease severity; a negative result must not override imaging or emergency evaluation when pretest probability is high or the patient is unstable.

High Causes

  • DVT or pulmonary embolism

  • DIC or other marked activation of coagulation and fibrinolysis

  • Recent surgery, trauma, bleeding, or hematoma

  • Pregnancy or increasing age

  • Cancer

  • Infection or inflammation

  • Liver disease

Low Causes

  • A negative result can help exclude VTE only within a validated low or selected intermediate pretest-probability pathway.

  • A negative result is not a stand-alone rule-out when pretest probability is high, symptoms are severe, or the patient is unstable.

Critical Concerns

  • Do not delay emergency assessment or indicated imaging for shock, hypoxemia, syncope, or other high-risk PE findings while waiting for D-dimer.

  • Bleeding or thrombosis with thrombocytopenia, prolonged clotting times, low fibrinogen, and a markedly elevated D-dimer raises concern for DIC and requires urgent cause-directed evaluation.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess respiratory and hemodynamic status first, then document symptom onset, unilateral limb findings, bleeding, recent surgery or trauma, pregnancy, cancer, infection, and anticoagulant use.

  • Verify the assay unit and cutoff and interpret the result only within the ordered diagnostic pathway; anticipate imaging or broader coagulation testing as ordered.

  • Notify the treating clinician promptly for new or worsening VTE or DIC findings rather than treating the D-dimer number itself.

Nclex Traps

  • D-dimer is a sensitive rule-out tool in selected low-probability pathways, not a positive diagnostic test.

  • FEU and DDU values are not numerically interchangeable, and a larger elevation does not by itself prove a larger or more dangerous clot.
