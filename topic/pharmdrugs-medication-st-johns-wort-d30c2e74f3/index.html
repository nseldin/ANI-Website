Contraindications

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Used To Treat

Self-treatment of mild depressive symptoms in some settings, but interaction risk often makes it unsafe with prescription therapies.

Mechanism

Hyperforin and related constituents affect monoamine signaling and induce pregnane X receptor pathways that increase CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein expression. The result can be both pharmacodynamic serotonin toxicity and pharmacokinetic loss of other medication effect.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for St. John's wort in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

St. John's wort is an herbal supplement with serotonergic activity and strong drug-interaction potential. Hyperforin induces CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein, which can lower many drug levels, while serotonergic activity can contribute to serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, linezolid, tramadol, triptans, or dextromethorphan.
