Description

Bisphosphonates are antiresorptive bone drugs that concentrate on mineralized bone and suppress osteoclast activity. They protect bone density and lower skeletal-event or hypercalcemia risk, but the bone-binding mechanism also explains slow onset, long persistence, hypocalcemia risk, jaw osteonecrosis concern, atypical femur-fracture warning, and strict oral administration rules.

Used To Treat

Osteoporosis, Paget disease, malignancy-related bone disease, and hypercalcemia of malignancy depending on agent/route.

Mechanism

They bind hydroxyapatite in bone and inhibit osteoclast-mediated bone resorption; nitrogen-containing agents disrupt farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase inside osteoclasts.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach administration posture/fasting rules for oral agents, dental/ONJ precautions, calcium/vitamin D adequacy, renal function, hypocalcemia, and esophageal symptoms.
