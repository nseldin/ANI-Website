Definition

Patellar apprehension sign is a bedside patellar-instability maneuver. It is a clue to lateral patellar instability. A positive finding means the patient becomes apprehensive, contracts the quadriceps, or stops the maneuver.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Patellar apprehension sign means the patient becomes apprehensive, contracts the quadriceps, or stops the maneuver.

Why It Matters

A positive Patellar apprehension sign response can signal lateral patellar instability. Prior instability sensitizes medial restraints and the patient anticipates lateral dislocation. Patellar apprehension sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lateral patellar instability

Assessment Method

With the knee slightly flexed, gently translate the patella laterally while observing the patient.

Positive Finding

The patient becomes apprehensive, contracts the quadriceps, or stops the maneuver.

Mechanism

Prior instability sensitizes medial restraints and the patient anticipates lateral dislocation.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Patellar apprehension sign response raises concern for lateral patellar instability because prior instability sensitizes medial restraints and the patient anticipates lateral dislocation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Patellar apprehension sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Patellar apprehension sign to focus evaluation of lateral patellar instability and the reviewed alternatives: patellofemoral pain, acute contusion, anxiety. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the knee slightly flexed, gently translate the patella laterally while observing the patient. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Patellar apprehension sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain and fear without true instability can be positive. Therefore, Patellar apprehension sign cannot confirm or exclude lateral patellar instability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • patellofemoral pain

  • acute contusion

  • anxiety

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from patellofemoral pain, acute contusion, anxiety, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Patellar apprehension sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Patellar apprehension sign can adjust concern for lateral patellar instability, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Patellar apprehension sign: Pain and fear without true instability can be positive.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Patellar apprehension sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the knee slightly flexed, gently translate the patella laterally while observing the patient. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Stop at apprehension; never intentionally dislocate the patella.
