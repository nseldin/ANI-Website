Definition

Puddle sign is a percussion-auscultation maneuver. It is a clue to small-volume ascites. A positive finding means a change in transmitted sound suggests a small dependent fluid puddle.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Puddle sign means a change in transmitted sound suggests a small dependent fluid puddle.

Why It Matters

A positive Puddle sign response can signal small-volume ascites. Gravity pools free fluid against the anterior abdominal wall while bowel floats above. Puddle sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • small-volume ascites

Assessment Method

Position the patient on hands and knees, then use percussion and auscultation over the dependent abdomen.

Positive Finding

A change in transmitted sound suggests a small dependent fluid puddle.

Mechanism

Gravity pools free fluid against the anterior abdominal wall while bowel floats above.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Puddle sign response raises concern for small-volume ascites because gravity pools free fluid against the anterior abdominal wall while bowel floats above. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Puddle sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Puddle sign to focus evaluation of small-volume ascites and the reviewed alternatives: normal bowel fluid, obesity, full bladder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Position the patient on hands and knees, then use percussion and auscultation over the dependent abdomen. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Puddle sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor reproducibility and uncomfortable positioning make it unreliable. Therefore, Puddle sign cannot confirm or exclude small-volume ascites by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal bowel fluid

  • obesity

  • full bladder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal bowel fluid, obesity, full bladder, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Puddle sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Obsolete and impractical; ultrasound is superior. Puddle sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude small-volume ascites. Apply the reviewed limitation for Puddle sign: Poor reproducibility and uncomfortable positioning make it unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Puddle sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Position the patient on hands and knees, then use percussion and auscultation over the dependent abdomen. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not place frail, dyspneic, pregnant, or unstable patients on hands and knees.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Puddle sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: A change in transmitted sound suggests a small dependent fluid puddle. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Position the patient on hands and knees, then use percussion and auscultation over the dependent abdomen. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, small-volume ascites, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
