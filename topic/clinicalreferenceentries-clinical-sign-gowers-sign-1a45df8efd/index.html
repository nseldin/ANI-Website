Definition

Gowers sign is an observed functional motor sign. It is a clue to proximal pelvic-girdle weakness. A positive finding means the patient uses the hands to climb up the thighs to extend the hips and trunk.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gowers sign means the patient uses the hands to climb up the thighs to extend the hips and trunk.

Why It Matters

The observed Gowers sign finding can signal proximal pelvic-girdle weakness. Weak hip and trunk extensors cannot lift the center of mass, so the arms substitute as external supports. Gowers sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • proximal pelvic-girdle weakness

Assessment Method

Ask the child or adult to rise from the floor without assistance while guarding against a fall.

Positive Finding

The patient uses the hands to climb up the thighs to extend the hips and trunk.

Mechanism

Weak hip and trunk extensors cannot lift the center of mass, so the arms substitute as external supports.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Gowers sign finding raises concern for proximal pelvic-girdle weakness because weak hip and trunk extensors cannot lift the center of mass, so the arms substitute as external supports. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gowers sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Gowers sign to focus evaluation of proximal pelvic-girdle weakness and the reviewed alternatives: hip disease, juvenile arthritis, general deconditioning. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the child or adult to rise from the floor without assistance while guarding against a fall. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Gowers sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity, pain, deconditioning, and normal young-child technique can mimic it. Therefore, Gowers sign cannot confirm or exclude proximal pelvic-girdle weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip disease

  • juvenile arthritis

  • general deconditioning

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hip disease, juvenile arthritis, general deconditioning.

False Negative Situations

Gowers sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of proximal pelvic-girdle weakness.

Modern Status

Current high-yield weakness sign. Interpret Gowers sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Obesity, pain, deconditioning, and normal young-child technique can mimic it.

Safety Precautions

Observe Gowers sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the child or adult to rise from the floor without assistance while guarding against a fall. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Guard closely and do not repeatedly fatigue a weak child.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Gowers sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The patient uses the hands to climb up the thighs to extend the hips and trunk. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask the child or adult to rise from the floor without assistance while guarding against a fall. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, proximal pelvic-girdle weakness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
