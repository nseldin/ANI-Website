Quick Answer

Disproportionate pain or pain with passive stretch can precede pulse loss and paralysis in acute compartment syndrome; never wait for all mnemonic findings before escalating.

Sections

  • Label

    Core assessment

    Text

    Assess pain and analgesic response, color, temperature, capillary refill, swelling or tension, pulses with Doppler when appropriate, sensation, and active movement. Compare with baseline and trend at the ordered frequency.

  • Label

    Why deterioration occurs

    Text

    Rising tissue pressure, arterial obstruction, venous congestion, edema, tight dressings, fracture displacement, or nerve injury can reduce perfusion or conduction. Findings differ by mechanism and stage.

  • Label

    Acute compartment syndrome

    Text

    Pain out of proportion, escalating analgesic need, pain with passive stretch, tense swelling, and evolving sensory change are high-risk. Pallor, paralysis, and absent pulses are late and cannot be used to rule out an earlier emergency.

  • Label

    Nursing action

    Text

    Escalate an acute change immediately, check for externally constricting equipment within policy, maintain ordered limb position, avoid masking deterioration with delay, and document exact bilateral findings and time.

Source Note

Original ANI assessment explanation emphasizes early danger signs and avoids treating the six Ps as a required complete set.

Summary

A neurovascular assessment compares perfusion, sensation, movement, pain, temperature, color, swelling, and pulses in an affected limb and against the opposite side when appropriate.

    Cue

    Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis, Poikilothermia

    Expansion
    • Cue

      P1

      Meaning

      Pain. Disproportionate pain or pain with passive stretch may be an early compartment-pressure warning.

    • Cue

      P2

      Meaning

      Pallor. Reduced perfusion can change color, but normal color does not exclude early compromise.

    • Cue

      P3

      Meaning

      Pulselessness. Absent distal pulses suggest severe arterial compromise and can be a late compartment-syndrome sign.

    • Cue

      P4

      Meaning

      Paresthesia. Tingling or altered sensation can reflect nerve ischemia or compression.

    • Cue

      P5

      Meaning

      Paralysis. New weakness can indicate advanced nerve or muscle compromise and is a late emergency sign.

    • Cue

      P6

      Meaning

      Poikilothermia. A cool limb may reflect impaired blood flow, though environment and baseline circulation affect temperature.

    Id

    mnemonic:neurovascular-six-ps

    Limitations

    • The six Ps are not equally early or sensitive.

    • Capillary refill, swelling, tension, pulse quality, sensation, movement, and analgesic trend must also be assessed.

    • Pulses may remain present in compartment syndrome.

    Nursing Application

    Cast, fracture, vascular procedure, and limb-injury checks. Comparison with baseline and the opposite limb. Escalation for suspected compartment syndrome or acute ischemia.

    Recall Target

    Urgent Exceptions

    • Escalate disproportionate pain, passive-stretch pain, tense swelling, rapidly evolving sensory loss, weakness, or perfusion change without waiting for all six findings.

    Why

    Six alliterative findings span pain, perfusion, sensory, and motor function. The full linked card supplies the clinical context and prevents the cue from being mistaken for a complete assessment.
