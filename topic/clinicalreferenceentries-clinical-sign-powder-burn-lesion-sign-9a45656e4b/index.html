Definition

Powder-burn lesion sign is a laparoscopic visual sign. It is a clue to pelvic endometriosis. A positive finding means blue-black, brown, red, or scarred implants resemble powder burns.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Powder-burn lesion sign means blue-black, brown, red, or scarred implants resemble powder burns.

Why It Matters

The observed Powder-burn lesion sign finding can signal pelvic endometriosis. Repeated bleeding, hemosiderin deposition, inflammation, and fibrosis alter implant color. Powder-burn lesion sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pelvic endometriosis

Assessment Method

Inspect peritoneal surfaces during laparoscopy.

Positive Finding

Blue-black, brown, red, or scarred implants resemble powder burns.

Mechanism

Repeated bleeding, hemosiderin deposition, inflammation, and fibrosis alter implant color.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Powder-burn lesion sign finding raises concern for pelvic endometriosis because repeated bleeding, hemosiderin deposition, inflammation, and fibrosis alter implant color. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Powder-burn lesion sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Powder-burn lesion sign to focus evaluation of pelvic endometriosis and the reviewed alternatives: malignancy, foreign-body granuloma, old hemorrhage. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect peritoneal surfaces during laparoscopy. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Powder-burn lesion sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal-colored or subtle lesions can contain endometriosis and dark lesions have mimics. Therefore, Powder-burn lesion sign cannot confirm or exclude pelvic endometriosis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • malignancy

  • foreign-body granuloma

  • old hemorrhage

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as malignancy, foreign-body granuloma, old hemorrhage.

False Negative Situations

Powder-burn lesion sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of pelvic endometriosis.
