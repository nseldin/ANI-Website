Definition

Frenzel-goggle fixation sign is a bedside vestibular observation. It is a clue to peripheral versus central nystagmus behavior. A positive finding means peripheral vestibular nystagmus often becomes more apparent without fixation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Frenzel-goggle fixation sign means peripheral vestibular nystagmus often becomes more apparent without fixation.

Why It Matters

The observed Frenzel-goggle fixation sign finding can signal peripheral versus central nystagmus behavior. Cerebellar visual fixation suppresses peripheral vestibular asymmetry; removing fixation unmasks it. Frenzel-goggle fixation sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • peripheral versus central nystagmus behavior

Assessment Method

Observe nystagmus with visual fixation removed using Frenzel goggles or video goggles.

Evidence Limitations

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as voluntary eye movement, ocular flutter, poor calibration.

False Negative Situations
