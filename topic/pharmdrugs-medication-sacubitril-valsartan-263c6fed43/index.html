Used To Treat

Chronic heart failure according to current labeling. In adults, benefit is clearest when left-ventricular ejection fraction is below normal; in symptomatic HFrEF, current guidelines recommend ARNI therapy to reduce morbidity and mortality when appropriate. Pediatric heart-failure use follows age- and weight-specific labeling.

Mechanism

Esterases convert sacubitril to LBQ657, which inhibits neprilysin. Neprilysin normally cleaves atrial and B-type natriuretic peptides, bradykinin, adrenomedullin, and other peptides. Preserving natriuretic peptides increases guanylyl-cyclase receptor activation and cyclic GMP, promoting natriuresis, diuresis, vasodilation, lower sympathetic tone, and less hypertrophy or fibrosis. Neprilysin also degrades angiotensin II, so inhibition alone could increase an opposing AT1 signal; valsartan selectively blocks AT1 and prevents that counter-response. BNP is a neprilysin substrate and may rise, whereas NT-proBNP is not directly degraded and often better reflects changing wall stress. Combined ACE and neprilysin inhibition would excessively impair bradykinin breakdown, explaining the angioedema contraindication and 36-hour separation.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Valsartan directly blocks fetal RAAS signaling. Stop sacubitril/valsartan as soon as pregnancy is detected. Do not give with an ACE inhibitor or within 36 hours because angioedema risk rises.

Contraindications

  • ACE inhibitor use or use within the previous 36 hours; history of angioedema related to previous ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy; pregnancy; and aliskiren use in diabetes.

  • Do not add a separate ARB because the product already contains valsartan. Clarify severe hypotension, hyperkalemia, or evolving AKI.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before the first dose, document the last ACE-inhibitor dose, angioedema history, pressure, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, and every hidden ARB ingredient.

  • Teach twice-daily adherence, daily weight and symptoms, orthostasis, potassium-product caution, and immediate reporting of lip, tongue, throat, voice, or swallowing change.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors are contraindicated and require 36-hour separation. Separate ARBs duplicate valsartan; potassium products, MRAs, NSAIDs, lithium, and diuretics require active monitoring.

  • PDE5 inhibitors and other vasodilators add hypotension. Renal impairment and volume depletion magnify exposure and hemodynamic effect.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume, weight, urine output, and heart-failure trajectory.

  • Interpret natriuretic peptides mechanistically: BNP may be altered by neprilysin inhibition, while NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate.

Nclex Traps

  • Entresto contains valsartan. Adding losartan or valsartan is duplicate ARB therapy.

  • The 36-hour ACE-inhibitor washout exists because ACE and neprilysin both clear vasoactive peptides; overlapping blockade increases bradykinin-mediated angioedema.

Description

Sacubitril/valsartan, branded Entresto, is an ARNI: sacubitril preserves natriuretic and other vasoactive peptides, while valsartan blocks the angiotensin-II AT1 receptor. It is not simply two blood-pressure drugs and not an ACE inhibitor. The dual mechanism increases natriuresis, vasodilation, cyclic-GMP signaling, and antifibrotic effects while suppressing vasoconstriction, aldosterone, sodium retention, and remodeling. That is why it can reduce heart-failure events rather than merely move a pressure number. The same biology explains the rules: hypotension can reflect strong vasodilation, potassium and creatinine change reflect AT1 blockade, BNP can be harder to interpret because neprilysin degrades BNP, fetal toxicity comes from valsartan, and angioedema risk requires avoiding ACE inhibitors and preserving a 36-hour washout. Entresto already contains valsartan, so adding another ARB is duplicate therapy.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, hyperkalemia, cough, dizziness, renal-function change, and angioedema are the major labeled effects.

  • Severe airway swelling, syncope or shock, acute renal failure, dangerous potassium elevation, and fetal injury require urgent response.
