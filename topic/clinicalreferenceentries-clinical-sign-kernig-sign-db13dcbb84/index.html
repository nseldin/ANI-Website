Definition

Kernig sign is a bedside meningeal maneuver. It is a clue to meningeal irritation. A positive finding means pain or marked resistance limits knee extension.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Kernig sign means pain or marked resistance limits knee extension.

Why It Matters

A positive Kernig sign response can signal meningeal irritation. Stretching inflamed meninges and lumbosacral nerve roots produces protective hamstring spasm and pain. Kernig sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • meningeal irritation

Assessment Method

Flex the hip and knee to 90 degrees, then gently extend the knee while the patient is supine.

Positive Finding

Pain or marked resistance limits knee extension.

Mechanism

Stretching inflamed meninges and lumbosacral nerve roots produces protective hamstring spasm and pain.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Kernig sign response raises concern for meningeal irritation because stretching inflamed meninges and lumbosacral nerve roots produces protective hamstring spasm and pain. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Kernig sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Kernig sign to focus evaluation of meningeal irritation and the reviewed alternatives: hamstring tightness, sciatica, hip or knee pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex the hip and knee to 90 degrees, then gently extend the knee while the patient is supine. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Kernig sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Published sensitivity is low and variable; negative testing cannot reassure against meningitis. Therefore, Kernig sign cannot confirm or exclude meningeal irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hamstring tightness

  • sciatica

  • hip or knee pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hamstring tightness, sciatica, hip or knee pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Kernig sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct with poor sensitivity; never rules out meningitis. Kernig sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude meningeal irritation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Kernig sign: Published sensitivity is low and variable; negative testing cannot reassure against meningitis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Kernig sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex the hip and knee to 90 degrees, then gently extend the knee while the patient is supine. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not delay lumbar-puncture or antimicrobial decisions for this maneuver and never force the knee.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex the hip and knee to 90 degrees, then gently extend the knee while the patient is supine. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, meningeal irritation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.
