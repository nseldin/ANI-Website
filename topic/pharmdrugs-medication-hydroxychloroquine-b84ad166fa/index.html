Used To Treat

Rheumatoid arthritis, systemic or discoid lupus erythematosus, and treatment or prophylaxis of susceptible malaria according to geography and resistance. It is not reliable therapy for chloroquine-resistant malaria and is not an acute analgesic for an autoimmune flare.

Mechanism

Uncharged hydroxychloroquine crosses membranes, becomes protonated and trapped in acidic lysosomes/endosomes, and raises intravesicular pH. The pH change inhibits peptide-processing enzymes and alters MHC class II antigen presentation. It also inhibits activation of the endosomal Toll-like receptors TLR7 and TLR9 by nucleic-acid immune complexes, which lowers downstream MyD88, interferon-regulatory-factor, NF-kappaB, type I interferon, and cytokine activity. In malaria, drug accumulation in the parasite food vacuole interferes with detoxification of heme released from hemoglobin digestion, but resistance patterns limit use. The drug has a very large distribution volume and long terminal elimination. Retinal pigment epithelium exposure accumulates with dose and duration; blockade of cardiac potassium channels plus myocardial lysosomal phospholipidosis contributes to QT and cardiomyopathy risk.

Contraindications

  • Known 4-aminoquinoline hypersensitivity; clarify retinal or macular disease, kidney impairment, cardiomyopathy, conduction disease, bradycardia, low potassium/magnesium, porphyria, psoriasis, and G6PD-related hemolysis risk.

  • Do not combine casually with other QT-prolonging drugs or assume a normal visual-acuity screen excludes early parafoveal toxicity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document actual body weight, daily mg/kg dose, baseline eye findings, duration, kidney function, tamoxifen, and other retinal risks; teach patients to report field defects, reading difficulty, or color change.

  • Assess ECG and electrolytes when cardiac risk or interacting drugs justify it, and recognize sweating, confusion, or weakness as possible severe hypoglycemia even without diabetes therapy.

Interactions

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs and electrolyte-lowering therapies increase arrhythmia risk; hydroxychloroquine can raise digoxin concentrations and alter insulin or antidiabetic needs.

  • Antacids can reduce absorption if taken too close; cimetidine may raise exposure; drugs causing retinal, cardiac, neuromuscular, or hypoglycemic toxicity add risk.

Key Labs

  • Baseline kidney/liver context, glucose when symptomatic, CBC when prolonged or clinically indicated, ECG/electrolytes for cardiac risk, and actual body-weight dose calculation

  • Baseline comprehensive ophthalmic evaluation with automated visual fields and spectral-domain OCT at guideline-appropriate intervals

Nclex Traps

  • Retinal toxicity is driven by actual daily dose, duration, and risk factors; a patient can read the eye chart normally while early field or OCT changes are developing.

  • Hydroxychloroquine is not hydroxyurea and is not a corticosteroid; its delayed immune effect comes from endosomal signaling modulation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Irreversible retinal damage is related to daily dose, cumulative exposure, duration, kidney impairment, tamoxifen, and macular disease. Dose is commonly kept at or below 5 mg/kg/day using actual body weight for retinal-risk reduction. Baseline ophthalmic examination and risk-timed screening are essential. Cardiomyopathy, conduction block, QT prolongation, torsades, severe hypoglycemia, neuropsychiatric effects, myopathy, and hemolysis can occur even though there is no black box.

Description

Hydroxychloroquine is a 4-aminoquinoline antimalarial and DMARD that accumulates in acidic endosomes and lysosomes, raises their pH, and inhibits TLR7/TLR9-driven immune-complex signaling in lupus and rheumatoid disease. The altered organelle pH changes antigen processing and dampens responses to RNA- and DNA-containing immune complexes. This helps reduce interferon and cytokine activation without the same broad marrow suppression as many conventional DMARDs. The same long tissue persistence and binding to melanin-rich structures help explain cumulative retinal toxicity, while lysosomal and ion-channel effects in cardiac muscle help explain cardiomyopathy, conduction disease, QT prolongation, and rare ventricular arrhythmia.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, headache, pruritus, rash, pigment change, blurred vision, and hypoglycemia

  • Irreversible retinopathy, cardiomyopathy, QT prolongation/torsades, conduction block, skeletal myopathy, neuropathy, severe skin reaction, hemolysis, and suicidal behavior
