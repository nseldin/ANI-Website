Used To Treat

Adult chronic idiopathic constipation, opioid-induced constipation in adults with chronic noncancer pain, and IBS-C in women age 18 years or older; effectiveness with methadone-like opioids is not established.

Mechanism

PKA-independent ClC-2 activation increases chloride movement from epithelial cells into the lumen; sodium and water follow to preserve electroneutrality. Hydration of intestinal contents improves transit and spontaneous bowel movement frequency. Systemic parent-drug levels are usually unquantifiable, and metabolism is largely carbonyl-reductase-mediated rather than CYP-dependent.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction

  • Avoid during severe diarrhea

  • Clarify significant hepatic impairment, unexplained vomiting/distention and product hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food and water and swallow the capsule whole; food reduces nausea even though it does not eliminate the drug's local action.

  • Verify the indication and sex/age labeling for IBS-C, and reassess the ongoing need periodically.

Interactions

  • Methadone and other diphenylheptane opioids may reduce chloride-channel activation and effectiveness.

  • Other laxatives add diarrhea/dehydration.

  • Minimal CYP metabolism limits classic systemic interactions.

Key Labs

  • Stool frequency/consistency, abdominal symptoms and hydration

  • Blood pressure and symptoms around dyspnea/syncope episodes

  • Electrolytes and renal function with severe diarrhea; hepatic function for dose adjustment

Nclex Traps

  • Lubiprostone is taken with food; linaclotide is generally taken before food.

  • It activates ClC-2, not GC-C/cGMP.

  • Dyspnea after dosing is recognized, but chest symptoms still require assessment rather than automatic attribution.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction. Severe diarrhea can cause syncope and hypotension. Acute dyspnea or chest tightness may occur after a dose and can recur; assess severity and alternative cardiopulmonary causes rather than dismissing the symptom. Dose adjustment is required for selected hepatic impairment.

Description

Lubiprostone is a locally acting prostaglandin-derived chloride-channel activator that opens apical intestinal ClC-2 channels. Chloride-rich secretion draws water into the lumen, softening stool and supporting motility without directly stimulating enteric nerves. It is taken with food and water to reduce nausea and must not be used through a suspected mechanical obstruction.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, headache, abdominal pain/distention and flatulence

  • Dyspnea or chest tightness, often shortly after dosing

  • Syncope/hypotension, vomiting and dehydration
