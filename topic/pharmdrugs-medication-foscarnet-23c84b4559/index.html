Used To Treat

AIDS-related CMV retinitis and acyclovir-resistant mucocutaneous HSV in immunocompromised patients under current labeling, with specialist use for resistant CMV or other severe herpesvirus disease. Toxicity generally reserves it for situations where safer activated nucleoside analogues are ineffective or intolerable.

Mechanism

During DNA synthesis, polymerase cleaves pyrophosphate from each incoming nucleotide triphosphate. Foscarnet mimics pyrophosphate and reversibly occupies its binding site on herpesvirus DNA polymerase, preventing pyrophosphate cleavage and the conformational progression needed to add the next nucleotide. It also inhibits HIV reverse transcriptase at higher clinically relevant exposure. Because the molecule is already active, kinase-deficient HSV or UL97-mutant CMV may remain susceptible. Mutations in the polymerase gene, including CMV UL54, can reduce foscarnet binding and may confer cross-resistance patterns. Foscarnet chelates ionized calcium and magnesium in plasma, while renal deposition and tubular exposure reduce filtration; electrolyte shifts can then trigger neuromuscular irritability, QT prolongation, and seizures.

Contraindications

  • Clinically significant hypersensitivity; inability to safely hydrate, monitor, or adjust for renal function makes use unsafe.

  • Correct clinically important electrolyte abnormalities and review QT, seizure, cardiac, and kidney risks before each treatment phase.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use an infusion pump, central or appropriately managed peripheral access, label-specified dilution and rate, and hydration. Faster infusion can abruptly lower ionized calcium and provoke symptoms.

  • Protect genital skin from prolonged contact with foscarnet-rich urine through hygiene and barrier care; ulceration is a drug-exposure effect, not automatically a new STI.

Interactions

  • Nephrotoxins such as amphotericin, aminoglycosides, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, or cidofovir markedly increase renal risk.

  • Pentamidine and other QT-, calcium-, or magnesium-altering drugs can magnify electrolyte and dysrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Frequent creatinine/creatinine clearance, ionized or total calcium with albumin context, magnesium, potassium, phosphate, CBC, and ECG when risk warrants

  • Hydration and urine output, neurologic examination, paresthesias/tetany, genital lesions, infusion site, CMV disease response, and resistance evaluation

Nclex Traps

  • Foscarnet needs no viral phosphorylation, which is why it can work against thymidine-kinase-deficient HSV or UL97-mutant CMV.

  • Seizures may result from electrolyte chelation even before a dramatic total calcium change is obvious.

Boxed Warning

Prominent label warning: renal impairment is the major toxicity and may be dose-limiting; calculate dose from current creatinine clearance, hydrate as directed, and monitor renal function frequently. Seizures have occurred because foscarnet alters calcium, magnesium, potassium, and phosphate, especially with renal dysfunction or other neurotoxic drugs. Correct electrolytes and evaluate paresthesias, tetany, weakness, confusion, or QT change before they progress. The drug should be used only by clinicians experienced with its toxicity and dosing.

Description

Foscarnet is an inorganic pyrophosphate analogue that binds directly to the pyrophosphate site of viral DNA polymerase and HIV reverse transcriptase. It does not need viral thymidine kinase, CMV UL97, or host phosphorylation, so it can inhibit HSV that cannot activate acyclovir and CMV with UL97-mediated ganciclovir resistance. The same highly charged molecule is eliminated by the kidney and binds divalent cations, which explains its defining toxicities: acute kidney injury, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, potassium and phosphate disturbances, QT-related dysrhythmia, paresthesia, and seizures. Hydration and slow controlled infusion reduce risk but do not make the drug routine.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, anemia, fever, headache, diarrhea, infusion symptoms, genital ulceration from drug-rich urine, and phlebitis

  • Acute kidney injury, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia or hyperkalemia, phosphate disturbance, QT prolongation, ventricular dysrhythmia, and seizures
