Definition

Abdominal rigidity is involuntary, persistent tightening of the abdominal muscles caused by irritation or inflammation of the peritoneum, the thin lining of the abdominal cavity. The patient cannot simply relax it away.

Plain Language

Abdominal rigidity means the belly stays hard or boardlike even when the person tries to relax.

Why It Matters

Rigidity can signal peritonitis from perforation, infection, ischemia, bleeding, or another acute surgical abdomen. It is a high-risk finding: do not keep pressing deeply, and escalate urgently when it occurs with severe pain, vomiting, fever, bleeding, pregnancy concern, shock, or rapid deterioration.

Assessment Method

After inspection and auscultation, use only gentle light palpation while the patient breathes slowly; assess whether firm resistance persists through expiration and distraction, and immediately integrate pain, vital signs, vomiting, bowel function, bleeding, pregnancy possibility, and shock findings.

Positive Finding

The abdominal wall remains involuntarily firm and resistant despite relaxation, slow expiration, and distraction, and it may feel boardlike.

Mechanism

Inflammation of the parietal peritoneum triggers a protective reflex contraction of the overlying abdominal muscles that the patient cannot voluntarily release.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Abdominal rigidity response raises concern for involuntary abdominal wall contraction from peritoneal inflammation, including peritonitis caused by perforation, infection, ischemia, hemorrhage, or another acute intra-abdominal process because inflammation of the parietal peritoneum triggers a protective reflex contraction of the overlying abdominal muscles that the patient cannot voluntarily release. Evidence Limitations

Pain, voluntary guarding, anxiety, abdominal wall spasm, scars, body habitus, and tense ascites can complicate assessment; absence of rigidity does not exclude early or localized peritonitis. Therefore, Abdominal rigidity cannot confirm or exclude involuntary abdominal wall contraction from peritoneal inflammation, including peritonitis caused by perforation, infection, ischemia, hemorrhage, or another acute intra-abdominal process by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • abdominal guarding

  • abdominal wall spasm

  • tense ascites

  • scar-related firmness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from abdominal guarding, abdominal wall spasm, tense ascites, scar-related firmness, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Abdominal rigidity may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Abdominal rigidity, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: After inspection and auscultation, use only gentle light palpation while the patient breathes slowly; assess whether firm resistance persists through expiration and distraction, and immediately integrate pain, vital signs, vomiting, bowel function, bleeding, pregnancy possibility, and shock findings. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not perform deep or repeated palpation in a rigid abdomen or delay urgent evaluation for imaging or laboratory confirmation; activate emergency response for shock, major bleeding, severe deterioration, or suspected perforation.
