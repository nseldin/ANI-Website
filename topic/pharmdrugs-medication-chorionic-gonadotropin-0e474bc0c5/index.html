Class

Placental glycoprotein hormone and long-acting LH/CG-receptor agonist

Used To Treat

Final follicular maturation and ovulation in selected anovulatory infertility after adequate follicle development, selected prepubertal cryptorchidism not caused by anatomic obstruction under product-specific labeling, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in males to stimulate testosterone and support spermatogenesis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Chorionic gonadotropin products have no current U.S. boxed warning, but safe ovulation induction requires experienced clinicians, serial ultrasound and estradiol monitoring. Do not give the trigger when ovarian response is excessive because hCG can initiate or worsen OHSS, which may include ascites, pleural effusion, hemoconcentration, oliguria, renal failure, torsion, rupture, and arterial or venous thrombosis. Multiple gestation can occur. hCG has no established effectiveness as a weight-loss drug; combining it with severe caloric restriction is not a legitimate mechanism-based obesity treatment.

Mechanism

hCG binds the Gs-coupled LH/CG receptor on ovarian theca-lutein cells and testicular Leydig cells. Adenylyl cyclase raises cAMP and protein kinase A, increasing steroidogenic acute regulatory protein, mitochondrial cholesterol transport, and steroid synthesis. In a mature follicle, the signal restarts oocyte meiosis, activates proteases and prostaglandins that weaken the follicular wall, triggers ovulation, and luteinizes granulosa/theca cells to produce progesterone. hCG has a longer half-life than endogenous LH because of glycosylation, providing sustained signaling. Excess luteinized follicular mass releases VEGF, making capillaries permeable; fluid moves into the peritoneal or pleural space while hemoconcentration and thrombosis risk rise. In boys or men, Leydig testosterone supports virilization and intratesticular conditions for spermatogenesis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Label it as the trigger and write the administration time clearly. A several-hour timing error can disrupt oocyte retrieval or insemination planning.

  • Teach that abdominal swelling, rapid weight gain, dyspnea, oliguria, severe pain or leg symptoms after the trigger are urgent even though no more injections are scheduled.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy for fertility dosing, primary gonadal failure when the target organ cannot respond, uncontrolled thyroid/adrenal dysfunction, pituitary or sex-hormone-dependent tumors, unexplained bleeding, and precocious puberty or prostate cancer in relevant male products.

  • Before an ovulation trigger, confirm follicle development, estradiol response, absence of unsafe ovarian enlargement, and the exact planned timing for intercourse, insemination or retrieval.

Interactions

  • FSH-containing gonadotropins create the follicular substrate that hCG triggers; combined effects determine OHSS and multiple-gestation risk.

  • Exogenous hCG can produce false-positive pregnancy testing for a limited period; interpret timing and quantitative trends rather than one isolated result.

Key Labs

  • Serial ultrasound follicle count and size, estradiol, pregnancy test before treatment, testosterone and semen parameters for male therapy

  • Weight, girth, urine output, hematocrit, electrolytes, creatinine, liver tests, oxygenation, ascites, thrombosis symptoms, and serial pregnancy testing after the medication-clearance window

Nclex Traps

  • hCG mimics LH, not FSH. It triggers final maturation and ovulation after follicles have grown.

  • Why can hCG worsen OHSS? It luteinizes a large stimulated follicular mass, increasing ovarian VEGF and capillary leak.

Description

Chorionic gonadotropin is hCG, a placental glycoprotein hormone whose beta subunit gives it identity but whose receptor action closely mimics LH. In fertility treatment it is the trigger: after FSH-containing drugs grow follicles, hCG activates the LH/CG receptor long enough to resume oocyte meiosis, rupture the follicle, and form a progesterone-producing corpus luteum. In males it stimulates Leydig-cell testosterone production. The long receptor signal is useful but can sharply amplify OHSS in an ovary containing many stimulated follicles, because luteinized cells release VEGF and increase vascular permeability. hCG also remains detectable in pregnancy assays after injection, so an early positive test can represent medication rather than implantation.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, irritability, fatigue, edema, injection-site pain, gynecomastia, pelvic discomfort, and ovarian enlargement

  • OHSS, ovarian torsion or rupture, hemoperitoneum, thromboembolism, multifetal gestation, precocious virilization, fluid retention, and severe hypersensitivity
