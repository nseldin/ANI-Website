Definition

SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped positive-sense RNA coronavirus that causes COVID-19. The organism and the disease must remain separate identities: a viral test detects current or recent viral material, while COVID-19 describes the clinical illness, which can range from asymptomatic infection to severe respiratory and multisystem disease.

    SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped positive-sense RNA coronavirus that causes COVID-19. The organism and the disease must remain separate identities: a viral test detects current or recent viral material, while COVID-19 describes the clinical illness, which can range from asymptomatic infection to severe respiratory and multisystem disease.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    The formal name severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 is abbreviated SARS-CoV-2. COVID-19 is the disease name, not a scientific synonym for the virus. Viral lineages and variants are descendants within the SARS-CoV-2 population rather than separate disease cards; they matter because mutations can change transmission, immune escape, treatment susceptibility, and diagnostic target performance.

    Id

    identity-classification

    Title

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    The virus carries a single-stranded positive-sense RNA genome inside a helical nucleocapsid and lipid envelope. Spike proteins bind host receptors and mediate membrane fusion. The RNA genome can be translated after entry, allowing replication in the cytoplasm. Because the envelope is lipid based, soap and appropriate disinfectants disrupt the particle, but respiratory spread can occur before surfaces are relevant.

    Id

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    People with infection are the main reservoir. Virus-containing respiratory particles are released during breathing, speaking, coughing, and sneezing; risk rises with close, prolonged indoor exposure and poor ventilation. Transmission can occur before symptoms or from people who never develop symptoms. A positive nucleic-acid test can persist after infectiousness has ended, so test type, timing, symptoms, and current guidance must be interpreted together.

    Id

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Infection begins in susceptible respiratory epithelium and can extend into lower-airway and alveolar tissue. Direct cellular injury and dysregulated host inflammation disrupt gas exchange; endothelial and coagulation effects can contribute thrombosis and organ injury. Severe disease is therefore not simply more virus in the nose: host response, age, immunity, comorbidity, and timing shape progression.

    Id

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    COVID-19 can cause fever, cough, sore throat, fatigue, myalgia, headache, altered smell or taste, and gastrointestinal symptoms, but no symptom pattern is fully specific. Severe disease may produce hypoxemia, pneumonia, thrombosis, cardiac or neurologic injury, and post-acute symptoms. Trouble breathing, chest pressure, confusion, cyanosis, shock, or rapidly falling oxygen saturation requires urgent evaluation.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Nucleic-acid amplification tests detect viral RNA with high sensitivity, while antigen tests detect viral proteins and are generally less sensitive. A negative antigen result is a snapshot and may require repeat testing or NAAT confirmation under current instructions. Antibody testing usually reflects prior infection or vaccination and does not diagnose current infection. Recent infection can leave residual RNA, so repeated NAAT use requires timing-aware interpretation.

    Id

    diagnostic-identification

    Title

    Diagnostic identification

  • Content

    Current vaccination reduces severe disease even as products and recommendations evolve. Respiratory hygiene, staying away from others while acutely ill, cleaner air, source control, and current disease-specific healthcare Transmission-Based Precautions reduce spread. Antiviral treatment is time sensitive and patient specific; eligibility, interactions, renal or hepatic function, immune status, disease severity, and circulating-variant susceptibility all matter.
