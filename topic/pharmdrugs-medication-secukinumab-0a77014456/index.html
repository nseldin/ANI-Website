Class

Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that selectively neutralizes interleukin-17A

Used To Treat

Label-specific plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, nonradiographic axial spondyloarthritis, enthesitis-related arthritis, juvenile psoriatic arthritis, and hidradenitis suppurativa. Subcutaneous and selected IV products have distinct dosing; it is not an inflammatory-bowel-disease treatment.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity and infections can occur. Evaluate for tuberculosis before treatment and avoid live vaccines. Mucocutaneous Candida and other infections increase with IL-17 blockade. New or worsening inflammatory bowel disease has occurred; exercise caution in known IBD and urgently evaluate persistent diarrhea, abdominal pain, weight loss, or blood. Severe eczematous eruptions have also been reported.

Mechanism

IL-17A homodimers and IL-17A/F heterodimers engage the IL-17RA/IL-17RC receptor complex. The adaptor ACT1 recruits TRAF proteins, activating NF-kappaB, MAPK, C/EBP, and stabilization of inflammatory mRNA. Keratinocytes and stromal cells then release CXCL chemokines, IL-6, GM-CSF, and antimicrobial peptides that recruit neutrophils and amplify barrier inflammation. Secukinumab sequesters IL-17A before receptor binding, reducing this effector program without directly blocking upstream IL-23 or IL-12. Because IL-17 supports neutrophil recruitment and mucocutaneous antifungal defense, concentration-related Candida and selected bacterial infections are mechanistically plausible. Direct downstream blockade differs from p19 or p40 inhibition and may have opposite implications in bowel disease.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen TB and vaccines, inspect for oral/genital Candida and skin infection, and ask about chronic diarrhea, blood, pain, or weight loss before and during treatment.

  • Track skin, nail, joint, enthesis, spine, and hidradenitis outcomes separately; verify IV versus subcutaneous product and loading schedule.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious secukinumab hypersensitivity; active clinically important infection requires resolution before dosing.

  • Clarify TB, inflammatory bowel disease or unexplained bowel symptoms, recurrent Candida, latex sensitivity for relevant devices, vaccination, and concomitant immunosuppression.

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines and do not combine with another biologic or JAK inhibitor without specialist evidence.

  • Major inflammatory improvement can normalize cytokine-suppressed CYP activity, so narrow-index substrates deserve clinical review even though secukinumab is not a direct CYP inducer.

Key Labs

  • TB and infection evaluation, vaccination review, disease-domain assessments, and CBC/liver context when clinically indicated

  • Bowel symptoms, Candida examination, eczematous eruption, injection/infusion reaction, and objective response

Nclex Traps

  • Secukinumab blocks IL-17A downstream; it does not block IL-23 p19 or shared p40.

  • Its psoriasis benefit does not make it a bowel-disease biologic; IL-17 blockade can worsen IBD.

Description

Secukinumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds IL-17A, a downstream effector cytokine produced by Th17 cells and other lymphocytes. IL-17A stimulates keratinocytes, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and epithelium to release chemokines and antimicrobial peptides, recruiting neutrophils and sustaining psoriatic skin, joint, enthesis, and axial inflammation. Neutralization can rapidly improve these domains, but IL-17A also supports mucosal defense against Candida and helps maintain bowel-barrier balance. That dual role explains mucocutaneous candidiasis and why new or worsening inflammatory bowel disease, severe diarrhea, or blood in stool is a target-linked warning rather than an unrelated side effect.

Adverse Effects

  • Nasopharyngitis, upper-respiratory infection, diarrhea, headache, injection reaction, and mucocutaneous Candida

  • Serious infection, TB, anaphylaxis, new or worsening Crohn disease/ulcerative colitis, severe eczematous eruption, and hypersensitivity
