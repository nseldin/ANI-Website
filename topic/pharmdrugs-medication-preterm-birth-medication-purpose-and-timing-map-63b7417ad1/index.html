Used To Treat

Threatened or anticipated preterm birth when a short delay or fetal preparation can improve outcome. Tocolytics may briefly delay delivery; antenatal corticosteroids accelerate fetal organ maturation; magnesium sulfate can provide fetal neuroprotection before anticipated early preterm birth and separately prevents or treats eclamptic seizures.

Description

These drugs are often taught together but they do different jobs. A tocolytic such as nifedipine or selected short-course indomethacin reduces contractions long enough to complete corticosteroids, give magnesium for neuroprotection, or transfer to a higher-level neonatal center; it does not cure the cause of preterm labor or guarantee term delivery. Betamethasone changes fetal gene expression so lungs, brain, and gut mature faster. Magnesium sulfate does not mature lungs and is not a reliable maintenance tocolytic; its obstetric roles are seizure prevention in severe preeclampsia/eclampsia and neuroprotection when early preterm delivery is imminent. Terbutaline may briefly relax the uterus in selected acute situations but cannot be used as prolonged outpatient maintenance because maternal cardiopulmonary toxicity can be fatal.

Mechanism

Nifedipine blocks L-type calcium entry into myometrium, reducing calcium-calmodulin activation of myosin-light-chain kinase. Indomethacin blocks cyclooxygenase and lowers prostaglandins that promote cervical change and contraction, but the same prostaglandins maintain fetal renal perfusion and ductus arteriosus patency. Terbutaline activates beta-2 receptors, increasing Gs, adenylyl cyclase, cAMP, and protein kinase A, which suppresses smooth-muscle myosin-light-chain kinase. Betamethasone binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors and induces surfactant proteins, phospholipid synthesis, epithelial sodium transport, and broader organ maturation. Magnesium modulates calcium entry, NMDA signaling, and neuromuscular transmitter release; its fetal neuroprotective mechanism is probably multifactorial and is not equivalent to stopping labor.

Boxed Warning

The window and stop rules are the safety lesson. Terbutaline has a boxed warning against prolonged tocolysis beyond 48 to 72 hours and against outpatient or home maintenance because maternal death, arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, pulmonary edema, hyperglycemia, and hypokalemia have occurred. Indomethacin and other NSAIDs can cause fetal renal dysfunction and oligohydramnios from about 20 weeks and premature ductus arteriosus closure around 30 weeks or later. Magnesium sulfate beyond 5 to 7 days in pregnancy can cause fetal hypocalcemia, skeletal demineralization, osteopenia, and neonatal fracture. Tocolysis is inappropriate when delivery is safer than delay.

Adverse Effects

  • Maternal hypotension, tachycardia, headache, flushing, tremor, nausea, weakness, or medication-specific renal and GI effects

  • Pulmonary edema, dysrhythmia, magnesium toxicity, fetal ductal constriction, oligohydramnios, neonatal hypoglycemia, or delayed delivery despite a condition that requires birth

Contraindications

  • Do not use tocolysis simply because contractions are present; confirm cervical change and exclude hemorrhage, infection, severe preeclampsia, fetal compromise, demise, or another reason delivery should proceed.

  • Choose by gestational age, maternal cardiovascular and renal status, fetal fluid and ductal risk, concurrent magnesium, and the actual goal of treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • State the goal before giving the drug: gain a short window, mature fetal organs, protect the fetal brain, or prevent maternal seizure. That single sentence prevents category errors.

  • Trend maternal vital signs, respiratory status, reflexes and urine output when magnesium is used; fetal status, contractions, fluid status, and drug-specific metabolic effects remain essential.

Interactions

  • Nifedipine plus magnesium or other vasodilators can increase hypotension and neuromuscular weakness; use protocol-specific monitoring.

  • NSAIDs add renal and bleeding risk; beta agonists plus sympathomimetics or corticosteroid-related hyperglycemia can magnify tachycardia and metabolic disturbance.

Key Labs

  • Gestational age, cervical change, membrane and infection assessment, fetal status, maternal blood pressure, glucose, electrolytes, renal function, and indication-specific preeclampsia labs

  • For magnesium: respiratory rate, deep-tendon reflexes, urine output, creatinine, and serum level when clinically indicated; for prolonged NSAID exposure: amniotic fluid and fetal ductal assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Tocolytics usually buy hours to roughly 48 hours for another intervention; they do not reverse all pathways leading to preterm birth.

  • Magnesium for neuroprotection is not the same as magnesium for eclampsia, and neither role means it is the preferred long-term contraction suppressant.
