Definition

Mitgehen is a bedside motor sign. It is a clue to catatonic automatic obedience. A positive finding means the limb moves with the pressure despite instruction to resist.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mitgehen means the limb moves with the pressure despite instruction to resist.

Why It Matters

The observed Mitgehen finding can signal catatonic automatic obedience. Exaggerated stimulus-bound compliance overrides intentional resistance. Mitgehen cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonic automatic obedience

Assessment Method

Apply very light fingertip pressure to a limb after explanation.

Positive Finding

The limb moves with the pressure despite instruction to resist.

Mechanism

Exaggerated stimulus-bound compliance overrides intentional resistance.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mitgehen finding raises concern for catatonic automatic obedience because exaggerated stimulus-bound compliance overrides intentional resistance. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mitgehen does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mitgehen to focus evaluation of catatonic automatic obedience and the reviewed alternatives: waxy flexibility, paratonia, normal cooperation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply very light fingertip pressure to a limb after explanation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Mitgehen as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Suggestion and misunderstanding affect response. Therefore, Mitgehen cannot confirm or exclude catatonic automatic obedience by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • waxy flexibility

  • paratonia

  • normal cooperation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as waxy flexibility, paratonia, normal cooperation.

False Negative Situations

Mitgehen may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonic automatic obedience.

Modern Status

Current specialist catatonia sign. Interpret Mitgehen in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Suggestion and misunderstanding affect response.

Safety Precautions

Observe Mitgehen with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Apply very light fingertip pressure to a limb after explanation. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use minimal pressure and never exploit automatic obedience.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
