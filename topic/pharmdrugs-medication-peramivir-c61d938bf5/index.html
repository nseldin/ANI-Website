Class

Intravenous neuraminidase inhibitor for acute uncomplicated influenza A and B

Used To Treat

Acute uncomplicated influenza in patients at least six months old who have been symptomatic for no more than two days under the U.S. label. IV access can help when oral or inhaled administration is impractical, but single-dose peramivir is not routinely preferred for hospitalized severe influenza because outcome evidence is limited.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious skin reactions, anaphylaxis, and neuropsychiatric events have been reported. Renal dose adjustment is required. Do not assume a one-time infusion covers prolonged severe viral replication, immunocompromised shedding, or hospitalized disease; duration and agent selection may need specialist modification. Observe for infusion hypersensitivity and continue to assess bacterial superinfection and respiratory decline.

Mechanism

Peramivir resembles the oxocarbenium-ion transition state formed during neuraminidase cleavage of sialic acid. It binds tightly to conserved residues in the viral enzyme's catalytic pocket, preventing cleavage of host-cell sialic acid from budding virions. Virions remain tethered and aggregate, reducing spread to uninfected airway cells. IV administration bypasses gut absorption and deposits active drug directly into plasma; no prodrug activation is required. Renal filtration clears most drug unchanged, giving creatinine clearance a direct effect on area under the curve. Neuraminidase mutations can reduce susceptibility and may overlap incompletely with oseltamivir or zanamivir resistance, so persistent viral replication in an immunocompromised host warrants expert evaluation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify symptom-onset time, uncomplicated versus hospitalized/progressive status, renal function, weight/age, and infusion duration before giving.

  • Continue monitoring after the infusion; a single dose does not rule out worsening viral pneumonia, secondary bacterial infection, or the need for a different regimen.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity to peramivir or a formulation component.

  • Recalculate dose for reduced creatinine clearance, including dialysis-specific guidance; a standard 600-mg adult dose can overexpose a patient with kidney failure.

Interactions

  • Separate from live attenuated intranasal influenza vaccine according to current CDC timing because peramivir can inhibit vaccine-virus replication.

  • Few CYP interactions are expected, but renal function and nephrotoxic illness determine exposure.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR or creatinine clearance, oxygenation, liver tests when indicated, and severity assessment

  • Infusion reaction, diarrhea and hydration, neurologic behavior, symptom progression, secondary infection, and resistance in prolonged disease

Nclex Traps

  • IV route does not automatically mean best therapy for hospitalized influenza; oral/enteric oseltamivir has stronger routine recommendation.

  • Peramivir is a neuraminidase inhibitor, not the endonuclease inhibitor baloxavir.

Description

Peramivir is an intravenously administered transition-state analogue that binds influenza neuraminidase and prevents release of progeny influenza A and B virions. A single infusion provides prolonged neuraminidase exposure for uncomplicated disease, avoiding oral absorption and inhaler technique. That convenience should not be mistaken for broader evidence: CDC generally recommends oral or enteric oseltamivir for hospitalized influenza because clinical outcome data are stronger, while peramivir's labeled single-dose evidence comes from uncomplicated early illness. The drug is eliminated largely unchanged by the kidney, so reduced creatinine clearance increases exposure and requires dose reduction even though treatment may involve only one infusion.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, neutropenia, vomiting, and infusion-related discomfort

  • Anaphylaxis, severe skin reaction, liver-test elevation, neuropsychiatric behavior, and treatment-emergent resistance
