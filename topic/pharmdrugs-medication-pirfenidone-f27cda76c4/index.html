Class

Oral pyridone antifibrotic for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis with an incompletely defined molecular target

Used To Treat

Treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in adults. Pirfenidone slows average lung-function decline and disease progression; it does not remove mature collagen scar, rapidly improve oxygenation, or treat an acute ILD exacerbation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Drug-induced liver injury can be severe or fatal; obtain ALT, AST, and bilirubin before therapy, monthly for six months, then every three months and when symptoms occur. Photosensitivity and rash require sun minimization, protective clothing, and high-SPF sunscreen. SJS, TEN, and DRESS have occurred; stop and urgently evaluate blistering, mucosal lesions, facial edema, fever, or systemic rash. Persistent GI intolerance, poor intake, and weight loss can undermine treatment.

Mechanism

Pirfenidone has antifibrotic, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant effects in experimental systems, including reduced TGF-beta-associated signaling, fibroblast proliferation, collagen transcription and deposition, and selected cytokines. The label states that its clinical mechanism has not been established; these supported pathway effects must not be mistaken for a proven single molecular target. The drug is metabolized predominantly by CYP1A2 with contributions from other CYP enzymes. Strong CYP1A2 inhibition raises parent-drug exposure and toxicity, while tobacco smoke induction lowers exposure. Photosensitivity likely reflects photoreactive drug or metabolite effects in skin, which explains why sun avoidance and high-SPF protection are pharmacologic safety measures rather than generic wellness advice.

Nursing Essentials

  • Titrate over fourteen days, give with food, track nausea, appetite, weight, sun exposure, rash, and adherence, and restart carefully after a prolonged interruption according to the label.

  • Teach liver-injury symptoms and distinguish ordinary photosensitivity from mucosal, blistering, febrile, or systemic rash that may be SCAR.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but severe hepatic impairment and end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis are not recommended

  • Confirmed severe cutaneous reaction requires permanent discontinuation; strong CYP1A2 inhibition or ongoing smoking requires treatment review

Interactions

  • Fluvoxamine should generally be stopped or pirfenidone substantially reduced; ciprofloxacin and other CYP1A2 inhibitors can require dose modification.

  • Smoking lowers exposure through CYP1A2 induction; avoid other photosensitizers when possible and monitor additive hepatic toxicity.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, bilirubin before treatment, monthly for six months, then every three months; weight, appetite, renal function, and skin assessment

  • FVC, oxygen requirement, exertional desaturation, symptoms, and acute exacerbations as disease outcomes

Nclex Traps

  • The exact human target is not established; TGF-beta reduction is a supported pathway effect, not a proven sole receptor mechanism.

  • Photosensitivity counseling is central drug safety, and smoking can lower drug exposure rather than merely worsen the lung disease.

Description

Pirfenidone is an oral antifibrotic pyridone that reduces fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and profibrotic signaling in experimental systems while slowing average lung-function decline in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Its exact molecular target in humans is not established, so its action is described through supported pathway effects rather than an unproven single receptor. Reduced mediators including TGF-beta can slow ongoing matrix deposition. Treatment is titrated over fourteen days and taken with food to improve tolerability. Liver injury, nausea, appetite and weight loss, photosensitivity, and rash are the practical limiting toxicities. Smoking induces CYP1A2 and lowers exposure, while fluvoxamine and other strong CYP1A2 inhibition can raise exposure sharply.

Adverse Effects
