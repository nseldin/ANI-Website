Definition

Gorlin sign is a bedside hypermobility sign. It is a clue to connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. A positive finding means the tongue reaches the nose tip.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gorlin sign means the tongue reaches the nose tip.

Why It Matters

The observed Gorlin sign finding can signal connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. Ligamentous and soft-tissue laxity plus tongue length increase excursion. Gorlin sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to attempt touching the tip of the nose with the tongue without force.

Positive Finding

The tongue reaches the nose tip.

Mechanism

Ligamentous and soft-tissue laxity plus tongue length increase excursion.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Gorlin sign finding raises concern for connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome because ligamentous and soft-tissue laxity plus tongue length increase excursion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gorlin sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Gorlin sign to focus evaluation of connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and the reviewed alternatives: long tongue, facial anatomy, learned flexibility. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to attempt touching the tip of the nose with the tongue without force. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Gorlin sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A positive result is not diagnostic and a negative result does not exclude EDS. Therefore, Gorlin sign cannot confirm or exclude connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • long tongue

  • facial anatomy

  • learned flexibility

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as long tongue, facial anatomy, learned flexibility.

False Negative Situations

Gorlin sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct; common enough in healthy people to be nonspecific. When performed as described, Gorlin sign can adjust concern for connective-tissue hypermobility, classically Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Gorlin sign: A positive result is not diagnostic and a negative result does not exclude EDS.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not encourage painful stretching or use this novelty maneuver instead of validated criteria.
