Symptom assessment with OPQRST

Nclex Essential

true

Quick Answer

Use OPQRST to organize the patient's own description, but pair it with immediate danger screening, objective assessment, medication history, and the patient's understanding and goals.

Sections

  • Label

    Patient-centered questioning

    Text

    Begin with an open question and let the patient describe the problem before narrowing details. Ask what makes it better or worse, where it is and whether it moves, how it feels, how severe it is, and how it has changed over time.

  • Label

    Beyond a number

    Text

    A severity score is incomplete without function, distress, trend, and context. A quiet patient can have a serious condition, while severe reported pain deserves assessment even when objective findings are limited.

  • Label

    Urgent exceptions

    Text

    Do not finish a long interview before acting on airway compromise, stroke findings, shock, severe bleeding, anaphylaxis, rapidly changing neurologic status, or another immediate threat.

  • Label

    Documentation

    Text

    Record the patient's words when clinically important, the complete time course, associated symptoms, interventions, response, and escalation.

Summary

OPQRST is a focused symptom-history structure for onset, provoking or relieving factors, quality, region or radiation, severity, and time course.

    Expansion
    • Cue

      O

      Meaning

      Onset. When and how a symptom began can separate sudden emergencies from gradual processes.

    • Cue

      P

      Meaning

      Provocation and Palliation. Triggers and relieving factors reveal mechanism and functional impact.

    • Cue

      Q

      Meaning

      Quality. The patient's own words help characterize the experience without forcing a diagnosis.

    • Cue

      R

      Meaning

      Region and Radiation. Location and spread can point toward the involved organ, tissue, or nerve pathway.

    • Cue

      S

      Meaning

      Severity. Intensity and interference with function establish baseline and response to treatment.

    • Cue

      T

      Meaning

      Timing. Duration, frequency, pattern, and change over time reveal trajectory.

    Limitations

    • Not every symptom fits every letter.

    • Associated symptoms, patient understanding, and objective findings remain necessary.
