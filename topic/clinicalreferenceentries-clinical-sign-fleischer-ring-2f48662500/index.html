Definition

Fleischer ring is an observed corneal sign. It is a clue to keratoconus. A positive finding means a partial or complete brown-green ring surrounds the cone base.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Fleischer ring means a partial or complete brown-green ring surrounds the cone base.

Why It Matters

The observed Fleischer ring finding can signal keratoconus. Iron from tears deposits in basal epithelium where tear pooling and epithelial turnover change. Fleischer ring cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • keratoconus

Assessment Method

Examine the corneal base with slit lamp, often cobalt-blue filter.

Positive Finding

A partial or complete brown-green ring surrounds the cone base.

Mechanism

Iron from tears deposits in basal epithelium where tear pooling and epithelial turnover change.

Clinical Interpretation

Diagnostic Usefulness

Evidence Limitations

Incomplete rings are subtle and must be distinguished from peripheral Descemet copper. Therefore, Fleischer ring cannot confirm or exclude keratoconus by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Kayser-Fleischer ring

  • Hudson-Stähli line

  • foreign body rust

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Kayser-Fleischer ring, Hudson-Stähli line, foreign body rust.

False Negative Situations

Fleischer ring may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of keratoconus.
