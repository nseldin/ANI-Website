Class

Protozoal dihydrofolate-reductase inhibitor requiring leucovorin marrow rescue

Used To Treat

Toxoplasmosis treatment or suppression in combination with sulfadiazine or clindamycin and leucovorin, selected PCP/toxoplasmosis prophylaxis combinations, and uncommon malaria protocols. It must not be used as an incomplete solo toxoplasmosis regimen.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Pyrimethamine is a potent drug with potentially fatal marrow suppression. Give leucovorin with every treatment regimen, monitor CBC frequently, and stop or modify therapy for clinically important cytopenia. Folate-deficient patients, pregnancy, renal/hepatic disease, malabsorption, alcoholism, and concurrent antifolates have less reserve. Severe rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and anaphylaxis can occur, particularly with sulfonamide partners.

Mechanism

Dihydrofolate reductase reduces dihydrofolate to tetrahydrofolate, which carries one-carbon units needed to synthesize thymidylate, purines, and methionine. Pyrimethamine occupies the parasite DHFR folate-binding site and depletes reduced folates. Sulfadiazine or dapsone acts upstream by mimicking para-aminobenzoic acid at dihydropteroate synthase, reducing new dihydrofolate formation. Blocking consecutive steps creates synergy and limits escape. Human cells obtain folate from diet but still need DHFR during nucleotide synthesis, so sustained high exposure suppresses marrow. Leucovorin is 5-formyltetrahydrofolate and can enter the reduced-folate pool without DHFR, rescuing host cells while the combined parasite pathway remains strongly inhibited.

Nursing Essentials

  • Treat leucovorin as a required medication with its own administration check. A missed rescue dose is a safety event, not a harmless supplement omission.

  • Trend CBC often during loading and induction. Mouth ulcers, fever, sore throat, bruising, pallor, or fatigue can precede severe laboratory cytopenia.

Contraindications

  • Documented megaloblastic anemia from folate deficiency and serious hypersensitivity.

  • Do not give without leucovorin or without a complete disease-specific partner regimen; correct folate deficiency and review marrow reserve first.

Interactions

  • Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, methotrexate, phenytoin, zidovudine, and other antifolate or marrow-suppressive drugs increase cytopenia risk.

  • Highly protein-bound drugs and hepatic interactions may alter exposure; sulfonamide partner toxicity must be monitored separately.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and serial CBC with differential and platelets, renal and liver function, folate status, pregnancy assessment, and disease-specific serology/imaging

  • Neurologic examination for cerebral toxoplasmosis, fever/infection, oral ulcers, bleeding, rash, adherence to leucovorin and partner drug, and treatment response

Nclex Traps

  • Leucovorin is required host-marrow rescue; ordinary folic acid is not interchangeable.

  • Pyrimethamine alone is not a complete toxoplasmosis regimen because sequential pathway blockade requires a partner.

Description

Pyrimethamine is a folate-antagonist antiprotozoal that inhibits Toxoplasma and Plasmodium dihydrofolate reductase more strongly than the human enzyme. Blocking tetrahydrofolate regeneration prevents thymidylate and purine synthesis, so parasite DNA replication slows. In toxoplasmosis it is paired with sulfadiazine, which blocks the upstream enzyme dihydropteroate synthase, creating sequential folate blockade. High treatment doses also inhibit human marrow folate metabolism; leucovorin supplies reduced folate downstream of DHFR and protects neutrophil, red-cell, and platelet production. This is why leucovorin is part of the regimen from the start and why folic acid is not an adequate substitute.

Adverse Effects

  • Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, headache, insomnia, and rash

  • Megaloblastic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, severe skin reaction, seizures after overdose, and liver injury
