Definition

Listeria monocytogenes is a clinically important bacterium associated with febrile gastroenteritis, bacteremia, meningitis, pregnancy-associated listeriosis, and neonatal infection. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Listeria monocytogenes is a Gram-positive bacillus with a facultative anaerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, motile, and facultative intracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Internalins promote cell entry, listeriolysin permits phagosomal escape, and actin-based cell-to-cell spread supports placental, central nervous system, and bloodstream invasion. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Listeria monocytogenes is associated with febrile gastroenteritis, bacteremia, meningitis, pregnancy-associated listeriosis, and neonatal infection. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
