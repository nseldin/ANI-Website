    Contact transmission is spread through physical transfer of a microorganism from a source to a susceptible person. Direct contact occurs without an intermediate object; indirect contact uses contaminated hands, equipment, environmental surfaces, or other objects as the bridge.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    The route succeeds when an organism survives long enough on skin or an object, reaches a portal of entry such as damaged skin or a mucous membrane, and escapes removal by hand hygiene, cleaning, or barrier protection. The chain therefore has several breakable links rather than one inevitable outcome.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Examples include transfer from a draining wound during hands-on care and transfer from contaminated shared equipment. A fomite is the contaminated object in an indirect-contact event; it is not itself a microorganism.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Recognizing contact spread guides hand hygiene, glove and gown decisions, patient placement, equipment dedication, wound containment, and environmental cleaning. It also explains why apparently clean hands or shared devices can connect patients when technique fails.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Contact is a route, not a diagnosis or a universal isolation order. The organism, syndrome, amount of uncontrolled drainage, care setting, and current public-health or facility guidance determine whether Standard Precautions alone are enough or Contact Precautions must be added.
