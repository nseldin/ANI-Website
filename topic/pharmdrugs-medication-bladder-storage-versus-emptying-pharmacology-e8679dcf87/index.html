Used To Treat

Mechanism-based comparison of drugs used for overactive storage symptoms versus selected nonobstructive urinary retention.

Description

Bladder drugs make sense only after identifying the failed phase. During storage, sympathetic beta-3 signaling relaxes detrusor and antimuscarinics block parasympathetic contractions. During voiding, acetylcholine activates M3 receptors to contract detrusor while the outlet relaxes. Mirabegron and oxybutynin therefore improve storage but can worsen emptying; bethanechol can stimulate emptying only when the outlet is open and detrusor muscle can respond.

Mechanism

Mirabegron raises detrusor cyclic AMP through Gs-coupled beta-3 receptors. Oxybutynin blocks Gq-coupled muscarinic calcium signaling. Bethanechol directly activates muscarinic receptors and resists acetylcholinesterase, increasing IP3-calcium-mediated contraction. None of these drugs removes a stone, prostate obstruction, urethral stricture, clot, or anatomic kink. Forcing contraction against obstruction can raise pressure, cause pain/reflux, and injure or rupture a vulnerable wall.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Confirm whether the patient has urgency from overactivity, frequency from incomplete emptying, or true nonobstructive retention. A medication aimed at the wrong phase can intensify retention or force contraction against a dangerous obstruction.

Adverse Effects

  • Storage drugs: retention; antimuscarinics add systemic anticholinergic toxicity

  • Bethanechol: cramping, diarrhea, sweating, salivation, bradycardia, hypotension and bronchospasm

  • Misclassification can delay catheterization or correction of an obstruction

Contraindications

  • Mechanical urinary or GI obstruction before bethanechol

  • Urinary retention before antimuscarinics

  • Severe uncontrolled hypertension before mirabegron

Nursing Essentials

  • Use bladder scan/post-void residual, history, medication review and outlet evaluation to identify storage versus emptying failure.

  • Acute painful retention requires decompression and cause assessment, not a trial-and-error oral drug.

Interactions

  • Antimuscarinics oppose bethanechol pharmacologically.

  • Other anticholinergics increase storage/retention effects.

  • Cholinesterase inhibitors and other cholinergic drugs intensify bethanechol toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Post-void residual and urine output

  • Renal function and hydronephrosis evaluation when retention is significant

  • Blood pressure, pulse, bowel and respiratory assessment according to mechanism

Nclex Traps

  • Urinary frequency can mean a full bladder emptying in tiny amounts.

  • Bethanechol is for selected nonobstructive retention, not BPH obstruction.

  • Relaxing detrusor treats urgency but does not relax the prostate or urethra.
