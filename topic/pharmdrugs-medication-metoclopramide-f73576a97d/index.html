Class

Dopamine D2 receptor antagonist antiemetic and prokinetic agent

Used To Treat

Gastroparesis, nausea/vomiting, migraine-associated nausea, and selected peri-procedure or chemotherapy-related regimens when ordered.

Mechanism

Blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the chemoreceptor trigger zone to reduce nausea and stimulates upper-GI motility through prokinetic 5-HT4/cholinergic effects, speeding gastric emptying.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess bowel obstruction/bleeding/perforation concern before prokinetic use, EPS, akathisia, dystonia, parkinsonism, diarrhea, sedation, depression, and tardive dyskinesia risk with duration.

Contraindications

  • Clarify GI obstruction, perforation, bleeding, pheochromocytoma, seizure disorder, Parkinson disease, history of tardive dyskinesia, or hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Antipsychotics and other dopamine blockers raise EPS/NMS risk. CNS depressants increase sedation. Levodopa/dopamine agonist benefit can be reduced.

Key Labs

  • Renal function for dosing context; glucose trend in diabetic gastroparesis when relevant.

Nclex Traps

  • Metoclopramide is not just an antiemetic; it moves the stomach. Do not use prokinetic logic when obstruction/perforation is possible.

Description

Metoclopramide is a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist antiemetic and prokinetic agent: it blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the chemoreceptor trigger zone to reduce nausea and stimulates upper-GI motility through prokinetic 5-HT4/cholinergic effects, speeding gastric emptying. It is used for gastroparesis, nausea/vomiting, migraine-associated nausea, and selected peri-procedure or chemotherapy-related regimens when ordered.
