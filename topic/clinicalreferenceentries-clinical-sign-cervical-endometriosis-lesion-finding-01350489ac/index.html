Definition

Cervical endometriosis lesion finding is an observed lesion finding. It is a clue to cervical endometriosis. A positive finding means blue-black, red, or dark nodules are visible on the cervix.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cervical endometriosis lesion finding means blue-black, red, or dark nodules are visible on the cervix.

Why It Matters

The observed Cervical endometriosis lesion finding finding can signal cervical endometriosis. Cyclic hemorrhage within ectopic endometrial implants leaves blood products and pigment. Cervical endometriosis lesion finding cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cervical endometriosis

Assessment Method

Inspect the cervix during indicated speculum examination.

Positive Finding

Blue-black, red, or dark nodules are visible on the cervix.

Mechanism

Cyclic hemorrhage within ectopic endometrial implants leaves blood products and pigment.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Cervical endometriosis lesion finding finding raises concern for cervical endometriosis because cyclic hemorrhage within ectopic endometrial implants leaves blood products and pigment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cervical endometriosis lesion finding does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cervical endometriosis lesion finding to focus evaluation of cervical endometriosis and the reviewed alternatives: nabothian cyst, trauma, hemangioma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the cervix during indicated speculum examination. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cervical endometriosis lesion finding as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Color is nonspecific and malignancy must be considered. Therefore, Cervical endometriosis lesion finding cannot confirm or exclude cervical endometriosis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • trauma

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as nabothian cyst, trauma, hemangioma.

False Negative Situations

Cervical endometriosis lesion finding may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of cervical endometriosis.
