Class

Diazabicyclooctane inhibitor with broad class A, C, and D Acinetobacter serine beta-lactamase coverage

Used To Treat

Used with sulbactam in Xacduro for susceptible Acinetobacter baumannii-calcoaceticus complex HABP/VABP in adults.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Durlobactam alone is not antibacterial and does not inhibit metallo-beta-lactamases. Use only as the studied sulbactam combination for a supported susceptible Acinetobacter indication.

Mechanism

Durlobactam reversibly carbamylates susceptible serine beta-lactamases and can regenerate after deacylation. Its broad class D activity is especially important because Acinetobacter commonly carries OXA-type carbapenemases. The pharmacodynamic index differs from sulbactam: durlobactam protection correlates with unbound AUC relative to MIC, while sulbactam killing correlates with fT>MIC. Co-packaging and coordinated renal dosing preserve both targets. Altered PBPs, MBLs, efflux, porin loss, and very high burden can still cause resistance or failure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the co-packaged preparation, total dose, renal function, infusion timing, organism, and susceptibility because one correctly prepared inhibitor cannot rescue missing sulbactam exposure.

  • Monitor hepatic, hematologic, potassium, renal, respiratory, and microbiologic response because critical Acinetobacter pneumonia can fail for host or source reasons as well as enzyme mismatch.

Contraindications

  • Known severe hypersensitivity to sulbactam, durlobactam, or other beta-lactams is a contraindication under the current Xacduro label.

  • Do not substitute durlobactam for avibactam, relebactam, or vaborbactam because enzyme spectra and approved partners differ.

Interactions

  • No clinically important direct interaction among durlobactam, sulbactam, imipenem, and cilastatin was observed in a healthy-volunteer study, but complete-regimen toxicity still matters.

  • Nephrotoxic salvage therapy can reduce renal reserve and raise combination exposure.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, resistance mechanism when available, creatinine/eGFR, AST/ALT/bilirubin, CBC, potassium, oxygenation, and respiratory source assessment.

  • A class B MBL result explains why durlobactam protection may fail despite broad serine-enzyme coverage.

Nclex Traps

  • Durlobactam is the shield; sulbactam is the direct PBP-active Acinetobacter drug.

  • Broad OXA inhibition does not mean MBL inhibition.

Description

Durlobactam is a non-beta-lactam diazabicyclooctane inhibitor built for the enzyme profile of Acinetobacter. It inhibits many class A ESBL/KPC, class C ADC, and broad class D OXA-type serine beta-lactamases that would otherwise hydrolyze sulbactam. Durlobactam has no clinically useful antibacterial activity by itself. Its job is to protect sulbactam, which directly binds Acinetobacter PBP1 and PBP3. It does not inhibit class B metallo-beta-lactamases, so the combination still requires susceptibility confirmation. This enzyme-to-partner fit is why the complete Xacduro product identity matters.

Adverse Effects

  • Xacduro can cause liver-test abnormalities, diarrhea, anemia, hypokalemia, hypersensitivity, CDI, and infusion complications.

  • Renal dysfunction raises both component exposures and requires coordinated adjustment.
