Definition

Sickle cells is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to hemoglobin S polymerization. A positive finding means elongated crescent or boat-shaped erythrocytes are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Sickle cells means elongated crescent or boat-shaped erythrocytes are present.

Why It Matters

The Sickle cells laboratory finding can signal hemoglobin S polymerization. Deoxygenated HbS polymers distort and damage the red-cell membrane. Sickle cells cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hemoglobin S polymerization

Assessment Method

Review peripheral smear, ideally with confirmatory hemoglobin analysis.

Positive Finding

Elongated crescent or boat-shaped erythrocytes are present.

Mechanism

Deoxygenated HbS polymers distort and damage the red-cell membrane.

Clinical Interpretation

The Sickle cells laboratory finding raises concern for hemoglobin S polymerization because deoxygenated HbS polymers distort and damage the red-cell membrane. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Sickle cells does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Sickle cells to focus evaluation of hemoglobin S polymerization and the reviewed alternatives: elliptocytes, smear artifact, hemoglobin C crystals. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review peripheral smear, ideally with confirmatory hemoglobin analysis. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Sickle cells as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Transfusion and high fetal hemoglobin can reduce visible sickling. Therefore, Sickle cells cannot confirm or exclude hemoglobin S polymerization by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • elliptocytes

  • smear artifact

  • hemoglobin C crystals

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with elliptocytes, smear artifact, hemoglobin C crystals.

False Negative Situations

Sickle cells may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.

Modern Status

Current important morphology; absence on one smear does not exclude disease. Interpret Sickle cells in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Transfusion and high fetal hemoglobin can reduce visible sickling.

Safety Precautions

For Sickle cells, verify the correct patient and the specimen, collection, handling, preparation, and laboratory method used to identify this finding. The reviewed precaution is: Confirm genotype and assess acute pain, chest symptoms, fever, neurologic deficit, and sequestration.

Communicate the finding with its suspected association, hemoglobin S polymerization, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
