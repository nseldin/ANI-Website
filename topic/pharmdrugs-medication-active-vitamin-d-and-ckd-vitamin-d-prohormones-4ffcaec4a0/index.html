Class Example Names

  • Calcitriol

Used To Treat

Calcitriol treats selected hypocalcemia/hypoparathyroidism and CKD secondary hyperparathyroidism; doxercalciferol treats secondary hyperparathyroidism in stage 3/4 CKD or dialysis according to formulation.

Description

Calcitriol is already the active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin-D3 hormone and does not need kidney 1-alpha-hydroxylation. Doxercalciferol is a 1-alpha-hydroxyvitamin-D2 prohormone activated in the liver to an active VDR ligand, also bypassing the failing renal activation step. Both suppress PTH but can overshoot into hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, vascular/soft-tissue calcification, digitalis toxicity and low-turnover adynamic bone.

Mechanism

Activated ligand binds the nuclear vitamin-D receptor, heterodimerizes with RXR and changes DNA transcription. Intestinal calcium and phosphate transport rises, while parathyroid PTH synthesis/proliferation falls. In CKD this interrupts the cycle of low calcitriol, hypocalcemic stimulus and secondary hyperparathyroidism, but raising both calcium and phosphate can increase calcium-phosphate loading; suppressing PTH too far removes needed bone turnover.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not combine casually with other vitamin-D compounds or high calcium intake. Hypercalcemia can cause confusion, constipation, polyuria, dehydration, arrhythmia and seizures; chronic calcium-phosphate excess promotes vascular and soft-tissue calcification. Digitalis toxicity becomes more likely when calcium rises.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria and hyperphosphatemia

  • Nausea, constipation, weakness, confusion, polyuria/polydipsia and arrhythmia

  • Adynamic bone disease from excessive PTH suppression

  • Anaphylaxis reported with doxercalciferol injection

Contraindications

  • Hypercalcemia, vitamin-D toxicity and serious product hypersensitivity

  • Avoid duplicate vitamin-D analog therapy unless intentionally managed

  • Dose from calcium, phosphorus and intact PTH trend rather than a fixed supplement mindset

Nursing Essentials

  • Know whether the goal is calcium replacement in hypoparathyroidism or PTH suppression in CKD; target and monitoring differ.

  • Hold/reduce according to protocol when calcium is high or PTH becomes abnormally low.

Interactions

  • Thiazides and calcium products increase hypercalcemia; magnesium antacids can cause hypermagnesemia in dialysis.

  • Hypercalcemia potentiates digoxin toxicity.

  • CYP inducers/inhibitors can alter doxercalciferol activation/clearance.

Key Labs

  • Calcium, phosphorus and magnesium

  • Intact PTH and alkaline phosphatase

  • Renal function, 25-OH vitamin D and calcium-phosphate trend

Nclex Traps

  • Calcitriol is active hormone; ordinary nutritional vitamin D still needs activation.

  • Doxercalciferol bypasses kidney activation by using hepatic conversion.

  • Lowering PTH too far can produce adynamic, fracture-prone bone.
