Used To Treat

COVID-19 in hospitalized and selected high-risk nonhospitalized adults and pediatric patients who meet current product-label age/weight, disease, timing, and course criteria. Duration differs by outpatient versus inpatient context and clinical progression.

Mechanism

Carboxylesterase 1 and/or cathepsin A convert remdesivir to a monophosphate intermediate, and host kinases produce active GS-443902 triphosphate. Viral RdRp preferentially incorporates it as an ATP analog; polymerase stalls around the i+3 position, and incorporated analog can also impair subsequent template-directed synthesis. Human DNA/RNA polymerases, including mitochondrial RNA polymerase, are much less sensitive. The IV route bypasses oral instability. Intracellular activation and viral replication timing explain both efficacy and the in-vitro antagonism concern with chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and infusion reactions can occur. Perform hepatic testing before and during treatment as clinically appropriate; follow current stopping criteria for marked ALT elevation or liver-inflammation symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to remdesivir or components.

  • Do not coadminister chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine because in-vitro antagonism can reduce antiviral activity; do not delay essential respiratory, anticoagulation, or anti-inflammatory care to administer it.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document symptom onset, risk eligibility, oxygen/disease phase, baseline hepatic tests, IV access, infusion time, and complete multi-day course plan. Observe during and after infusion for hypersensitivity according to the label.

  • Explain that stopping viral replication is time-sensitive and does not produce an immediate negative test or reverse established alveolar injury.

Interactions

  • Chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine are not recommended with remdesivir due to antagonism in cell culture.

  • Classic CYP interactions are less central than infusion delivery, disease stage, hepatic monitoring, and concurrent evidence-based COVID treatment.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, prothrombin time as clinically appropriate, renal function for overall illness, oxygen requirement, and syndrome-specific coagulation/inflammation testing.

  • No routine remdesivir or intracellular triphosphate level guides bedside dosing.

Nclex Traps

  • Remdesivir is a nucleotide prodrug, not an antibody or corticosteroid.

  • Current labeling permits use without renal dose adjustment, including dialysis.

Class Pathway

  • Intracellular phosphoramidate activation

  • ATP-analog incorporation by viral RdRp

  • Delayed RNA-chain termination and template inhibition

Description

Remdesivir is an IV phosphoramidate prodrug that cells convert into an active adenosine triphosphate analog. The metabolite competes with ATP at the SARS-CoV-2 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, becomes incorporated into growing viral RNA, and causes delayed chain termination and impaired use of the RNA as a template. It therefore reduces new viral RNA production rather than directly repairing lung injury or suppressing late host inflammation. That mechanism explains why early treatment during active viral replication is more useful than waiting until irreversible inflammatory, thrombotic, or fibrotic injury dominates. Current U.S. labeling recommends no dose adjustment for any degree of renal impairment, including dialysis; older cyclodextrin-based avoidance teaching is outdated.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea and aminotransferase elevation are common important effects.

  • Infusion/anaphylactic reactions can include hypotension or hypertension, tachycardia or bradycardia, hypoxia, fever, dyspnea, wheeze, rash, nausea, diaphoresis, or shivering; severe hepatic injury is possible.
