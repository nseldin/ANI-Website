Definition

Carpet-tack sign is a scale-undersurface sign. It is a clue to discoid lupus erythematosus. A positive finding means horny plugs project from the underside like carpet tacks.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Carpet-tack sign means horny plugs project from the underside like carpet tacks.

Why It Matters

The observed Carpet-tack sign finding can signal discoid lupus erythematosus. Hyperkeratin fills dilated follicular openings and remains attached to the scale. Carpet-tack sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • discoid lupus erythematosus

Assessment Method

Gently lift an already loosened adherent scale from a lesion.

Positive Finding

Horny plugs project from the underside like carpet tacks.

Mechanism

Hyperkeratin fills dilated follicular openings and remains attached to the scale.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Carpet-tack sign finding raises concern for discoid lupus erythematosus because hyperkeratin fills dilated follicular openings and remains attached to the scale. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Carpet-tack sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Carpet-tack sign to focus evaluation of discoid lupus erythematosus and the reviewed alternatives: tinea, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently lift an already loosened adherent scale from a lesion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Carpet-tack sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Not every lesion has removable scale and trauma can scar. Therefore, Carpet-tack sign cannot confirm or exclude discoid lupus erythematosus by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tinea

  • psoriasis

  • seborrheic dermatitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tinea, psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis.

False Negative Situations

Carpet-tack sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of discoid lupus erythematosus.

Modern Status

Historical but characteristic clue; avoid traumatic removal. Carpet-tack sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude discoid lupus erythematosus. Apply the reviewed limitation for Carpet-tack sign: Not every lesion has removable scale and trauma can scar.

Safety Precautions

Observe Carpet-tack sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Gently lift an already loosened adherent scale from a lesion. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not forcibly detach adherent scale from fragile scarring plaques.
