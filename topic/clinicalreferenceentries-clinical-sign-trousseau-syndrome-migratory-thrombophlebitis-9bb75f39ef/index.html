Definition

Trousseau syndrome migratory thrombophlebitis is a clinical vascular constellation. It is a clue to malignancy-associated hypercoagulability. A positive finding means episodes of superficial thrombophlebitis migrate or recur without a clear trigger.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Trousseau syndrome migratory thrombophlebitis means episodes of superficial thrombophlebitis migrate or recur without a clear trigger.

Why It Matters

The Trousseau syndrome migratory thrombophlebitis pattern can signal malignancy-associated hypercoagulability. Tumor mucins, tissue factor, inflammatory cytokines, and platelet activation drive systemic thrombosis. Trousseau syndrome migratory thrombophlebitis cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • malignancy-associated hypercoagulability

Assessment Method

Episodes of superficial thrombophlebitis migrate or recur without a clear trigger.

Evidence Limitations

Many thromboses are nonmalignant and cancer may be occult without migration. Therefore, Trousseau syndrome migratory thrombophlebitis cannot confirm or exclude malignancy-associated hypercoagulability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.
