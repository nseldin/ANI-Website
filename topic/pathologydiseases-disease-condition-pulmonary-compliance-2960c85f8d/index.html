Pronunciation

PUL-muh-nair-ee kum-PLY-ens

Plain Meaning

How easily the lungs and chest system expand for a given pressure.

Pathology

High compliance means lungs inflate easily but may not recoil well, as in emphysema. Low compliance means stiff lungs that require more pressure to inflate, as in ARDS, pulmonary edema, fibrosis, atelectasis, obesity/chest-wall restriction, or abdominal pressure. Low compliance raises work of breathing and ventilator-pressure risk.

Etiology

ARDS, pulmonary edema, pneumonia, atelectasis, fibrosis, surfactant deficiency, chest-wall restriction, abdominal distention, and ventilator overdistention.

Signs Symptoms

  • Rapid shallow breathing, increased work of breathing, fatigue, high ventilator pressures, low tidal volumes if pressure-limited

  • Hypoxemia when stiff/collapsed units reduce gas exchange

Diagnostics

  • Ventilator plateau pressure and driving pressure in intubated clients

  • Respiratory assessment

  • Chest imaging

  • PFTs for chronic restrictive disease

Treatments

  • Treat underlying cause

  • Lung-protective ventilation

  • PEEP/recruitment when appropriate

  • Diuresis/antibiotics/anti-inflammatory therapy as indicated

  • Positioning, pain control, and secretion management

Nursing Priorities

  • Notice rising ventilator pressures or worsening fatigue as stiffness clues

  • Protect against volutrauma/barotrauma by following lung-protective strategies

  • Assess pain, abdominal distention, fluid status, secretions, and tube problems that can worsen mechanics

Complications

  • Respiratory failure

  • Ventilator-induced lung injury

  • Barotrauma

  • Hypoxemia

  • Fatigue/arrest

Patient Education

  • Explain that stiff lungs make breathing harder even when the airway is open.

Nclex Traps

  • Wheezing is airflow obstruction; low compliance is stiffness. They can coexist but are not the same problem.

  • In ARDS, stiff lungs need protective ventilation, not just bigger breaths.
