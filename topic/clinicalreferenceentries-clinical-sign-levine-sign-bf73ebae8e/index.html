Definition

Levine sign is an observed patient-gesture sign. It is a clue to ischemic chest discomfort. A positive finding means a clenched fist is placed over the sternum.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Levine sign means a clenched fist is placed over the sternum.

Why It Matters

The observed Levine sign finding can signal ischemic chest discomfort. Diffuse visceral cardiac afferent input is difficult to localize, so patients may use a broad pressure gesture rather than one fingertip. Levine sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ischemic chest discomfort

Assessment Method

Observe how a patient spontaneously indicates the location or quality of chest discomfort.

Positive Finding

A clenched fist is placed over the sternum.

Mechanism

Diffuse visceral cardiac afferent input is difficult to localize, so patients may use a broad pressure gesture rather than one fingertip.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Levine sign finding raises concern for ischemic chest discomfort because diffuse visceral cardiac afferent input is difficult to localize, so patients may use a broad pressure gesture rather than one fingertip. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Levine sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Levine sign to focus evaluation of ischemic chest discomfort and the reviewed alternatives: reflux, panic, musculoskeletal chest pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe how a patient spontaneously indicates the location or quality of chest discomfort. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Levine sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The gesture neither confirms nor excludes myocardial ischemia. Therefore, Levine sign cannot confirm or exclude ischemic chest discomfort by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • reflux

  • panic

  • musculoskeletal chest pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as reflux, panic, musculoskeletal chest pain.

False Negative Situations

Levine sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of ischemic chest discomfort.

Modern Status

Historical clue with limited diagnostic specificity. Levine sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude ischemic chest discomfort. Apply the reviewed limitation for Levine sign: The gesture neither confirms nor excludes myocardial ischemia.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Treat the reported chest pain and risk profile, not the gesture, as the clinical evidence.
