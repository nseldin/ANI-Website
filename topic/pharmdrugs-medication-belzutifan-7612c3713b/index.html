Class

Hypoxia-inducible factor 2-alpha inhibitor

Description

Belzutifan blocks HIF-2 alpha, a transcription factor that becomes abnormally stabilized when the von Hippel-Lindau protein pathway is lost. HIF-2 alpha drives genes for tumor survival, angiogenesis, and erythropoietin. Inhibiting it can suppress VHL-associated and selected clear-cell tumors, but reduced erythropoietin signaling also causes predictable anemia, and interference with hypoxia adaptation can cause clinically important low oxygen.

Mechanism

HIF-2 alpha normally pairs with HIF-1 beta and activates hypoxia-response genes. Functional VHL protein targets HIF-2 alpha for degradation when oxygen is adequate. VHL loss allows HIF-2 alpha to accumulate. Belzutifan binds a pocket in HIF-2 alpha and prevents heterodimerization, reducing transcription of VEGF, cyclin D1, erythropoietin, and other survival signals. The same on-target reduction in erythropoietin explains falling hemoglobin.

Used To Treat

Current label-specific uses include selected VHL disease-associated renal cell carcinoma, CNS hemangioblastoma, or pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor not requiring immediate surgery; advanced clear-cell renal cell carcinoma after specified prior therapy; and locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma in eligible adults and adolescents.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: embryo-fetal toxicity. Verify pregnancy status before treatment and use effective non-hormonal contraception because belzutifan can cause fetal harm and can reduce the effectiveness of some hormonal contraceptives.

Contraindications

  • The current label lists no formal contraindication, but pregnancy exposure requires immediate specialist action and severe anemia or hypoxia must be corrected and managed before continued dosing.

  • Do not rely on hormonal contraception alone because belzutifan can lower exposure to some CYP3A substrates, including contraceptive hormones.

Adverse Effects

  • Anemia, fatigue, headache, dizziness, nausea, dyspnea, increased creatinine, and musculoskeletal pain.

  • Severe anemia requiring transfusion, clinically significant or life-threatening hypoxia, embryo-fetal harm, and possible impaired fertility.

Interactions

  • UGT2B17 or CYP2C19 inhibitors can increase belzutifan exposure and worsen anemia or hypoxia.

  • Belzutifan can lower concentrations of sensitive CYP3A4 substrates; reduced efficacy is dangerous when the companion drug has a narrow therapeutic margin. Hormonal contraceptive effectiveness can fall.

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure hemoglobin and oxygen saturation before treatment and periodically. Ask about exertional dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, headache, fatigue, and new oxygen need.

  • Withhold, reduce, or discontinue according to anemia and hypoxia severity; life-threatening hypoxia requires permanent discontinuation under the label.

  • Verify pregnancy status and contraception, and coordinate disease-specific imaging and neurologic, renal, or endocrine assessment.

Key Labs

  • Hemoglobin and CBC

  • Resting and exertional oxygen saturation

  • Pregnancy test before treatment

  • Renal, hepatic, and tumor-response monitoring appropriate to the indication

Administration

  • Take once daily at the same time with or without food and swallow tablets whole.

  • Do not compensate for a missed or vomited dose with extra tablets; use the current label instructions.

Patient Education

  • Report shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, severe fatigue, rapid heartbeat, or bluish color urgently because anemia and hypoxia can worsen before the next visit.

  • Use effective non-hormonal contraception and do not breastfeed during treatment and for the label-defined period afterward.

Nclex Traps

  • Fatigue or dyspnea during belzutifan therapy requires hemoglobin and oxygen assessment because HIF-2 alpha inhibition can cause both anemia and hypoxia.

  • Belzutifan can reduce hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness; non-hormonal contraception is the safety anchor.

Routes

  • Oral tablet taken once daily; swallow whole and follow oncology instructions for missed doses and dose holds.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Severe anemia requiring transfusion, dose interruption, or other intervention.

  • Severe hypoxia, including oxygen desaturation that can require supplemental oxygen, hospitalization, or discontinuation.

  • Embryo-fetal toxicity.

Toxicity Management

  • There is no specific antidote. Hold treatment, assess oxygenation and hemoglobin urgently, provide oxygen or transfusion and supportive care when indicated, and contact oncology or poison control for excessive exposure.

Special Populations

  • Belzutifan can cause fetal harm and reduce hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness; use label-directed non-hormonal contraception and avoid breastfeeding during and after therapy for the stated interval.

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established. Older adults require close anemia, oxygenation, and interaction monitoring.

  • Follow current labeling in renal or hepatic impairment because evidence is limited at the most severe levels and exposure may change.
