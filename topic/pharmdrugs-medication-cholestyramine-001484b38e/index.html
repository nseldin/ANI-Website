Used To Treat

Adjunctive LDL-C lowering in primary hypercholesterolemia, relief of pruritus associated with partial biliary obstruction, and common off-label treatment of bile-acid diarrhea when bile acids drive colonic secretion.

Mechanism

About 95 percent of bile acids are normally reabsorbed in the terminal ileum. Cholestyramine's quaternary ammonium groups exchange chloride for bile acids and prevent their reabsorption. Reduced return lowers the hepatic bile-acid pool and FXR signaling, increasing cholesterol 7-alpha-hydroxylase and conversion of cholesterol to bile acids. Falling hepatocyte cholesterol activates SREBP2 and LDL-receptor expression, while HMG-CoA reductase increases compensatorily; statins therefore add mechanistic synergy. Because binding is physicochemical rather than receptor selective, many drugs and nutrients are also retained in the lumen. Reduced vitamin K absorption can increase bleeding, while reduced bile-acid stimulation of colonic chloride and water secretion explains benefit in bile-acid diarrhea.

Contraindications

  • Complete biliary obstruction and serious hypersensitivity are principal label contraindications. Severe triglyceride elevation, bowel obstruction, dysphagia, severe constipation, and prior triglyceride pancreatitis are major practical barriers.

  • Pruritus without bile flow into the intestine will not respond through this mechanism; complete obstruction requires diagnostic and procedural management.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check triglycerides, bowel and swallowing history, biliary diagnosis, all medicine times, anticoagulation, thyroid therapy, vitamins, pregnancy, and hydration. Mix each dose thoroughly and allow gradual titration to limit constipation.

  • Create a drug-specific spacing schedule. A common rule is other medicines at least one hour before or four to six hours after, but the exact product and drug may require a different interval.

Interactions

  • It can reduce absorption of warfarin, levothyroxine, digoxin, thiazides, propranolol, anticonvulsants, transplant drugs, oral contraceptives, ezetimibe, and fat-soluble vitamins. Separate and monitor the affected endpoint.

  • Statins provide complementary LDL lowering, while triglyceride-raising therapies or uncontrolled diabetes can make the VLDL response dangerous.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel with triglycerides, INR, TSH, digoxin or anticonvulsant levels when indicated, vitamin A/D/E/K and folate during prolonged high-risk use, and clinical bowel assessment.

  • A rising triglyceride value matters because severe elevation can shift the risk from long-term atherosclerosis to acute pancreatitis.

Nclex Traps

  • Cholestyramine lowers LDL but can raise triglycerides. Read the entire lipid panel.

  • Nonabsorbed does not mean no interactions; it can prevent another drug from ever reaching the bloodstream.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Never take cholestyramine powder dry. It can swell and cause choking, esophageal impaction, or GI obstruction. Severe constipation, abdominal distension, vomiting, dysphagia, absent stool or gas, rectal bleeding, or rising triglycerides requires prompt review.

Class Pathway

  • Intestinal bile-acid binding

  • Interrupted enterohepatic recycling

  • More hepatic cholesterol conversion to bile acids and LDL-receptor expression

Description

Cholestyramine, branded Questran or Prevalite, is a nonabsorbed positively charged resin powder that binds negatively charged bile acids in the intestine. The complex leaves in stool instead of returning to the liver. The liver converts more cholesterol into replacement bile acids and increases LDL receptors, lowering LDL-C. The same physical binding explains both useful nonlipid effects and most hazards: it can remove colonic bile acids that cause watery diarrhea, but it can also trap warfarin, levothyroxine, digoxin, thiazides, anticonvulsants, oral contraceptives, and vitamins A, D, E, and K. It often raises triglycerides by increasing hepatic VLDL production and therefore should not be used casually in severe hypertriglyceridemia. Powder must be mixed completely with fluid or soft food and never swallowed dry because it can swell, lodge, and obstruct.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, bloating, flatulence, nausea, dyspepsia, abdominal discomfort, fecal impaction, hemorrhoidal bleeding, and hypertriglyceridemia are common or important.

  • Long-term use can cause vitamin A, D, E, K or folate deficiency, hypoprothrombinemia and bleeding, metabolic acidosis in vulnerable patients, obstruction, and rare calcified material in the biliary or pancreatic system.
