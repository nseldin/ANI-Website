Definition

Buerger test is a bedside limb-perfusion maneuver. It is a clue to severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency. A positive finding means elevation causes pallor at a low angle and dependency causes delayed rubor.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Buerger test means elevation causes pallor at a low angle and dependency causes delayed rubor.

Why It Matters

A positive Buerger test response can signal severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency. Low arterial pressure cannot maintain flow against gravity; dependency then produces reactive hyperemia in chronically dilated arterioles. Buerger test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency

Assessment Method

Elevate the supine leg while observing color, then lower it slowly only if safe.

Positive Finding

Elevation causes pallor at a low angle and dependency causes delayed rubor.

Mechanism

Low arterial pressure cannot maintain flow against gravity; dependency then produces reactive hyperemia in chronically dilated arterioles.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Buerger test response raises concern for severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency because low arterial pressure cannot maintain flow against gravity; dependency then produces reactive hyperemia in chronically dilated arterioles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Buerger test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Buerger test to focus evaluation of severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: acrocyanosis, neuropathy, venous congestion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Elevate the supine leg while observing color, then lower it slowly only if safe. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Buerger test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Lighting, anemia, skin tone, and venous disease affect color. Therefore, Buerger test cannot confirm or exclude severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • acrocyanosis

  • neuropathy

  • venous congestion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from acrocyanosis, neuropathy, venous congestion, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Buerger test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; objective vascular testing is preferred. Buerger test remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe lower-extremity arterial insufficiency. Apply the reviewed limitation for Buerger test: Lighting, anemia, skin tone, and venous disease affect color.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Buerger test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Elevate the supine leg while observing color, then lower it slowly only if safe. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not elevate a critically ischemic painful limb for prolonged testing.
