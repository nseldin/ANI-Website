Definition

Bragard sign is a bedside nerve-tension maneuver used with the straight-leg raise. Gentle ankle dorsiflexion (moving the toes toward the shin) that reproduces the patient's familiar radiating leg pain supports lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, but it does not prove the cause.

Plain Language

After the leg is raised and the pain eases slightly, gently pulling the toes toward the shin makes the same shooting leg pain return.

Why It Matters

Bragard sign can help separate familiar nerve-related leg pain from a simple muscle stretch. It is only one part of the neurologic assessment: the history, strength, sensation, reflexes, straight-leg raise, and indicated imaging or electrodiagnostic testing remain important.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

With the patient supine and the knee extended, gently perform a straight-leg raise until familiar radiating pain appears, lower the leg slightly until the pain eases, then gently dorsiflex the ankle by moving the toes toward the shin without re-raising or forcing the leg.

Positive Finding

The same familiar pain radiating below the knee returns or intensifies with ankle dorsiflexion; isolated back, hamstring, or calf stretch is not a positive response.

Mechanism

Ankle dorsiflexion with the knee extended increases mechanical tension through the tibial and sciatic neural tissues and lumbosacral roots, so an irritated or sensitized nerve root may reproduce the patient's familiar radiating pain.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Bragard sign response raises concern for lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1, in a patient with radiating leg pain because ankle dorsiflexion with the knee extended increases mechanical tension through the tibial and sciatic neural tissues and lumbosacral roots, so an irritated or sensitized nerve root may reproduce the patient's familiar radiating pain. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bragard sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Reliable universal sensitivity and specificity are not established because studies use different techniques and selected populations; a negative result does not exclude radiculopathy and a positive result does not identify a specific structural cause. Therefore, Bragard sign cannot confirm or exclude lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1, in a patient with radiating leg pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hamstring tightness

  • calf muscle tightness

  • hip disease

  • peripheral neuropathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hamstring tightness, calf muscle tightness, hip disease, peripheral neuropathy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Bragard sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current limited adjunct to the straight-leg raise; it is not a standalone diagnostic test and published accuracy depends on technique and population. When performed as described, Bragard sign can adjust concern for lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1, in a patient with radiating leg pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Safety Precautions

Before assessing Bragard sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the patient supine and the knee extended, gently perform a straight-leg raise until familiar radiating pain appears, lower the leg slightly until the pain eases, then gently dorsiflex the ankle by moving the toes toward the shin without re-raising or forcing the leg. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Obtain consent, move slowly, stop when familiar symptoms reproduce, and never force or repeatedly provoke pain; new bladder or bowel dysfunction, saddle or perineal numbness, or progressive weakness requires emergency evaluation rather than more testing.
