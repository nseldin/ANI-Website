Definition

Griesinger sign is an observed and palpated postauricular sign. It is a clue to lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection. A positive finding means tender edema develops behind the mastoid.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Griesinger sign means tender edema develops behind the mastoid.

Why It Matters

The observed Griesinger sign finding can signal lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection. Thrombosis and inflammation extend through the mastoid emissary vein into superficial tissue. Griesinger sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate behind the mastoid over the emissary-vein region.

Positive Finding

Tender edema develops behind the mastoid.

Mechanism

Thrombosis and inflammation extend through the mastoid emissary vein into superficial tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Griesinger sign finding raises concern for lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection because thrombosis and inflammation extend through the mastoid emissary vein into superficial tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Griesinger sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Griesinger sign to focus evaluation of lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection and the reviewed alternatives: lymphadenitis, abscess, trauma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect and palpate behind the mastoid over the emissary-vein region. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Griesinger sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is uncommon and absence does not exclude venous thrombosis. Therefore, Griesinger sign cannot confirm or exclude lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as lymphadenitis, abscess, trauma.

False Negative Situations

Griesinger sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of lateral sinus thrombosis complicating ear infection.

Apply the reviewed limitation for Griesinger sign: It is uncommon and absence does not exclude venous thrombosis.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Fever, headache, neurologic change, or postauricular swelling needs urgent imaging and IV therapy.
