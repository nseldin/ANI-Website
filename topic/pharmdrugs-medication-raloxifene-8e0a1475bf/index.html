Contraindications

  • Active or past VTE, including DVT, PE or retinal-vein thrombosis

  • Pregnancy

  • Not recommended in premenopausal women; use caution in hepatic impairment and high stroke risk

Nursing Essentials

  • Stop at least 72 hours before and during prolonged immobilization according to labeling; encourage movement during travel when appropriate.

  • Teach unilateral leg swelling, chest pain, dyspnea, focal neurologic deficit and vision change as urgent warnings.

Interactions

  • Cholestyramine markedly reduces absorption.

  • Highly protein-bound drugs such as warfarin require monitoring when therapy changes.

  • Systemic estrogen combination is not routinely recommended.

Key Labs

  • Bone density and calcium/vitamin-D status

  • VTE/stroke/cardiovascular risk assessment

  • Liver function and INR response when clinically relevant

Nclex Traps

  • Raloxifene can worsen hot flashes.

  • It reduces breast-cancer risk but does not treat established cancer or recurrence.

  • Its best-established fracture benefit is vertebral, not universal proof of hip-fracture reduction.

Used To Treat

Treatment/prevention of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women and reduction of invasive-breast-cancer risk in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis or otherwise high risk. It does not treat existing breast cancer or menopausal vasomotor symptoms.

Mechanism

The raloxifene-estrogen-receptor complex recruits different coactivators and corepressors according to tissue. Bone transcription reduces osteoclast-coupling and turnover; breast transcription is antagonistic. Hepatic changes in coagulation proteins shift toward thrombosis. This tissue selectivity explains the useful bone/breast profile, hot flashes rather than relief, and the need to stop before immobilization.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Raloxifene increases venous thromboembolism risk and is contraindicated with active or prior VTE. A trial in postmenopausal women with coronary disease or high coronary risk found increased death from stroke; weigh stroke risk carefully.

Description

Raloxifene is a tissue-selective estrogen receptor modulator that activates estrogen signaling in bone to reduce turnover while antagonizing estrogen signaling in breast tissue. This improves vertebral-fracture outcomes and reduces risk of invasive estrogen-receptor-positive cancer in indicated groups. It does not stimulate the endometrium like estrogen, but hepatic estrogenic effects increase coagulation and create serious VTE and fatal-stroke risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, leg cramps, peripheral edema and influenza-like symptoms

  • Deep-vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and retinal-vein thrombosis

  • Fatal-stroke risk in susceptible populations

  • May worsen vasomotor symptoms
