Used To Treat

Chronic transfusional iron overload in eligible patients and selected non-transfusion-dependent thalassemia iron overload meeting liver-iron and ferritin criteria. Jadenu and Exjade formulations have different strengths and bioavailability and are not milligram-for-milligram interchangeable. The goal is controlled net iron removal without overchelation.

Mechanism

Ferric iron has six coordination sites. Two deferasirox molecules each donate three binding groups, forming a stable 2:1 complex with Fe3+ and lowering non-transferrin-bound labile iron that drives oxidative tissue injury. The complex is eliminated predominantly through feces after hepatobiliary excretion. Deferasirox is not a selective vacuum for only dangerous iron; when body burden falls, continued high exposure can overchelate and injure renal tubular cells, producing Fanconi syndrome, electrolyte wasting, and AKI. Hepatic processing and direct toxicity explain liver monitoring, while local/systemic GI injury contributes to ulceration, bleeding, and perforation. Formulation bioavailability differences explain why Exjade and Jadenu doses require conversion rather than direct substitution.

Contraindications

  • eGFR below 40 mL/min/1.73 m2, poor performance status, high-risk MDS, advanced malignancy, platelet count below 50 x 10^9/L, or serious hypersensitivity under current labeling

  • Avoid severe hepatic impairment and reduce for moderate hepatic or baseline renal impairment according to the label

  • Do not combine with other iron chelators without evidence and specialist direction

Nursing Essentials

  • Check creatinine/eGFR, urinalysis/tubular markers, liver tests, CBC, and ferritin before and during therapy. Intensify monitoring during dehydration, nephrotoxic exposure, or falling iron burden.

  • Know the formulation. Jadenu tablets/granules and Exjade suspension tablets differ in bioavailability and administration; a direct milligram swap can overdose.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs, corticosteroids, oral bisphosphonates, anticoagulants, nephrotoxins, and hepatotoxins increase GI or organ injury.

  • Avoid aluminum antacids; deferasirox can raise repaglinide, theophylline, busulfan, and selected transporter/CYP substrate exposure.

Key Labs

  • Serum ferritin monthly, liver iron concentration when indicated, creatinine/eGFR, urinalysis/protein and tubular electrolytes, AST/ALT/bilirubin, and CBC

  • Annual or symptom-driven auditory and ophthalmic assessment, hydration, GI bleeding, growth, and transfusion iron input

Nclex Traps

  • Deferasirox treats iron overload, not iron-deficiency anemia.

  • A low or rapidly falling ferritin can signal dangerous overchelation; more iron removal is not always better.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Deferasirox can cause serious and fatal acute kidney injury including dialysis and Fanconi syndrome, hepatic injury/failure, and gastrointestinal hemorrhage. Evaluate renal function in duplicate before starting, monitor kidney and liver tests closely, interrupt during volume depletion or acute injury, and stop for suspected GI ulceration or hemorrhage. Use the minimum dose needed to control iron burden.

Description

Deferasirox is an oral tridentate chelator: each molecule grips three of ferric iron's six coordination sites, so two molecules surround one Fe3+ ion and the complex is excreted mainly in bile and feces. This gradually lowers labile and stored iron accumulated from transfusion or thalassemia physiology. The same drug can cause fatal kidney failure, liver failure, and GI hemorrhage, especially during dehydration, advanced hematologic disease, low platelets, or overchelation. A falling ferritin is not an invitation to continue the same dose indefinitely; as iron burden approaches normal, toxicity rises because the chelator begins removing needed metal and injuring organs.

Adverse Effects

  • AKI, Fanconi syndrome, proteinuria, electrolyte wasting, hepatic failure, or overchelation

  • GI irritation, ulceration, hemorrhage, perforation, nausea, diarrhea, or abdominal pain

  • Cytopenia, severe skin reaction, rash, auditory or ocular toxicity
