Used To Treat

Product- and age-specific rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, plaque psoriasis, hidradenitis suppurativa, and noninfectious uveitis. It controls chronic immune-mediated inflammation and is not an acute analgesic or treatment for septic arthritis.

Mechanism

Adalimumab binds an epitope on soluble TNF-alpha trimers and transmembrane TNF, forming stable complexes. It blocks TNF from engaging and activating the TNFR1 and TNFR2 receptors; therefore adaptor-driven NF-kappaB and MAPK signaling, endothelial adhesion molecules, chemokines, metalloproteinases, acute-phase signals, and inflammatory-cell trafficking decrease. Binding transmembrane TNF can also produce reverse signaling and, through its IgG1 Fc, complement-dependent or antibody-dependent cellular effects in experimental settings. Because TNF receptor signaling is required for organized granulomas that contain mycobacteria and some fungi, blockade weakens that architecture, explaining disseminated or extrapulmonary presentations rather than merely increasing ordinary colds. Antidrug antibodies can increase clearance and reduce response, with immunogenicity influenced by disease, product, adherence, and concomitant therapy.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: serious infections and malignancy. Increased risk includes tuberculosis, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal infection such as histoplasmosis, and other opportunistic pathogens that may cause hospitalization or death. Test for latent TB before treatment and during risk-based follow-up, treat latent infection, do not start during an active infection, and discontinue for serious infection or sepsis. Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal, have been reported in children and adolescents receiving TNF blockers; hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma has occurred particularly in young patients with inflammatory bowel disease receiving combined immunosuppression.

Contraindications

  • Do not initiate during a serious active infection or after prior serious hypersensitivity; resolve TB, hepatitis B, vaccine, wound, and recurrent-infection plans first.

  • Use heightened caution with moderate/severe heart failure, demyelinating disease, chronic hepatitis B, malignancy, cytopenia, older age, endemic-fungal exposure, and combined immunosuppression.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before treatment document TB testing and exposure, hepatitis B serology, vaccines, infection and wound status, CBC/liver context, malignancy/skin history, heart failure, neurologic disease, and pregnancy plan.

  • Teach patients to report fever, night sweats, weight loss, persistent cough, dyspnea, painful rash or shingles, jaundice, new weakness/numbness, edema, or injection hypersensitivity before the next dose.

Interactions

  • Do not routinely combine with abatacept, anakinra, another TNF blocker, another biologic DMARD, or a JAK inhibitor because serious infection rises without established added benefit.

  • Avoid live vaccines during treatment. Other immunosuppressants and corticosteroids add infection and malignancy risk; biologic therapy can alter formation of antibodies to vaccines.

Key Labs

  • TB assessment, hepatitis B serology, CBC, liver tests, infection evaluation, and disease-specific activity measures

  • Skin/malignancy surveillance, heart-failure symptoms, neurologic examination, injection reaction, and therapeutic-drug/antibody testing only when clinically useful

Nclex Traps

  • A negative baseline TB test does not rule out later infection; monitor new exposure and symptoms throughout treatment.

  • TNF blockade can reduce fever and inflammation while a dangerous infection progresses, so improvement in arthritis does not validate every new symptom as a flare.

Description

Adalimumab is a fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds tumor necrosis factor alpha before TNF can activate its p55/TNFR1 and p75/TNFR2 receptors. TNF is a central amplifier of endothelial activation, leukocyte recruitment, fever, synovial destruction, granuloma maintenance, and several bowel, skin, eye, and joint inflammatory programs. Neutralization can improve disease and prevent structural damage over time, but the same loss of granuloma and macrophage coordination can reactivate tuberculosis or disseminate endemic fungi. Adalimumab also binds transmembrane TNF and has an intact Fc region, so complement- and Fc-receptor-mediated effects are possible. It is therefore more than a generic injection for arthritis and requires target-linked infection, neurologic, cardiac, hepatic, and malignancy surveillance.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reaction, headache, upper-respiratory symptoms, rash, infection, and antibody-mediated loss of response

  • TB/HBV reactivation, invasive fungal or opportunistic infection, lymphoma/malignancy, demyelination, heart-failure worsening, cytopenia, liver injury, lupus-like syndrome, and severe hypersensitivity
