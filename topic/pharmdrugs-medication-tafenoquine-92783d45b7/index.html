Class

Long-acting 8-aminoquinoline for P. vivax radical cure or product-specific malaria prophylaxis

Used To Treat

Krintafel provides single-dose radical cure of P. vivax malaria in eligible patients at least 16 years old who received appropriate chloroquine therapy; Arakoda is a distinct product for malaria prophylaxis in eligible adults. Products, indications, loading schedules, and tablet strengths are not interchangeable.

Boxed Warning

Hemolytic anemia can occur in G6PD deficiency; perform quantitative testing and do not use when activity is deficient or unknown. Tafenoquine is contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding when infant G6PD status is deficient or unknown. Krintafel is contraindicated in history of psychotic disorder or current psychotic symptoms; Arakoda carries psychiatric warnings and product-specific restrictions. Monitor for delayed hemolysis, methemoglobinemia, hypersensitivity, anxiety, depression, insomnia, hallucination, or psychosis because the long half-life prolongs adverse exposure.

Mechanism

Tafenoquine undergoes slow metabolism and partitions into tissues and red cells. Its exact dominant parasite target is not singular, but oxidative redox-active metabolites generate reactive oxygen species, disrupt mitochondrial electron transport, and damage membranes and macromolecules in liver-stage hypnozoites and other susceptible stages. Human red cells require G6PD-derived NADPH to restore reduced glutathione and neutralize peroxide. Low or intermediate activity permits progressive hemoglobin oxidation and membrane injury. Because tafenoquine has a terminal half-life of roughly two weeks, oxidant pressure persists after a single dose. This explains why quantitative rather than qualitative G6PD testing is required and why heterozygous females with intermediate activity may be at risk despite a normal point-of-care screen.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact brand/indication, quantitative G6PD result, age, pregnancy and breastfeeding status, psychiatric history, and accompanying blood-stage regimen.

  • Teach that symptoms can emerge after the dose because the drug remains in the body for weeks; delayed dark urine, jaundice, dyspnea, or behavioral change still requires urgent evaluation.

Contraindications

  • G6PD deficiency or unknown status, pregnancy, incompatible breastfeeding status, serious hypersensitivity, and product-specific psychotic-disorder restrictions.

  • Do not substitute Krintafel and Arakoda: radical cure and prophylaxis use different strengths, eligibility rules, and schedules.

Interactions

  • Tafenoquine inhibits renal transporters OCT2 and MATE in vitro and may raise exposure to susceptible substrates; review narrow-therapeutic-index drugs.

  • Other oxidant, marrow-toxic, or psychiatric-active drugs can compound risk.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative G6PD, baseline CBC/hemoglobin, pregnancy testing, psychiatric assessment, and species/indication confirmation

  • Follow-up CBC and hemolysis markers when symptomatic or high-risk, methemoglobin, renal function, and delayed psychiatric or hypersensitivity surveillance

Nclex Traps

  • Single-dose radical cure improves adherence but prolongs toxicity if the patient is ineligible.

  • Krintafel and Arakoda contain tafenoquine but are not interchangeable products.

Description

Tafenoquine is a long-acting 8-aminoquinoline antimalarial whose oxidative metabolites damage Plasmodium liver stages, including dormant Plasmodium vivax hypnozoites, enabling radical cure or product-specific prophylaxis. Its long half-life supports a single radical-cure dose or weekly prophylaxis after loading, but the same persistence removes the safety valve of stopping daily therapy: if hemolysis or psychiatric toxicity occurs, drug exposure continues for weeks. Quantitative G6PD activity is therefore mandatory, not merely a screening checkbox. Tafenoquine also has product-specific psychiatric restrictions and cannot be used during pregnancy or when a breastfed infant's G6PD status is unknown.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness, nausea, vomiting, headache, decreased hemoglobin, methemoglobinemia, and GI symptoms

  • Hemolytic anemia, hypersensitivity, keratopathy without proven functional loss, psychiatric effects, and delayed adverse reactions from long persistence
