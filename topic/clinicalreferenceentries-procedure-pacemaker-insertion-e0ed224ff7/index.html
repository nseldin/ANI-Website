Summary

Pacemaker insertion is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Implants a device that provides electrical pacing when the heart rate or conduction system cannot maintain adequate rhythm and perfusion.

Quick Answer

Pacemaker insertion: Implants a device that provides electrical pacing when the heart rate or conduction system cannot maintain adequate rhythm and perfusion. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Implants a device that provides electrical pacing when the heart rate or conduction system cannot maintain adequate rhythm and perfusion.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess rhythm, symptoms, electrolytes, digoxin/beta-blocker/calcium-channel blocker exposure, consent, allergies, anticoagulants, and baseline neurovascular status of the affected side.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Monitor rhythm capture, pacing spikes, heart rate, blood pressure, incision bleeding, infection, chest pain, hiccups/diaphragmatic pacing, pneumothorax symptoms, and arm restrictions per protocol.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Failure to capture, failure to sense, bradycardia with symptoms, syncope, chest pain, dyspnea, unilateral absent breath sounds, bleeding/hematoma, fever, or incision drainage.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Do not raise the affected arm above shoulder level early after insertion if restricted by protocol. Check rhythm and client perfusion, not just the pacemaker site.
