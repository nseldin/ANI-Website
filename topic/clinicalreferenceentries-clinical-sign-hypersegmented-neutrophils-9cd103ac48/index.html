Definition

Hypersegmented neutrophils is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to impaired DNA synthesis. A positive finding means neutrophils have excessive nuclear segmentation by laboratory criteria.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hypersegmented neutrophils means neutrophils have excessive nuclear segmentation by laboratory criteria.

Why It Matters

The Hypersegmented neutrophils laboratory finding can signal impaired DNA synthesis. Delayed nuclear maturation from defective DNA synthesis permits additional lobulation. Hypersegmented neutrophils cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • impaired DNA synthesis

Assessment Method

Count neutrophil nuclear lobes on a quality smear.

Positive Finding

Neutrophils have excessive nuclear segmentation by laboratory criteria.

Mechanism

Delayed nuclear maturation from defective DNA synthesis permits additional lobulation.

Clinical Interpretation

The Hypersegmented neutrophils laboratory finding raises concern for impaired DNA synthesis because delayed nuclear maturation from defective DNA synthesis permits additional lobulation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hypersegmented neutrophils does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hypersegmented neutrophils to focus evaluation of impaired DNA synthesis and the reviewed alternatives: uremia, iron deficiency, medication effect. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Count neutrophil nuclear lobes on a quality smear. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hypersegmented neutrophils as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Criteria and observer agreement vary; absence does not exclude deficiency. Therefore, Hypersegmented neutrophils cannot confirm or exclude impaired DNA synthesis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • uremia

  • iron deficiency

  • medication effect

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with uremia, iron deficiency, medication effect.

False Negative Situations

Hypersegmented neutrophils may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
