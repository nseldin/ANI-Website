Definition

Chevassu sign is a bimanual urologic palpation sign. It is a clue to upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy. A positive finding means bleeding from the ureteric orifice increases with renal palpation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chevassu sign means bleeding from the ureteric orifice increases with renal palpation.

Why It Matters

A positive Chevassu sign response can signal upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy. Pressure on a bleeding upper-tract lesion may expel blood down the ureter. Chevassu sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy

Assessment Method

During specialist examination under appropriate conditions, palpate the kidney while observing ureteric bleeding.

Positive Finding

Bleeding from the ureteric orifice increases with renal palpation.

Mechanism

Pressure on a bleeding upper-tract lesion may expel blood down the ureter.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Chevassu sign response raises concern for upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy because pressure on a bleeding upper-tract lesion may expel blood down the ureter. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chevassu sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chevassu sign to focus evaluation of upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy and the reviewed alternatives: infection, renal trauma, vascular lesion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During specialist examination under appropriate conditions, palpate the kidney while observing ureteric bleeding. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chevassu sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor specificity and deliberate provocation add risk. Therefore, Chevassu sign cannot confirm or exclude upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • infection

  • renal trauma

  • vascular lesion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from infection, renal trauma, vascular lesion, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Chevassu sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Obsolete and potentially harmful; modern imaging is preferred. Chevassu sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude upper urinary-tract tumor historically assessed during cystoscopy. Apply the reviewed limitation for Chevassu sign: Poor specificity and deliberate provocation add risk.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Chevassu sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: During specialist examination under appropriate conditions, palpate the kidney while observing ureteric bleeding. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not deliberately provoke hematuria; use imaging and ureteroscopy.
