Definition

Square-window sign is a neonatal wrist-flexibility maneuver. It is a clue to gestational maturity and flexor tone. A positive finding means a smaller palm-to-forearm angle reflects greater maturity.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Square-window sign means a smaller palm-to-forearm angle reflects greater maturity.

Why It Matters

A positive Square-window sign response can signal gestational maturity and flexor tone. Increasing fetal flexor tone and connective-tissue maturation permit greater passive wrist flexion. Square-window sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gestational maturity and flexor tone

Assessment Method

Gently flex the neonate's hand toward the forearm and observe the wrist angle.

Positive Finding

A smaller palm-to-forearm angle reflects greater maturity.

Mechanism

Increasing fetal flexor tone and connective-tissue maturation permit greater passive wrist flexion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Square-window sign response raises concern for gestational maturity and flexor tone because increasing fetal flexor tone and connective-tissue maturation permit greater passive wrist flexion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Square-window sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Square-window sign to focus evaluation of gestational maturity and flexor tone and the reviewed alternatives: arthrogryposis, prematurity, normal variation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently flex the neonate's hand toward the forearm and observe the wrist angle. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Square-window sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Edema, neuromuscular disease, and injury affect the angle. Therefore, Square-window sign cannot confirm or exclude gestational maturity and flexor tone by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • arthrogryposis

  • prematurity

  • normal variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from arthrogryposis, prematurity, normal variation, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Square-window sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current Ballard assessment component. Interpret Square-window sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Edema, neuromuscular disease, and injury affect the angle.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Square-window sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Gently flex the neonate's hand toward the forearm and observe the wrist angle. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use gentle pressure and do not force the wrist.

Communicate the finding with its suspected association, gestational maturity and flexor tone, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.
