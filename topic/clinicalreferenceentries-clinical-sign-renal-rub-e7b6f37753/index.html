Definition

Renal rub is an auscultatory sign. It is a clue to renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease. A positive finding means a localized friction sound varies with respiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Renal rub means a localized friction sound varies with respiration.

Why It Matters

A positive Renal rub response can signal renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease. Inflamed perirenal surfaces move against adjacent tissue during breathing. Renal rub cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease

Assessment Method

Auscultate over the flanks in a quiet setting.

Positive Finding

A localized friction sound varies with respiration.

Mechanism

Inflamed perirenal surfaces move against adjacent tissue during breathing.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Renal rub response raises concern for renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease because inflamed perirenal surfaces move against adjacent tissue during breathing. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Renal rub does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Renal rub to focus evaluation of renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease and the reviewed alternatives: pleural rub, bowel sound, clothing artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate over the flanks in a quiet setting. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Renal rub as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is extremely uncommon and transmitted pleural sounds are more likely. Therefore, Renal rub cannot confirm or exclude renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pleural rub

  • bowel sound

  • clothing artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from pleural rub, bowel sound, clothing artifact, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Renal rub may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical rare sign. Renal rub remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude renal infarction, inflammation, or perinephric disease. Apply the reviewed limitation for Renal rub: It is extremely uncommon and transmitted pleural sounds are more likely.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Renal rub, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate over the flanks in a quiet setting. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not delay imaging for a rare auscultatory finding.
