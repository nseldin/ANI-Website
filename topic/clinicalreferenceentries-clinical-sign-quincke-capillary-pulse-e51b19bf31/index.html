Definition

Quincke capillary pulse is an observed vascular sign. It is a clue to marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means alternating blanching and flushing occurs with each pulse.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Quincke capillary pulse means alternating blanching and flushing occurs with each pulse.

Why It Matters

The observed Quincke capillary pulse finding can signal marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation. Large systolic-diastolic pressure oscillations become visible in superficial capillaries. Quincke capillary pulse cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Apply light pressure to the distal nail bed or illuminate the lip while observing color.

Positive Finding

Alternating blanching and flushing occurs with each pulse.

Mechanism

Large systolic-diastolic pressure oscillations become visible in superficial capillaries.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Quincke capillary pulse finding raises concern for marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation because large systolic-diastolic pressure oscillations become visible in superficial capillaries. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Quincke capillary pulse does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Use Quincke capillary pulse to focus evaluation of marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: fever, thyrotoxicosis, normal variation. Evidence Limitations

Lighting, pressure, fever, and normal subtle pulsation reduce reliability. Therefore, Quincke capillary pulse cannot confirm or exclude marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • fever

  • normal variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as fever, thyrotoxicosis, normal variation.

False Negative Situations

Quincke capillary pulse may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of marked pulse-pressure widening, classically aortic regurgitation.
