Mechanism

Digoxin blocks sodium-potassium ATPase (Na/K-ATPase) in cardiac myocytes, raising intracellular sodium and reducing sodium-calcium exchange so calcium accumulates for stronger systolic contraction. Vagal effects slow AV nodal conduction, which can help atrial fibrillation rate control but also causes bradycardia or heart block when effect is excessive.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning commonly emphasized; narrow therapeutic index and toxicity are the major safety issues.

Contraindications

  • Ventricular fibrillation.

  • Use extreme caution with significant AV block, severe bradycardia, or hypokalemia.

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check apical pulse before giving per policy.

  • Monitor digoxin level, potassium, renal function.

  • Toxicity: GI upset, confusion, vision changes, dysrhythmias.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Hypokalemia from loop/thiazide diuretics increases toxicity risk.

  • Amiodarone, verapamil, macrolides can increase digoxin levels.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • digoxin level

  • potassium

  • magnesium

  • creatinine

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • Digoxin toxicity often starts with vague GI symptoms before dramatic rhythm changes.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Used To Treat

Heart failure symptom support and selected atrial fibrillation rate control when level, renal function, electrolytes, and pulse/rhythm are safe.

Description

Digoxin is a cardiac glycoside that inhibits the sodium-potassium ATPase pump, increasing intracellular calcium to improve contractility while increasing vagal tone to slow the AV node. The first-read safety frame is narrow therapeutic index: bradycardia, AV block, dysrhythmias, GI symptoms, confusion, yellow-green vision, renal impairment, and low potassium can turn benefit into toxicity.
