Definition

Raindrop pigmentation is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to chronic arsenic exposure. A positive finding means mottled hyperpigmented and hypopigmented macules create a raindrop pattern.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Raindrop pigmentation means mottled hyperpigmented and hypopigmented macules create a raindrop pattern.

Why It Matters

The observed Raindrop pigmentation finding can signal chronic arsenic exposure. Chronic arsenic alters melanocyte activity and keratinocyte turnover unevenly. Raindrop pigmentation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic arsenic exposure

Assessment Method

Inspect trunk and flexures for mixed pigment change.

Positive Finding

Mottled hyperpigmented and hypopigmented macules create a raindrop pattern.

Mechanism

Chronic arsenic alters melanocyte activity and keratinocyte turnover unevenly.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Raindrop pigmentation finding raises concern for chronic arsenic exposure because chronic arsenic alters melanocyte activity and keratinocyte turnover unevenly. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Raindrop pigmentation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Raindrop pigmentation to focus evaluation of chronic arsenic exposure and the reviewed alternatives: tinea versicolor, postinflammatory change, dyschromia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect trunk and flexures for mixed pigment change. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Raindrop pigmentation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sun damage and other pigment disorders mimic it. Therefore, Raindrop pigmentation cannot confirm or exclude chronic arsenic exposure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tinea versicolor

  • postinflammatory change

  • dyschromia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tinea versicolor, postinflammatory change, dyschromia.

False Negative Situations

Raindrop pigmentation may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic arsenic exposure.

Observe Raindrop pigmentation with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect trunk and flexures for mixed pigment change. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Assess neuropathy, keratoses, cardiovascular disease, and malignancy risk with exposure confirmation.
