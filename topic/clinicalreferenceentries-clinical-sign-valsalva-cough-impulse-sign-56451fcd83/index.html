Definition

Valsalva cough-impulse sign is a bedside groin-mass maneuver. It is a clue to communicating hernia. A positive finding means the mass expands or transmits an impulse.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Valsalva cough-impulse sign means the mass expands or transmits an impulse.

Why It Matters

A positive Valsalva cough-impulse sign response can signal communicating hernia. Raised intra-abdominal pressure pushes peritoneal contents through a patent defect. Valsalva cough-impulse sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • communicating hernia

Assessment Method

Observe and lightly palpate a groin swelling during cough or Valsalva.

Positive Finding

The mass expands or transmits an impulse.

Mechanism

Raised intra-abdominal pressure pushes peritoneal contents through a patent defect.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Valsalva cough-impulse sign response raises concern for communicating hernia because raised intra-abdominal pressure pushes peritoneal contents through a patent defect. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Valsalva cough-impulse sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Valsalva cough-impulse sign to focus evaluation of communicating hernia and the reviewed alternatives: lymph node, lipoma, aneurysm. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe and lightly palpate a groin swelling during cough or Valsalva. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Valsalva cough-impulse sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity, small defects, and inability to cough reduce accuracy. Therefore, Valsalva cough-impulse sign cannot confirm or exclude communicating hernia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lymph node

  • lipoma

  • aneurysm

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from lymph node, lipoma, aneurysm, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Valsalva cough-impulse sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; absence does not exclude hernia. When performed as described, Valsalva cough-impulse sign can adjust concern for communicating hernia, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Valsalva cough-impulse sign: Obesity, small defects, and inability to cough reduce accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Valsalva cough-impulse sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Observe and lightly palpate a groin swelling during cough or Valsalva. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not provoke with severe pain, incarceration, postoperative restriction, or cardiopulmonary instability.
