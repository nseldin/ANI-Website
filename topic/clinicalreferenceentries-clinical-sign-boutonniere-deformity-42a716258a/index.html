Definition

Boutonnière deformity is an observed joint-deformity sign. It is a clue to central extensor-slip disruption. A positive finding means the PIP joint flexes while the DIP joint hyperextends.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Boutonnière deformity means the PIP joint flexes while the DIP joint hyperextends.

Why It Matters

The observed Boutonnière deformity finding can signal central extensor-slip disruption. Central slip failure lets lateral bands migrate volarly at the PIP and increase distal extension force. Boutonnière deformity cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • central extensor-slip disruption

Evidence Limitations

Acute swelling can hide deformity and pseudo-boutonnière has different anatomy. Therefore, Boutonnière deformity cannot confirm or exclude central extensor-slip disruption by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as swan-neck deformity, PIP volar-plate injury, trigger finger.

False Negative Situations

Boutonnière deformity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of central extensor-slip disruption.
