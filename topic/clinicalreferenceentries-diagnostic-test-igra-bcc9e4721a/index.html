Summary

IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay) is a high-yield Respiratory diagnostic reference. Blood test screen for TB infection that measures immune response to TB antigens.

Quick Answer

IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay): Blood test screen for TB infection that measures immune response to TB antigens. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    No TB immune response is detected. TB infection is less likely, but active TB can still require evaluation when symptoms, recent exposure, or immunosuppression are present.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    The immune system responded to TB antigens, meaning TB infection is likely. It does not prove active disease.

  • Label

    Indeterminate / borderline

    Meaning

    The result cannot be interpreted cleanly and may require repeat testing or another TB test depending risk and symptoms.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Blood test screen for TB infection that measures immune response to TB antigens.

    • Before / preparation

    • Confirm the purpose of screening, age/risk eligibility, prep requirements, and whether abnormal results require diagnostic confirmation.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Teach that screening estimates risk; it does not always diagnose. Promote follow-up and reduce fear with clear next steps.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Positive screening with symptoms, high-risk history, severe abnormality, or failure to follow up on a positive result.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Do not treat a screen as a final diagnosis unless the test is designed and interpreted that way.
