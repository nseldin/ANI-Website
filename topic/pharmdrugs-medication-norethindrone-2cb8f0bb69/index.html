Class

First-generation 19-nortestosterone progestin used in progestin-only contraception and gynecologic therapy

Used To Treat

Daily 0.35-mg progestin-only oral contraception; higher-dose norethindrone acetate products treat secondary amenorrhea, endometriosis, and abnormal uterine bleeding due to hormonal imbalance. Combination products add estrogen and have a different risk profile.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Progestin-only norethindrone has no current U.S. boxed warning, but products and combinations must not be conflated. The 0.35-mg pill requires strict daily timing; more than three hours late requires taking the pill promptly and using backup according to current guidance. It does not protect against sexually transmitted infections. Pregnancy that occurs during use requires evaluation for ectopic pregnancy when pain or bleeding is present. Higher-dose norethindrone acetate is contraindicated in pregnancy and has thrombotic, hepatic, breast-cancer, and unexplained-bleeding precautions; estrogen-containing combinations add estrogen-specific thrombotic warnings.

Mechanism

Norethindrone binds intracellular progesterone receptors and changes gene transcription in cervical, endometrial, hypothalamic and pituitary tissue. Cervical glands produce thick, low-volume mucus that impedes sperm within hours, but the barrier wanes when a low-dose pill is late. Endometrium becomes thin and less receptive, and tubal motility changes. At sufficient exposure, slower GnRH pulses and reduced LH blunt ovulation, but traditional 0.35-mg POPs do not suppress every ovulation, which is why adherence timing is less forgiving than with a long-acting implant. Norethindrone acetate is hydrolyzed to norethindrone and at higher doses more strongly suppresses endometrial proliferation and ectopic endometrial tissue. It has some androgen-receptor activity and hepatic metabolism, creating acne, lipid, and interaction effects in susceptible patients.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify strength and purpose. A 0.35-mg contraceptive tablet and 5-mg norethindrone acetate tablet have different indications, dosing, missed-dose rules, and risks.

  • For the minipill, teach same-time daily use, the greater-than-three-hour late rule, backup interval, postpartum start plan, and pregnancy testing/ectopic warning symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected pregnancy for therapeutic products, current breast cancer or another progestin-sensitive malignancy, active liver disease or tumor, unexplained uterine bleeding, and product-specific thromboembolic disease.

  • Before treating abnormal bleeding, exclude pregnancy and structural or malignant causes rather than suppressing the symptom indefinitely.

Interactions

  • CYP3A inducers such as rifampin, selected antiseizure drugs and St. John's wort can reduce progestin exposure and contraceptive reliability.

  • Other hormones change bleeding and thrombotic risk; ulipristal emergency contraception requires delayed progestin restart under current guidance.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy assessment and evaluation of unexplained bleeding; liver and metabolic testing is symptom- and risk-directed rather than routine for every minipill user

  • Adherence time, bleeding pattern, medication inducers, mood, headache change, pregnancy symptoms, unilateral pelvic pain, VTE symptoms, and response of endometriosis or bleeding

Nclex Traps

  • Traditional norethindrone POPs rely strongly on cervical mucus and have a narrow timing window because low systemic exposure falls quickly.

  • Norethindrone and norethindrone acetate are related but not dose-equivalent products; do not transfer the minipill missed-dose rules to high-dose gynecologic therapy.

Display Name

Norethindrone

Description

Norethindrone is an oral synthetic progestin that binds progesterone receptors to thicken cervical mucus and suppress endometrium, while the traditional 0.35-mg minipill does not prevent every ovulation. The dose is low and exposure falls quickly, so taking it at nearly the same clock time every day matters. The three-hour late rule exists because the low dose does not maintain a long pharmacologic buffer: cervical-mucus protection can wane before the next tablet, so backup contraception is needed after a late dose. Therefore the timing rule is a consequence of short exposure and mucus-barrier dependence, not an arbitrary alarm-clock convention. Higher-dose norethindrone acetate provides stronger endometrial and gonadotropin suppression for endometriosis or bleeding disorders and is not interchangeable tablet-for-tablet with the contraceptive minipill. The drug's partial androgenic activity and conversion of a small fraction of acetate exposure to ethinyl estradiol help explain formulation-specific metabolic and thrombotic considerations.

Class Pathway

  • Progestin medication

  • Progesterone-receptor agonist

  • Cervical-mucus barrier with dose-dependent ovulation and endometrial suppression

Adverse Effects

  • Irregular bleeding, spotting or amenorrhea, headache, breast tenderness, nausea, acne, mood change, bloating, and weight change

  • Ectopic pregnancy if method failure occurs, thrombosis in susceptible or estrogen-combination settings, liver dysfunction, severe depression, migraine change, or delayed diagnosis of abnormal bleeding
