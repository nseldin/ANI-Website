Definition

Ferning sign is a microscopic fluid-crystallization test. It is a clue to amniotic fluid leakage or estrogenized cervical mucus depending context. A positive finding means branching fern-like crystals appear.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ferning sign means branching fern-like crystals appear.

Why It Matters

The Ferning sign laboratory finding can signal amniotic fluid leakage or estrogenized cervical mucus depending context. Sodium chloride and proteins crystallize in a branching pattern as the fluid dries. Ferning sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • amniotic fluid leakage or estrogenized cervical mucus depending context

Sodium chloride and proteins crystallize in a branching pattern as the fluid dries.

Evidence Limitations

Blood, semen, mucus, antiseptics, and technique cause false results. Therefore, Ferning sign cannot confirm or exclude amniotic fluid leakage or estrogenized cervical mucus depending context by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with urine, normal vaginal secretion, cervical mucus.

False Negative Situations

Ferning sign may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
