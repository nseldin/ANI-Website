Definition

Scarf sign is a neonatal tone maneuver. It is a clue to gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone. A positive finding means the elbow crosses farther toward or beyond midline when tone is lower.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Scarf sign means the elbow crosses farther toward or beyond midline when tone is lower.

Why It Matters

A positive Scarf sign response can signal gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone. Maturation increases passive flexor tone and limits shoulder excursion. Scarf sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone

Assessment Method

Draw the infant's arm gently across the chest like a scarf while stabilizing the shoulder.

Positive Finding

The elbow crosses farther toward or beyond midline when tone is lower.

Mechanism

Maturation increases passive flexor tone and limits shoulder excursion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Scarf sign response raises concern for gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone because maturation increases passive flexor tone and limits shoulder excursion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Scarf sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Scarf sign to focus evaluation of gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone and the reviewed alternatives: joint laxity, fracture, pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Draw the infant's arm gently across the chest like a scarf while stabilizing the shoulder. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Scarf sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Illness, sedation, and asymmetry reduce accuracy. Therefore, Scarf sign cannot confirm or exclude gestational maturity and shoulder-girdle tone by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • joint laxity

  • fracture

  • pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from joint laxity, fracture, pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Scarf sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Scarf sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Draw the infant's arm gently across the chest like a scarf while stabilizing the shoulder. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never pull against resistance or use with suspected clavicle or humerus injury.
