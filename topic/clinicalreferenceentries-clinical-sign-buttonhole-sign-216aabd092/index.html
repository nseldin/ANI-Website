Definition

Buttonhole sign is a palpation sign. It is a clue to cutaneous neurofibroma. A positive finding means the lesion invaginates through a defect in the dermis like a button through a hole.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Buttonhole sign means the lesion invaginates through a defect in the dermis like a button through a hole.

Why It Matters

A positive Buttonhole sign response can signal cutaneous neurofibroma. A soft dermal neurofibroma is poorly tethered and can be pushed into subcutaneous tissue. Buttonhole sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cutaneous neurofibroma

Assessment Method

Press gently on a soft papule or nodule.

Positive Finding

The lesion invaginates through a defect in the dermis like a button through a hole.

Mechanism

A soft dermal neurofibroma is poorly tethered and can be pushed into subcutaneous tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Buttonhole sign response raises concern for cutaneous neurofibroma because a soft dermal neurofibroma is poorly tethered and can be pushed into subcutaneous tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Buttonhole sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Buttonhole sign to focus evaluation of cutaneous neurofibroma and the reviewed alternatives: intradermal nevus, lipoma, acrochordon. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Press gently on a soft papule or nodule. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Buttonhole sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Other soft lesions may indent and deep plexiform lesions behave differently. Therefore, Buttonhole sign cannot confirm or exclude cutaneous neurofibroma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • intradermal nevus

  • lipoma

  • acrochordon

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from intradermal nevus, lipoma, acrochordon, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Buttonhole sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current characteristic clue. Interpret Buttonhole sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Other soft lesions may indent and deep plexiform lesions behave differently.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Buttonhole sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Press gently on a soft papule or nodule. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not press painful or rapidly changing lesions; assess NF1 criteria systematically.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Buttonhole sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The lesion invaginates through a defect in the dermis like a button through a hole. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Press gently on a soft papule or nodule. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, cutaneous neurofibroma, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
