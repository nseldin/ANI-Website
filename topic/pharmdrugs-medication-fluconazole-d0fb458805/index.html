Used To Treat

Susceptible oropharyngeal, esophageal, vaginal, urinary, peritoneal, bloodstream, and deep Candida infections; cryptococcal meningitis consolidation/maintenance; selected coccidioidal and other fungal indications. It does not reliably treat Aspergillus or Mucorales.

Mechanism

Fluconazole's triazole nitrogens coordinate the heme iron of fungal CYP51, blocking conversion of lanosterol to ergosterol. Mammalian sterol demethylation is less sensitive, but fluconazole still inhibits human CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and to a lesser degree CYP3A4, which explains interactions with warfarin, sulfonylureas, phenytoin, tacrolimus, and other substrates. Concentration rises when GFR falls because renal elimination slows. The same predictable absorption and CSF/urine distribution that make routine TDM uncommon also explain why a susceptible organism and correct renal dose are more useful than simply calling it a broad antifungal.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hepatic injury, anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reactions, QT prolongation/torsades, embryo-fetal risk with selected high-dose or prolonged exposure, and clinically important CYP interactions can occur.

Contraindications

  • Serious azole hypersensitivity and selected QT/CYP-dependent co-medications are major barriers under current labeling.

  • Do not use empirically when resistant Candida, prior azole exposure, invasive mold, or a nonfungal diagnosis is plausible without obtaining diagnostic evidence.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain cultures/speciation and susceptibility for bloodstream or other significant isolates. Verify renal dose, liver history, pregnancy context, potassium/magnesium, QT risks, and complete medication list.

  • Teach urgent reporting of jaundice, dark urine, blistering rash, syncope/palpitations, severe hypoglycemia, or bleeding. For candidemia, do not step down until stability, negative cultures, and susceptibility criteria are met.

Interactions

  • Raises warfarin effect/INR, sulfonylurea hypoglycemia, phenytoin, cyclosporine/tacrolimus, and selected benzodiazepine, opioid, statin, or antiarrhythmic exposure through CYP inhibition.

  • QT-active drugs and low potassium/magnesium compound torsades risk. Strong inducers can reduce fluconazole exposure.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR for dose, AST/ALT/bilirubin, potassium/magnesium and ECG when risk exists, species/susceptibility, cultures for clearance, and INR/glucose or immunosuppressant levels for interacting therapy.

  • Routine fluconazole serum levels are uncommon because exposure is relatively predictable, but unusual absorption, resistance, or CNS disease may prompt specialist testing.

Nclex Traps

  • Fluconazole treats susceptible yeasts and reaches urine/CSF; it is not an anti-Aspergillus drug.

  • One oral dose is a regimen for selected uncomplicated vaginal candidiasis, not a universal dose for thrush, esophagitis, candidemia, or meningitis.

Class Pathway

  • CYP51/ERG11 inhibition

  • Ergosterol depletion and 14-methyl-sterol accumulation

  • Fungistatic activity against susceptible yeasts with high urine and CSF penetration

Description

Fluconazole is a water-soluble systemic triazole that inhibits fungal CYP51/lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, depleting ergosterol and accumulating abnormal sterols so susceptible fungi cannot maintain normal membrane growth. It is highly orally bioavailable, penetrates cerebrospinal fluid, and is excreted largely unchanged in urine, making it especially useful when the organism is susceptible and those compartments matter. It is usually fungistatic against Candida, so source control and immunity remain important. Candida krusei is intrinsically resistant, Candida glabrata often has reduced susceptibility, and fluconazole has no dependable Aspergillus or Mucorales activity. Renal function determines maintenance dosing because the kidney clears the drug.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, headache, rash, and aminotransferase elevation are common or important.

  • Rare severe hepatotoxicity, anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN/DRESS, agranulocytosis, seizures, QT prolongation/torsades, and interaction-mediated bleeding or hypoglycemia.
