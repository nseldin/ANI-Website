  • Content

    A biofilm is an organized microbial community attached to a surface and embedded in a self-produced matrix.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    The matrix promotes adherence, limits penetration of some host factors and antimicrobials, supports metabolic diversity, and facilitates communication and gene exchange. Slow-growing or protected cells may persist even when free-floating organisms are more susceptible.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Biofilms are important on teeth, chronic wounds, airway mucus, catheters, prosthetic joints, heart valves, and other implanted material.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Biofilm-associated infection can relapse unless the source is controlled. Management may require drainage, debridement, or device evaluation in addition to an active antimicrobial chosen from organism and susceptibility data.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Not every persistent infection is a biofilm infection, and an in-vitro biofilm observation does not automatically determine bedside treatment.
