Used To Treat

Product- and age-specific prevention and chronic treatment of asthma, prevention of exercise-induced bronchoconstriction, and relief of seasonal or perennial allergic rhinitis when alternatives are inadequate or not tolerated. It is preventive and does not reverse acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to montelukast or product components

  • Do not use as rescue treatment, and avoid casual use for mild allergic rhinitis when effective alternatives are available

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask directly about mood, sleep, dreams, behavior, attention, hallucinations, self-harm, and suicidal thinking at baseline and follow-up; generic 'any side effects?' is not enough.

  • Teach patients to keep their rapid reliever and ICS-containing plan. Do not substitute an extra montelukast dose for acute wheeze.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP inducers such as phenobarbital or rifampin can lower exposure; gemfibrozil can increase exposure through CYP2C8 inhibition.

  • Do not abruptly taper systemic or inhaled corticosteroids when starting montelukast; steroid reduction can unmask adrenal or eosinophilic disease.

Key Labs

  • Asthma symptoms, nighttime waking, exercise response, rescue use, exacerbations, mood, behavior, sleep, and adherence

  • No routine serum level; assess eosinophils, liver tests, or vasculitic findings only when the clinical picture indicates

Nclex Traps

  • Montelukast blocks CysLT1 receptors; zileuton inhibits leukotriene synthesis through 5-lipoxygenase.

  • It is not a rescue bronchodilator, and a chewable tablet does not make neuropsychiatric risk pediatric-only.

Mechanism

Arachidonic acid released from cell membranes is converted by 5-lipoxygenase and FLAP to leukotriene A4, then to LTC4, LTD4, and LTE4. LTD4 activates Gq-coupled CysLT1 receptors on airway smooth muscle and inflammatory cells, increasing phospholipase C, IP3, intracellular calcium, bronchoconstriction, edema, mucus, and eosinophil trafficking. Montelukast binds CysLT1 with high affinity and prevents cysteinyl-leukotriene signaling without blocking histamine H1 receptors, cyclooxygenase, or 5-lipoxygenase itself. It therefore modifies one inflammatory mediator pathway rather than broadly suppressing inflammation. In aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease, excess cysteinyl-leukotriene signaling helps explain benefit, but montelukast does not make aspirin exposure safe.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Serious neuropsychiatric events have been reported, including agitation, aggression, depression, abnormal dreams, insomnia, hallucinations, attention or memory change, and suicidal thoughts and behavior including suicide. Discuss benefit and risk before treatment, monitor patients and caregivers for new symptoms, stop montelukast and contact a clinician immediately if they occur, and reserve allergic-rhinitis use for inadequate response or intolerance to alternatives. The mechanism of these events is not established and prior psychiatric illness is not required.

Description

Montelukast is an oral leukotriene-receptor antagonist that blocks the CysLT1 receptor used mainly by leukotriene D4. Cysteinyl leukotrienes constrict airway smooth muscle, increase vascular permeability and edema, stimulate mucus, and recruit eosinophilic inflammation. Blocking that signal can reduce exercise-, allergen-, or aspirin-associated bronchoconstriction, but it does not relax a severely constricted airway within minutes and is generally less effective than inhaled corticosteroids for preventing asthma exacerbations. The defining safety issue is its boxed warning for serious neuropsychiatric events, including suicidal thoughts or behavior; allergic-rhinitis use should therefore be reserved for inadequate response or intolerance to safer alternatives.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhea, fever, upper-respiratory symptoms, cough, or otitis in pediatric studies

  • Agitation, aggression, anxiety, abnormal dreams, insomnia, depression, hallucinations, suicidal thinking or behavior, hypersensitivity, or rare eosinophilic conditions
