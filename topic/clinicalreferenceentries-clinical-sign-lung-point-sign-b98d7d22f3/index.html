Definition

Lung-point sign is an ultrasound imaging sign. It is a clue to pneumothorax boundary. A positive finding means a point alternates between normal sliding and absent-sliding patterns with respiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lung-point sign means a point alternates between normal sliding and absent-sliding patterns with respiration.

Why It Matters

The Lung-point sign imaging pattern can signal pneumothorax boundary. The visceral pleura intermittently contacts the chest wall at the edge of pleural air. Lung-point sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pneumothorax boundary

Assessment Method

Scan the pleura systematically with point-of-care ultrasound.

Positive Finding

A point alternates between normal sliding and absent-sliding patterns with respiration.

Mechanism

The visceral pleura intermittently contacts the chest wall at the edge of pleural air.

Clinical Interpretation

The Lung-point sign imaging pattern raises concern for pneumothorax boundary because the visceral pleura intermittently contacts the chest wall at the edge of pleural air. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lung-point sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lung-point sign to focus evaluation of pneumothorax boundary and the reviewed alternatives: bleb, prior pleurodesis, subcutaneous emphysema. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Scan the pleura systematically with point-of-care ultrasound. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lung-point sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It may be absent in very large pneumothorax and location does not reliably quantify volume. Therefore, Lung-point sign cannot confirm or exclude pneumothorax boundary by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • bleb

  • subcutaneous emphysema

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with bleb, prior pleurodesis, subcutaneous emphysema.

False Negative Situations

Lung-point sign may be missed when the associated process is early or subtle, the relevant region is not included, or acquisition timing and resolution are inadequate.
