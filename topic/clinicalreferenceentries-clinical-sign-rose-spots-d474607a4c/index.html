Definition

Rose spots is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to enteric fever. A positive finding means faint blanching salmon-pink macules or papules appear in crops.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rose spots means faint blanching salmon-pink macules or papules appear in crops.

Why It Matters

The observed Rose spots finding can signal enteric fever. Bacterial embolic or immune involvement of superficial dermal vessels produces transient lesions. Rose spots cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • enteric fever

Assessment Method

Inspect trunk skin in good light during compatible systemic illness.

Positive Finding

Faint blanching salmon-pink macules or papules appear in crops.

Mechanism

Bacterial embolic or immune involvement of superficial dermal vessels produces transient lesions.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Rose spots finding raises concern for enteric fever because bacterial embolic or immune involvement of superficial dermal vessels produces transient lesions. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Rose spots does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

They are absent in most cases and subtle on darker skin. Therefore, Rose spots cannot confirm or exclude enteric fever by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • secondary syphilis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as secondary syphilis, insect bites, urticaria.

False Negative Situations

Rose spots may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of enteric fever.
