Definition

Aphthous ulcers are painful round or oval sores inside the mouth with a shallow yellow-white or gray center and a red border. They are commonly called canker sores and are not contagious.

Plain Language

Aphthous ulcers are painful canker sores that form only inside the mouth.

Why It Matters

A typical small ulcer heals on its own, but frequent, large, persistent, or unusually severe ulcers can impair hydration and nutrition or point to deficiency, medication injury, immune disease, inflammatory bowel disease, celiac disease, or another diagnosis.

Assessment Method

Inspect all internal oral surfaces in good light and document number, size, depth, location, red halo, surface covering, pain, preceding burning, recurrence, healing time, scarring, hydration and intake, recent medications, immune status, fever, skin or eye inflammation, genital ulcers, and gastrointestinal symptoms.

Positive Finding

One or more sharply outlined painful round or oval ulcers have a shallow yellow-white or gray fibrinous center with an erythematous halo and occur inside the mouth without an earlier vesicle.

Mechanism

The precise cause is unsettled; local epithelial injury and T-cell-mediated inflammation damage oral mucosa in a susceptible person, while trauma, stress, deficiency, or systemic inflammation can trigger or mimic the pattern.

Evidence Limitations

Major and herpetiform variants differ in size, number, duration, and scarring; herpes lesions usually begin as vesicles, and persistent traumatic, medication-related, immune, infectious, and malignant ulcers may resemble aphthae. Therefore, Aphthous ulcers cannot confirm or exclude recurrent aphthous stomatitis or an aphthous-type lesion associated with local trauma, hereditary susceptibility, nutritional deficiency, immune disease, inflammatory bowel disease, celiac disease, medication, or systemic illness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as herpes simplex ulcer, traumatic ulcer, oral cancer, Behcet-type ulcer, hand-foot-and-mouth disease.

False Negative Situations
