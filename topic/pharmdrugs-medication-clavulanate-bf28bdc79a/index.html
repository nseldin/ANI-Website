Class

Beta-lactam-structured suicide inhibitor of selected class A beta-lactamases

Used To Treat

Used with amoxicillin to restore activity against selected beta-lactamase-producing respiratory, skin, bite, urinary, and mixed aerobic/anaerobic pathogens when the combination is appropriate.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Amoxicillin/clavulanate is strongly associated with idiosyncratic cholestatic or mixed liver injury, often appearing after therapy ends. Prior combination-associated hepatic dysfunction or jaundice is a contraindication to re-exposure.

Mechanism

Clavulanate acts as a mechanism-based or suicide substrate. A beta-lactamase attacks its ring as though it were an antibiotic, but rearranged acyl-enzyme products persist and disable the enzyme. The partner amoxicillin can then acylate PBPs and stop peptidoglycan cross-linking. This explains why clavulanate adds no atypical coverage and cannot rescue amoxicillin when resistance comes from altered PBPs, porin loss, efflux, overwhelming enzyme expression, or an inhibitor-insensitive beta-lactamase. Starting the combination with a meal improves amoxicillin/clavulanate absorption and reduces GI intolerance, while different formulations are not automatically interchangeable because their component ratios differ.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give the combination at the start of a meal because food improves tolerance and the labeled absorption relationship, then teach delayed jaundice and pruritus reporting because hepatic injury can begin after the last dose.

  • Review prior amoxicillin-clavulanate liver injury and current liver symptoms before administration because recurrence can be more severe.

Contraindications

  • Do not use clavulanate as a standalone antibiotic because it does not provide clinically adequate PBP-mediated killing by itself.

  • Do not assume every Augmentin tablet or suspension has an equivalent amoxicillin:clavulanate ratio; verify the exact product before substitution.

Interactions

  • Warfarin effect/INR can rise during antibiotic illness or microbiome change, so monitor when clinically relevant.

  • Allopurinol can increase amoxicillin-associated rash; this does not mean clavulanate treats or causes hyperuricemia.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, liver tests when symptoms or prolonged therapy warrant them, renal function for the complete product, and INR in susceptible anticoagulated patients.

  • There is no routine serum clavulanate level; failure should prompt organism, enzyme, site, adherence, formulation, and source-control review.

Nclex Traps

  • Clavulanate protects amoxicillin; it is not the component that directly binds PBPs for the main antibacterial effect.

  • Clavulanate does not cover metallo-beta-lactamases or make amoxicillin effective against MRSA or atypical organisms.

Class Pathway

  • Enter bacterial periplasm

  • Covalently trap susceptible serine beta-lactamases

  • Protect amoxicillin from hydrolysis

Description

Clavulanate is a beta-lactamase inhibitor with little useful antibacterial activity by itself. Its beta-lactam-like ring enters the active site of susceptible serine beta-lactamases and is hydrolyzed into reactive intermediates that covalently inactivate the enzyme. This protects amoxicillin long enough to reach bacterial PBPs. The shield is selective: clavulanate inhibits many class A penicillinases and ESBLs in vitro but is unreliable against AmpC, KPC and other carbapenemases, OXA enzymes, and metallo-beta-lactamases. The fixed amoxicillin-to-clavulanate ratio matters because more clavulanate increases GI and hepatic exposure without proportionally improving every infection.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, candidiasis, rash, and CDI can occur; GI intolerance rises with unnecessary clavulanate exposure.

  • Cholestatic or mixed hepatitis can cause pruritus, jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, and prolonged alkaline-phosphatase elevation after the course.
