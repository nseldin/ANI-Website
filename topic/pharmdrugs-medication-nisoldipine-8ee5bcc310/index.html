Class

Extended-release dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker for hypertension

Used To Treat

Hypertension as a once-daily extended-release maintenance medication. It is not an acute rescue treatment and does not provide AV-node rate control.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Avoid grapefruit products before and after dosing and generally avoid known CYP3A inhibitors or inducers because exposure can change dramatically. Take on an empty stomach and swallow the extended-release tablet whole; do not bite, divide, chew, or crush. Peripheral edema, headache, dizziness, and hypotension increase with dose. Use a low starting dose in older adults or hepatic impairment because exposure is substantially higher, and assess heart failure or compromised ventricular function carefully because safety is not established in that population.

Mechanism

Nisoldipine inhibits Cav1.2 channels in arterial smooth muscle, reducing calcium-calmodulin activation of myosin light-chain kinase and relaxing arterioles. Lower systemic vascular resistance reduces pressure, while transient baroreflex sympathetic activation can increase heart rate. Precapillary dilation exceeds venous dilation, which raises capillary hydrostatic pressure and explains dependent edema without requiring generalized volume overload. Extensive intestinal and hepatic CYP3A metabolism creates low, variable bioavailability; grapefruit inhibits intestinal metabolism and a high-fat meal can raise peak concentration, while enzyme inducers reduce exposure. Hepatic cirrhosis therefore has a much larger effect than mild-to-moderate renal impairment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact tablet strength because marketed strengths/formulations differ, empty-stomach timing, grapefruit avoidance, ability to swallow whole, pressure/orthostasis, pulse, edema, liver disease, age, and all CYP3A drugs.

  • Explain that the food and grapefruit instructions prevent unpredictable peak exposure, not merely stomach upset. Review edema timing and dose relationship while screening for pulmonary congestion, renal/hepatic disease, and unilateral red flags.

Contraindications

  • Known serious hypersensitivity; product labeling emphasizes avoidance rather than casual coadministration with CYP3A inhibitors/inducers and grapefruit.

  • Do not crush/split the extended-release tablet or take it with a high-fat meal. Severe hepatic impairment, advanced age, unstable heart failure, or marked hypotension requires lower exposure and close monitoring.

Interactions

  • Grapefruit produced about a threefold mean Cmax rise and almost twofold AUC rise, with much larger individual changes. CYP3A inhibitors raise exposure and inducers lower it; alternative therapy may be safer than trying to guess the dose effect.

  • Other antihypertensives, nitrates, PDE5 inhibitors, alcohol, and volume depletion add hypotension. A high-fat meal can raise peak concentration and should be avoided around dosing.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, pulse, orthostatic symptoms, edema/weight/lung findings, and liver function in hepatic disease. Renal function still informs overall antihypertensive safety even though mild-to-moderate renal impairment has less pharmacokinetic effect.

  • No routine nisoldipine level is used; assess electrolytes, creatinine, liver/cardiac studies, or venous testing according to symptoms and differential diagnosis.

Nclex Traps

  • Nisoldipine is the extended-release DHP with strict empty-stomach, whole-tablet, and grapefruit precautions; it is not interchangeable with amlodipine instructions.

  • Edema and headache are prominent dose-related vasodilator effects, while the drug does not provide AV-node rate control.

Description

Nisoldipine is an oral extended-release dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that lowers blood pressure by relaxing arterial resistance vessels. Its distinguishing practical issue is unusually strong sensitivity to administration conditions and CYP3A metabolism. The tablet should be swallowed whole on an empty stomach, generally one hour before or two hours after a meal, and grapefruit products should be avoided because grapefruit has produced roughly threefold mean peak concentrations with some subjects reaching about sevenfold. Older adults have about two- to threefold higher exposure, and cirrhosis can raise concentrations four- to fivefold. Peripheral edema and headache are common, and edema rises with dose because precapillary arteriolar dilation increases dependent capillary hydrostatic pressure.

Adverse Effects

  • Peripheral edema, headache, dizziness, flushing/vasodilation, palpitations, chest discomfort, nausea, rash, and reflex tachycardia.

  • Severe hypotension, syncope, worsening angina or heart failure, hepatic accumulation, angioedema or severe hypersensitivity, and prolonged shock in overdose.
