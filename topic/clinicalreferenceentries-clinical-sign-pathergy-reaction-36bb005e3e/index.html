Definition

Pathergy reaction is a bedside or historical trauma-response sign. It is a clue to neutrophilic inflammatory disease. A positive finding means a papule or pustule develops at the minor-injury site.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pathergy reaction means a papule or pustule develops at the minor-injury site.

Why It Matters

The observed Pathergy reaction finding can signal neutrophilic inflammatory disease. Exaggerated innate immune recruitment converts trivial trauma into sterile neutrophilic inflammation. Pathergy reaction cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • neutrophilic inflammatory disease

Positive Finding

A papule or pustule develops at the minor-injury site.

Mechanism

Exaggerated innate immune recruitment converts trivial trauma into sterile neutrophilic inflammation.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pathergy reaction finding raises concern for neutrophilic inflammatory disease because exaggerated innate immune recruitment converts trivial trauma into sterile neutrophilic inflammation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pathergy reaction does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pathergy reaction to focus evaluation of neutrophilic inflammatory disease and the reviewed alternatives: bacterial folliculitis, contact reaction, traumatic papule. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe a standardized sterile needle site after the protocol interval rather than creating unnecessary trauma. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pathergy reaction as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Performance varies greatly by population, needle, and reading method. Therefore, Pathergy reaction cannot confirm or exclude neutrophilic inflammatory disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • bacterial folliculitis

  • contact reaction

  • traumatic papule

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as bacterial folliculitis, contact reaction, traumatic papule.

False Negative Situations

Pathergy reaction may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of neutrophilic inflammatory disease.

Current adjunct in selected Behçet criteria; geographic sensitivity varies. When performed as described, Pathergy reaction can adjust concern for neutrophilic inflammatory disease, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis.
