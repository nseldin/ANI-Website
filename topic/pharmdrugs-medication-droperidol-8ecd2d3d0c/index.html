Used To Treat

Reduction of nausea and vomiting associated with diagnostic and surgical procedures; because of the boxed proarrhythmic risk, reserve labeled use for patients with inadequate response or intolerable effects from other appropriate treatments.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - QT prolongation, torsades de pointes and death. Reserve INAPSINE for patients who fail other adequate treatments. Obtain a 12-lead ECG before dosing; do not administer for QTc above the label thresholds or known/suspected long-QT syndrome. Correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia, review bradycardia, heart failure and QT drugs, and continue ECG monitoring for 2 to 3 hours after treatment when benefit is judged to outweigh risk. Start low and titrate cautiously.

Mechanism

Droperidol blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the chemoreceptor trigger zone and mesolimbic/basal-ganglia pathways, reducing nausea but also creating extrapyramidal effects and rare neuroleptic malignant syndrome. Peripheral alpha-1 blockade reduces vascular resistance and can cause hypotension. Cardiac potassium-current inhibition delays repolarization, prolonging QT and enabling early afterdepolarizations and torsades in susceptible myocardium.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before dosing, verify indication and alternative failure, ECG/QTc, potassium, magnesium, heart rate, cardiac disease, age, alcohol use and all QT-prolonging or sedating drugs.

  • Monitor rhythm, blood pressure, oxygenation, sedation, akathisia and dystonia. Have fluids/pressor support available for hypotension and treat torsades with emergency magnesium/defibrillation protocol rather than more sedative.

Key Labs

  • 12-lead ECG and QTc before treatment, continuous/serial ECG for 2 to 3 hours when used, potassium, magnesium, renal/hepatic context, blood pressure and neurologic/extrapyramidal assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • A low antiemetic dose does not eliminate the labeled torsades risk; fatal cases occurred at or below recommended doses.

  • Restlessness after droperidol may be akathisia rather than anxiety or inadequate sedation.

Description

Droperidol is a potent parenteral drug that blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the area postrema/chemoreceptor trigger zone, suppressing emetic signaling and reducing procedural nausea and vomiting. D2 blockade in other central pathways and peripheral alpha-adrenergic effects contribute tranquilization, sedation, akathisia and hypotension. Its defining safety issue is dose-related QT prolongation and torsades, including fatal cases at or below recommended doses.
