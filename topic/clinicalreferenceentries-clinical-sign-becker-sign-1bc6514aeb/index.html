Definition

Becker sign is an observed retinal vascular sign. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means retinal arterial pulsation is unusually prominent.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Becker sign means retinal arterial pulsation is unusually prominent.

Why It Matters

The observed Becker sign finding can signal severe aortic regurgitation. Wide pulse-pressure transmission produces visible retinal arterial caliber change. Becker sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Examine retinal arteries with ophthalmoscopy by a trained clinician.

Positive Finding

Retinal arterial pulsation is unusually prominent.

Mechanism

Wide pulse-pressure transmission produces visible retinal arterial caliber change.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Becker sign finding raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation because wide pulse-pressure transmission produces visible retinal arterial caliber change. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Becker sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Becker sign to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: glaucoma, hyperdynamic state, ophthalmoscopic artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine retinal arteries with ophthalmoscopy by a trained clinician. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Becker sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Subtle motion is observer dependent and not specific. Therefore, Becker sign cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • glaucoma

  • hyperdynamic state

  • ophthalmoscopic artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as glaucoma, hyperdynamic state, ophthalmoscopic artifact.

False Negative Situations

Becker sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe aortic regurgitation.

Modern Status

Historical specialist adjunct. Becker sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Becker sign: Subtle motion is observer dependent and not specific.

Safety Precautions

Observe Becker sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Examine retinal arteries with ophthalmoscopy by a trained clinician. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not confuse spontaneous venous pulsation with arterial pulsation.
