Used To Treat

Major depression and drug-specific anxiety, obsessive-compulsive, trauma-related, eating, panic, premenstrual, or pediatric disorders.

Description

SSRIs all inhibit the serotonin transporter, but they differ in half-life, CYP inhibition, indications, activation, GI burden and withdrawal. Fluoxetine and norfluoxetine persist for weeks, reducing abrupt-withdrawal symptoms but prolonging interactions and requiring a 5-week washout before an MAOI. Sertraline has broad anxiety/OCD/PTSD/PMDD indications and often causes early diarrhea. Escitalopram is highly SERT-selective with fewer CYP interactions, although QT, hyponatremia, bleeding, sexual dysfunction and serotonin toxicity still matter.

Mechanism

SERT normally recaptures released serotonin into the presynaptic neuron. Competitive inhibition raises extracellular serotonin, first activating inhibitory autoreceptors and later driving their adaptation and downstream plasticity. Platelets also depend on SERT to acquire serotonin, so blockade impairs platelet amplification and explains added bleeding with NSAIDs or anticoagulants. Excess serotonergic stimulation, especially 5-HT2A/1A during drug interactions, produces clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation, diaphoresis, diarrhea and hyperthermia rather than the lead-pipe rigidity typical of NMS.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Use product-specific MAOI washouts; fluoxetine requires the longest pre-MAOI interval because active drug persists for weeks.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea/diarrhea, headache, insomnia or somnolence

  • Sexual dysfunction and emotional blunting

  • Hyponatremia/SIADH and bleeding

  • Serotonin syndrome, activation/mania and discontinuation symptoms

Contraindications

  • MAOI use within prohibited intervals; pimozide and additional product-specific interactions

  • Use caution with bipolar disorder, seizure risk, prolonged QT, low sodium and bleeding risk

  • Do not combine duplicate serotonergic products without an intentional plan

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach the exact washout and taper plan; do not generalize fluoxetine timing to every SSRI.

  • Urgently assess clonus, hyperreflexia, fever and autonomic instability after a serotonergic addition or dose change.

Interactions

  • Fluoxetine strongly inhibits CYP2D6 and persists after stopping.

  • All add to MAOI, tramadol, linezolid, IV methylene blue and other serotonergic toxicity.

  • NSAIDs, antiplatelets and anticoagulants increase bleeding.

Key Labs

  • Sodium in high-risk patients

  • Bleeding and medication-interaction review

  • ECG when QT risk or interacting drugs warrant

Nclex Traps

  • Fluoxetine's long half-life lowers withdrawal but lengthens interaction risk.

  • Sexual dysfunction is a common adherence issue and should be asked about directly.

  • Serotonin syndrome emphasizes clonus and hyperreflexia; NMS emphasizes severe rigidity and dopamine blockade.

    Note

    Use current product labeling and an indication-specific risk-benefit assessment. Untreated psychiatric illness also carries maternal, fetal, bonding, sleep, and safety risks, so abrupt self-discontinuation can be harmful.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adult caution

    Note

    Use lower starting doses when appropriate and watch orthostasis, falls, hyponatremia, anticholinergic burden, QT risk, renal/hepatic clearance, swallowing, and polypharmacy. Antipsychotics are not approved for dementia-related psychosis.

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric caution

    Note

    Age approvals differ by diagnosis and formulation. Monitor growth, weight, metabolic effects, activation, suicidality, movement disorders, school function, and family observations rather than extrapolating an adult indication.
