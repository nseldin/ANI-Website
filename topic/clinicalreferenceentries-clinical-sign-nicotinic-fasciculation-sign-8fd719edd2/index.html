Definition

Nicotinic fasciculation sign is an observed motor sign. It is a clue to nicotinic cholinergic excess. A positive finding means fine widespread fasciculations progress to weakness.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Nicotinic fasciculation sign means fine widespread fasciculations progress to weakness.

Why It Matters

The observed Nicotinic fasciculation sign finding can signal nicotinic cholinergic excess. Excess acetylcholine repeatedly depolarizes neuromuscular junctions before receptor desensitization and paralysis. Nicotinic fasciculation sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • nicotinic cholinergic excess

Assessment Method

Observe muscles, strength, and breathing in suspected pesticide exposure.

Positive Finding

Fine widespread fasciculations progress to weakness.

Mechanism

Excess acetylcholine repeatedly depolarizes neuromuscular junctions before receptor desensitization and paralysis.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Nicotinic fasciculation sign finding raises concern for nicotinic cholinergic excess because excess acetylcholine repeatedly depolarizes neuromuscular junctions before receptor desensitization and paralysis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nicotinic fasciculation sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Nicotinic fasciculation sign to focus evaluation of nicotinic cholinergic excess and the reviewed alternatives: electrolyte disorder, benign fasciculation, seizure. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe muscles, strength, and breathing in suspected pesticide exposure. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Nicotinic fasciculation sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fasciculations may disappear as weakness worsens. Therefore, Nicotinic fasciculation sign cannot confirm or exclude nicotinic cholinergic excess by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • electrolyte disorder

  • benign fasciculation

  • seizure

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as electrolyte disorder, benign fasciculation, seizure.

False Negative Situations

Nicotinic fasciculation sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of nicotinic cholinergic excess.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Monitor respiratory strength because loss of twitching can mean paralysis, not recovery.
