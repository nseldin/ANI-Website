Used To Treat

Rapid platelet inhibition during percutaneous coronary intervention in clients not already treated with an oral P2Y12 inhibitor and not receiving a glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for cangrelor in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Cangrelor directly and reversibly blocks platelet P2Y12 ADP receptors, preventing ADP-driven activation of glycoprotein IIb/IIIa and platelet aggregation. It does not require hepatic activation, and platelet function begins recovering quickly after discontinuation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor access sites, bleeding, hemoglobin, platelets, blood pressure, neurologic status, and exact bolus-to-infusion timing.

  • Coordinate oral transition precisely: clopidogrel and prasugrel are given after the cangrelor infusion because cangrelor can block their active metabolite from binding; ticagrelor timing differs.

Key Labs

  • CBC/hemoglobin/platelets, bleeding assessment, renal/hepatic context, and procedure-specific platelet or coagulation testing when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Cangrelor wears off quickly. A missed oral transition can leave the new coronary stent suddenly unprotected.

Description

Cangrelor is an IV, direct, reversible P2Y12 inhibitor with near-immediate platelet inhibition and rapid offset after the infusion stops. That makes it useful during PCI when oral absorption or timing is unreliable, but bleeding and correct transition to an oral P2Y12 drug are the entire nursing safety story.
