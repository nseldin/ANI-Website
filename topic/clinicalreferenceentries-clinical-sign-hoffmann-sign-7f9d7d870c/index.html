Definition

Hoffmann sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability. A positive finding means the thumb flexes or adducts and the index finger may flex.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hoffmann sign means the thumb flexes or adducts and the index finger may flex.

Why It Matters

A positive Hoffmann sign response can signal cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability. Sudden finger-flexor stretch spreads through hyperexcitable cervical reflex circuits when descending inhibition is reduced. Hoffmann sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability

Assessment Method

Hold the relaxed middle finger and flick its distal phalanx downward.

Positive Finding

The thumb flexes or adducts and the index finger may flex.

Mechanism

Sudden finger-flexor stretch spreads through hyperexcitable cervical reflex circuits when descending inhibition is reduced.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Hoffmann sign response raises concern for cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability because sudden finger-flexor stretch spreads through hyperexcitable cervical reflex circuits when descending inhibition is reduced. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hoffmann sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hoffmann sign to focus evaluation of cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability and the reviewed alternatives: constitutional brisk reflexes, thyrotoxicosis, anxiety. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Hold the relaxed middle finger and flick its distal phalanx downward. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hoffmann sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A positive sign alone has limited specificity and a negative sign does not exclude cervical myelopathy. Therefore, Hoffmann sign cannot confirm or exclude cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • constitutional brisk reflexes

  • thyrotoxicosis

  • anxiety

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from constitutional brisk reflexes, thyrotoxicosis, anxiety, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Hoffmann sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; can occur in healthy people. When performed as described, Hoffmann sign can adjust concern for cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Hoffmann sign: A positive sign alone has limited specificity and a negative sign does not exclude cervical myelopathy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Hoffmann sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Hold the relaxed middle finger and flick its distal phalanx downward. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Ensure the hand is relaxed and do not equate an isolated bilateral response with cord compression.
