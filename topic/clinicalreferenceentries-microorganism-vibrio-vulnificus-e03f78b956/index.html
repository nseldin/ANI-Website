Definition

Vibrio vulnificus is a clinically important bacterium associated with severe wound infection, primary septicemia, and seafood-associated gastroenteritis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Vibrio vulnificus is a Gram-negative curved rod with a facultative anaerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, motile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

  • Content

    High-risk people should avoid raw oysters and seawater exposure of open wounds; prompt wound cleansing and evaluation of rapidly progressive symptoms are important. The reviewed route description is the reviewed foodborne transmission and waterborne transmission route categories. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    A capsule, iron-responsive growth, and tissue-damaging factors can produce rapidly progressive wound or bloodstream infection, especially with chronic liver disease. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

  • Content

    Vibrio vulnificus is associated with severe wound infection, primary septicemia, and seafood-associated gastroenteritis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
