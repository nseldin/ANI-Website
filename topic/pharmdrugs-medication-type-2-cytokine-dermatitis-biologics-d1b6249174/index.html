Class Example Names

  • Dupilumab

  • Tralokinumab

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis inadequately controlled by topical therapy; dupilumab has multiple additional type-2-inflammatory indications, whereas tralokinumab is an IL-13-specific dermatitis biologic.

Description

Dupilumab and tralokinumab both reduce type-2 inflammation but at different control points. Dupilumab binds IL-4 receptor alpha, blocking both IL-4 signaling through the type-I receptor and shared IL-4/IL-13 signaling through the type-II receptor. Tralokinumab binds IL-13 itself and prevents receptor interaction. This difference explains why their labels, age cutoffs, dosing, and non-dermatology indications must not be inferred from the word 'biologic.'

Mechanism

IL-4 and IL-13 activate JAK-STAT6 programs that promote IgE class switching, eosinophilic inflammation, pruritus, mucus, and epidermal-barrier dysfunction. Blocking IL-4R alpha removes both cytokine inputs; neutralizing IL-13 leaves IL-4 signaling intact. Neither drug is a broad cytotoxic immunosuppressant, but conjunctival inflammation, hypersensitivity, eosinophilic syndromes, helminths, and live-vaccine planning remain clinically important.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Treat pre-existing helminth infection before therapy and avoid live vaccines during treatment. New or worsening conjunctivitis, eye pain, visual change, anaphylaxis/angioedema, or systemic eosinophilic symptoms require assessment. Do not stop systemic corticosteroids abruptly when initiating a biologic.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reactions, conjunctivitis, blepharitis or keratitis

  • Eosinophilia and upper-respiratory infections

  • Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis or angioedema

  • Dupilumab-specific indication contexts can include arthralgia and herpes viral events

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to the biologic or excipients

  • Treat helminth infection before starting

  • Delay live vaccines and clarify active severe ocular disease or unexplained eosinophilic systemic illness

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify target, age, loading dose, maintenance schedule, injection device and indication rather than substituting biologics by class name.

  • Assess skin severity, itch/sleep, infection, eyes, asthma control and steroid use over time.

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines; complete age-appropriate immunizations before therapy when possible.

  • Taper systemic corticosteroids gradually under supervision.

  • No routine CYP interaction is expected, but disease improvement can normalize cytokine-suppressed CYP activity for narrow-therapeutic-index drugs.

Key Labs

  • No routine therapeutic level

  • CBC/eosinophils when clinically indicated

  • Ocular examination for persistent symptoms and disease-specific pulmonary/GI/skin assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Dupilumab blocks IL-4R alpha and therefore both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling; tralokinumab neutralizes IL-13 only.

  • Conjunctivitis is a pathway-linked adverse effect, not an unrelated nuisance.

  • These are maintenance biologics, not rescue drugs for acute bronchospasm or anaphylaxis.
