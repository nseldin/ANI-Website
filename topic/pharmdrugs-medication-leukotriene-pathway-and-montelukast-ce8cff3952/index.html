Used To Treat

Selected maintenance treatment of asthma, exercise-induced bronchoconstriction, and product-specific allergic rhinitis. Leukotriene modifiers are not rapid rescue bronchodilators and generally provide less asthma protection than an inhaled corticosteroid.

Description

When mast cells and eosinophils activate, 5-lipoxygenase converts arachidonic acid into leukotrienes. LTC4, LTD4, and LTE4 are cysteinyl leukotrienes that tighten airway smooth muscle, increase vascular leak and edema, promote mucus, and recruit inflammatory cells. Montelukast and zafirlukast block the CysLT1 receptor, mainly preventing LTD4 signaling; they do not stop leukotriene synthesis. Zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase upstream and therefore reduces several leukotrienes, but carries different liver and interaction concerns. This pathway explains why montelukast can help aspirin-exacerbated or exercise-triggered disease yet cannot rapidly reverse established severe bronchospasm.

Mechanism

Phospholipase A2 releases arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids. In leukocytes, 5-lipoxygenase with FLAP generates leukotriene A4, which becomes LTB4 or the cysteinyl sequence LTC4, LTD4, and LTE4. LTD4 activates Gq-coupled CysLT1 receptors on airway smooth muscle and inflammatory cells, increasing IP3, calcium, contraction, mucus, and edema. Montelukast competitively blocks CysLT1 without blocking cyclooxygenase, histamine receptors, or 5-lipoxygenase. In aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease, cyclooxygenase-1 inhibition can shift mediator balance toward cysteinyl leukotrienes, making the pathway particularly relevant, but specialist diagnosis and broader anti-inflammatory treatment remain necessary.

Boxed Warning

Montelukast carries a boxed warning for serious neuropsychiatric events, including agitation, aggression, depression, abnormal dreams or sleep, and suicidal thoughts or behavior. Reserve allergic-rhinitis use for inadequate response or intolerance to alternatives. Discuss risk before treatment, stop montelukast and contact a clinician promptly for new behavioral or mood symptoms, and do not assume a prior psychiatric history is required.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, abdominal symptoms, fever, upper-respiratory symptoms, and neuropsychiatric effects

  • Rare eosinophilic conditions may appear when systemic corticosteroids are reduced; this does not mean montelukast alone caused every case

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity and inability to monitor or respond to serious neuropsychiatric change

  • Not a substitute for rescue bronchodilation, inhaled corticosteroid control, or emergency treatment of status asthmaticus

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask specifically about sleep, nightmares, irritability, aggression, anxiety, depression, attention, hallucinations, and suicidal thinking at baseline and follow-up.

  • Confirm the indication; mild allergic rhinitis usually has safer alternatives, while asthma benefit must be weighed against risk.

Interactions

  • Strong enzyme inducers can lower montelukast exposure; zileuton and zafirlukast have different hepatic and CYP interaction profiles.

  • Do not abruptly reduce corticosteroids because symptoms are improved; taper only under the treatment plan.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, exacerbations, rescue use, mood/behavior, sleep, and adherence

  • Liver tests for synthesis inhibitors or when clinically indicated; routine montelukast levels are not useful

Nclex Traps

  • Montelukast blocks CysLT1; it does not inhibit 5-lipoxygenase.

  • A tablet used for asthma maintenance is not an acute bronchodilator.
