Definition

Landau reflex is an infant postural-reflex sign. It is a clue to normal developing extensor tone. A positive finding means the head lifts and trunk and legs extend; head flexion causes flexion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Landau reflex means the head lifts and trunk and legs extend; head flexion causes flexion.

Why It Matters

A positive Landau reflex response can signal normal developing extensor tone. Vestibular and proprioceptive systems coordinate antigravity extensor tone. Landau reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • normal developing extensor tone

Assessment Method

Support the infant horizontally in ventral suspension.

Positive Finding

The head lifts and trunk and legs extend; head flexion causes flexion.

Mechanism

Vestibular and proprioceptive systems coordinate antigravity extensor tone.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Landau reflex response raises concern for normal developing extensor tone because vestibular and proprioceptive systems coordinate antigravity extensor tone. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Landau reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Landau reflex to focus evaluation of normal developing extensor tone and the reviewed alternatives: opisthotonus, floppy infant posture, cerebral palsy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Support the infant horizontally in ventral suspension. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Landau reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Prematurity, fatigue, and distress affect posture. Therefore, Landau reflex cannot confirm or exclude normal developing extensor tone by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • opisthotonus

  • floppy infant posture

  • cerebral palsy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from opisthotonus, floppy infant posture, cerebral palsy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Landau reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current developmental adjunct. When performed as described, Landau reflex can adjust concern for normal developing extensor tone, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Landau reflex: Prematurity, fatigue, and distress affect posture.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Landau reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Support the infant horizontally in ventral suspension. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Support chest and airway and interpret by corrected age.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Landau reflex observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The head lifts and trunk and legs extend; head flexion causes flexion. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Support the infant horizontally in ventral suspension. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, normal developing extensor tone, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Landau reflex on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
