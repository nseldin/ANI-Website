Definition

Macule means a flat, clearly outlined area of skin color change. You can see the spot, but you cannot feel a bump, depression, or fluid-filled blister.

Plain Language

The key distinction is flatness: a macule changes color without changing the height of the skin.

Why It Matters

Recognizing a flat macule helps separate it from raised papules and plaques or fluid-filled vesicles. The pattern, blanching, symptoms, medication timing, and systemic findings then guide urgency and the differential diagnosis.

Associated Conditions

  • localized skin color change without elevation, depression, or a solid palpable component; causes include altered pigment, inflammation, vascular change, medication reactions, infection, and blood outside vessels

Assessment Method

Inspect the entire skin and visible mucosa under neutral light, compare with the patient's baseline, view the lesion from the side, gently palpate it, assess blanching when appropriate, and document color, border, size, distribution, surface, symptoms, evolution, exposures, and systemic findings.

Positive Finding

A circumscribed area differs in color from surrounding skin but remains flat and nonpalpable, with no raised solid or fluid-filled component.

Mechanism

Pigment, superficial blood flow, inflammation, or blood deposited in tissue changes the visible color while leaving the skin surface level.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Macule finding raises concern for localized skin color change without elevation, depression, or a solid palpable component; causes include altered pigment, inflammation, vascular change, medication reactions, infection, and blood outside vessels because pigment, superficial blood flow, inflammation, or blood deposited in tissue changes the visible color while leaving the skin surface level. Evidence Limitations

Size cutoffs and color descriptions vary, lighting and skin pigmentation affect visibility, and a macule's appearance alone does not identify its cause. Therefore, Macule cannot confirm or exclude localized skin color change without elevation, depression, or a solid palpable component; causes include altered pigment, inflammation, vascular change, medication reactions, infection, and blood outside vessels by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as patch, papule, petechia, postinflammatory pigment change.

False Negative Situations

Macule may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of localized skin color change without elevation, depression, or a solid palpable component; causes include altered pigment, inflammation, vascular change, medication reactions, infection, and blood outside vessels.

Modern Status

Current descriptive morphology, not a diagnosis; recognizing that a lesion is flat rather than raised is an early step in narrowing the differential and deciding whether nonblanching, rapidly changing, mucosal, or systemic findings need urgent evaluation.
