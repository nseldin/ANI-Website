Used To Treat

ADHD; methylphenidate products also have formulation-specific narcolepsy indications, while atomoxetine is a noncontrolled nonstimulant option.

Description

Methylphenidate blocks dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, improving prefrontal signal-to-noise quickly but also increasing reward-circuit catecholamines, appetite suppression, pulse, blood pressure and misuse risk. Atomoxetine selectively blocks NET; in prefrontal cortex, where NET clears both norepinephrine and dopamine, this raises both transmitters without the same rapid striatal dopamine surge. Atomoxetine benefit accumulates over weeks and it is not a controlled substance, but it still can increase pulse/BP, cause liver injury, urinary/sexual effects, and suicidal ideation in children/adolescents.

Mechanism

DAT/NET blockade by methylphenidate reduces catecholamine reuptake within minutes to hours, strengthening task-relevant prefrontal networks while higher exposure recruits motor and reward pathways. Atomoxetine selectively blocks presynaptic NET; prefrontal dopamine rises indirectly because that region has little DAT and relies on NET for dopamine clearance. Slower network adaptation explains why atomoxetine cannot be judged after one dose. CYP2D6 poor metabolism or inhibition can markedly raise atomoxetine exposure, while methylphenidate formulation engineering changes release timing and makes milligram-for-milligram substitution unsafe.

Boxed Warning

Methylphenidate BOXED WARNING: high potential for abuse, misuse and addiction, with overdose and death risk; assess, educate, store securely and reassess. Atomoxetine BOXED WARNING: increased suicidal ideation in children and adolescents; monitor behavior closely.

Adverse Effects

  • Both: appetite loss, GI symptoms, insomnia and increased heart rate/blood pressure

  • Methylphenidate: misuse/addiction, psychosis/mania, growth suppression, tics, priapism and Raynaud phenomenon

  • Atomoxetine: suicidal ideation, severe liver injury, urinary retention/hesitancy and sexual adverse effects

Contraindications

  • MAOI use within 14 days for both

  • Avoid stimulant use in serious structural cardiac disease when risk is unacceptable

  • Assess glaucoma, pheochromocytoma and severe cardiovascular disease under atomoxetine labeling

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline cardiac history, blood pressure/pulse, height/weight, appetite, sleep, tics, mood and substance-use risk.

  • Compare effect across the entire school/work day and rebound period, not only the first hour after a dose.

Interactions

  • MAOIs can produce hypertensive crisis.

  • CYP2D6 inhibitors such as fluoxetine/paroxetine raise atomoxetine and may require dosing changes.

  • Acidifying/alkalinizing and formulation-specific interactions differ; never assume all long-acting stimulant products are interchangeable.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, pulse, height, weight and growth trajectory

  • Liver tests if atomoxetine injury symptoms occur

  • Diversion/misuse, sleep, appetite, mood and functional response

Nclex Traps

  • Nonstimulant does not mean cardiovascular- or suicide-risk free.

  • Atomoxetine is scheduled daily and delayed; it is not an as-needed focus pill.

  • Do not crush or exchange extended-release stimulant formulations by equal milligrams without product guidance.
