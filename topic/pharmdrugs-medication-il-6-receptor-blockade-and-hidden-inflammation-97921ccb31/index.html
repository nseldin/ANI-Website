Used To Treat

Tocilizumab is used for several label-specific conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, giant cell arteritis, selected juvenile arthritis, systemic-sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease, cytokine release syndrome, and hospitalized COVID-19 contexts. Sarilumab is used for rheumatoid arthritis, polymyalgia rheumatica, and selected polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis. Routes and indications are not interchangeable.

Description

IL-6 drives fever, hepatic C-reactive protein production, neutrophil trafficking, thrombopoiesis, hepcidin signaling, and inflammatory suppression of drug-metabolizing enzymes. Tocilizumab and sarilumab bind IL-6 receptor rather than IL-6 itself, blocking both membrane and soluble receptor signaling through gp130. This can rapidly lower CRP and blunt fever even while infection is developing, so bedside findings cannot be dismissed because inflammatory markers look reassuring. The same pathway explains neutropenia, lipid changes, liver-test abnormalities, bowel-perforation concern, and altered exposure to CYP-metabolized drugs.

Mechanism

IL-6 binds membrane IL-6R or soluble IL-6R; the complex recruits and dimerizes gp130, activates JAK1/JAK2/TYK2, and phosphorylates STAT3 with MAPK and PI3K signaling. Tocilizumab and sarilumab occupy soluble and membrane IL-6R alpha, preventing formation of the signaling complex. Hepatocyte CRP transcription falls, which explains both therapeutic biomarker decline and the possibility of a deceptively low CRP during infection. Chronic inflammation suppresses several CYP enzymes; removing IL-6 signaling can restore CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4 activity, increasing metabolism and lowering concentrations of affected substrates. IL-6 also supports mucosal repair, so diverticulitis plus corticosteroids or NSAIDs can magnify perforation risk.

Boxed Warning

Tocilizumab and sarilumab carry boxed warnings for serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death, including bacterial, viral, invasive fungal, tuberculosis, and opportunistic infections. Test for latent TB, treat when indicated, and interrupt therapy for serious infection. Because CRP and fever can be pharmacologically suppressed, new abdominal pain, cough, dyspnea, confusion, hypotension, or localized infection requires assessment even when laboratory inflammation appears low.

Adverse Effects

  • Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, elevated liver tests, lipid elevation, injection or infusion reaction, and serious infection

  • GI perforation, hypersensitivity, TB or opportunistic infection, and clinically important changes in CYP-substrate exposure

Contraindications

  • Serious active infection, severe hypersensitivity, low baseline neutrophils or platelets, or marked transaminase elevation may preclude initiation under the product-specific threshold.

  • Diverticulitis history, concurrent steroids/NSAIDs, liver disease, dyslipidemia, TB risk, and narrow-index CYP drugs require an explicit plan.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend symptoms and examination, not CRP alone. Ask specifically about abdominal pain and bowel change because perforation may present without a dramatic inflammatory marker rise.

  • Reconcile warfarin, cyclosporine, theophylline, anticonvulsants, and other narrow-index CYP substrates when IL-6 blockade starts or stops.

Interactions

  • Biologic DMARDs and JAK inhibitors add infection risk and are not routinely combined; live vaccines are avoided during treatment.

  • Restored CYP activity can lower exposure to CYP substrates; the effect may persist for weeks because biologic receptor blockade outlasts the injection day.

Key Labs

  • CBC with ANC and platelets, ALT/AST, lipids, TB assessment, hepatitis/infection context, and product-specific monitoring schedule

  • Abdominal findings, fever-independent infection cues, disease activity, and concentrations or effects of narrow-index CYP substrates

Nclex Traps

  • Normal CRP does not rule out infection after IL-6 receptor blockade.

  • A biologic can create a drug interaction by reversing inflammation-mediated CYP suppression even when it neither inhibits nor induces CYP directly.
