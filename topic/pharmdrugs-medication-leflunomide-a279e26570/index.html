Class

Pyrimidine-synthesis-inhibiting conventional synthetic DMARD and teriflunomide prodrug

Used To Treat

Adults with active rheumatoid arthritis to reduce signs and symptoms, inhibit structural joint damage, and improve physical function. It is a long-term disease-modifying drug, not rapid pain relief, and its active metabolite can remain in the body for months to years without an elimination procedure.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warnings: embryo-fetal toxicity and hepatotoxicity. Exclude pregnancy before treatment, use effective contraception, and perform accelerated elimination if pregnancy occurs or when required before conception. Severe liver injury and fatal liver failure have occurred. Avoid pre-existing acute/chronic liver disease or baseline ALT above the label threshold, monitor ALT monthly for the first six months and then every six to eight weeks, and start washout plus weekly testing when drug-induced liver injury is suspected.

Mechanism

After first-pass conversion, teriflunomide inhibits mitochondrial DHODH, the fourth enzyme in de novo pyrimidine synthesis that oxidizes dihydroorotate to orotate while coupling to the respiratory chain. Uridine nucleotide availability falls, activated lymphocytes arrest in G1, and autoantibody/cytokine-driven synovitis declines. Resting cells use pyrimidine salvage more effectively, creating relative selectivity rather than complete immune ablation. The active metabolite is highly protein-bound, has a roughly two-week half-life, and is recycled through bile and intestine. An eleven-day cholestyramine or activated-charcoal procedure markedly increases elimination; two concentrations below the label threshold, separated in time, verify reproductive washout when required.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain pregnancy testing, CBC, ALT/AST, blood pressure, TB assessment, and vaccine review before treatment; trend diarrhea, neuropathy, cough/dyspnea, rash, wounds, infection, and joint response.

  • Know the washout: cholestyramine or activated charcoal interrupts enterohepatic recycling. Stopping the tablet alone does not promptly clear teriflunomide.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, severe hepatic impairment, leflunomide hypersensitivity, and current teriflunomide treatment are contraindications under the current label.

  • Clarify active infection, cytopenias, TB risk, lung disease, neuropathy, uncontrolled blood pressure, vaccination, alcohol/hepatotoxin exposure, and reproductive plans.

Interactions

  • Other hepatotoxic or marrow-suppressive drugs add toxicity. Live vaccines are avoided; effects may persist after stopping because the metabolite remains.

  • Teriflunomide affects transporters including BCRP and OATP pathways; rosuvastatin and other substrates require label-based review. Warfarin INR and CYP substrates can also change.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test, ALT/AST/bilirubin, CBC with platelets, blood pressure, TB assessment, and active-metabolite concentration when reproductive washout verification is required

  • Respiratory assessment, neuropathy, skin/mucosa, infection, diarrhea, and rheumatoid disease activity

Nclex Traps

  • The active drug is teriflunomide and it persists after tablets stop; washout is a pharmacokinetic intervention, not a detox slogan.

  • Leflunomide and teriflunomide should not be taken together because one becomes the other.

Description

Leflunomide is an oral prodrug DMARD that is rapidly converted to long-lived teriflunomide, which inhibits mitochondrial dihydroorotate dehydrogenase and restricts de novo pyrimidine synthesis in activated lymphocytes. Activated T and B lymphocytes need large amounts of newly synthesized pyrimidines for clonal expansion; blocking de novo uridine production slows their cell-cycle progression while resting cells can rely more on salvage pathways. This explains disease modification rather than immediate analgesia. Teriflunomide undergoes extensive enterohepatic recycling, so simply stopping tablets does not rapidly remove fetal, liver, marrow, lung, or skin risk. Cholestyramine or activated charcoal washout interrupts recycling when accelerated elimination is required.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain, headache, rash, alopecia, hypertension, transaminase elevation, and respiratory infection

  • Fatal liver failure, pancytopenia/agranulocytosis, serious infection, SJS/TEN/DRESS, interstitial lung disease, peripheral neuropathy, skin ulcer, and fetal harm
