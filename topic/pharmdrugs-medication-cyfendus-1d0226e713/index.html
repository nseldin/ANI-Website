Class

Anthrax vaccine adsorbed, adjuvanted with CpG 7909

Used To Treat

Postexposure prophylaxis of disease following suspected or confirmed Bacillus anthracis exposure in adults 18 through 65 years when given with recommended antibacterial drugs because vaccine immunity does not develop quickly enough to cover early spore germination. It is not labeled as a routine pre-exposure vaccine or treatment for active anthrax because it cannot kill bacilli or reverse established toxin injury.

Boxed Warning

FETAL-HARM AND ALLERGY WARNING: the CYFENDUS label warns of fetal harm and cites a higher first-trimester major-birth-defect signal with BioThrax. Severe reaction after CYFENDUS or BioThrax is a contraindication. A lethal aerosol exposure still requires explicit emergency benefit-risk planning and a complete antimicrobial course.

Mechanism

Protective-antigen proteins stimulate antibodies that block the binding and pore-forming steps used by anthrax lethal and edema toxins to enter host cells. CpG 7909 activates TLR9 in innate immune cells, increasing antigen presentation and promoting a faster antibody response. That acceleration permits a shorter two-dose PEP schedule, but immunity still takes time and does not kill dormant spores. Antibiotics remain necessary because they kill bacilli as spores germinate before and during vaccine response.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify CYFENDUS, PEP indication, age 18 through 65 years, route, dose number, lot, and second appointment because it is not interchangeable with BioThrax.

  • Screen for severe allergy and observe according to vaccine policy with epinephrine available because repeat hypersensitivity can progress rapidly to airway obstruction or shock.

  • Explain that the adjuvant speeds immunity but does not neutralize active disease or justify shortening the antimicrobial course.

Contraindications

  • BioThrax-associated anaphylaxis or another severe allergic reaction is a labeled contraindication to CYFENDUS, as is a severe reaction after CYFENDUS or to a vaccine component. A high-risk exposure still requires immediate public-health selection of another protection strategy.

  • Do not use CYFENDUS alone after exposure or give it as treatment for symptomatic anthrax because immunity is delayed and the vaccine neither kills germinating bacilli nor reverses established toxin injury.

Interactions

  • Immunosuppressive treatment can reduce vaccine response, making antimicrobial adherence and public-health follow-up particularly important.

  • BioThrax and CYFENDUS are alternative product-specific schedules; do not mix them or duplicate vaccination without explicit public-health direction.

Key Labs

  • Routine antibody testing is not required for standard PEP; document exposure, antibiotic, vaccine product, lot, route, site, and dates.

  • Any compatible symptom triggers anthrax cultures, imaging, meningitis assessment, and organ studies rather than vaccine-titer testing.

Nclex Traps

  • CYFENDUS is two-dose PEP with antibiotics; BioThrax is a different product and schedule.

  • CYFENDUS is not a routine pre-exposure vaccine.

  • Completing the vaccine does not end the antibiotic course.

Population Risks

  • Population

    People younger than 18 or older than 65 years

    Note

    Because the approved age range does not include them and vaccine protection cannot be assumed, antibiotics generally continue 60 days after aerosol exposure while emergency authorities select any vaccine strategy.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    The label states CYFENDUS can cause fetal harm and cites a higher first-trimester major-birth-defect signal after BioThrax exposure. Because inhalational anthrax can be fatal, obstetric and public-health teams must document emergency maternal-fetal benefit-risk rather than simply withhold protection.

  • Population

    Lactating or immunocompromised people

    Note

    After aerosol exposure, antibiotics generally continue 60 days because immune response may be reduced and dormant spores can germinate late; the vaccine decision requires public-health expertise.

Description

CYFENDUS is an adjuvanted anthrax vaccine used with recommended antibiotics to prevent disease after suspected or confirmed exposure to Bacillus anthracis. It combines anthrax vaccine adsorbed antigen with CpG 7909, a toll-like-receptor-9 agonist adjuvant that accelerates and strengthens the immune response, allowing a two-dose postexposure series two weeks apart. Antibiotics remain essential because vaccination cannot kill bacilli that emerge before immunity develops. For a healthy, nonpregnant vaccinated adult age 18 through 65, CDC 2023 continues antibiotics until 42 days after the first vaccine dose or 14 days after the last dose, whichever is later; specified aerosol-exposure groups require 60 days. The product is not a spelling variant of BioThrax. The label also warns that CYFENDUS can cause fetal harm and cites a higher first-trimester major-birth-defect signal observed with BioThrax exposure, so pregnancy requires explicit emergency benefit-risk reasoning rather than describing the data as merely limited.

Administration Timing

  • After public-health confirmation of a credible aerosol exposure, start an effective antimicrobial promptly and give the first CYFENDUS dose according to the current label.

  • Give the second PEP dose two weeks after the first; verify product and schedule rather than substituting BioThrax's three-dose course.

  • For a healthy, nonpregnant vaccinated adult age 18 through 65, continue antibiotics until 42 days after the first vaccine dose or 14 days after the second dose, whichever is later. Use 60 days after aerosol exposure for no vaccine, age 66 or older, immunocompromise, pregnancy, lactation, or childhood.

  • A healthy adult age 18 through 65 with a nonaerosol cutaneous or ingestion exposure generally receives seven days of antibiotics without vaccine; incident authorities decide exceptions.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site pain, tenderness, warmth, redness, swelling, limited motion, fatigue, headache, and myalgia can occur.

  • Severe hypersensitivity is rare; syncope and falls require routine vaccination precautions.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Anaphylaxis or a severe systemic vaccine reaction requires emergency care and reporting.

  • Fever, respiratory symptoms, chest pain, edema, neurologic change, shock, or a suspicious lesion during PEP requires immediate anthrax treatment evaluation.

Evidence Limitations

  • Approval uses the Animal Rule with immune bridging and human safety data, not controlled human anthrax exposure trials.

  • Age, exposure duration, susceptibility, and emergency authorities can modify the operational plan, so current CDC direction is required.

Regulatory Status

FDA approved under the Animal Rule for postexposure prophylaxis in adults 18 through 65 years when administered with recommended antibacterial drugs.
