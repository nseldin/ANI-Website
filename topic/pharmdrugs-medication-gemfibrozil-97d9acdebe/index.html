Contraindications

  • Severe kidney or liver dysfunction, primary biliary cirrhosis, preexisting gallbladder disease, and concurrent simvastatin, repaglinide, or dasabuvir are major current contraindication patterns.

  • Do not use it as routine prevention in a patient whose triglyceride level and risk do not justify the interaction and gallstone burden.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give 30 minutes before breakfast and the evening meal. Reconcile exact statin, diabetes drugs, anticoagulants, renal function, gallbladder disease, thyroid status, and muscle symptoms.

  • Escalate severe weakness, dark urine, reduced urine, RUQ pain, jaundice, rash, or bleeding. Recheck lipids and stop if an adequate trial does not produce the planned response.

Interactions

  • Simvastatin is contraindicated; other statins are avoided or limited because OATP1B1 and glucuronidation inhibition raises exposure. Repaglinide is contraindicated because profound hypoglycemia can occur.

  • Warfarin effect can increase and requires close INR-guided adjustment. Colchicine adds neuromuscular risk; selected OATP1B1 substrates can accumulate.

Key Labs

  • Fasting triglycerides and full lipid panel, ALT/AST/bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, CBC when prolonged or symptomatic, INR with warfarin, glucose with diabetes drugs, and CK/creatinine/potassium/urinalysis for significant muscle symptoms.

  • If response is inadequate after secondary causes and adherence are addressed, continuing exposure only adds harm and should be reassessed.

Nclex Traps

  • Gemfibrozil is not simply another name for fenofibrate. Its statin interaction is much stronger because it inhibits transport and metabolism.

  • Take it before meals, not with the meal by default. The schedule is part of its pharmacokinetics.

Used To Treat

Adjunct to diet for very high triglycerides and selected type IIb dyslipidemia in carefully chosen patients under current labeling. Its role is limited by interaction burden and lack of broad benefit as routine statin add-on therapy.

Mechanism

Gemfibrozil activates PPAR-alpha and changes transcription of lipoprotein lipase, ApoC-III, fatty-acid oxidation, and ApoA proteins, accelerating VLDL and chylomicron triglyceride removal. It is glucuronidated to metabolites, including a glucuronide that inhibits OATP1B1 and selected CYP or conjugation pathways. Blocking OATP1B1 prevents statins such as simvastatin acid from entering hepatocytes efficiently, leaving higher plasma and muscle exposure. Inhibiting glucuronidation further slows clearance. This pharmacokinetic amplification combines with the fibrate's own muscle toxicity and is why the interaction is much stronger than a generic 'two lipid drugs' warning. Gallstone risk follows increased biliary cholesterol excretion and saturation. Renal dysfunction and hypothyroidism further reduce the muscle safety margin.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Gemfibrozil with simvastatin is contraindicated because severe myopathy and rhabdomyolysis can cause acute renal failure and death. Review every statin, kidney function, thyroid status, and myotoxic co-medication before use.

Class Pathway

  • PPAR-alpha-mediated triglyceride clearance

  • OATP1B1 and glucuronidation inhibition

  • High statin myopathy interaction burden

Description

Gemfibrozil, branded Lopid, is a fibric-acid PPAR-alpha agonist that lowers triglycerides by increasing lipoprotein-lipase-mediated VLDL and chylomicron clearance, decreasing ApoC-III, and reducing hepatic VLDL output; it is taken as 600 mg twice daily 30 minutes before the morning and evening meals. Its defining safety difference is inhibition of hepatic OATP1B1 uptake and statin glucuronidation by gemfibrozil and its metabolite. This can greatly increase statin exposure: simvastatin plus gemfibrozil is contraindicated, and many other statin combinations are avoided or dose restricted. Fenofibrate is usually preferred when a fibrate is truly needed with a statin. Gemfibrozil also increases cholesterol saturation of bile, can produce gallstones, and can potentiate warfarin. Meal timing matters because premeal administration produces the intended absorption profile.

Adverse Effects

  • Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, fatigue, headache, and rash are common or important effects.

  • Gallstones, cholecystitis, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, cytopenias, myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, and severe hypersensitivity or cutaneous reactions can occur.
