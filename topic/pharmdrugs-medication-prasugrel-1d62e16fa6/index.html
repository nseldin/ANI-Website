Used To Treat

Prevention or treatment of arterial thrombosis, including MI/acute coronary syndrome, coronary stents, ischemic stroke/TIA prevention, and peripheral arterial disease.

Mechanism

Prasugrel works by blocking the platelet P2Y12 ADP receptor, reducing GP IIb/IIIa activation and platelet aggregation. This prevents arterial thrombosis after ACS/stents but increases bleeding; adherence after stent placement is a major safety point.

Boxed Warning

Bleeding is the major class safety issue; selected agents have drug-specific boxed warnings.

Contraindications

  • Active bleeding

  • serious hypersensitivity

  • hemorrhagic stroke history for selected agents

  • Active bleeding, suspected intracranial hemorrhage, recent major surgery/trauma, severe uncontrolled hypertension, and other protocol-specific exclusions.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess bleeding, bruising, black stools, procedure timing, and recent stent/stroke/MI indication.

  • Do not stop after stent unless prescriber directs.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Prasugrel is categorized here as P2Y12 receptor inhibitor antiplatelet. Pathway: Hematology medication > Antiplatelet > P2Y12 receptor inhibitor. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants, NSAIDs, SSRIs/SNRIs, thrombolytics increase bleeding risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • CBC

  • hemoglobin/hematocrit

  • platelets

  • Airway, breathing, circulation, mental status, ECG, glucose, electrolytes, renal/liver function, coagulation studies, drug levels when available, poison-control guidance, and recurrence after short-acting antidotes.

Nclex Traps

  • Stopping antiplatelet therapy after a coronary stent can cause stent thrombosis.

  • Antidote does not replace ABCs. Stabilize airway/breathing/circulation and monitor for recurrent toxicity.
