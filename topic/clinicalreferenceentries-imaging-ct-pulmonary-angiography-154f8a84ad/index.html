Summary

CT pulmonary angiography (computed tomography pulmonary angiography) is a high-yield Respiratory diagnostic reference. Contrast CT study used to evaluate pulmonary embolism.

Quick Answer

CT pulmonary angiography (computed tomography pulmonary angiography): Contrast CT study used to evaluate pulmonary embolism. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    No pulmonary embolus is seen on the study, assuming image quality is adequate.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    A filling defect in pulmonary arteries supports pulmonary embolism and changes anticoagulation, thrombolysis, or intervention decisions based on severity.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Right-heart strain, hypoxemia, syncope, hypotension, high clot burden, or worsening dyspnea makes the result urgent.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Contrast CT study used to evaluate pulmonary embolism.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess iodine/contrast allergy history, renal risk, pregnancy status, IV access, and ability to lie flat.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Explain the procedure, protect safety during transport, monitor after contrast or sedation if used, and act on urgent findings instead of waiting passively.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Airway symptoms after contrast, new neuro change, unstable vital signs, severe pain, bleeding, or imaging results that suggest hemorrhage, obstruction, embolus, or ischemia.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • A negative D-dimer can help in low-risk clients; high-risk PE often needs definitive imaging instead of reassurance.
