Class

Extended-spectrum mold-active triazole antifungal; fungal CYP51 inhibitor

Used To Treat

Treatment of invasive aspergillosis with specified formulations and age groups; prophylaxis of invasive Aspergillus and Candida infection in severely immunocompromised patients; oropharyngeal candidiasis with suspension; and guideline-directed treatment/salvage of mucormycosis. Indications differ by formulation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Formulations are not interchangeable. Serious hepatic toxicity, QT prolongation/torsades, severe hypersensitivity, and life-threatening CYP3A interactions can occur.

Mechanism

CYP51 inhibition accumulates methylated sterol precursors and depletes ergosterol. The delayed-release tablet protects drug through the stomach and releases it at higher intestinal pH, improving exposure compared with suspension in patients who cannot take a full meal. Severe diarrhea, mucositis, graft-versus-host disease, tube administration, or altered anatomy can still reduce absorption. Because posaconazole inhibits CYP3A4, concentrations of sirolimus, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, selected statins, benzodiazepines, and QT-active substrates can rise. A trough can distinguish malabsorption or interaction-driven underexposure from resistance or source failure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Say and document the formulation at every step. Verify loading regimen, food/tube instructions, immunosuppressant adjustment, ECG/electrolytes, liver tests, and trough when treating invasive disease or absorption is uncertain.

  • Watch for hypertension with hypokalemia as a clue to posaconazole-associated mineralocorticoid excess, not merely poor dietary potassium.

Contraindications

  • Selected CYP3A substrates such as sirolimus, certain statins, ergot alkaloids, and QT-risk drugs are contraindicated under current labeling; verify exact list and formulation.

  • Do not substitute suspension for delayed-release tablets using the same dose. Do not crush delayed-release tablets.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibition can sharply raise tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus, vincristine/other vinca alkaloids, selected statins, benzodiazepines, and antiarrhythmics.

  • Strong inducers and acid/GI factors can lower exposure, especially with suspension, risking breakthrough mold infection.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT/bilirubin, potassium/magnesium, blood pressure, ECG when indicated, posaconazole trough for selected treatment/prophylaxis questions, renal function for IV vehicle context, and organism/site response.

  • A trough must be paired with formulation and draw time; otherwise it can mislabel an administration error as biologic resistance.

Nclex Traps

  • Posaconazole is Mucorales active; voriconazole is not.

  • Noxafil suspension and delayed-release tablets are not interchangeable even though the active drug name is identical.

Description

Posaconazole is an extended-spectrum triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP51), depletes ergosterol, weakens the cell membrane, and covers many Candida, Aspergillus, and Mucorales. Its major practical lesson is formulation: oral suspension, delayed-release tablets, delayed-release powder, and IV product have different absorption, doses, age/weight indications, and administration. They are not dose-for-dose substitutable. The suspension depends heavily on food, gastric environment, and GI function; delayed-release tablets produce more reliable exposure. Posaconazole inhibits CYP3A4, can prolong QT, and can cause hepatic injury, so transplant, oncology, antiarrhythmic, and statin regimens require deliberate review.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, headache, fever, rash, hypokalemia, and liver-test elevation.

  • Severe hepatic injury, QT-mediated dysrhythmia, adrenal/pseudoaldosterone effects in selected patients, hypersensitivity, and interaction-mediated immunosuppressant or statin toxicity.
