Plain Meaning

Predictive value tells what a positive or negative test result means for this tested population, and it changes with disease prevalence and pretest probability.

Pathology

Positive predictive value is the chance that a person with a positive result truly has the condition. Negative predictive value is the chance that a person with a negative result truly does not have it. Unlike sensitivity and specificity, predictive values shift when prevalence changes.

Pathophysiology

When disease prevalence is low, even a good test can have more false positives than expected. When prevalence or pretest probability is high, a negative test may be less reassuring. This is why screening in low-risk populations and testing high-risk symptomatic clients require different thinking.

Signs Symptoms

  • False-positive clusters when screening low-prevalence groups

  • False reassurance when a high-risk symptomatic client has one negative test

  • Confusion when a test's advertised accuracy does not match bedside probability

Diagnostics

  • Combine sensitivity, specificity, prevalence, pretest probability, likelihood ratios, and clinical picture

  • Use confirmatory testing when predictive value is not strong enough for a major decision

Treatments

  • Avoid acting on screening results as final diagnosis when predictive value is limited

  • Escalate further evaluation when pretest probability remains high despite a negative result

Nursing Priorities

  • Ask: what did this result do to probability for this client, not just in a textbook?

  • Teach that screening positives often need confirmation

  • Watch for high-risk symptoms that override false reassurance

Complications

  • Overtreatment

  • Missed diagnosis

  • Delayed isolation or treatment

  • Anxiety from false positives

Patient Education

  • A positive screen may not equal a diagnosis, and a negative screen may not end evaluation if symptoms are concerning.

  • Follow the recommended confirmatory test or repeat-test plan.

Nclex Traps

  • Predictive value changes with prevalence.

  • Sensitivity/specificity describe the test; PPV/NPV describe what the result means in a population.

  • Clinical probability matters before and after the test.
