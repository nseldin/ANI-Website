Definition

Durkan compression test is a bedside nerve-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel. A positive finding means median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Durkan compression test means median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Durkan compression test response can signal median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel. Direct pressure raises hydrostatic pressure within the fixed-volume carpal tunnel, transiently impairing conduction in an already compressed median nerve. Durkan compression test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel

Assessment Method

Apply steady thumb pressure over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds.

Positive Finding

Median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Mechanism

Direct pressure raises hydrostatic pressure within the fixed-volume carpal tunnel, transiently impairing conduction in an already compressed median nerve.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Durkan compression test response raises concern for median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel because direct pressure raises hydrostatic pressure within the fixed-volume carpal tunnel, transiently impairing conduction in an already compressed median nerve. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Durkan compression test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Durkan compression test to focus evaluation of median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel and the reviewed alternatives: flexor tenosynovitis, arthritis, pronator syndrome. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply steady thumb pressure over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Durkan compression test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pressure amount is not standardized and local pain can confuse. Therefore, Durkan compression test cannot confirm or exclude median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • flexor tenosynovitis

  • arthritis

  • pronator syndrome

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from flexor tenosynovitis, arthritis, pronator syndrome, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Durkan compression test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Durkan compression test can adjust concern for median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Durkan compression test: Pressure amount is not standardized and local pain can confuse.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Durkan compression test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply steady thumb pressure over the carpal tunnel for up to 30 seconds. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid strong pressure over recent surgery, mass, or acute trauma.
