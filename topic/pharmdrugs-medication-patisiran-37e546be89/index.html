Class

IV lipid-nanoparticle transthyretin-directed small interfering RNA

Used To Treat

Polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis in adults.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current ONPATTRO labeling has no boxed warning. Premedicate with a corticosteroid, acetaminophen, H1 blocker and H2 blocker before every infusion. Slow or interrupt for infusion-related reaction and permanently stop a serious or life-threatening reaction. Supplement only the recommended daily allowance of vitamin A and refer new ocular symptoms.

Mechanism

After apolipoprotein-assisted hepatocyte uptake, the lipid nanoparticle releases double-stranded siRNA into the cytoplasm. The guide strand loads into the RNA-induced silencing complex, base-pairs with a conserved TTR mRNA sequence, and directs catalytic cleavage. Reduced TTR also reduces circulating retinol-binding-protein transport, so measured serum vitamin A falls. The lipid nanoparticle enters hepatocytes and releases siRNA, which loads into RISC and directs repeated cleavage of both mutant and wild-type TTR mRNA. Less hepatic TTR means less amyloid precursor, thereby slowing additional deposition rather than removing all existing nerve injury. TTR normally transports retinol-binding protein, so lowering TTR reduces circulating vitamin A and explains supplementation plus symptom-based eye assessment. Lipid-nanoparticle exposure can activate infusion-reaction pathways, which is why corticosteroid, antihistamine, and acetaminophen premedication and monitored infusion are required.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify weight-based dose/cap, q3-week schedule, full premedication timing, filter and infusion rate, prior reaction, respiratory/cardiac reserve, pregnancy and vitamin A plan.

  • Monitor flushing, back or abdominal pain, nausea, dyspnea, headache, hypotension or hypertension during infusion; trend neuropathy, autonomic symptoms, gait, nutrition and cardiac manifestations rather than using TTR reduction alone as success.

Key Labs

  • Clinical neuropathy and autonomic assessment, liver/renal function as indicated, vitamin A-related eye symptoms, and continuous/serial vital signs around infusion; TTR may document target engagement but is not the sole outcome.

Nclex Traps

  • Patisiran is a lipid-nanoparticle siRNA, not an antisense oligonucleotide.

  • Do not chase a low serum vitamin A level with megadoses; TTR reduction changes circulating transport and high-dose vitamin A can be toxic.

Description

Patisiran is an IV lipid-nanoparticle small interfering RNA that enters hepatocytes, recruits RNA interference against both mutant and wild-type TTR mRNA, and lowers the circulating transthyretin precursor that forms hereditary ATTR amyloid. It treats adult hATTR polyneuropathy; its q3-week infusion, mandatory premedication, infusion-reaction risk, and vitamin A consequences distinguish it from subcutaneous GalNAc silencers.
