Definition

Vitamin D deficiency develops when insufficient vitamin D intake, sun synthesis, absorption, hepatic activation, or renal activation reduces calcium-phosphate support for mineralization. The characteristic pattern is low 25-hydroxyvitamin D in an at-risk person, sometimes with proximal weakness, bone pain, high PTH, osteomalacia, or no symptoms. Confirm or stage it with serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D when testing is clinically indicated; distinguish it from osteoporosis, primary hyperparathyroidism, myopathy, metastatic bone pain, and phosphate disorders by chemistry and clinical context. The priority is to replace vitamin D using a cause- and severity-appropriate regimen because osteomalacia can complicate Vitamin D deficiency. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Weakness, sweating, tremor, palpitations, mental-status change, weight change, thirst/polyuria, muscle cramps/spasm, paresthesias, fatigue, temperature intolerance, or abnormal growth/nutrition cues can reveal metabolic instability.

  • Escalate severe hypo/hyperglycemia, seizure, dysrhythmia, tetany/laryngospasm, adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, refeeding electrolyte collapse, or DKA/HHS signs.

Diagnostics

  • serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D when testing is clinically indicated

  • calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, PTH, creatinine, and magnesium when disease is significant

  • evaluation for malabsorption, kidney, liver, medication, and limited-intake causes

  • Distinguish Vitamin D deficiency from osteoporosis, primary hyperparathyroidism, myopathy, metastatic bone pain, and phosphate disorders by chemistry and clinical context.

Labs

  • glucose may be high or low depending on condition

  • sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphate changes can become life-threatening

  • anion gap and ketones matter in DKA

Treatments

  • replace vitamin D using a cause- and severity-appropriate regimen

  • ensure safe calcium intake and treat malabsorption or organ disease

  • recheck chemistry and level when high-dose or prolonged therapy warrants it

Nursing Priorities

  • Assess diet, sunlight exposure, skin pigmentation, malabsorption, bariatric surgery, kidney and liver disease, medicines, falls, weakness, and bone pain because deficiency usually reflects reduced supply, absorption, activation, or increased breakdown.

  • Interpret 25-hydroxyvitamin D with calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, parathyroid hormone, kidney function, symptoms, and assay context because an isolated value does not identify osteomalacia or its cause.

  • Give the prescribed replacement and calcium plan while checking formulation and cumulative supplement dose because under-replacement delays mineralization whereas excessive vitamin D can cause hypercalcemia and kidney injury.

  • Trend strength, gait, falls, pain, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, calcium, and renal function at the ordered interval because biochemical recovery and skeletal recovery occur at different rates.

  • Escalate for confusion, persistent vomiting, dehydration, polyuria, kidney-stone pain, arrhythmia, pathologic fracture, or progressive proximal weakness because severe deficiency or replacement toxicity can cause acute complications.

Complications

  • osteomalacia can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • fracture can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • falls can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • secondary hyperparathyroidism can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • hypocalcemic symptoms can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • hypercalcemia can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • stones can complicate Vitamin D deficiency.

  • kidney injury from excessive treatment

Red Flags

  • Fragility fracture, inability to bear weight, or severe focal bone pain

  • Confusion, repeated vomiting, dehydration, polyuria, or suspected hypercalcemia

  • Renal colic, hematuria, oliguria, or rapidly worsening kidney function

  • Progressive proximal weakness, recurrent falls, or painful muscle spasms or tetany

Patient Education

  • Use the prescribed strength and include all multivitamins in the total because vitamin D products vary greatly and excessive dosing can raise calcium dangerously.

  • Keep follow-up testing rather than treating fatigue alone because the laboratory trend, calcium balance, and underlying cause determine safe treatment duration.

Nclex Traps

  • The active hormone 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is not the routine test for body stores

  • More supplementation is not always safer
