Used To Treat

Gepants treat migraine through product-specific roles: rimegepant, ubrogepant, and intranasal zavegepant treat an attack; atogepant prevents episodic or chronic migraine; rimegepant also prevents episodic migraine on an every-other-day schedule. The roles are not interchangeable even though all four block the same receptor, because each formulation, exposure pattern, dose, and clinical trial program supports a different labeled regimen.

Description

Gepants are nonpeptide small molecules that block the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor, a key amplifier of trigeminal pain signaling in migraine. They are not opioids, NSAIDs, or triptans. Unlike triptans, they do not activate vascular 5-HT1B receptors and therefore do not produce the same direct vasoconstrictor mechanism; however, CGRP normally helps dilate and protect blood vessels, so postmarketing hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon remain clinically meaningful class warnings rather than proof that vascular risk is absent.

Mechanism

During migraine, activated trigeminal sensory fibers release CGRP. CGRP binds a Gs-coupled receptor complex formed mainly by calcitonin receptor-like receptor (CLR) and receptor activity-modifying protein 1 (RAMP1), activates adenylyl cyclase, raises intracellular cAMP, and amplifies peripheral and central nociceptive transmission. Gepants occupy that receptor and reduce downstream signaling. The acute-versus-preventive distinction comes from the drug's approved dosing and evidence, not from a different molecular target. Receptor blockade explains the therapeutic pathway, but current labels do not claim it explains every migraine symptom or every adverse effect.

Boxed Warning

Current U.S. gepant labels have no boxed warning. That does not mean they are risk-free: serious immediate or delayed hypersensitivity, new or worsening hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon are product warnings. Reduced CGRP-mediated vasodilatory reserve is a biologically plausible context for the vascular findings, but postmarketing association does not prove one exclusive mechanism. Interaction rules differ sharply because these are small molecules: CYP3A and P-gp dominate rimegepant and ubrogepant, CYP3A and OATP influence atogepant, and OATP1B3/NTCP plus intranasal decongestants matter for zavegepant.

Adverse Effects

  • Common effects are product specific: nausea with rimegepant or ubrogepant; nausea, constipation, fatigue or somnolence, decreased appetite, and weight loss with atogepant; and taste disturbance, nausea, nasal discomfort, or vomiting with zavegepant.

  • Serious class concerns include hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon. A pale, blue, cold, numb, or painful digit is a perfusion warning, not a cosmetic complaint.

Contraindications

  • A history of serious hypersensitivity to the selected drug or its components is a contraindication; ubrogepant is also contraindicated with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors because markedly increased exposure can amplify toxicity.

  • Severe hepatic or renal impairment may require avoidance or product-specific dose limits. These are not one class-wide rule, so verify the exact drug rather than transferring instructions between agents.

Interactions

  • Rimegepant and ubrogepant exposure can rise with CYP3A or P-gp inhibition and fall enough to lose efficacy with CYP3A induction. Atogepant dosing changes with CYP3A inhibitors or inducers and OATP inhibitors. Zavegepant should be avoided with OATP1B3 or NTCP inhibitors or inducers, and an intranasal decongestant can reduce nasal absorption.

  • An interaction matters because it can either raise exposure and adverse effects or lower exposure below an effective level; the remedy may be avoidance, dose reduction, or a longer interval depending on the exact product.

Nursing Essentials

  • First identify intent: attack treatment or prevention, oral ODT/tablet or nasal spray, and exact product. Then verify the maximum daily dose, any monthly exposure boundary, hepatic and renal suitability, pregnancy, blood pressure, digital perfusion, and every CYP3A/P-gp/OATP/NTCP interaction.

  • When a label says safety beyond a number of treated attacks or doses is not established, that is an evidence boundary rather than a magic toxicity threshold. Exceeding it means the regimen has moved outside adequately studied exposure and should trigger reassessment of migraine control, medication-overuse patterns, preventive therapy, and the diagnosis.

  • Investigate a sudden worst headache, focal deficit, fever or meningismus, pregnancy-related red flags, or a major pattern change instead of automatically treating it as the patient's usual migraine.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum drug level measures effect. Follow headache days, functional disability, acute-medication use, blood pressure, digital vascular symptoms, allergy, hepatic and renal suitability, weight and bowel function when relevant, and interaction changes.

Nclex Traps

  • Atogepant is prevention only; ubrogepant and zavegepant are attack treatment only; rimegepant has separate acute and preventive schedules.

  • Gepants block the CGRP receptor. Among preventive CGRP antibodies, erenumab also blocks the receptor, while fremanezumab, galcanezumab, and eptinezumab bind the CGRP ligand.

  • No direct triptan-like vasoconstriction does not mean no vascular monitoring.

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Human pregnancy data are limited and product pregnancy registries may be available. Migraine itself can be associated with maternal risk, so weigh untreated disease against uncertain fetal exposure rather than assuming either treatment or avoidance is automatically harmless.

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric use

    Note

    Current U.S. indications for these four gepants are adult indications; safety and effectiveness under age 18 have not been established.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adults

    Note

    Trials include limited numbers of older adults. Age alone may not require adjustment, but renal or hepatic reserve, vascular disease, polypharmacy, and transporter or CYP interactions become more important.
