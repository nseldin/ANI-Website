Class

Ovine Fab-fragment antivenom for North American crotaline envenomation

Used To Treat

Clinical envenomation by North American crotalid snakes, including rattlesnakes, copperheads, and cottonmouths, when local injury is progressive, systemic venom effects are present, or hematologic toxicity is developing. This canonical card describes CroFab; it must not be confused with ANAVIP, a different F(ab')2 antivenom with its own label and dosing.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): Anaphylaxis can occur during infusion, and a prior papain, papaya, sheep-protein, or antivenom reaction raises hypersensitivity concern; monitor the airway, breathing, blood pressure, skin, and symptoms continuously and keep epinephrine and resuscitation support immediately available. Recurrent coagulopathy can emerge after apparent initial control as venom leaves tissue depots, so repeat platelets, fibrinogen, PT/INR, and clinical bleeding assessment during observation and follow-up.

Mechanism

Fab fragments bind multiple crotaline venom components and form complexes that can no longer reach their usual tissue, platelet, and coagulation targets. This reduces further proteolysis, capillary leak, neurotoxicity, platelet dysfunction, and consumption of fibrinogen when the relevant toxins are neutralized. The fragments and their complexes are then cleared. Venom can continue to leave a bite-site depot after the circulating Fab concentration falls, which explains why swelling or coagulopathy can recur after apparent initial control and why scheduled post-control dosing and follow-up laboratory surveillance matter. Binding does not regenerate consumed fibrinogen or reverse necrosis already established, so clinical recovery may lag behind neutralization.

Nursing Essentials

  • Remove rings and constricting items, keep the patient at rest, establish IV access away from the affected limb when possible, outline swelling with time stamps, and measure circumference at reproducible landmarks so true progression can be distinguished from imprecise visual impressions.

  • During infusion, continuously watch airway, breathing, blood pressure, skin, and symptoms because immediate hypersensitivity can occur; have epinephrine and resuscitation capability available without delaying indicated antivenom.

  • Avoid ice, tourniquets, incision, suction, and routine prophylactic fasciotomy because these measures do not neutralize venom and can worsen ischemia or tissue injury. Elevation and wound/limb management should follow poison-center and surgical guidance.

  • Teach that recurrent bruising, gum or nose bleeding, dark stool, severe headache, renewed swelling, fever, rash, or joint pain requires prompt reassessment because coagulopathy can recur and serum sickness can emerge days later.

Contraindications

  • A prior papaya, papain, sheep-protein, or antivenom reaction raises hypersensitivity concern but does not create a simple absolute prohibition when envenomation threatens life or limb; treat in a monitored setting with expert risk-benefit planning.

  • Do not use the CroFab card as a dosing substitute for ANAVIP, coral-snake antivenin, scorpion antivenom, or antivenoms outside the labeled species because antibody targets and regimens differ.

Key Labs

  • Serial CBC with platelet count, fibrinogen, PT/INR, hemoglobin, and clinically indicated aPTT; repeat after control and during follow-up because a single normalized panel does not exclude recurrent venom effect.

  • Trend creatinine, urinalysis, CK, electrolytes, type and screen, ECG, and other studies according to systemic findings; record limb measurements, neurovascular status, pain, urine output, every vial, lot, infusion time, and response.

Nclex Traps

  • Antivenom dose follows venom burden and clinical response, not patient weight; a child does not automatically receive fewer vials.

  • Initial control is not the end of care. CroFab-treated patients need recurrence surveillance because tissue-depot venom may outlast circulating Fab.

  • Do not treat a dry bite solely because fang marks are present; serial clinical assessment determines whether envenomation is progressing.

Description

Crotalidae immune Fab is a venom-binding antivenom made of purified ovine Fab antibody fragments. It is used when a North American pit-viper bite is producing meaningful envenomation rather than for every dry bite. The practical goal is initial control: stop progression of swelling and tissue findings, stabilize systemic effects, and improve venom-related coagulopathy. Antivenom neutralizes circulating and newly available venom, but it cannot instantly restore tissue already injured or replace airway, hemodynamic, pain, wound, and compartment-assessment care. Children receive a venom-directed vial dose rather than a smaller body-size dose because the snake injects a venom amount independent of the patient's weight.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion-related urticaria, wheeze, angioedema, hypotension, and anaphylaxis can occur. Stop or slow the infusion and treat the reaction while the toxicology team decides how to complete necessary neutralization.

  • Delayed serum sickness can produce fever, rash, pruritus, myalgia, and arthralgia. Recurrent coagulopathy is a venom-pharmacokinetic problem rather than an ordinary allergic adverse effect, but it is a major post-treatment safety risk.

Administration Timing

  • Call Poison Help or a regional poison center early, mark and serially measure the leading edge of swelling, and begin the active CroFab label's initial-control regimen when progressive local, systemic, or hematologic toxicity warrants treatment. Do not wait for dramatic laboratory failure if clinical envenomation is advancing.

  • Reassess after each initial dose for arrest of local progression, improving systemic findings, and a favorable platelet/fibrinogen/coagulation trend. After initial control, use the current label's maintenance regimen and specialist plan; CroFab and ANAVIP regimens are not interchangeable.

  • Observe long enough to detect early recurrence and arrange explicit outpatient laboratory and return follow-up because delayed thrombocytopenia, hypofibrinogenemia, or bleeding may appear after discharge.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Rapidly advancing swelling, shock, neurotoxicity, airway symptoms, spontaneous bleeding, falling platelets or fibrinogen, rising INR, or failure to achieve initial control requires immediate repeat label-directed antivenom and specialist escalation.

  • Recurrent swelling or coagulopathy after initial response requires renewed poison-center assessment, repeat laboratories, and sometimes additional antivenom. Give blood components for clinically important bleeding or procedures under specialist direction; replacement alone may be consumed if venom remains active.

Evidence Limitations

  • Bedside decisions depend on species, geography, severity, time course, and response. The DailyMed CroFab label current in April 2026 and regional poison-center protocols control preparation and dosing.

  • Comparative antivenom evidence and recurrence patterns do not make Fab and F(ab')2 products interchangeable. This entry intentionally preserves the product distinction.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled CroFab use for management of adult and pediatric patients with North American crotalid envenomation. The active product label was updated in April 2026; verify the stocked label and poison-center protocol.
