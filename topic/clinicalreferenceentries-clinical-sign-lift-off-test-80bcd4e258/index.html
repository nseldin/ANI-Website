Definition

Lift-off test is a bedside resisted-shoulder maneuver. It is a clue to subscapularis dysfunction. A positive finding means the hand cannot lift off or strength is reduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lift-off test means the hand cannot lift off or strength is reduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Lift-off test response can signal subscapularis dysfunction. The subscapularis is the primary internal rotator in this behind-back position. Lift-off test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subscapularis dysfunction

Assessment Method

Place the dorsum of the hand on the lower back and ask the patient to lift it away.

Positive Finding

The hand cannot lift off or strength is reduced.

Mechanism

The subscapularis is the primary internal rotator in this behind-back position.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Lift-off test response raises concern for subscapularis dysfunction because the subscapularis is the primary internal rotator in this behind-back position. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lift-off test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lift-off test to focus evaluation of subscapularis dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: adhesive capsulitis, belly pain, latissimus injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place the dorsum of the hand on the lower back and ask the patient to lift it away. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lift-off test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Limited internal rotation makes the test impossible without proving weakness. Therefore, Lift-off test cannot confirm or exclude subscapularis dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • adhesive capsulitis

  • belly pain

  • latissimus injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from adhesive capsulitis, belly pain, latissimus injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Lift-off test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; requires adequate motion. When performed as described, Lift-off test can adjust concern for subscapularis dysfunction, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Lift-off test: Limited internal rotation makes the test impossible without proving weakness.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Lift-off test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place the dorsum of the hand on the lower back and ask the patient to lift it away. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force the hand behind the back in a stiff or postoperative shoulder.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Lift-off test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The hand cannot lift off or strength is reduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Place the dorsum of the hand on the lower back and ask the patient to lift it away. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, subscapularis dysfunction, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Lift-off test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not force the hand behind the back in a stiff or postoperative shoulder. Base escalation for Lift-off test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
