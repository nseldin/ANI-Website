Definition

Kayser-Fleischer ring is an observed corneal sign. It is a clue to copper deposition in Descemet membrane. A positive finding means a golden-brown or green ring encircles the corneal periphery.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Kayser-Fleischer ring means a golden-brown or green ring encircles the corneal periphery.

Why It Matters

The observed Kayser-Fleischer ring finding can signal copper deposition in Descemet membrane. Copper deposits first superiorly and inferiorly in Descemet membrane and later becomes circumferential. Kayser-Fleischer ring cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • copper deposition in Descemet membrane

Assessment Method

Examine the peripheral cornea with slit lamp and gonioscopy when needed.

Positive Finding

A golden-brown or green ring encircles the corneal periphery.

Mechanism

Copper deposits first superiorly and inferiorly in Descemet membrane and later becomes circumferential.

Evidence Limitations

Absence does not exclude hepatic Wilson disease and non-Wilson cholestasis can rarely mimic it. Therefore, Kayser-Fleischer ring cannot confirm or exclude copper deposition in Descemet membrane by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as arcus senilis, Fleischer ring, drug deposits.

False Negative Situations
