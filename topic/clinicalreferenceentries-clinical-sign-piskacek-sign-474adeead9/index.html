Definition

Piskacek sign is a palpated uterine contour sign. It is a clue to asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy. A positive finding means one uterine horn or implantation region feels asymmetrically enlarged.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Piskacek sign means one uterine horn or implantation region feels asymmetrically enlarged.

Why It Matters

The observed Piskacek sign finding can signal asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy. Localized decidual and myometrial growth around implantation changes the early uterine contour. Piskacek sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy

Assessment Method

During an indicated bimanual examination, assess uterine contour.

Positive Finding

One uterine horn or implantation region feels asymmetrically enlarged.

Mechanism

Localized decidual and myometrial growth around implantation changes the early uterine contour.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Piskacek sign finding raises concern for asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy because localized decidual and myometrial growth around implantation changes the early uterine contour. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Piskacek sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Piskacek sign to focus evaluation of asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy and the reviewed alternatives: adenomyosis, adnexal mass, uterine anomaly. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During an indicated bimanual examination, assess uterine contour. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Piskacek sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fibroids and normal anatomy mimic it and palpation cannot localize a safe intrauterine pregnancy. Therefore, Piskacek sign cannot confirm or exclude asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • adenomyosis

  • adnexal mass

  • uterine anomaly

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as adenomyosis, adnexal mass, uterine anomaly.

False Negative Situations

Piskacek sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable presumptive sign. Piskacek sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude asymmetric early implantation in pregnancy. Apply the reviewed limitation for Piskacek sign: Fibroids and normal anatomy mimic it and palpation cannot localize a safe intrauterine pregnancy.

Safety Precautions

Observe Piskacek sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: During an indicated bimanual examination, assess uterine contour. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use ultrasound for location; do not reassure against ectopic pregnancy from palpation.
