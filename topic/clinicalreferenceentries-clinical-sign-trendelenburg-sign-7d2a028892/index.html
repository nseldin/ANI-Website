Definition

Trendelenburg sign is a bedside gait and hip sign. It is a clue to hip abductor weakness on the stance side. A positive finding means the pelvis drops on the unsupported side.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Trendelenburg sign means the pelvis drops on the unsupported side.

Why It Matters

The observed Trendelenburg sign finding can signal hip abductor weakness on the stance side. Weak stance-side gluteus medius and minimus cannot counter the body's adduction moment. Trendelenburg sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hip abductor weakness on the stance side

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to stand on one leg while observing pelvic level and providing fall support.

Positive Finding

The pelvis drops on the unsupported side.

Mechanism

Weak stance-side gluteus medius and minimus cannot counter the body's adduction moment.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Trendelenburg sign finding raises concern for hip abductor weakness on the stance side because weak stance-side gluteus medius and minimus cannot counter the body's adduction moment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Trendelenburg sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Trendelenburg sign to focus evaluation of hip abductor weakness on the stance side and the reviewed alternatives: hip arthritis, leg-length discrepancy, cerebellar imbalance. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to stand on one leg while observing pelvic level and providing fall support. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Trendelenburg sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, balance difficulty, and poor effort reduce specificity. Therefore, Trendelenburg sign cannot confirm or exclude hip abductor weakness on the stance side by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip arthritis

  • leg-length discrepancy

  • cerebellar imbalance

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hip arthritis, leg-length discrepancy, cerebellar imbalance.

False Negative Situations

Trendelenburg sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of hip abductor weakness on the stance side.

Modern Status

Current orthopedic-neurologic adjunct. When performed as described, Trendelenburg sign can adjust concern for hip abductor weakness on the stance side, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Trendelenburg sign: Pain, balance difficulty, and poor effort reduce specificity.

Safety Precautions

Observe Trendelenburg sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to stand on one leg while observing pelvic level and providing fall support. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: The side of pelvic drop is opposite the weak stance hip; do not reverse the laterality.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Trendelenburg sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The pelvis drops on the unsupported side. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask the patient to stand on one leg while observing pelvic level and providing fall support. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, hip abductor weakness on the stance side, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Trendelenburg sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: The side of pelvic drop is opposite the weak stance hip; do not reverse the laterality. Base escalation for Trendelenburg sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
