Definition

Coin test sign is a percussion-auscultation maneuver. It is a clue to large pneumothorax. A positive finding means a clear metallic bell-like sound transmits across the chest.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Coin test sign means a clear metallic bell-like sound transmits across the chest.

Why It Matters

A positive Coin test sign response can signal large pneumothorax. Pleural air forms a resonant cavity that transmits high-frequency metallic tones. Coin test sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large pneumothorax

Assessment Method

Place one coin on the anterior chest, strike it with another, and auscultate posteriorly only as a historical demonstration.

Positive Finding

A clear metallic bell-like sound transmits across the chest.

Mechanism

Pleural air forms a resonant cavity that transmits high-frequency metallic tones.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Coin test sign response raises concern for large pneumothorax because pleural air forms a resonant cavity that transmits high-frequency metallic tones. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Coin test sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Coin test sign to focus evaluation of large pneumothorax and the reviewed alternatives: metallic artifact, thin chest wall, cavitary lesion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place one coin on the anterior chest, strike it with another, and auscultate posteriorly only as a historical demonstration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Coin test sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor standardization and modern ultrasound or radiography make it unnecessary. Therefore, Coin test sign cannot confirm or exclude large pneumothorax by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • metallic artifact

  • thin chest wall

  • cavitary lesion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from metallic artifact, thin chest wall, cavitary lesion, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Coin test sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and obsolete; do not use clinically. Coin test sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude large pneumothorax. Apply the reviewed limitation for Coin test sign: Poor standardization and modern ultrasound or radiography make it unnecessary.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Coin test sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place one coin on the anterior chest, strike it with another, and auscultate posteriorly only as a historical demonstration. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not strike objects on an injured chest or delay point-of-care ultrasound.
