Definition

Campbell sign is an observed breathing sign. It is a clue to severe chronic hyperinflation. A positive finding means the trachea descends noticeably with each inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Campbell sign means the trachea descends noticeably with each inspiration.

Why It Matters

The observed Campbell sign finding can signal severe chronic hyperinflation. Forceful diaphragmatic descent and negative intrathoracic pressure tug the mediastinal structures downward. Campbell sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe chronic hyperinflation

Assessment Method

Observe the trachea and thyroid cartilage during inspiration.

Positive Finding

The trachea descends noticeably with each inspiration.

Mechanism

Forceful diaphragmatic descent and negative intrathoracic pressure tug the mediastinal structures downward.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Campbell sign finding raises concern for severe chronic hyperinflation because forceful diaphragmatic descent and negative intrathoracic pressure tug the mediastinal structures downward. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Campbell sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Campbell sign to focus evaluation of severe chronic hyperinflation and the reviewed alternatives: normal tracheal motion, goiter, tracheomalacia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the trachea and thyroid cartilage during inspiration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Campbell sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Neck anatomy and respiratory effort make the finding inconsistent. Therefore, Campbell sign cannot confirm or exclude severe chronic hyperinflation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal tracheal motion

  • goiter

  • tracheomalacia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal tracheal motion, goiter, tracheomalacia.

False Negative Situations

Campbell sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe chronic hyperinflation.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; imaging and spirometry are preferred. Campbell sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe chronic hyperinflation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Campbell sign: Neck anatomy and respiratory effort make the finding inconsistent.

Safety Precautions

Observe Campbell sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe the trachea and thyroid cartilage during inspiration. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not manipulate the trachea or use this sign to quantify COPD.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Campbell sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The trachea descends noticeably with each inspiration. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Observe the trachea and thyroid cartilage during inspiration. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, severe chronic hyperinflation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Campbell sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not manipulate the trachea or use this sign to quantify COPD. Base escalation for Campbell sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
