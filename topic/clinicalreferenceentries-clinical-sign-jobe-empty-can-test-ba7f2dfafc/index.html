Definition

Jobe empty-can test is a bedside resisted-shoulder maneuver. It is a clue to supraspinatus weakness or pain. A positive finding means pain or weakness is greater on the affected side.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Jobe empty-can test means pain or weakness is greater on the affected side.

Why It Matters

A positive Jobe empty-can test response can signal supraspinatus weakness or pain. The position emphasizes supraspinatus contribution to abduction under load. Jobe empty-can test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • supraspinatus weakness or pain

Assessment Method

Elevate arms in the scapular plane with thumbs down and apply gentle downward resistance.

Positive Finding

Pain or weakness is greater on the affected side.

Mechanism

The position emphasizes supraspinatus contribution to abduction under load.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Jobe empty-can test response raises concern for supraspinatus weakness or pain because the position emphasizes supraspinatus contribution to abduction under load. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Jobe empty-can test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Jobe empty-can test to focus evaluation of supraspinatus weakness or pain and the reviewed alternatives: deltoid weakness, cervical radiculopathy, arthritis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Elevate arms in the scapular plane with thumbs down and apply gentle downward resistance. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Jobe empty-can test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain inhibition can mimic true weakness. Therefore, Jobe empty-can test cannot confirm or exclude supraspinatus weakness or pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • deltoid weakness

  • cervical radiculopathy

  • arthritis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from deltoid weakness, cervical radiculopathy, arthritis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Jobe empty-can test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; full-can may be better tolerated. When performed as described, Jobe empty-can test can adjust concern for supraspinatus weakness or pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Jobe empty-can test: Pain inhibition can mimic true weakness.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Jobe empty-can test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Elevate arms in the scapular plane with thumbs down and apply gentle downward resistance. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Compare with full-can position and avoid excessive resistance.

Nursing Relevance
