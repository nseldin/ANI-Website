Definition

Opsoclonus is an observed ocular motor sign. It is a clue to brainstem-cerebellar network dysfunction, often paraneoplastic or postinfectious. A positive finding means rapid, chaotic, multidirectional saccades occur without an intersaccadic interval.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Opsoclonus means rapid, chaotic, multidirectional saccades occur without an intersaccadic interval.

Why It Matters

The observed Opsoclonus finding can signal brainstem-cerebellar network dysfunction, often paraneoplastic or postinfectious. Loss of inhibitory control in saccadic burst and cerebellar circuits releases continuous multidirectional eye movements. Opsoclonus cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • brainstem-cerebellar network dysfunction, often paraneoplastic or postinfectious

Assessment Method

Observe eye movements in primary gaze and during fixation.

Positive Finding

Rapid, chaotic, multidirectional saccades occur without an intersaccadic interval.

Mechanism

Loss of inhibitory control in saccadic burst and cerebellar circuits releases continuous multidirectional eye movements.

Evidence Limitations

Brief observation may miss intermittent movements and nystagmus can be misclassified. Therefore, Opsoclonus cannot confirm or exclude brainstem-cerebellar network dysfunction, often paraneoplastic or postinfectious by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as ocular flutter, nystagmus, voluntary saccades.

False Negative Situations
