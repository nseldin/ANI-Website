Definition

Hill sign is a blood-pressure comparison sign. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means leg systolic pressure substantially exceeds arm pressure.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hill sign means leg systolic pressure substantially exceeds arm pressure.

Why It Matters

A positive Hill sign response can signal severe aortic regurgitation. Pulse-pressure amplification in lower-extremity arteries is exaggerated by the large stroke volume and rapid runoff. Hill sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Measure systolic pressure in an arm and leg using appropriate cuff sizes and technique.

Positive Finding

Leg systolic pressure substantially exceeds arm pressure.

Mechanism

Pulse-pressure amplification in lower-extremity arteries is exaggerated by the large stroke volume and rapid runoff.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Hill sign response raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation because pulse-pressure amplification in lower-extremity arteries is exaggerated by the large stroke volume and rapid runoff. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hill sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hill sign to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: coarctation, arterial calcification, measurement error. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Measure systolic pressure in an arm and leg using appropriate cuff sizes and technique. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hill sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Different cuffs, positions, arterial stiffness, and PAD make numeric thresholds unreliable. Therefore, Hill sign cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • coarctation

  • arterial calcification

  • measurement error

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from coarctation, arterial calcification, measurement error, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Hill sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; thresholds vary and modern imaging is preferred. Hill sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Hill sign: Different cuffs, positions, arterial stiffness, and PAD make numeric thresholds unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Hill sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Measure systolic pressure in an arm and leg using appropriate cuff sizes and technique. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never compare values obtained with mismatched cuff size or technique.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Hill sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Leg systolic pressure substantially exceeds arm pressure. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Measure systolic pressure in an arm and leg using appropriate cuff sizes and technique. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, severe aortic regurgitation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Hill sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Never compare values obtained with mismatched cuff size or technique. Base escalation for Hill sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
