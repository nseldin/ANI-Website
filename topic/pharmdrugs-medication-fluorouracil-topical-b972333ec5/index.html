Route Specific Card

true

Class

Topical fluoropyrimidine antimetabolite; thymidylate-synthase and RNA-disrupting field therapy

Used To Treat

Multiple actinic keratoses; selected 5% products also treat superficial basal-cell carcinoma when conventional treatment is impractical and diagnosis/follow-up are assured.

Description

Topical fluorouracil is a field-directed antimetabolite that preferentially kills rapidly dividing dysplastic keratinocytes by inhibiting thymidylate synthase, depleting thymidine for DNA synthesis, and disrupting RNA through FUTP incorporation. The treatment field predictably progresses through erythema, inflammation, erosion and re-epithelialization. This is not cosmetic irritation: it is the visible consequence of killing abnormal cells, with strict pregnancy and DPD-deficiency safety rules.

Mechanism

FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with thymidylate synthase and reduced folate, blocking dUMP-to-dTMP conversion and creating thymineless DNA stress. FUTP misincorporation disrupts RNA processing/function; FdUTP can also enter DNA. Sun-damaged dysplastic keratinocytes cycle more rapidly and accumulate greater injury than surrounding cells. Systemic absorption is usually limited but rises over inflamed/eroded or occluded fields, and absent DPD catabolism can make small exposure dangerous.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy because fetal harm and miscarriage can occur, and contraindicated in dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase deficiency. Stop and urgently assess severe mucositis, diarrhea, vomiting, fever, neurologic symptoms or cytopenic/infectious signs because rare systemic fluoropyrimidine toxicity is possible.

Adverse Effects

  • Expected erythema, burning, tenderness, erosion, crusting, photosensitivity and delayed healing

  • Ulceration, infection, scarring or persistent pigment change

  • Rare systemic mucositis, diarrhea, cytopenias, neurotoxicity and cardiotoxicity

  • Ocular or mucosal injury after accidental transfer

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy or potential pregnancy without strict avoidance

  • Known DPD deficiency

  • Serious fluorouracil hypersensitivity

  • Avoid ocular/mucosal use, unconfirmed lesions and uncontrolled application to damaged/occluded large fields

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm lesion/field, strength, schedule and expected inflammatory endpoint; use gloves/applicator and wash hands to prevent transfer.

  • Provide sun protection, wound-care instructions, infection red flags and follow-up after healing to identify residual cancer.

Interactions

  • Occlusion and barrier disruption increase absorption.

  • Brivudine, sorivudine and other potent DPD inhibitors can cause catastrophic fluoropyrimidine toxicity.

  • Other irritants, peels, retinoids and UV exposure amplify dermatitis.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy assessment and DPD history before treatment

  • CBC, metabolic panel and systemic-toxicity evaluation if severe symptoms occur

  • Biopsy/pathology and serial examination for persistent or suspicious lesions

Nclex Traps

  • Topical 5-FU is still fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy at the cellular level.

  • Field inflammation is expected, but diarrhea, mucositis, fever or confusion suggests systemic toxicity.

  • Accessible isolated BCC is generally treated surgically; topical use is for selected superficial lesions when conventional methods are impractical.
