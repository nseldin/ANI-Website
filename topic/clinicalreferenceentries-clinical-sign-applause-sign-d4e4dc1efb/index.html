Definition

Applause sign is a bedside motor-program sign. It is a clue to progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction. A positive finding means the patient claps more than three times despite understanding the instruction.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Applause sign means the patient claps more than three times despite understanding the instruction.

Why It Matters

The observed Applause sign finding can signal progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction. Impaired frontal response inhibition prevents termination of a learned motor sequence. Applause sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to clap exactly three times after demonstration.

Positive Finding

The patient claps more than three times despite understanding the instruction.

Mechanism

Impaired frontal response inhibition prevents termination of a learned motor sequence.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Applause sign finding raises concern for progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction because impaired frontal response inhibition prevents termination of a learned motor sequence. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Applause sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Applause sign to focus evaluation of progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: aphasia, delirium, poor instruction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to clap exactly three times after demonstration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Applause sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Education, comprehension, impulsivity, and hearing affect performance. Therefore, Applause sign cannot confirm or exclude progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • aphasia

  • delirium

  • poor instruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as aphasia, delirium, poor instruction.

False Negative Situations

Applause sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct; not specific for progressive supranuclear palsy. When performed as described, Applause sign can adjust concern for progressive supranuclear palsy or frontal dysfunction, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Applause sign: Education, comprehension, impulsivity, and hearing affect performance.

Safety Precautions

Observe Applause sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to clap exactly three times after demonstration. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Demonstrate clearly and do not diagnose PSP from perseveration alone.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
