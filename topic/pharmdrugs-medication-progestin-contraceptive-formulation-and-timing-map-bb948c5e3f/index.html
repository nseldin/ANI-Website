Used To Treat

Prevention of pregnancy through injectable DMPA, etonogestrel implant, levonorgestrel emergency or intrauterine products, and daily norethindrone progestin-only pills. Some formulations also treat heavy bleeding, endometriosis-associated pain, or other gynecologic conditions.

Description

All four activate progesterone receptors, but the formulation determines which mechanism dominates and how forgiving the method is. DMPA creates a long systemic depot that reliably suppresses ovulation for months but delays drug clearance and fertility return. The etonogestrel implant continuously suppresses the LH surge and thickens cervical mucus for up to five years under the current U.S. label. A levonorgestrel IUD acts mainly inside the uterus by thickening mucus and suppressing the endometrium, while emergency levonorgestrel works mainly by delaying or inhibiting ovulation before the LH surge and does not end an implanted pregnancy. Low-dose norethindrone pills have a shorter forgiveness window because cervical-mucus and systemic levels fall quickly when doses are late.

Mechanism

Progestins bind intracellular progesterone receptors, and the receptor-ligand complex changes transcription in hypothalamic, pituitary, cervical, endometrial, tubal, and breast tissue. Sufficient sustained systemic exposure slows GnRH pulse frequency, blunts FSH/LH, and prevents the midcycle LH surge. Cervical glands produce scant viscous mucus that impedes sperm penetration, while endometrial glands become atrophic and less proliferative. DMPA's crystal depot releases slowly and suppresses estradiol enough to reduce bone formation and increase resorption. The implant maintains systemic etonogestrel without daily adherence. The LNG-IUD produces high local endometrial exposure with lower systemic levels. Norethindrone's low oral dose and short exposure make clock-time adherence clinically important.

Boxed Warning

DMPA carries a boxed warning for significant bone-mineral-density loss that increases with duration and may not be completely reversible; use longer than two years is not recommended unless other methods are inadequate. Current 2026 NEXPLANON labeling carries a boxed warning for complications from improper insertion and removal and requires trained clinicians under a REMS; deep or intravascular migration can require surgery. Levonorgestrel and norethindrone products do not share those boxes, but ectopic pregnancy must be considered if pregnancy occurs, CYP3A inducers can reduce systemic progestin effectiveness, and no method protects against sexually transmitted infection.

Adverse Effects

  • Irregular bleeding or amenorrhea, headache, breast symptoms, acne, mood change, weight change, and abdominal or pelvic discomfort

  • Bone loss with DMPA, implant nerve/vascular injury or migration, ectopic pregnancy if method failure occurs, thrombosis in susceptible contexts, liver disease, or delayed recognition of pregnancy

Contraindications

  • Current breast cancer or another progestin-sensitive malignancy, unexplained uterine bleeding, significant active liver disease, pregnancy, and product-specific thrombotic or device contraindications.

  • For an IUD, exclude current pelvic infection, uterine distortion, and pregnancy; for an implant, confirm placement and palpability; for DMPA, reassess long-term bone risk.

Nursing Essentials

  • Name the product and duration, not just the hormone. Levonorgestrel emergency pill, IUD, and combination pill have different doses, sites, timing, and counseling.

  • Teach expected bleeding changes, when backup contraception is needed, late-dose rules, warning symptoms, expiration/removal date, and how quickly fertility is expected to return.

Interactions

  • CYP3A inducers such as rifampin, selected antiseizure drugs, and St. John's wort can lower systemic progestin exposure; the magnitude and alternative plan depend on formulation.

  • Ulipristal emergency contraception interacts functionally with progestins, so timing of starting or resuming hormonal contraception follows current CDC guidance.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy assessment and indication-specific blood pressure, liver, bone, bleeding, or STI evaluation; routine broad laboratory screening is not required for every method

  • Bleeding pattern, injection or replacement schedule, implant palpability, IUD strings and symptoms, medication inducers, mood, bone risk, and pregnancy warning signs

Nclex Traps

  • Emergency levonorgestrel delays ovulation; it is not an abortifacient and will not disrupt an implanted pregnancy.

  • DMPA's delayed return to fertility reflects slow depot clearance, not permanent infertility. The implant and IUD usually permit rapid fertility return after removal.
