Definition

Plaque means a broad, palpable skin lesion that is raised or distinctly thickened, commonly more than one centimeter across. It may form when multiple papules merge.

Plain Language

A plaque is a wider plate-like area that you can feel, even when it is not dramatically raised.

Why It Matters

Plaque shape, surface, distribution, duration, and symptoms help clinicians distinguish causes such as inflammatory disease, infection, metabolic change, medication reaction, and skin cancer; the word plaque alone is not a diagnosis.

Associated Conditions

  • epidermal or dermal thickening, inflammation, infiltration, or coalescence of smaller papules into a broader palpable lesion

Assessment Method

Inspect in neutral light and from the side, palpate the surface and edge, and document size, elevation or thickening, border, shape, scale, color, tenderness, distribution, symmetry, symptoms, evolution, medications, and systemic findings.

Positive Finding

A circumscribed broad palpable area, commonly more than one centimeter across, is elevated or distinctly thickened and may be flat topped, rounded, scaly, smooth, or formed by merged papules.

Mechanism

Expansion of epidermal or dermal tissue, inflammation, edema, or multiple adjoining papules creates a broader plate-like area that can be felt.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Plaque finding raises concern for epidermal or dermal thickening, inflammation, infiltration, or coalescence of smaller papules into a broader palpable lesion because expansion of epidermal or dermal tissue, inflammation, edema, or multiple adjoining papules creates a broader plate-like area that can be felt. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Plaque does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Size thresholds overlap, some plaques are thickened without obvious elevation, and the term does not specify cause, chronicity, infectiousness, or malignancy risk. Therefore, Plaque cannot confirm or exclude epidermal or dermal thickening, inflammation, infiltration, or coalescence of smaller papules into a broader palpable lesion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as patch, coalescent papules, nodule, scar, localized edema.

False Negative Situations

Plaque may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of epidermal or dermal thickening, inflammation, infiltration, or coalescence of smaller papules into a broader palpable lesion.
