Definition

Irish node is a palpated lymph-node sign. It is a clue to upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy. A positive finding means a hard enlarged left axillary node is found without local explanation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Irish node means a hard enlarged left axillary node is found without local explanation.

Why It Matters

The observed Irish node finding can signal upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy. Lymphatic spread from abdominal malignancy can reach axillary nodes through unusual channels. Irish node cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy

Assessment Method

Palpate the left axilla.

Positive Finding

A hard enlarged left axillary node is found without local explanation.

Mechanism

Lymphatic spread from abdominal malignancy can reach axillary nodes through unusual channels.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Irish node finding raises concern for upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy because lymphatic spread from abdominal malignancy can reach axillary nodes through unusual channels. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Irish node does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Irish node to focus evaluation of upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy and the reviewed alternatives: breast malignancy, skin infection, reactive node. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate the left axilla. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Irish node as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The association is rare and local breast or arm causes are more common. Therefore, Irish node cannot confirm or exclude upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • breast malignancy

  • skin infection

  • reactive node

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as breast malignancy, skin infection, reactive node.

False Negative Situations

Irish node may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of upper gastrointestinal or other abdominal malignancy.

Modern Status
