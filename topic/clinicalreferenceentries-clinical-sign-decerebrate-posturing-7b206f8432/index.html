Definition

Decerebrate posturing is an observed motor sign. It is a clue to severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus. A positive finding means arms extend and internally rotate while legs extend and plantar-flex.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Decerebrate posturing means arms extend and internally rotate while legs extend and plantar-flex.

Why It Matters

The observed Decerebrate posturing finding can signal severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus. Loss of cortical and rubrospinal influence leaves vestibulospinal and pontine reticulospinal extensor drive unopposed. Decerebrate posturing cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus

Assessment Method

Observe spontaneous or stimulus-associated posture in an unresponsive patient.

Positive Finding

Arms extend and internally rotate while legs extend and plantar-flex.

Mechanism

Loss of cortical and rubrospinal influence leaves vestibulospinal and pontine reticulospinal extensor drive unopposed.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Decerebrate posturing finding raises concern for severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus because loss of cortical and rubrospinal influence leaves vestibulospinal and pontine reticulospinal extensor drive unopposed. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Decerebrate posturing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Decerebrate posturing to focus evaluation of severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus and the reviewed alternatives: decorticate posturing, tonic seizure, opisthotonus. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe spontaneous or stimulus-associated posture in an unresponsive patient. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Decerebrate posturing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Drug effects, spinal injury, and seizures can change posture. Therefore, Decerebrate posturing cannot confirm or exclude severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • decorticate posturing

  • tonic seizure

  • opisthotonus

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as decorticate posturing, tonic seizure, opisthotonus.

False Negative Situations

Decerebrate posturing may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe brainstem injury at or below the red nucleus.
