Definition

Nagayama spots is an observed oral-mucosal sign. It is a clue to roseola infantum. A positive finding means small erythematous papules occur at the soft-palate and uvular junction.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Nagayama spots means small erythematous papules occur at the soft-palate and uvular junction.

Why It Matters

The observed Nagayama spots finding can signal roseola infantum. HHV-6 or HHV-7 mucosal inflammation produces a transient enanthem. Nagayama spots cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • roseola infantum

Assessment Method

Inspect the soft palate and uvula in a child with compatible fever history.

Positive Finding

Small erythematous papules occur at the soft-palate and uvular junction.

Mechanism

HHV-6 or HHV-7 mucosal inflammation produces a transient enanthem.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Nagayama spots finding raises concern for roseola infantum because hHV-6 or HHV-7 mucosal inflammation produces a transient enanthem. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nagayama spots does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Nagayama spots to focus evaluation of roseola infantum and the reviewed alternatives: Forchheimer spots, herpangina, trauma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the soft palate and uvula in a child with compatible fever history. Evidence Limitations

They are subtle, transient, and not specific. Therefore, Nagayama spots cannot confirm or exclude roseola infantum by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Forchheimer spots, herpangina, trauma.

False Negative Situations

Nagayama spots may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of roseola infantum.
