Used To Treat

Uncomplicated or severe malaria, relapse prevention, and selected prophylaxis. Drug choice depends on severe versus uncomplicated disease, Plasmodium species, where infection was acquired, resistance, pregnancy, age/weight, prior prophylaxis, oral tolerance, QT risk, and quantitative G6PD activity.

Description

Malaria drugs make sense only when attached to parasite stage. Blood-stage parasites cause fever, hemolysis, microvascular injury, and severe disease, so rapidly active blood schizonticides are urgent. IV artesunate is preferred for severe malaria because dihydroartemisinin rapidly damages circulating parasites, but its short exposure does not complete eradication; every patient then needs a full oral follow-on regimen. Artemether-lumefantrine and atovaquone-proguanil combine fast and slower complementary targets for uncomplicated disease. Quinine requires a partner such as doxycycline, tetracycline, or clindamycin. P. vivax and P. ovale can leave dormant liver hypnozoites, so blood-stage cure alone permits relapse; primaquine or tafenoquine supplies radical cure after quantitative G6PD testing.

Mechanism

Artesunate and artemether contain an endoperoxide bridge activated by parasite iron and heme, generating reactive intermediates that alkylate proteins, lipids, and membranes. Lumefantrine and quinine concentrate in the acidic food vacuole and disrupt detoxification of heme released from hemoglobin digestion. Atovaquone inhibits the parasite mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex, collapsing membrane potential; proguanil's metabolite cycloguanil inhibits parasite dihydrofolate reductase and proguanil also enhances mitochondrial collapse. Primaquine and tafenoquine form oxidative metabolites that injure liver hypnozoites and gametocytes through redox and mitochondrial stress. The same oxidant chemistry can overwhelm G6PD-deficient erythrocytes, which cannot regenerate enough reduced glutathione to protect hemoglobin and membrane proteins.

Boxed Warning

Warnings are drug-specific. Quinine has a boxed warning against treatment or prevention of nocturnal leg cramps because potentially fatal thrombocytopenia, hemolytic-uremic syndrome/thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, QT dysrhythmia, and hypersensitivity outweigh an unproven benefit. Primaquine and tafenoquine can cause life-threatening hemolysis in G6PD deficiency and must not be given without appropriate testing; tafenoquine also has pregnancy and psychosis-related restrictions. Artesunate can cause delayed post-artemisinin hemolytic anemia after parasites have cleared, so follow-up blood counts are required.

Adverse Effects

  • Artemisinin regimens: GI symptoms, dizziness, QT concerns, and delayed hemolysis after IV artesunate

  • Quinine: cinchonism, hypoglycemia, QT prolongation, thrombocytopenia and hemolysis; 8-aminoquinolines: hemolysis and methemoglobinemia

Contraindications

  • Do not delay IV artesunate for severe malaria while waiting for species confirmation; severe criteria reflect organ threat.

  • Do not use primaquine or tafenoquine until quantitative G6PD activity is known, and avoid tafenoquine in pregnancy, breastfeeding when infant G6PD status is unknown, and history of psychotic disorder.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain thick and thin smears, quantify parasitemia, repeat counts in severe disease, assess organ dysfunction, and involve malaria expertise early.

  • After IV artesunate, verify both the full oral follow-on regimen and weekly hemolysis surveillance for four weeks; clinical improvement after three IV doses is not a completed course.

Interactions

  • Multiple QT-prolonging drugs increase risk with quinine or lumefantrine. CYP3A4 modifiers can alter lumefantrine or quinine exposure.

  • Atovaquone-proguanil absorption improves with food or a milky drink; rifampin/rifabutin can reduce atovaquone exposure. Antioxidant or marrow-toxic contexts complicate 8-aminoquinoline monitoring.

Key Labs

  • Serial parasite density, CBC, glucose, creatinine, liver tests, lactate/acid-base status, and ECG when QT-active therapy or severe disease warrants it

  • Quantitative G6PD before primaquine/tafenoquine; post-artesunate hemoglobin, reticulocyte count, bilirubin, LDH, and haptoglobin when hemolysis is assessed

Nclex Traps

  • IV artesunate rapidly stabilizes severe malaria but must be followed by a complete oral regimen.

  • Blood-stage treatment does not eradicate P. vivax or P. ovale hypnozoites; radical cure is a separate G6PD-dependent decision.
