Class Example Names

  • Tranexamic Acid

Used To Treat

Product- and protocol-specific bleeding states driven by excessive fibrinolysis; labeled uses differ from emergency, trauma, obstetric, surgical, dental, and menstrual protocols.

Description

Tranexamic acid and aminocaproic acid are synthetic lysine analogs that stabilize formed fibrin rather than creating clotting factors. Tranexamic acid has greater plasminogen affinity and is available in distinct IV and oral products. The benefit-risk balance depends heavily on indication, timing, renal clearance, thrombosis risk, dose, and route.

Mechanism

Both occupy lysine-binding sites on plasminogen and plasmin, reducing docking to lysine residues exposed on fibrin. This slows conversion and action of fibrin-bound plasmin and protects the clot from premature enzymatic breakdown. They do not reverse heparin, warfarin, or a missing coagulation factor and do not replace surgical source control.

Boxed Warning

Current CYKLOKAPRON injection has a boxed route-error warning: accidental intrathecal/neuraxial administration has caused seizures, cardiac arrhythmias, permanent injury, and death; it is IV only. Additional concerns include thrombosis, seizures at high exposure, visual disturbances, hypotension with rapid IV administration, and renal accumulation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify product, indication, timing, route, renal dose, thrombotic history, concurrent prothrombotic drugs and whether bleeding reflects fibrinolysis before administration.

  • Physically separate IV tranexamic acid from neuraxial medications, trace every line, read the vial and route aloud, and never rely on similar ampule appearance.

Key Labs

  • CBC/hemoglobin, coagulation and fibrinogen studies as clinically indicated, renal function, bleeding trend, thrombosis assessment, and visual or neurologic symptoms during prolonged/high-dose treatment.

Nclex Traps

  • Tranexamic acid preserves fibrin; it is not a general procoagulant and does not supply fibrinogen.

  • The current boxed warning is specifically about catastrophic wrong-route neuraxial administration, not merely generic thrombosis risk.
