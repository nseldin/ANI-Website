Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe inflammatory arthritis, acute gout, bursitis and tendinitis according to formulation; protocol-based short-course tocolysis in selected earlier preterm labor; and IV indomethacin formulations for patent ductus arteriosus closure in selected preterm infants.

Mechanism

Indomethacin reversibly occupies the arachidonic-acid channel of cyclooxygenase-1 and cyclooxygenase-2, preventing conversion of arachidonic acid to PGH2, the precursor of prostaglandins, prostacyclin, and thromboxane. Lower PGE2/PGF2-alpha reduces inflammatory nociceptor sensitization, vasodilation, fever, myometrial calcium signaling, gap-junction activity, and cervical matrix remodeling. Loss of gastric COX-1 prostaglandins reduces mucus, bicarbonate, and mucosal perfusion, explaining ulcer and bleeding risk. Loss of renal prostaglandins constricts afferent arterioles when perfusion depends on vasodilatory support, causing sodium retention, hypertension, and kidney injury. In the fetus, lower renal perfusion reduces urine-derived amniotic fluid, and lower PGE2 constricts the ductus arteriosus.

Boxed Warning

Indomethacin and other non-aspirin NSAIDs carry boxed warnings for serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, and serious GI bleeding, ulceration, and perforation that can occur without warning and can be fatal. It is contraindicated around CABG surgery. In pregnancy, use at about 20 weeks or later can cause fetal renal dysfunction and oligohydramnios; if treatment must continue beyond 48 hours, ultrasound monitoring may be needed. Avoid around 30 weeks or later because premature ductus arteriosus closure can cause fetal right-heart strain and pulmonary hypertension.

Contraindications

  • NSAID or aspirin hypersensitivity, aspirin-sensitive asthma, active major GI bleeding, CABG perioperative pain, and product-specific renal or hepatic restrictions.

  • For tocolysis, avoid when gestational age, fetal fluid/ductal status, maternal renal/GI/bleeding risk, infection, or a reason for delivery makes prostaglandin suppression unsafe.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask why this NSAID was selected and document the stop date. Obstetric use should be explicitly time- and gestation-limited rather than left as an open-ended PRN order.

  • Monitor GI bleeding, blood pressure, edema, urine output, creatinine, and neurologic effects. During pregnancy, assess amniotic fluid and fetal status according to duration and protocol.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, SSRIs/SNRIs, corticosteroids, alcohol, and other NSAIDs increase GI bleeding; ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and diuretics increase renal risk and may lose effect.

  • Lithium and methotrexate exposure can rise; cyclosporine and other nephrotoxins compound kidney injury.

Key Labs

  • CBC, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, liver tests and blood pressure when risk or duration warrants; stool or endoscopic evaluation for suspected bleeding

  • Pain and inflammation response, GI symptoms, edema and urine output; in obstetric use, contractions, cervical change, amniotic-fluid volume and fetal ductal assessment when indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Indomethacin can intentionally close a neonatal PDA yet dangerously constrict a fetal ductus before birth because prostaglandin dependence is the same mechanism in different contexts.

  • Oligohydramnios after NSAID exposure can reflect reduced fetal urine production from renal hypoperfusion, not merely maternal dehydration.

Description

Indomethacin is a potent nonselective NSAID that blocks prostaglandin synthesis. In joints it reduces inflammatory pain, swelling, and fever. In the uterus it lowers PGE2 and PGF2-alpha, reducing contractions and cervical signaling, so specialists may use a short course for selected preterm labor. The same prostaglandin suppression can reduce fetal renal blood flow and amniotic fluid and can constrict the fetal ductus arteriosus, which is why gestational age and duration sharply limit obstetric use. Those fetal effects are the mirror image of neonatal PDA treatment, where intentional ductal constriction is the goal. Indomethacin is therefore not a generic pain pill in pregnancy and not a harmless long-term tocolytic.

Adverse Effects

  • Dyspepsia, nausea, abdominal pain, headache, dizziness, somnolence, edema, hypertension, and reversible renal dysfunction

  • GI hemorrhage or perforation, MI or stroke, acute kidney injury, hyperkalemia, hepatotoxicity, severe skin reaction, aseptic meningitis, fetal oligohydramnios or ductal closure, and neonatal renal injury
