Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, panic disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, social anxiety disorder, and premenstrual dysphoric disorder.

Mechanism

Sertraline binds SERT and prevents presynaptic serotonin reuptake, increasing serotonin available to stimulate postsynaptic receptors. Immediate transporter blockade is followed by gradual autoreceptor desensitization, altered stress-network signaling, gene expression and neuroplasticity, which separates early adverse effects from later therapeutic benefit. Serotonin also regulates enteric secretion and motility, explaining early loose stool/nausea. Platelets depend on SERT to acquire serotonin for aggregation; blockade depletes platelet serotonin over time, explaining additive bleeding risk with NSAIDs, antiplatelets or anticoagulants.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Screen for bipolar disorder before treatment because unopposed antidepressant therapy can precipitate mania or mixed activation in a susceptible patient.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent or recent MAOI treatment and serotonergic linezolid/methylene blue outside emergency guidance

  • Pimozide coadministration

  • Sertraline oral concentrate with disulfiram because that formulation contains alcohol

Nursing Essentials

  • Start low and titrate when panic/anxiety is prominent because early serotonergic activation can transiently worsen jitteriness before adaptive benefit develops.

  • Ask directly about stool frequency, sodium symptoms, bruising/bleeding and sexual effects; these predictable mechanisms often determine adherence.

Interactions

  • MAOIs and other serotonergic agents add serotonin-toxicity risk.

  • NSAIDs, aspirin, antiplatelets and anticoagulants add bleeding risk because platelet serotonin is reduced.

  • Diuretics increase hyponatremia risk; protein-bound/CYP interactions and QT-risk combinations deserve patient-specific review.

Key Labs

  • Sodium in high-risk or symptomatic patients

  • Bleeding assessment and INR/anticoagulant monitoring when applicable

  • Mood, suicidality, mania, GI tolerance, sexual function and weight

Nclex Traps

  • New diarrhea early in treatment can be serotonergic, but fever, clonus, agitation and autonomic instability suggest serotonin syndrome rather than a routine GI effect.

  • SSRIs can increase bleeding without lowering platelet count.

  • Do not use sertraline concentrate with disulfiram; tablets do not contain the same alcohol vehicle.

Description

Sertraline is an SSRI that blocks the serotonin transporter (SERT), increasing extracellular serotonin to treat depression, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety and PMDD. Repeated treatment remodels feedback receptors and mood/fear circuits over weeks. Its first-screen clinical fingerprint is broad anxiety-spectrum usefulness, relatively frequent nausea or diarrhea during initiation, sexual dysfunction, and the class risks of activation, serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia and bleeding.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, dyspepsia, sweating, tremor, insomnia or somnolence

  • Sexual dysfunction, reduced libido and delayed orgasm/ejaculation

  • Serotonin syndrome, SIADH/hyponatremia, abnormal bleeding and discontinuation symptoms

  • Activation, akathisia-like restlessness, mania/hypomania, seizures and rare angle-closure glaucoma
