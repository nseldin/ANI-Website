Used To Treat

Apremilast treats label-specific psoriatic arthritis, plaque psoriasis, and oral ulcers of Behcet disease, including selected pediatric uses. It is an oral small molecule rather than an antibody. Roflumilast also inhibits PDE4 but is a different molecule with COPD or dermatologic product-specific roles and cannot substitute for apremilast.

Description

PDE4 normally terminates cyclic-AMP signals inside inflammatory cells. Apremilast inhibits PDE4, allowing cyclic AMP to persist and shift transcription away from several proinflammatory mediators while increasing selected anti-inflammatory signaling. The effect is broader than blocking one cytokine but generally less immunosuppressive than many biologics or JAK inhibitors. Because PDE4 and cyclic AMP also regulate intestinal secretion, motility, appetite, and neural signaling, early nausea and diarrhea, weight loss, and mood concerns arise from the same intracellular pathway that produces benefit.

Mechanism

PDE4 hydrolyzes cyclic AMP to AMP in monocytes, dendritic cells, T cells, keratinocytes, and other cells. Apremilast competitively inhibits PDE4, raises intracellular cyclic AMP, activates protein kinase A, and changes CREB and NF-kappaB-regulated transcription. Downstream effects include reduced TNF-alpha, IL-23, interferon-gamma, and selected chemokines with increased IL-10 in experimental systems; the exact contribution of each mediator to clinical response is not fully established. Strong CYP3A4 induction lowers apremilast exposure, while severe renal impairment reduces clearance and requires dose-frequency adjustment. Titration reduces the abrupt gastrointestinal cyclic-AMP effect.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting can cause dehydration or hypotension, especially in older adults or patients taking volume-lowering drugs. Depression and suicidal thoughts or behavior require active screening rather than waiting for spontaneous disclosure. Monitor weight; clinically important unexplained loss warrants evaluation and possible discontinuation. Strong enzyme inducers can make treatment ineffective.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, headache, upper-respiratory symptoms, and reduced appetite or weight

  • Depression, suicidal thinking or behavior, hypersensitivity, angioedema, and dehydration-related hypotension or kidney injury

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity; clarify significant depression or suicidality, low body weight, severe renal impairment, dehydration vulnerability, and strong inducer use.

  • It is not an acute analgesic and should not be confused with roflumilast simply because both inhibit PDE4.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use the starter titration, monitor stool/vomiting burden, hydration, blood pressure, renal context, weight, appetite, mood, sleep, and suicidal thoughts.

  • Assess skin, joint, enthesis, and oral-ulcer outcomes separately and set expectations that benefit is gradual rather than immediate.

Interactions

  • Rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, and other strong CYP induction can lower exposure and cause loss of efficacy.

  • Additive gastrointestinal or weight-loss effects matter with other drugs even when no formal metabolic interaction exists.

Key Labs

  • No universal routine laboratory panel is mandated solely by apremilast, but renal function determines dosing and weight/mood are active safety measurements.

  • Disease activity, hydration, blood pressure, stool/vomiting frequency, appetite, and medication reconciliation

Nclex Traps

  • Apremilast is an oral targeted small molecule, not a biologic antibody and not a JAK inhibitor.

  • Titration improves tolerability; it does not prevent depression, clinically important weight loss, or inducer-related treatment failure.
