Class

Anthrax vaccine adsorbed containing protective-antigen proteins

Used To Treat

Active immunization for prevention of disease caused by Bacillus anthracis in adults 18 through 65 years at high pre-exposure risk and, with recommended antibiotics, for postexposure prophylaxis after suspected or confirmed exposure. Antibiotics remain necessary because vaccine immunity develops too slowly to protect against spores that germinate early.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): BioThrax PEP never replaces the full antibiotic course because dormant spores can germinate before protective immunity is established. It must not be confused with the two-dose CYFENDUS schedule or treatment of active disease because the products have different schedules and vaccine cannot reverse established infection or toxin injury.

Mechanism

Protective antigen binds host cells and forms the transport channel used by lethal factor and edema factor. Vaccine-induced antibodies block that binding or pore formation, preventing the enzymatic toxin components from entering cells. Immunity takes time to develop, so antibiotics are required during PEP to kill bacilli that emerge before antibody protection is established. The vaccine does not kill bacteria already causing active disease and cannot directly neutralize all toxin already inside cells.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify BioThrax rather than CYFENDUS, indication, age, route, dose number, lot, and next date because the two anthrax products have different PEP schedules.

  • Screen for prior severe vaccine reaction and observe for anaphylaxis with epinephrine available because repeat hypersensitivity can progress rapidly to airway obstruction or shock.

  • Teach that local pain and swelling do not represent anthrax and that feeling well is the goal of prophylaxis, not evidence that further doses are unnecessary.

Contraindications

  • Severe allergic reaction after a previous dose or to a component is a contraindication to routine subsequent dosing, but emergency exposure decisions require public-health risk assessment.

  • Do not use BioThrax as sole therapy for active anthrax or as a reason to omit antibiotics after exposure because vaccine immunity is delayed and cannot kill bacilli or reverse toxin injury already causing disease.

Interactions

  • Immunosuppressive therapy can reduce antibody response, so timing and additional protection require occupational or public-health guidance.

  • BioThrax and CYFENDUS schedules are not interchangeable, while antibiotics remain an intentional partner rather than an interaction to avoid.

Key Labs

  • No antibody titer is routinely required for standard administration; document exposure classification, vaccine history, lot, route, site, and date.

  • Symptoms trigger anthrax diagnostic cultures, imaging, and organ assessment rather than vaccine-response testing.

Nclex Traps

  • BioThrax is not CYFENDUS.

  • For a healthy vaccinated adult age 18 through 65, antibiotics continue to the later of 42 days after dose one or 14 days after the final dose because dormant aerosolized spores can germinate after early doses; 60 days applies to specified groups when vaccine protection cannot be relied on.

  • Nonaerosol adult cutaneous or ingestion exposure generally uses seven days without vaccine.

Population Risks

Description

BioThrax is anthrax vaccine adsorbed, not an antibiotic and not an anthrax antitoxin. It exposes the immune system primarily to protective antigen so neutralizing antibodies can block assembly and cellular entry of lethal and edema toxins. The pre-exposure series and boosters differ from the postexposure series. For a healthy, nonpregnant vaccinated adult age 18 through 65 after aerosol exposure, CDC 2023 continues antibiotics until 42 days after the first vaccine dose or 14 days after the last vaccine dose, whichever is later. Sixty days applies after aerosol exposure without vaccine and to adults age 66 or older, immunocompromised people, pregnant or lactating patients, and children. The product is distinct from CYFENDUS, which adds a CpG adjuvant and uses a two-dose PEP schedule.

Administration Timing

  • For occupational pre-exposure prevention, follow the complete current primary and booster schedule rather than importing the emergency PEP series.

  • For eligible postexposure adults after aerosol exposure, give the current three-dose series over four weeks with recommended antibiotics; public health determines exact timing and route.

  • In a healthy, nonpregnant vaccinated adult age 18 through 65, continue antibiotics until 42 days after the first vaccine dose or 14 days after the last dose, whichever is later. Use 60 days for no vaccine, age 66 or older, immunocompromise, pregnancy, lactation, or childhood after aerosol exposure.

  • Do not use BioThrax for a healthy adult age 18 through 65 with only a nonaerosol cutaneous or ingestion exposure; CDC generally uses seven days of antibiotics without vaccine, subject to incident exceptions.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site tenderness, pain, erythema, swelling, induration, limited arm motion, headache, fatigue, and myalgia can occur.

  • Serious hypersensitivity is rare; syncope can follow vaccination and requires fall precautions.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Anaphylaxis, severe generalized reaction, or neurologic symptoms after vaccination requires urgent care and reporting.

  • Any anthrax-compatible symptom during PEP requires immediate treatment evaluation because prophylaxis can fail or have started after germination.

Evidence Limitations

  • PEP efficacy cannot be established through controlled human aerosol challenge, so animal protection and immune bridging support the indication.

  • Incident-specific susceptibility and vaccine access can modify recommendations, making current CDC guidance essential.

Regulatory Status

FDA licensed for pre-exposure prevention and, with recommended antibiotics, postexposure prophylaxis in adults 18 through 65 years.
