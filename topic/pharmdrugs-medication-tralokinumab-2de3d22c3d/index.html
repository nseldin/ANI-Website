Class

Human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that neutralizes interleukin-13

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis in adults and adolescents age 12 years and older whose disease is inadequately controlled with topical prescription therapy or when topical therapy is inadvisable.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and angioedema can occur. Treat pre-existing helminths before starting, interrupt if infection fails antihelminthic therapy, and avoid live vaccines. New or worsening conjunctivitis, keratitis, eye pain or visual change requires assessment.

Mechanism

IL-13 normally binds IL-13R-alpha1, recruits IL-4R-alpha, and activates JAK-STAT6 transcription; IL-13R-alpha2 also binds the cytokine and participates in its tissue handling/signaling context. Tralokinumab sequesters IL-13 before receptor engagement, lowering downstream chemokines, IgE-related type-2 activity and barrier-disrupting transcription. The antibody is not broadly cytotoxic, but ocular-surface inflammation, eosinophilia, helminths and hypersensitivity remain target-linked concerns.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give the correct loading and maintenance dose: adults and adolescents use different current regimens, and selected controlled adults may step down to every 4 weeks.

  • Teach injection rotation/storage and track itch, sleep, BSA, eye symptoms, infection and eosinophilic symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to tralokinumab-ldrm or excipients

  • Treat pre-existing helminth infection

  • Avoid live vaccination and clarify severe active ocular symptoms

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines and complete age-appropriate vaccination before treatment.

  • Topical corticosteroids may be continued as prescribed; taper systemic steroids only under supervision.

  • No classic CYP metabolism interaction is expected from the monoclonal antibody.

Key Labs

  • No therapeutic drug level

  • CBC/eosinophils when clinically indicated

  • Skin severity and ocular examination for persistent symptoms

Nclex Traps

  • Tralokinumab neutralizes IL-13 only; it does not block both IL-4 and IL-13 like dupilumab.

  • It is maintenance treatment, not emergency therapy for an allergic reaction.

  • Conjunctivitis is a known treatment-associated problem and deserves active screening.

Description

Tralokinumab-ldrm is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds soluble IL-13. It prevents IL-13 from engaging IL-13R-alpha1 and IL-13R-alpha2, reducing a central type-2 cytokine signal involved in epidermal-barrier dysfunction, itch and inflammation in atopic dermatitis. Unlike dupilumab, it does not block IL-4R-alpha and therefore does not directly block IL-4 signaling; that target distinction is the core educational identity of this card.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, conjunctivitis and keratitis

  • Injection-site reactions and eosinophilia

  • Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and angioedema

  • Herpes viral events reported postmarketing/within class context
