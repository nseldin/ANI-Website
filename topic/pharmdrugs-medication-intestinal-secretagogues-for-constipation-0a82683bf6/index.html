Class Example Names

  • Linaclotide

Used To Treat

IBS with constipation, chronic idiopathic constipation, pediatric functional constipation for age-approved linaclotide use, and opioid-induced constipation for lubiprostone according to product-specific labeling.

Description

Linaclotide and lubiprostone increase intestinal luminal fluid but use different epithelial switches. Linaclotide activates guanylate cyclase-C, raises cGMP, and drives CFTR-mediated chloride/bicarbonate secretion while reducing visceral pain signaling. Lubiprostone directly activates apical ClC-2 chloride channels. Both can improve stool passage, but their ages, indications, meal timing, boxed warnings, and obstruction precautions are not interchangeable.

Mechanism

Electrolyte secretion creates an osmotic gradient that draws water into the lumen, hydrates stool, and supports propulsion. Linaclotide's extracellular cGMP also reduces afferent nociceptor activity, helping IBS-C pain. Lubiprostone acts locally with minimal systemic concentration; its prostaglandin-derived structure does not make it a general systemic prostaglandin agonist.

Boxed Warning

Linaclotide carries a boxed warning for serious dehydration in children younger than 2 years and is contraindicated in that age group. Lubiprostone has no boxed warning but is contraindicated in known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction. For either drug, severe diarrhea, syncope, hypotension or inability to maintain hydration requires interruption and clinical assessment.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal pain/distention, flatulence and nausea

  • Lubiprostone-associated dyspnea, chest tightness, syncope or hypotension

  • Severe pediatric dehydration with linaclotide exposure below the approved age

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected mechanical GI obstruction

  • Linaclotide in children younger than 2 years

  • Avoid or clarify severe active diarrhea, significant dehydration, unexplained obstruction symptoms, or product hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the constipation phenotype, age, indication, obstruction screen, baseline bowel pattern and hydration before giving.

  • Teach the exact administration: linaclotide is generally taken on an empty stomach before a meal; lubiprostone is taken with food and water to reduce nausea.

Interactions

  • Other laxatives can add diarrhea and dehydration.

  • Diphenylheptane opioids such as methadone may reduce lubiprostone effectiveness.

  • Minimal systemic absorption limits CYP interactions, but severe diarrhea can alter absorption of other oral medicines.

Key Labs

  • Stool frequency/consistency, abdominal distention/pain, hydration, orthostatic vitals and weight

  • Sodium, potassium, bicarbonate and renal function when diarrhea is severe or the patient is vulnerable

  • Pregnancy, pediatric age and obstruction assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Linaclotide's pediatric boxed warning is age-specific and current labeling must be checked because approved pediatric indications have expanded.

  • Lubiprostone's food instruction differs from linaclotide's empty-stomach instruction.

  • Secretory diarrhea is an adverse effect, not evidence that a larger dose is needed.
