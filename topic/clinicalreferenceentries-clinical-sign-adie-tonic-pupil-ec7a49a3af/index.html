Definition

Adie tonic pupil is an observed pupillary sign. It is a clue to postganglionic parasympathetic denervation of the iris. A positive finding means one pupil is dilated with poor light response, slow tonic near constriction, and slow redilation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Adie tonic pupil means one pupil is dilated with poor light response, slow tonic near constriction, and slow redilation.

Why It Matters

The observed Adie tonic pupil finding can signal postganglionic parasympathetic denervation of the iris. Ciliary-ganglion injury causes denervation followed by aberrant reinnervation and supersensitivity. Adie tonic pupil cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • postganglionic parasympathetic denervation of the iris

Assessment Method

Compare direct light response, near response, and redilation in dim light.

Positive Finding

Ciliary-ganglion injury causes denervation followed by aberrant reinnervation and supersensitivity.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as third nerve palsy, pharmacologic dilation, traumatic mydriasis.

False Negative Situations
