Definition

Pronator drift is a bedside motor sign. It is a clue to subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness. A positive finding means one arm pronates and drifts downward.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pronator drift means one arm pronates and drifts downward.

Why It Matters

The observed Pronator drift finding can signal subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness. Supinator weakness exceeds pronator weakness in an upper motor neuron lesion, so the affected arm turns inward and falls. Pronator drift cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to extend both arms forward with palms up and eyes closed for about 20 to 30 seconds.

Positive Finding

One arm pronates and drifts downward.

Mechanism

Supinator weakness exceeds pronator weakness in an upper motor neuron lesion, so the affected arm turns inward and falls.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pronator drift finding raises concern for subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness because supinator weakness exceeds pronator weakness in an upper motor neuron lesion, so the affected arm turns inward and falls. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pronator drift does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pronator drift to focus evaluation of subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness and the reviewed alternatives: shoulder injury, cerebellar disease, sensory ataxia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to extend both arms forward with palms up and eyes closed for about 20 to 30 seconds. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pronator drift as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, orthopedic restriction, inattention, and cerebellar drift can mimic or modify the pattern. Therefore, Pronator drift cannot confirm or exclude subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • shoulder injury

  • cerebellar disease

  • sensory ataxia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as shoulder injury, cerebellar disease, sensory ataxia.

False Negative Situations

Pronator drift may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness.

Modern Status

Current high-yield localization adjunct. When performed as described, Pronator drift can adjust concern for subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Pronator drift: Pain, orthopedic restriction, inattention, and cerebellar drift can mimic or modify the pattern.

Safety Precautions

Observe Pronator drift with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to extend both arms forward with palms up and eyes closed for about 20 to 30 seconds. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Watch pronation separately from simple downward drift and protect a weak arm from falling.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Pronator drift observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: One arm pronates and drifts downward. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask the patient to extend both arms forward with palms up and eyes closed for about 20 to 30 seconds. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, subtle contralateral corticospinal weakness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Pronator drift on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
