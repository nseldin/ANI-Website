Used To Treat

Type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes when insulin is needed, gestational diabetes when indicated, diabetic ketoacidosis/HHS protocols, and hyperkalemia shifting therapy with glucose.

Mechanism

Insulin degludec works by activating insulin receptors so glucose moves into cells and potassium shifts intracellularly. Onset, peak, and meal timing determine the hypoglycemia risk window.

Boxed Warning

No universal boxed warning for all insulin products; hypoglycemia and medication errors are major high-alert risks.

Contraindications

  • Hypoglycemia

  • hypersensitivity to product

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check glucose, meal status, insulin type/onset/peak, potassium when IV insulin/DKA.

  • Treat hypoglycemia promptly.

  • Independent double-check per policy.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Beta blockers can mask hypoglycemia.

  • Steroids raise glucose.

  • Alcohol increases hypoglycemia risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • blood glucose

  • potassium for IV insulin/DKA

  • A1c

  • Blood glucose, hypoglycemia symptoms, potassium in DKA/hyperkalemia protocols, meal timing, and injection-site rotation.

Nclex Traps

  • In DKA, check potassium before insulin; insulin can crash potassium.

  • Hypoglycemia is the immediate danger. Potassium must be watched closely in DKA treatment.
