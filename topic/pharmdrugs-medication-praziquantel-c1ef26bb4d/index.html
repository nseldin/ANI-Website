Used To Treat

Schistosomiasis and liver-fluke infections under U.S. labeling, with guideline-supported treatment of many tapeworm infections. Species, parasite maturity, tissue location, meal timing, and inflammatory response determine regimen and follow-up.

Mechanism

Praziquantel interacts with parasite membrane calcium handling, including schistosome transient-receptor-potential melastatin channels, and increases calcium permeability. Cytosolic calcium rises, actin-myosin contraction becomes sustained, and the worm detaches or becomes immobile. Tegument membranes develop blebs and vacuoles, exposing normally hidden antigens and allowing antibody, complement, and phagocytic attack. The drug is rapidly metabolized by CYP3A4, so enzyme inducers can make exposure ineffective. Adult worms are more susceptible than immature schistosomula; waiting six to eight weeks after final exposure permits maturation into the drug-sensitive stage. Host immunity affects cure, which explains occasional retreatment even without classic resistance.

Contraindications

  • Ocular cysticercosis and concomitant rifampin; serious hypersensitivity.

  • Assess CNS, ocular, and cardiac parasite location before treatment when exposure or symptoms raise concern.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give tablets with liquid during meals and avoid driving on the treatment day and the following day because dizziness and drowsiness are common.

  • For schistosomiasis, time treatment after worms mature and arrange stool/urine follow-up; lightly infected patients may need repeat treatment because host response and stage affect clearance.

Interactions

  • Rifampin is contraindicated because strong CYP3A induction can nearly eliminate exposure. Carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, and dexamethasone can also lower levels.

  • Cimetidine and some CYP3A inhibitors can increase exposure; chloroquine may lower praziquantel concentrations.

Key Labs

  • Liver function when disease or interactions warrant, pregnancy assessment, ECG in cardiac-risk disease, and CBC/eosinophils as clinically useful

  • Species-directed stool or urine egg examination, CNS/ocular imaging, seizures and intracranial pressure, inflammatory symptoms, and cure testing

Nclex Traps

  • Treatment is delayed after recent schistosome exposure because praziquantel works best against mature adult worms.

  • Worsening neurologic symptoms can come from inflammation around dying parasites, so lesion location must be known.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Inflammatory reactions to dying parasites can cause fever, rash, eosinophilia, neurologic worsening, cerebral edema, seizures, or cardiac complications depending on parasite location. Avoid use for ocular cysticercosis because destruction can cause irreversible eye injury. Hospitalize or closely observe patients with significant CNS or cardiac schistosomiasis when appropriate. Strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampin can reduce praziquantel to ineffective concentrations and are contraindicated.

Description

Praziquantel is a broad trematode and cestode anthelmintic that rapidly increases calcium entry into susceptible worms. Sudden calcium elevation causes tetanic contraction and paralysis, while tegument vacuolization exposes parasite antigens to host antibodies and phagocytes. The host immune response therefore helps clear damaged organisms, and symptoms can temporarily worsen as antigens are released. Praziquantel is highly effective against adult Schistosoma, but immature worms are less susceptible; treatment soon after freshwater exposure may miss developing parasites, which explains the recommended delay and sometimes repeat dosing. In neurocysticercosis, inflammatory killing can worsen edema and seizures; ocular cysticercosis can suffer permanent damage.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness, headache, drowsiness, malaise, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, fever, sweating, pruritus, and urticaria

  • Seizures or neurologic worsening, arrhythmia, severe hypersensitivity, inflammatory organ injury, and treatment failure from enzyme induction
