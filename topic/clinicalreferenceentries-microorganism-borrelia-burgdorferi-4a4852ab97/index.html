Definition

Borrelia burgdorferi is a clinically important bacterium associated with early localized Lyme disease, early disseminated Lyme disease, Lyme carditis and neuroborreliosis, and Lyme arthritis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Borrelia burgdorferi is a Gram-reaction indeterminate spiral with a microaerophile oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, motile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Avoid tick habitat exposure, use repellents and protective clothing, perform tick checks, and remove attached ticks promptly and correctly. The reviewed route description is the reviewed vector-borne transmission route categories. The reviewed incubation estimate is 3 to 30 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Tick inoculation is followed by tissue dissemination and changing outer-surface protein expression, producing stage-dependent skin, neurologic, cardiac, and joint disease. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Borrelia burgdorferi is associated with early localized Lyme disease, early disseminated Lyme disease, Lyme carditis and neuroborreliosis, and Lyme arthritis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Erythema migrans can be a clinical diagnosis; otherwise use recommended two-tier serology with stage-aware interpretation and selective direct testing. A positive result can represent infection, colonization, past exposure, residual material, or contamination depending on the method and source; a negative result can reflect timing, specimen quality, prior therapy, or an untested target.
