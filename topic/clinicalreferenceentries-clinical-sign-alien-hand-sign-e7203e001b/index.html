Definition

Alien hand sign is an observed behavioral-motor sign. It is a clue to callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion. A positive finding means a limb performs involuntary complex actions or conflicts with the other hand.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Alien hand sign means a limb performs involuntary complex actions or conflicts with the other hand.

Why It Matters

The observed Alien hand sign finding can signal callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion. Disconnection between motor planning, interhemispheric integration, and conscious agency releases autonomous action. Alien hand sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion

Assessment Method

Observe for purposeful-appearing limb actions the patient does not experience as self-directed.

Positive Finding

A limb performs involuntary complex actions or conflicts with the other hand.

Mechanism

Disconnection between motor planning, interhemispheric integration, and conscious agency releases autonomous action.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Alien hand sign finding raises concern for callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion because disconnection between motor planning, interhemispheric integration, and conscious agency releases autonomous action. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Alien hand sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Alien hand sign to focus evaluation of callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion and the reviewed alternatives: chorea, utilization behavior, functional movement disorder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe for purposeful-appearing limb actions the patient does not experience as self-directed. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Alien hand sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Dystonia, neglect, and functional movement can be confused with loss of agency. Therefore, Alien hand sign cannot confirm or exclude callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • chorea

  • utilization behavior

  • functional movement disorder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as chorea, utilization behavior, functional movement disorder.

False Negative Situations

Alien hand sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of callosal or frontal-parietal network lesion.
