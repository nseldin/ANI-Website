Class Example Names

  • Lactulose

  • Rifaximin

Used To Treat

Treatment and prevention of recurrent overt hepatic encephalopathy in cirrhosis or portosystemic shunting; lactulose is titrated to clinical response and stool output, and rifaximin is commonly added to reduce recurrence.

Description

Hepatic-encephalopathy therapy targets the intestine because gut nitrogen is a major source of ammonia and other neurotoxins. Lactulose acidifies colonic contents, traps diffusible ammonia as ammonium, changes bacterial nitrogen handling, and increases fecal clearance. Rifaximin remains mainly in the gut and suppresses bacterial transcription, altering ammonia-producing microbial activity. These drugs complement one another; neither repairs failed hepatic detoxification or the portosystemic shunt itself.

Mechanism

Ammonia crosses the blood-brain barrier and is incorporated into astrocyte glutamine; osmotic and signaling consequences contribute to astrocyte dysfunction, altered neurotransmission, and cerebral edema in severe disease. Lowering intestinal ammonia delivery reduces this burden. Clinical management also searches for precipitants such as infection, GI bleeding, constipation, dehydration, hypokalemia, sedatives, renal failure, or excess diuresis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not chase an ammonia number while ignoring the patient. Mental status, airway protection, asterixis, precipitating causes, hydration, electrolytes, stool output, and medication exposure guide urgency. Excess lactulose can cause dehydration and hypokalemia that worsen encephalopathy; rifaximin does not cover invasive bloody diarrhea and can still be associated with C. difficile infection.

Adverse Effects

  • Lactulose: bloating, cramping, flatulence, diarrhea, hypernatremia, hypokalemia and dehydration

  • Rifaximin: nausea, edema, dizziness, fatigue, ascites, ALT elevation, hypersensitivity and C. difficile-associated diarrhea

  • Aspiration or severe diarrhea-related instability in a patient with impaired consciousness

Contraindications

  • Lactulose: requirement for a low-galactose diet and product hypersensitivity; protect the airway when consciousness is impaired

  • Rifaximin: hypersensitivity to rifaximin, another rifamycin, or formulation components

  • Clarify bowel obstruction, severe dehydration, invasive diarrhea, and severe hepatic dysfunction with increased rifaximin exposure

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess level of consciousness, orientation, behavior, asterixis, airway/gag reflex, bowel pattern, intake/output and possible precipitant before focusing on the laboratory value.

  • For chronic lactulose, titrate to the prescribed soft-stool target rather than uncontrolled diarrhea. Confirm adherence to both drugs when recurrent episodes occur.

Interactions

  • Other laxatives can make stool count falsely appear therapeutic without reproducing lactulose's colonic acidification.

  • Cyclosporine markedly increases systemic rifaximin exposure through transporter inhibition.

  • Sedatives, opioids, anticholinergics, diuretics and electrolyte-depleting drugs can worsen the clinical encephalopathy context.

Key Labs

  • Mental status and neurologic examination, stool count/consistency and hydration

  • Sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, BUN/creatinine, glucose, CBC, liver tests and infection/bleeding evaluation

  • Ammonia can support context but should not replace serial clinical assessment

Nclex Traps

  • More diarrhea is not automatically better; dehydration and hypokalemia can worsen encephalopathy.

  • Rifaximin is usually recurrence prevention and is commonly used with lactulose, not as proof that lactulose is unnecessary.

  • A normal or falling ammonia value does not overrule worsening consciousness or loss of airway protection.
