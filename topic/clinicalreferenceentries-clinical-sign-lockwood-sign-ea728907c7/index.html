Definition

Lockwood sign is a palpation sign. It is a clue to chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described. A positive finding means a trickling or gurgling sensation is repeatedly felt.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lockwood sign means a trickling or gurgling sensation is repeatedly felt.

Why It Matters

A positive Lockwood sign response can signal chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described. Local cecal and ileal gas movement was historically attributed to appendiceal irritation. Lockwood sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described

Assessment Method

Palpate the right iliac fossa for repeated flatulent gurgling.

Positive Finding

A trickling or gurgling sensation is repeatedly felt.

Mechanism

Local cecal and ileal gas movement was historically attributed to appendiceal irritation.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Lockwood sign response raises concern for chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described because local cecal and ileal gas movement was historically attributed to appendiceal irritation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lockwood sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lockwood sign to focus evaluation of chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described and the reviewed alternatives: constipation, gastroenteritis, ileocecal disease. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate the right iliac fossa for repeated flatulent gurgling. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lockwood sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal bowel sounds and gas make the sign nonspecific. Therefore, Lockwood sign cannot confirm or exclude chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • constipation

  • gastroenteritis

  • ileocecal disease

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from constipation, gastroenteritis, ileocecal disease, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Lockwood sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Obsolete and unreliable. Lockwood sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude chronic appendiceal irritation, historically described. Apply the reviewed limitation for Lockwood sign: Normal bowel sounds and gas make the sign nonspecific.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Lockwood sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Palpate the right iliac fossa for repeated flatulent gurgling. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not present this historical curiosity as evidence for chronic appendicitis.
