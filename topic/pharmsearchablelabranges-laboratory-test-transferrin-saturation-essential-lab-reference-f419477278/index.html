Range

Transferrin saturation (TSAT) estimates the percentage of circulating iron-binding sites occupied by iron and is commonly calculated as serum iron divided by total iron-binding capacity (TIBC) times 100. One MedlinePlus adult example lists iron saturation of 20%-50% for males and 15%-45% for females, but the performing laboratory's method and interval govern interpretation. Serum iron varies during the day and after iron intake, while inflammation, liver disease, low transferrin, pregnancy, estrogen exposure, recent transfusion, and the collection instructions can change the result; follow the ordered fasting or supplement instructions instead of assuming they are universal. Hemochromatosis evaluation and CKD iron-treatment pathways use persistent TSAT together with ferritin and other setting-, sex-, genotype-, and dialysis-specific criteria, not one universal diagnostic, emergency, or treatment threshold.

Why

A low Transferrin saturation result signals limited circulating iron available for red-cell production, while a persistently high result raises concern for excessive circulating iron; both require ferritin, CBC, and clinical context. Low TSAT is associated with systemic iron deficiency from blood loss, increased demand, low intake, or malabsorption and with inflammation-related iron-restricted erythropoiesis, whereas persistent high TSAT with elevated ferritin can support evaluation for hereditary hemochromatosis, transfusional loading, excess supplementation, or liver disease. Promptly assess fatigue, pallor, exercise intolerance, pica, restless legs, dyspnea, chest pain, dizziness or syncope, menstrual or gastrointestinal bleeding, pregnancy, diet and donation history, bowel or bariatric surgery, infection or inflammation, kidney disease or dialysis, transfusions, iron products, alcohol and liver risk, joint symptoms, skin change, diabetes, and family history. Cause-directed evaluation can include repeat iron studies under the ordered collection conditions, ferritin, CBC with indices and reticulocytes, CRP or another inflammation assessment, kidney and liver tests, investigation for blood loss or malabsorption, and clinician-selected HFE testing or hepatic iron assessment when a persistent overload pattern fits. Escalate immediately for major bleeding, chest pain, syncope, dyspnea at rest, shock, altered consciousness, or rapid deterioration; there is no TSAT-only emergency threshold, and urgency comes from anemia, bleeding, organ injury, and clinical stability. One TSAT result does not diagnose iron deficiency, iron-deficiency anemia, inflammation-related anemia, hemochromatosis, or organ iron overload and does not by itself authorize oral or intravenous iron, phlebotomy, genetic testing, or another treatment.

High Causes

  • Hereditary hemochromatosis when persistent TSAT elevation is paired with ferritin, genotype, and any required hepatic-iron evidence

  • Repeated transfusion or another secondary iron-loading condition

  • Recent iron ingestion or parenteral iron administration affecting the collection

  • Low transferrin or TIBC from inflammation, liver dysfunction, or another protein-related state, which can increase the calculated percentage

  • Hemolysis, specimen issues, assay variation, or acute liver injury requiring confirmation and context

Low Causes

  • Systemic iron deficiency from gastrointestinal or uterine blood loss, pregnancy demand, frequent donation, low intake, or malabsorption

  • Inflammation-related iron-restricted erythropoiesis, including in chronic kidney disease

  • Higher transferrin or TIBC during pregnancy or estrogen exposure

  • Collection timing, recent illness, or laboratory variation affecting serum iron

Critical Concerns

  • Major bleeding, chest pain, syncope, dyspnea at rest, shock, altered consciousness, or rapid deterioration requires emergency assessment regardless of TSAT.

  • Persistent high TSAT with elevated ferritin, abnormal liver findings, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, arthropathy, skin change, or a compatible family history needs timely cause and organ assessment, not automatic phlebotomy.

  • Low TSAT with high or normal ferritin can reflect inflammation-related iron restriction; iron safety and benefit cannot be decided from TSAT alone.

Nursing Considerations

  • Verify the specimen timing and ordered fasting or supplement instructions, document recent oral or intravenous iron and transfusions, and compare results only with the reported method and interval.

  • Trend TSAT with ferritin, CBC and indices, reticulocytes, kidney and liver status, inflammation, symptoms, and the investigated source of blood loss or malabsorption.

  • Do not independently start or stop iron or prepare phlebotomy from one saturation value; CKD, pregnancy, active inflammation, and hemochromatosis use different cause-specific pathways.

Nclex Traps

  • Ferritin estimates stores but also rises with inflammation; TSAT estimates circulating availability, so the pair can be discordant.

  • A low TSAT does not automatically mean depleted total-body stores, and a high TSAT does not automatically diagnose hemochromatosis.

  • A recent iron dose, diurnal serum-iron variation, or low transferrin can distort the percentage; confirm the collection and the whole iron-study pattern.
