Definition

Forchheimer spots is an observed oral-mucosal sign. It is a clue to rubella or other viral enanthem. A positive finding means small red petechial macules appear on the soft palate.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Forchheimer spots means small red petechial macules appear on the soft palate.

Why It Matters

The observed Forchheimer spots finding can signal rubella or other viral enanthem. Viral mucosal inflammation causes focal capillary dilation and leakage. Forchheimer spots cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • rubella or other viral enanthem

Assessment Method

Inspect the soft palate.

Positive Finding

Small red petechial macules appear on the soft palate.

Mechanism

Viral mucosal inflammation causes focal capillary dilation and leakage.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Forchheimer spots finding raises concern for rubella or other viral enanthem because viral mucosal inflammation causes focal capillary dilation and leakage. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Forchheimer spots does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

They are neither sensitive nor specific for rubella. Therefore, Forchheimer spots cannot confirm or exclude rubella or other viral enanthem by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as streptococcal petechiae, trauma, enterovirus.

False Negative Situations
