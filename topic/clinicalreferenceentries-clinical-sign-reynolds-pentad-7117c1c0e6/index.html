Definition

Reynolds pentad is an emergency clinical constellation. It is a clue to severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy. A positive finding means fever, RUQ pain, jaundice, hypotension, and altered mental status coexist.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Reynolds pentad means fever, RUQ pain, jaundice, hypotension, and altered mental status coexist.

Why It Matters

The Reynolds pentad pattern can signal severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy. Biliary sepsis progresses from localized obstruction to systemic vasodilation, hypoperfusion, and cerebral dysfunction. Reynolds pentad cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy

Assessment Method

Assess Charcot triad plus blood pressure and mental status.

Positive Finding

Fever, RUQ pain, jaundice, hypotension, and altered mental status coexist.

Mechanism

Biliary sepsis progresses from localized obstruction to systemic vasodilation, hypoperfusion, and cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical Interpretation

The Reynolds pentad pattern raises concern for severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy because biliary sepsis progresses from localized obstruction to systemic vasodilation, hypoperfusion, and cerebral dysfunction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Reynolds pentad does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Reynolds pentad to focus evaluation of severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy and the reviewed alternatives: septic shock from another source, hepatic encephalopathy, pancreatitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess Charcot triad plus blood pressure and mental status. Evidence Limitations

Absence of the complete pentad does not indicate mild disease. Therefore, Reynolds pentad cannot confirm or exclude severe suppurative cholangitis with shock and encephalopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.
