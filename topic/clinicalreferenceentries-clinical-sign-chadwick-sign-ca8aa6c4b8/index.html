Definition

Chadwick sign is an observed mucosal color sign. It is a clue to early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion. A positive finding means the cervix and vaginal walls appear bluish-purple.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chadwick sign means the cervix and vaginal walls appear bluish-purple.

Why It Matters

The observed Chadwick sign finding can signal early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion. Increased pelvic blood flow and venous congestion alter mucosal color. Chadwick sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion

Assessment Method

Inspect the cervix and vaginal mucosa only during an indicated pelvic examination.

Positive Finding

The cervix and vaginal walls appear bluish-purple.

Mechanism

Increased pelvic blood flow and venous congestion alter mucosal color.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Chadwick sign finding raises concern for early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion because increased pelvic blood flow and venous congestion alter mucosal color. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chadwick sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chadwick sign to focus evaluation of early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion and the reviewed alternatives: cervicitis, pelvic tumor, venous obstruction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the cervix and vaginal mucosa only during an indicated pelvic examination. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chadwick sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Lighting and baseline pigmentation affect appearance and other conditions cause congestion. Therefore, Chadwick sign cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cervicitis

  • pelvic tumor

  • venous obstruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cervicitis, pelvic tumor, venous obstruction.

False Negative Situations

Chadwick sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion.

Modern Status

Historical presumptive pregnancy sign; not diagnostic. Chadwick sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related pelvic vascular congestion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Chadwick sign: Lighting and baseline pigmentation affect appearance and other conditions cause congestion.

Safety Precautions

Observe Chadwick sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect the cervix and vaginal mucosa only during an indicated pelvic examination. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use consent and chaperone; confirm pregnancy with testing rather than mucosal color.
