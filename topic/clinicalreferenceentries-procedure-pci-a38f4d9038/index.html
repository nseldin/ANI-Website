Summary

PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention) is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Percutaneous coronary intervention opens narrowed or blocked coronary arteries, commonly with balloon angioplasty and/or stent placement.

Quick Answer

PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention): Percutaneous coronary intervention opens narrowed or blocked coronary arteries, commonly with balloon angioplasty and/or stent placement. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Percutaneous coronary intervention opens narrowed or blocked coronary arteries, commonly with balloon angioplasty and/or stent placement.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess chest pain, perfusion, allergies, kidney function/contrast risk, anticoagulant/antiplatelet status, baseline pulses, and whether consent and emergency readiness are complete.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Monitor access site bleeding/hematoma, distal pulses, chest pain, rhythm, blood pressure, contrast reaction, kidney function, and prescribed antiplatelet therapy teaching.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • New chest pain, ST changes, hypotension, access-site bleeding, expanding hematoma, absent distal pulse, stroke symptoms, acute dyspnea, or contrast reaction.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • After stent placement, antiplatelet adherence is a safety issue. Stopping therapy early can cause stent thrombosis.
