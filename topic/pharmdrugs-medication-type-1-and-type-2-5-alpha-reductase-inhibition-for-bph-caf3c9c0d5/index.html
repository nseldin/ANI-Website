Used To Treat

Symptomatic BPH with an enlarged prostate to improve symptoms over months and reduce acute retention or surgery risk; indication and dose differ from hair-loss products.

Description

Finasteride and dutasteride shrink androgen-dependent prostate tissue by blocking conversion of testosterone to the more potent androgen dihydrotestosterone. They change the disease process rather than relaxing smooth muscle immediately, so symptom benefit is slow but prostate volume and long-term retention risk can fall. Finasteride primarily inhibits type 2 5-alpha-reductase; dutasteride inhibits type 1 and type 2 and has a much longer terminal half-life.

Mechanism

5-alpha-reductase reduces the testosterone A-ring to form DHT. DHT binds the androgen receptor more avidly and supports prostate epithelial/stromal growth. Enzyme inhibition lowers intraprostatic DHT, triggering gradual tissue involution. PSA falls about 50 percent after several months, so a new on-treatment baseline is required; a confirmed rise from the nadir can be more concerning than a value that appears normal against an untreated reference range.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: These drugs are not prostate-cancer prevention therapy. Labels warn about an observed increase in high-grade prostate cancer diagnoses in prevention trials. Pregnancy exposure to crushed tablets or leaking dutasteride capsules can disrupt external genital development in a male fetus.

Adverse Effects

  • Decreased libido, erectile or ejaculatory dysfunction and reduced semen volume

  • Gynecomastia, breast tenderness and rare male breast cancer reports

  • Depressed mood and persistent sexual symptoms reported postmarketing

  • PSA suppression that can delay cancer recognition if not interpreted correctly

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy exposure and hypersensitivity to 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors

  • Not a rapid rescue for acute urinary retention

  • Evaluate prostate cancer, infection, stricture and neurogenic causes before attributing all symptoms to BPH

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain that alpha-1 blockers improve flow quickly while 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors take months because the gland must physically involute.

  • Document baseline PSA/prostate evaluation and establish the new PSA nadir after treatment.

Interactions

  • Dutasteride exposure rises with chronic strong CYP3A inhibitors.

  • Combination with an alpha-1 blocker can improve symptoms but adds orthostatic risk from the alpha blocker.

  • Do not combine duplicate finasteride/dutasteride products.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and serial PSA interpreted against the on-treatment nadir

  • Prostate size, symptom score, post-void residual and renal consequences of obstruction

  • Liver function and reproductive counseling when clinically relevant

Nclex Traps

  • A lower PSA on therapy does not prove cancer absence.

  • Dutasteride blocks both major isoenzymes and persists for months; finasteride is more type-2 selective.

  • These drugs shrink prostate tissue but do not directly contract the bladder.
