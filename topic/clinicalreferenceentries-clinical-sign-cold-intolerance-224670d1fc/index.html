Definition

Cold intolerance means a person feels excessively cold or needs unusual warming in an environment they previously tolerated. It is a reported symptom and does not automatically mean the core temperature is low.

Plain Language

The person feels much colder than expected in ordinary surroundings.

Why It Matters

Persistent cold intolerance can be a clue to hypothyroidism, anemia, low body mass, malnutrition, poor circulation, or medication effects, but symptoms alone cannot diagnose the cause. A low core temperature with confusion, slow breathing, bradycardia, hypotension, or cold exposure requires urgent hypothermia treatment.

Associated Conditions

  • hypothyroidism, anemia, low body mass or malnutrition, reduced peripheral perfusion, autonomic dysfunction, medication effects, aging, or environmental exposure

Assessment Method

Ask what temperatures trigger symptoms, whether others are comfortable, and about fatigue, weight and skin or hair change, bleeding risk, diet, medications, color change or pain in digits, and duration; measure temperature, perfusion, heart rate, blood pressure, and mental status when clinically indicated.

Positive Finding

The patient feels excessively cold or needs unusual warming in an environment previously tolerated, without necessarily having a low core temperature.

Mechanism

Reduced metabolic heat production, inadequate insulation or nutrition, anemia, peripheral vasoconstriction, autonomic dysfunction, medication effects, or altered thermal perception can reduce cold tolerance.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Cold intolerance symptom raises concern for hypothyroidism, anemia, low body mass or malnutrition, reduced peripheral perfusion, autonomic dysfunction, medication effects, aging, or environmental exposure because reduced metabolic heat production, inadequate insulation or nutrition, anemia, peripheral vasoconstriction, autonomic dysfunction, medication effects, or altered thermal perception can reduce cold tolerance. Evidence Limitations

Thermal comfort varies with environment, clothing, age, body composition, activity, anxiety, circulation, and baseline; symptoms alone cannot measure thyroid function or core temperature. Therefore, Cold intolerance cannot confirm or exclude hypothyroidism, anemia, low body mass or malnutrition, reduced peripheral perfusion, autonomic dysfunction, medication effects, aging, or environmental exposure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • environmental cold exposure

  • Raynaud phenomenon

  • low body mass

  • medication effect

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as environmental cold exposure, Raynaud phenomenon, low body mass, medication effect, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Cold intolerance may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.
