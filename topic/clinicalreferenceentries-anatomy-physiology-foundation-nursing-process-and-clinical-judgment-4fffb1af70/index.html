Quick Answer

The labels differ among curricula, but the core reasoning is continuous: assess, interpret, plan, act, reassess, and revise when the outcome is not achieved.

Sections

  • Label

    Why the sequence matters

    Text

    Assessment supplies the evidence for judgment. Interpretation connects cues into priorities. Planning defines measurable outcomes and interventions. Implementation carries out the safest authorized actions. Evaluation compares observed with expected results and sends the nurse back through the cycle when the plan is not working.

  • Label

    Common terminology variants

    Text

    ADPIE uses Diagnosis, ADOPIE explicitly adds Outcomes Identification, and AAPIE uses Analysis. The 2026 NCLEX-RN Test Plan emphasizes assessment, analysis, planning, implementation, evaluation, and the linked clinical-judgment functions. ANI preserves these variants rather than pretending one classroom acronym is universal.

  • Label

    Emergency exception

    Text

    A complete routine assessment should not delay an immediately lifesaving action. In an airway obstruction, arrest, severe hemorrhage, or another time-critical threat, the nurse acts within emergency protocols while assessment continues.

  • Label

    Documentation and delegation

    Text

    Document relevant cues, judgments, interventions, response, and escalation. Tasks may be delegated within role and policy, but the RN retains accountability for nursing judgment, supervision, and evaluation.

Summary

The nursing process is a recurring, patient-centered method for gathering cues, interpreting needs, setting outcomes, acting safely, and evaluating response.
