Sections

  • Content

    Infectious disease serology measures host antibodies or another defined immune response to an infectious agent rather than detecting the intact organism directly.

    Id

    principle

    Title

    Principle

  • Content

    Serology can support evidence of recent infection, past exposure, immune response, or vaccination when the assay and antibody pattern are appropriate to the clinical question.

    Id

    purpose

    Title

    Purpose

  • Content

    Timing is essential because antibodies require time to develop and may persist after infection. The requested antibody class, antigen target, and need for paired samples vary by disease.

    Id

    specimen-preparation

    Title

    Specimen and preparation

  • Content

    Use the validated screening, supplemental, confirmatory, avidity, or paired-sample algorithm required for the suspected infection; do not substitute one antibody result for the complete algorithm.

    Id

    procedure

    Title

    Method

  • Content

    Antibody class, magnitude, change over time, vaccination history, prior infection, and immune status together determine whether a result supports recent, remote, or uncertain exposure.

    Id

    interpretation

    Title

    Interpretation

  • Content

    Window periods, cross-reactivity, passive antibody, vaccination, immunosuppression, and persistent antibodies can mislead. A positive antibody result does not automatically mean active infection.

    Id

    limitations

    Title

    Limitations

  • Content

    Treat specimens with Standard Precautions and communicate uncertainty clearly when confirmatory or repeat testing is needed.

    Id

    safety

    Title

    Safety

  • Content

    NCBI diagnostic principles distinguish host-response serology from direct organism detection and emphasize timing and confirmatory interpretation.
