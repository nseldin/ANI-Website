Contraindications

  • Pregnancy; ACE inhibitor or ARB coadministration in diabetes; serious hypersensitivity; and pediatric use below two years. Current treatment eligibility starts at age six and at least 50 kg.

  • Avoid cyclosporine or itraconazole and clarify severe renal dysfunction, potassium elevation, volume depletion, or unstable pressure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the true indication is hypertension, expand every RAAS drug on the medication list, and check diabetes before accepting combination therapy.

  • Administer consistently with respect to meals and avoid high-fat meals around dosing because exposure falls; this instruction protects efficacy, not stomach comfort.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine and itraconazole markedly raise aliskiren through P-glycoprotein inhibition and should be avoided; grapefruit can also raise exposure.

  • Furosemide exposure may fall, while NSAIDs, diuretics, potassium products, MRAs, lithium, ACE inhibitors, and ARBs create renal or electrolyte hazards.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, volume, urine output, and pregnancy testing when relevant.

  • No routine drug level exists; interpret blood-pressure response with adherence, meal consistency, P-glycoprotein interactions, and safety labs.

Nclex Traps

  • Aliskiren lowers angiotensin II production; an ARB blocks the receptor while angiotensin II remains present. That distinction is the mechanism.

  • A more upstream target did not produce better outcomes when stacked with ACE inhibitor or ARB in diabetes; pathway completeness is not the same as patient benefit.

Class

Oral direct renin active-site inhibitor antihypertensive

Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric patients at least six years old who weigh at least 50 kg. It is not an outcome-proven substitute for ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy in heart failure, post-MI care, or albuminuric CKD.

Mechanism

Renin is an aspartyl protease released by juxtaglomerular cells when renal perfusion, macula-densa sodium delivery, or sympathetic input signals low effective volume. Aliskiren occupies the renin active site and prevents angiotensinogen from becoming angiotensin I. Downstream angiotensin II and aldosterone fall, reducing AT1-mediated Gq vascular contraction, sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, and remodeling. Plasma renin concentration rises through feedback, but plasma renin activity remains inhibited while exposure is adequate. Efferent dilation can lower GFR in RAAS-dependent kidneys; less aldosterone reduces distal potassium secretion. The drug's approximately 2.5% oral bioavailability, high-fat meal effect, and P-glycoprotein transport explain why administration consistency and cyclosporine or itraconazole interactions matter.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Aliskiren has a current U.S. boxed warning for fetal toxicity. It is also contraindicated with ACE inhibitors or ARBs in diabetes because renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and hypotension increase.

Description

Aliskiren is an oral direct renin inhibitor that blocks the first enzymatic step of the RAAS. It binds renin and prevents angiotensinogen cleavage, reducing angiotensin I, angiotensin II, and aldosterone production. This is a real target-level mechanism, not generic 'cardiovascular medication' language. The result is lower vascular tone and sodium retention, but also lower efferent-arteriole support, less potassium excretion, hypotension, and fetal RAAS toxicity. Aliskiren is labeled for hypertension, not for routine heart-failure or kidney-outcome replacement. Combining it with an ACE inhibitor or ARB in diabetes is contraindicated because trials showed more renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and hypotension. Oral bioavailability is very low and falls further with high-fat meals; consistent meal timing protects predictable exposure. Aliskiren is also a P-glycoprotein substrate, so strong inhibitors can sharply raise concentrations.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, dizziness, cough, rash, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and creatinine increase can occur.

  • Angioedema or anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reactions, acute renal failure, severe hyperkalemia, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
