Definition

Astereognosis is a bedside cortical sensory sign. It is a clue to contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction. A positive finding means the object cannot be identified by touch alone.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Astereognosis means the object cannot be identified by touch alone.

Why It Matters

The observed Astereognosis finding can signal contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction. Parietal association cortex fails to integrate texture, shape, weight, and proprioception into object identity. Astereognosis cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction

Assessment Method

Place a familiar object in one hand with eyes closed after confirming primary sensation and motor exploration.

Positive Finding

The object cannot be identified by touch alone.

Mechanism

Parietal association cortex fails to integrate texture, shape, weight, and proprioception into object identity.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Astereognosis finding raises concern for contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction because parietal association cortex fails to integrate texture, shape, weight, and proprioception into object identity. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Astereognosis does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Astereognosis to focus evaluation of contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: primary sensory loss, aphasia, poor cooperation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place a familiar object in one hand with eyes closed after confirming primary sensation and motor exploration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Astereognosis as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Weakness, sensory loss, aphasia, and unfamiliar objects invalidate the test. Therefore, Astereognosis cannot confirm or exclude contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • primary sensory loss

  • aphasia

  • poor cooperation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as primary sensory loss, aphasia, poor cooperation.

False Negative Situations

Astereognosis may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction.

Modern Status

Current cortical sensory adjunct. When performed as described, Astereognosis can adjust concern for contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Astereognosis: Weakness, sensory loss, aphasia, and unfamiliar objects invalidate the test.

Safety Precautions

Observe Astereognosis with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Place a familiar object in one hand with eyes closed after confirming primary sensation and motor exploration. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use common safe objects and compare hands without giving auditory clues.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Astereognosis observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The object cannot be identified by touch alone. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Place a familiar object in one hand with eyes closed after confirming primary sensation and motor exploration. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, contralateral parietal cortical dysfunction, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Astereognosis, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Use common safe objects and compare hands without giving auditory clues. Base escalation for Astereognosis on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
