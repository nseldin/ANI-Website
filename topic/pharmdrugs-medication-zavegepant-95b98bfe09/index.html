Class

Intranasal CGRP receptor antagonist gepant for acute migraine

Used To Treat

Acute migraine with or without aura in adults; it is not indicated for prevention.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current ZAVZPRET labeling has no boxed warning. Anaphylaxis, facial swelling and urticaria can occur. Monitor hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon. Avoid OATP1B3 or NTCP inhibitors and inducers, severe hepatic impairment, and creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min; intranasal decongestants can reduce exposure and should be delayed at least 1 hour if unavoidable.

Mechanism

Zavegepant occupies the CLR/RAMP1 CGRP receptor complex and reduces Gs-adenylyl-cyclase-cAMP signaling in trigeminovascular pain pathways. Nasal absorption produces rapid systemic exposure without swallowing a tablet, which is useful when migraine causes nausea or vomiting. The complete relationship between receptor antagonism and clinical benefit remains unknown, and the drug should not be described as simply constricting dilated vessels.

Nursing Essentials

  • Do not prime or test spray: remove one device only when ready and deliver its single spray into one nostril. Maximum is one device in 24 hours, and safety for treating more than 8 attacks in 30 days is unestablished.

  • Assess nasal technique, taste disturbance, nausea/vomiting, hypersensitivity, blood pressure, digital perfusion, renal/hepatic function, transporter interactions and headache red flags.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to zavegepant or an excipient.

  • Avoid severe hepatic impairment and creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min because the labeled exposure and safety evidence do not support use.

Interactions

  • Avoid OATP1B3 or NTCP inhibitors because they can increase exposure and avoid inducers because they can lower exposure and efficacy.

  • Intranasal decongestants can reduce zavegepant absorption. If unavoidable, administer the decongestant at least 1 hour after zavegepant so the migraine dose has time for nasal uptake.

Key Labs

  • No routine level. Monitor acute response, monthly use, blood pressure, Raynaud symptoms, allergy and renal/hepatic suitability; review OATP1B3/NTCP-active drugs and nasal decongestants.

Nclex Traps

  • The device contains one complete dose; priming expels medication before use, and spraying both nostrils exceeds the labeled daily regimen.

  • More than 8 treated attacks in 30 days is an unestablished evidence boundary, not a proven ninth-attack toxicity threshold; frequent attacks need reassessment and preventive planning.

  • Zavegepant treats attacks only and is not a nasal preventive schedule.

Description

Zavegepant is a single-use 10-mg intranasal CGRP receptor antagonist that blocks CGRP signaling to treat an adult migraine attack when nausea or vomiting may limit oral therapy. One spray into one nostril is the entire daily dose; it is not preventive therapy, and safety for treating more than 8 attacks in 30 days is not established. That boundary reflects limited studied exposure and should prompt migraine reassessment, not the claim that a ninth treated attack is known to be toxic. Dysgeusia is common, and OATP1B3/NTCP transporter interactions, vascular effects, hypersensitivity, and renal/hepatic restrictions are part of safe use.

Adverse Effects

  • Taste disorder or dysgeusia is most characteristic; nausea, nasal discomfort, and vomiting can occur.

  • Serious hypersensitivity, postmarketing hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon are the high-consequence risks.
