    Clostridioides difficile toxin A, abbreviated TcdA, is one of the major glucosylating toxins produced by toxigenic C. difficile strains and contributes to intestinal epithelial injury and inflammation.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    After entering susceptible intestinal cells, TcdA modifies Rho-family regulatory proteins. Cytoskeletal signaling and tight-junction integrity fail, epithelial permeability rises, and inflammatory and secretory responses promote diarrhea and colitis. The mechanism connects a molecular toxin effect to fluid loss and mucosal damage.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    TcdA is often taught with TcdB, but they are distinct toxins and not interchangeable names. A strain's toxin profile must be demonstrated by appropriate microbiological methods rather than assumed from the species name.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    TcdA contributes to the pathophysiology of symptomatic CDI. The clinically important question remains whether toxin-mediated disease is occurring in a compatible patient, not merely whether C. difficile DNA or colonization is present.

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    TcdA is not required as the sole toxin in every clinically significant strain; TcdA-negative and TcdB-positive disease exists. A toxin result must be interpreted within the assay and diagnostic algorithm, and this concept card does not replace the C. difficile diagnostic-test card.
