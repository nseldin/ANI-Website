Definition

Splenomegaly means enlargement of the spleen. It is usually a finding caused by another disorder, such as infection, portal hypertension, a blood disorder, cancer, inflammation, congestion, or infiltrative disease.

Plain Language

Splenomegaly means an enlarged spleen, the organ in the upper-left abdomen that filters blood and supports immune function.

Why It Matters

An enlarged spleen can trap blood cells, cause early fullness after eating, and become easier to rupture even after relatively minor trauma. Avoid vigorous palpation, contact sports, abdominal injury, and heavy lifting; sudden left-upper-abdominal or left-shoulder pain, fainting, low blood pressure, or guarding can signal rupture and requires emergency care.

Assessment Method

Inspect and gently palpate from the right lower abdomen toward the left costal margin during inspiration, use percussion only as an adjunct, assess left-upper-quadrant pain, early satiety, infection and bleeding findings, review the blood count, and confirm suspected enlargement with ultrasound or other ordered imaging.

Positive Finding

The spleen is palpated below the left costal margin in an appropriate examination or imaging confirms that it is enlarged.

Mechanism

Immune-cell proliferation, sequestration or destruction of blood cells, venous congestion, infiltrative material, tumor, or infection increases splenic volume.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus, gastric or colonic contents, left pleural disease, renal or other masses, and examiner technique affect palpation and percussion; a normal spleen can rarely be palpable and a nonpalpable spleen can still be enlarged. Therefore, Splenomegaly cannot confirm or exclude spleen enlargement secondary to infection, portal hypertension or liver disease, hematologic disease, malignancy, inflammation, congestion, or infiltrative and storage disorders by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

False Negative Situations

Splenomegaly may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of spleen enlargement secondary to infection, portal hypertension or liver disease, hematologic disease, malignancy, inflammation, congestion, or infiltrative and storage disorders.
