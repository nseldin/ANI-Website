Definition

Barré leg sign is a bedside motor sign. It is a clue to subtle lower-limb paresis. A positive finding means the affected leg slowly falls or cannot maintain flexion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Barré leg sign means the affected leg slowly falls or cannot maintain flexion.

Why It Matters

The observed Barré leg sign finding can signal subtle lower-limb paresis. Weakness becomes visible when gravity opposes sustained knee flexion. Barré leg sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subtle lower-limb paresis

Assessment Method

With the patient prone, flex both knees to roughly 90 degrees and ask them to hold the position.

Positive Finding

The affected leg slowly falls or cannot maintain flexion.

Mechanism

Weakness becomes visible when gravity opposes sustained knee flexion.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Barré leg sign finding raises concern for subtle lower-limb paresis because weakness becomes visible when gravity opposes sustained knee flexion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Barré leg sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Barré leg sign to focus evaluation of subtle lower-limb paresis and the reviewed alternatives: knee pain, hip disease, functional weakness. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient prone, flex both knees to roughly 90 degrees and ask them to hold the position. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Barré leg sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Joint pain, poor effort, obesity, and inability to lie prone reduce usefulness. Therefore, Barré leg sign cannot confirm or exclude subtle lower-limb paresis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • knee pain

  • hip disease

  • functional weakness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as knee pain, hip disease, functional weakness.

False Negative Situations

Barré leg sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of subtle lower-limb paresis.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; positioning may be impractical. When performed as described, Barré leg sign can adjust concern for subtle lower-limb paresis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Barré leg sign: Joint pain, poor effort, obesity, and inability to lie prone reduce usefulness.

Safety Precautions

Observe Barré leg sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: With the patient prone, flex both knees to roughly 90 degrees and ask them to hold the position. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not use prone positioning when spinal precautions, respiratory compromise, or late pregnancy make it unsafe.
