Definition

Pneumocystis jirovecii is a host-adapted fungus that causes Pneumocystis pneumonia, commonly abbreviated PCP or PJP, primarily in people with impaired cellular immunity. The older human-organism name P. carinii remains historically searchable but is no longer the preferred human species name. Detecting fungal material is not enough by itself; organism burden, specimen type, host risk, and compatible lung disease determine clinical significance.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    P. jirovecii is a fungus despite lacking many familiar features of routinely cultured yeasts. PCP names the pneumonia, not the organism. The abbreviation PCP historically meant Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia and persists even after the human species was renamed. Keeping those terms as qualified search identities preserves access without incorrectly merging this fungus with the distinct Pneumocystis species found in other mammals.

    Id

    identity-classification

    Title

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    The organism has trophic and cyst-like or ascus forms and resides closely along alveolar surfaces. It does not grow on routine clinical fungal media, so ordinary culture cannot confirm disease. Its cell biology differs across life-cycle stages, and beta-D-glucan in the cell wall can contribute a sensitive but nonspecific blood marker. The organism is nonmotile and depends on the oxygen-rich pulmonary environment.

    Id

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Humans are the relevant reservoir, and available evidence supports airborne person-to-person spread, but clinical disease overwhelmingly reflects inadequate immune control rather than ordinary exposure alone. Risk rises with advanced HIV, prolonged high-dose corticosteroids, transplantation, malignancy, and other T-cell impairment. The interval from acquisition or colonization to pneumonia is uncertain, and reactivation versus new acquisition can be difficult to distinguish.

    Id

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Organisms attach along alveolar epithelium and proliferate when cellular immunity cannot contain them. Foamy proteinaceous material and inflammatory cells fill alveolar spaces, thickening the diffusion barrier and producing ventilation-perfusion mismatch. Oxygenation can fall sharply during exertion before dramatic auscultatory findings appear. This mechanism explains the common combination of progressive dyspnea, dry cough, diffuse imaging abnormalities, and hypoxemia.

    Id

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    PCP often presents subacutely with fever, nonproductive cough, worsening dyspnea, tachypnea, and exertional or resting hypoxemia. Onset may be more abrupt in non-HIV immune suppression. Severe disease can cause respiratory failure, pneumothorax, and death. Escalating oxygen need, marked work of breathing, confusion, cyanosis, or hemodynamic instability requires emergency management.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Diagnosis uses induced sputum or bronchoalveolar-lavage material for microscopy, immunofluorescence, or molecular detection in a compatible host. PCR is sensitive but may detect colonization, so organism load and clinical context matter. Serum beta-D-glucan is often elevated and can support the diagnosis but is not specific for Pneumocystis. Routine culture is not available, and a negative weak specimen does not reliably exclude disease.

    Id

    diagnostic-identification

    Title

    Diagnostic identification

  • Content

    Standard Precautions apply; healthcare isolation policy should follow current setting-specific guidance rather than assuming the fungus behaves like tuberculosis. Effective antiretroviral therapy and indicated antimicrobial prophylaxis reduce risk in vulnerable populations. Treatment and prophylaxis depend on immune status, severity, oxygenation, allergy, renal function, blood counts, interactions, and adverse-effect monitoring.
