Used To Treat

Treatment-resistant schizophrenia after adequate antipsychotic trials and reduction of recurrent suicidal behavior in schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder. It is not a routine first-line antipsychotic because its unique benefit comes with unique multisystem toxicity.

Mechanism

Clozapine has lower, more transient D2 occupancy than high-potency antipsychotics and substantial D4/5-HT2A/2C antagonism, helping preserve nigrostriatal and tuberoinfundibular dopamine and lowering EPS/prolactin. H1/5-HT2C blockade drives sedation, appetite and metabolic effects; alpha-1 blockade causes orthostasis; M1 and other muscarinic actions create complex salivation, tachycardia and profound bowel hypomotility. Reactive immune/metabolic mechanisms are implicated in neutropenia and myocarditis but are not fully defined. CYP1A2 is central to clearance, so infection/inflammation and abrupt smoking cessation can sharply raise concentrations and toxicity.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Severe neutropenia; orthostatic hypotension, bradycardia and syncope; seizures; myocarditis, pericarditis and cardiomyopathy; and increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis. REMS enrollment/reporting is no longer required, but baseline and ongoing ANC monitoring remains necessary. Slow titration reduces early cardiovascular collapse and seizure risk.

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity and inability to perform safe hematologic/clinical monitoring

  • Do not rechallenge severe neutropenia, myocarditis or other serious toxicity except under specialist labeling/guidance

  • Assess ileus/constipation, seizure, cardiac, metabolic and CYP1A2/smoking status before initiation

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline ANC and, while it remains in the normal range, test weekly from day 1 through month 6, every 2 weeks during months 7 through 12, and monthly from month 13 onward. A falling ANC requires faster repeat testing and may require interruption or hematology consultation; fever at or above 38.5 C (101.3 F) requires interruption and an ANC check.

  • Ask about every bowel movement; new constipation, distention, vomiting or pain can precede fatal hypomotility and deserves active prevention/escalation.

Interactions

  • Smoking induces CYP1A2; abrupt cessation, ciprofloxacin, fluvoxamine or inflammation can raise levels sharply.

  • Other marrow suppressants, anticholinergics, opioids, sedatives and hypotensive drugs add serious toxicity.

  • Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers and apply label-directed CYP1A2/2D6/3A4 dose changes.

Key Labs

  • Baseline ANC generally at least 1500/microliter, or at least 1000/microliter with documented benign ethnic neutropenia/Duffy-null associated neutrophil count; confirm unexpected declines and use the ANC-specific testing and dose-interruption thresholds

  • Weight, glucose/A1c, lipids, blood pressure and bowel tracking

  • Early myocarditis surveillance with symptoms, pulse and protocol-based troponin/CRP; level/EEG assessment when toxicity or seizures are suspected

Nclex Traps

  • The REMS ended; ANC monitoring did not.

  • Fever plus tachycardia early in therapy can be myocarditis or neutropenic infection, not routine adjustment.

  • Constipation is a potentially fatal organ toxicity, not a minor comfort issue.

Description

Clozapine is the most important exception in antipsychotic pharmacology: it can work when adequate trials of other antipsychotics fail and can reduce recurrent suicidal behavior, while causing relatively little prolactin elevation and EPS. Its broad D4/D2, 5-HT2A/2C, H1, muscarinic and alpha-1 effects also create severe neutropenia, myocarditis/cardiomyopathy, seizures, profound orthostasis, sialorrhea, metabolic disease and potentially fatal GI hypomotility. FDA removed the clozapine REMS on June 13, 2025, but the boxed neutropenia risk and label-directed ANC monitoring remain.

Adverse Effects

  • Severe neutropenia and infection

  • Myocarditis/pericarditis/cardiomyopathy, tachycardia and orthostatic syncope

  • Seizures and dose/concentration-related neurotoxicity

  • Sialorrhea, sedation, major metabolic effects and severe constipation/ileus/megacolon
