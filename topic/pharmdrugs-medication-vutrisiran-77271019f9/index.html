Class

Subcutaneous GalNAc-conjugated transthyretin-directed small interfering RNA

Used To Treat

Adult hereditary ATTR polyneuropathy and adult wild-type or hereditary ATTR cardiomyopathy to reduce cardiovascular mortality, cardiovascular hospitalizations and urgent heart-failure visits.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current AMVUTTRA labeling has no boxed warning. Treatment decreases serum vitamin A; provide only the recommended daily allowance and obtain ophthalmologic assessment for night blindness, dry eyes or other deficiency symptoms. Injection-site reactions and arthralgia or dyspnea require context because ATTR itself can produce neurologic and cardiac progression.

Mechanism

The triantennary GalNAc ligand binds hepatocyte asialoglycoprotein receptors and internalizes the siRNA. Its guide strand enters the RNA-induced silencing complex, recognizes a conserved TTR transcript sequence, and drives catalytic mRNA cleavage. Lower hepatic TTR synthesis reduces serum TTR and tissue deposition but also lowers retinol-binding-protein/vitamin A transport.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact q3-month date, indication, cardiac versus neurologic baseline, administration by a healthcare professional, pregnancy context and vitamin A plan.

  • Trend neuropathy, orthostasis, gait, pain, GI/autonomic function, weight, edema, dyspnea, NT-proBNP, heart-failure events and functional capacity; lowering TTR does not replace diuresis or rhythm management.

Key Labs

  • Clinical neurologic/cardiac response, volume status, renal/liver function, NT-proBNP/troponin when indicated, and ophthalmologic evaluation for vitamin A symptoms; no routine therapeutic drug level.

Nclex Traps

  • Vutrisiran is a quarterly GalNAc siRNA and is not the same formulation or infusion process as patisiran.

  • Wild-type ATTR-CM has no TTR mutation but still uses wild-type TTR as amyloid precursor, so hepatic TTR lowering can be relevant.

Description

Vutrisiran is a long-acting GalNAc-conjugated RNA-interference modulator (siRNA) that is taken up selectively by hepatocytes and degrades both mutant and wild-type TTR mRNA, lowering amyloid precursor production after a subcutaneous dose every 3 months. It spans neurologic hATTR polyneuropathy and cardiac wild-type or hereditary ATTR indications, unlike patisiran's current U.S. polyneuropathy-only label.
