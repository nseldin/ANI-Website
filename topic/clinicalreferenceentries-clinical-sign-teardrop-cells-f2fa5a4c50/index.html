Definition

Teardrop cells is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis. A positive finding means dacrocytes with a single tapered end are increased.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Teardrop cells means dacrocytes with a single tapered end are increased.

Why It Matters

The Teardrop cells laboratory finding can signal marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis. Red cells deform while exiting a fibrotic or infiltrated marrow or traversing abnormal splenic tissue. Teardrop cells cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis

Assessment Method

Review peripheral smear for consistently oriented pear-shaped cells.

Positive Finding

Dacrocytes with a single tapered end are increased.

Mechanism

Red cells deform while exiting a fibrotic or infiltrated marrow or traversing abnormal splenic tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

The Teardrop cells laboratory finding raises concern for marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis because red cells deform while exiting a fibrotic or infiltrated marrow or traversing abnormal splenic tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Teardrop cells does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Teardrop cells to focus evaluation of marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis and the reviewed alternatives: megaloblastic anemia, iron deficiency, artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review peripheral smear for consistently oriented pear-shaped cells. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Teardrop cells as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Randomly oriented pointed cells may be smear artifact. Therefore, Teardrop cells cannot confirm or exclude marrow distortion or extramedullary hematopoiesis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • megaloblastic anemia

  • iron deficiency

  • artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with megaloblastic anemia, iron deficiency, artifact.

False Negative Situations

Teardrop cells may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
