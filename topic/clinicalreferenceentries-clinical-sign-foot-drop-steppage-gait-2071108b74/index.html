Definition

Foot drop steppage gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to ankle dorsiflexor weakness. A positive finding means the hip and knee lift excessively and the forefoot may slap down.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Foot drop steppage gait means the hip and knee lift excessively and the forefoot may slap down.

Why It Matters

The observed Foot drop steppage gait finding can signal ankle dorsiflexor weakness. Weak dorsiflexors fail to clear the toes during swing and control plantarflexion at heel strike. Foot drop steppage gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ankle dorsiflexor weakness

Assessment Method

Observe walking, including heel strike and toe clearance, with fall precautions.

Positive Finding

The hip and knee lift excessively and the forefoot may slap down.

Mechanism

Weak dorsiflexors fail to clear the toes during swing and control plantarflexion at heel strike.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Foot drop steppage gait finding raises concern for ankle dorsiflexor weakness because weak dorsiflexors fail to clear the toes during swing and control plantarflexion at heel strike. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Foot drop steppage gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Foot drop steppage gait to focus evaluation of ankle dorsiflexor weakness and the reviewed alternatives: painful gait, limb-length discrepancy, ataxia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe walking, including heel strike and toe clearance, with fall precautions. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Foot drop steppage gait as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Compensatory gait can be subtle with braces or bilateral disease. Therefore, Foot drop steppage gait cannot confirm or exclude ankle dorsiflexor weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • painful gait

  • limb-length discrepancy

  • ataxia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as painful gait, limb-length discrepancy, ataxia.

False Negative Situations

Foot drop steppage gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of ankle dorsiflexor weakness.
