Definition

Obturator sign is a bedside abdominal maneuver. It is a clue to pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation. A positive finding means hypogastric or right-lower-quadrant pain occurs.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Obturator sign means hypogastric or right-lower-quadrant pain occurs.

Why It Matters

A positive Obturator sign response can signal pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation. Rotation stretches the obturator internus adjacent to an inflamed pelvic appendix or abscess. Obturator sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation

Assessment Method

Flex the right hip and knee, then gently internally rotate the hip.

Positive Finding

Hypogastric or right-lower-quadrant pain occurs.

Mechanism

Rotation stretches the obturator internus adjacent to an inflamed pelvic appendix or abscess.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Obturator sign response raises concern for pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation because rotation stretches the obturator internus adjacent to an inflamed pelvic appendix or abscess. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Obturator sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Obturator sign to focus evaluation of pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation and the reviewed alternatives: septic hip, pelvic inflammatory disease, muscle strain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex the right hip and knee, then gently internally rotate the hip. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Obturator sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Hip pathology and low sensitivity limit standalone value. Therefore, Obturator sign cannot confirm or exclude pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • septic hip

  • pelvic inflammatory disease

  • muscle strain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from septic hip, pelvic inflammatory disease, muscle strain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Obturator sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with limited sensitivity. When performed as described, Obturator sign can adjust concern for pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Obturator sign: Hip pathology and low sensitivity limit standalone value.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Obturator sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex the right hip and knee, then gently internally rotate the hip. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force rotation in an injured or arthritic hip.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Obturator sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Hypogastric or right-lower-quadrant pain occurs. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex the right hip and knee, then gently internally rotate the hip. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, pelvic appendicitis or obturator muscle irritation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Obturator sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
