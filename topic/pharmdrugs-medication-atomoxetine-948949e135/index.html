Contraindications

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI use

  • Narrow-angle glaucoma, pheochromocytoma or severe cardiovascular disorder expected to deteriorate with meaningful BP/HR increases

  • Assess severe liver disease, urinary outflow obstruction, bipolar/psychosis and suicidality before treatment

Nursing Essentials

  • Set expectations that benefit builds over weeks; lack of a same-hour effect is not proof of failure and extra PRN doses only increase toxicity.

  • Record baseline and follow-up pulse, blood pressure, height, weight, appetite, sleep, mood and urinary function; ask the family about behavioral change.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors such as fluoxetine, paroxetine and quinidine raise exposure and may require label-directed dose changes.

  • MAOIs can produce dangerous monoamine excess; systemic albuterol and other sympathomimetics can add BP/HR effects.

  • Antihypertensives may be opposed; other QT/cardiac-risk drugs require individualized review.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, pulse, height, weight and growth trajectory

  • Liver tests promptly for jaundice, dark urine, pruritus, right-upper-quadrant pain or unexplained flu-like illness

  • Suicidality, aggression, psychosis/mania, urinary/sexual function and CYP2D6 interaction review

Nclex Traps

  • Nonstimulant means lower rapid reward/abuse potential, not no cardiovascular or psychiatric risk.

  • Atomoxetine is not PRN and may need several weeks for full functional effect.

  • Fluoxetine can both treat comorbidity and substantially raise atomoxetine exposure through CYP2D6 inhibition.

Class

Selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor nonstimulant for ADHD

Used To Treat

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder in pediatric patients and adults as part of a comprehensive treatment program. It is taken on a schedule rather than as an immediate as-needed concentration aid.

Mechanism

Atomoxetine inhibits presynaptic NET, increasing norepinephrine across noradrenergic pathways and increasing norepinephrine plus dopamine in prefrontal cortex because dopamine there is substantially cleared by NET. It has little direct DAT blockade in nucleus accumbens/striatum, helping explain why it is not a controlled stimulant and produces less immediate euphoria. Functional improvement emerges over repeated daily exposure as attention and impulse-control networks adapt. CYP2D6 forms less active metabolites; genetic poor metabolizers or strong CYP2D6 inhibitors such as fluoxetine/paroxetine can multiply exposure, prolong half-life and increase tachycardia, hypertension, appetite loss or urinary effects.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Atomoxetine increased suicidal ideation in short-term studies of children and adolescents with ADHD. Monitor new or worsening suicidality, hostility, agitation, irritability, unusual behavior and abrupt mood change, especially in the first months or after dose changes.

Description

Atomoxetine is a nonstimulant ADHD medication that selectively blocks the norepinephrine transporter. In prefrontal cortex, NET clears both norepinephrine and much of extracellular dopamine, so NET inhibition raises both catecholamines where executive control is organized without producing the same rapid striatal reward signal as a classic stimulant. This explains its lower abuse potential and delayed, cumulative benefit, but not an absence of risk: suicidal thinking in youth, blood-pressure/pulse increases, liver injury, urinary/sexual effects, growth change and psychosis/mania still require surveillance.

Adverse Effects

  • Decreased appetite, nausea/abdominal pain, fatigue, dizziness and sleep disturbance

  • Increased heart rate/blood pressure, orthostasis and rare serious cardiovascular events

  • Urinary hesitation/retention, erectile/ejaculatory dysfunction and rare priapism

  • Suicidal ideation in youth, severe liver injury, psychosis/mania and growth slowing
