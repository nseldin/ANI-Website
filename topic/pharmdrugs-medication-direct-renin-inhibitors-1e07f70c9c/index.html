Used To Treat

Hypertension with aliskiren in labeled adult and selected pediatric populations. The class has not replaced ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or ARNIs for heart-failure, post-MI, or proteinuric-kidney outcome roles.

Description

Direct renin inhibitors block the first committed enzymatic step of the RAAS. Aliskiren binds renin's active site and prevents cleavage of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, lowering downstream angiotensin II and aldosterone. This differs from an ARB, which leaves angiotensin II present but blocks AT1, and from an ACE inhibitor, which also preserves bradykinin. The blood-pressure effect is real, but suppressing plasma renin activity does not automatically produce superior clinical outcomes. Aliskiren combination studies in diabetes showed more renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and hypotension when added to ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy, leading to a contraindication. The class therefore teaches an important evidence rule: a pathway biomarker can move in the expected direction while the patient's net outcomes worsen.

Mechanism

Renin is an aspartyl protease released by juxtaglomerular cells. Aliskiren occupies the S1/S3 binding pocket and prevents angiotensinogen cleavage, reducing angiotensin I substrate for ACE and lowering angiotensin II and aldosterone. Renin concentration rises through feedback, but its enzymatic activity remains inhibited while drug exposure is adequate. Less AT1 and aldosterone signaling relaxes vessels and reduces sodium retention, but also dilates the efferent arteriole and reduces distal potassium secretion. That explains hypotension, creatinine change, AKI in RAAS-dependent perfusion, and hyperkalemia. Because the drug is a P-glycoprotein substrate with very low oral bioavailability, high-fat meals and strong P-gp modifiers can substantially alter exposure.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Aliskiren carries this boxed warning. It is contraindicated with an ACE inhibitor or ARB in diabetes because renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and hypotension increase.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, dizziness, cough, rash, hyperkalemia, hypotension, and renal-function change.

  • Angioedema, anaphylaxis, severe skin reactions, acute kidney injury, severe hyperkalemia, and fetal toxicity.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy; ACE inhibitor or ARB coadministration in diabetes; hypersensitivity; and pediatric use below the label's age restrictions.

  • Avoid or clarify in severe renal dysfunction, significant volume depletion, dangerous potassium elevation, and with strong P-gp inhibitors.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm that the indication is hypertension and that no ACE inhibitor or ARB is being combined in a patient with diabetes. Check pregnancy, pressure, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume, age/weight eligibility, and interacting drugs.

  • Teach consistent administration with respect to meals because high-fat meals reduce exposure; explain that consistency protects predictable effect rather than preventing stomach upset.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors or ARBs in diabetes are contraindicated. NSAIDs, diuretics, potassium products, MRAs, and lithium create familiar RAAS renal or electrolyte risks.

  • Cyclosporine and itraconazole markedly raise aliskiren through P-glycoprotein inhibition and should be avoided; grapefruit and high-fat meals alter exposure.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, volume, urine output, and pregnancy testing when relevant.

  • There is no routine aliskiren level. Clinical pressure response and safety labs guide therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • Aliskiren lowers angiotensin II production; ARBs do not. This target distinction should be stated accurately.

  • Upstream blockade is not automatically stronger or better. Outcome evidence and combination harm determine clinical use.
