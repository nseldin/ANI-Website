Definition

Nodule means a solid lesion that extends into deeper skin or the tissue beneath it. It is usually deeper, and often larger, than a papule and may be easier to feel than to see.

Plain Language

A nodule is a deeper solid lump in or under the skin.

Why It Matters

Many nodules are benign, but a new or changing nodule can represent inflammation, infection, a cyst, or cancer. Rapid growth, fixation, bleeding, ulceration, severe pain, fever, or failure to heal changes how urgently it should be examined and whether biopsy is needed.

Associated Conditions

  • focal inflammation, infection, cellular growth, deposited material, cyst formation, or neoplasia extending into the dermis or subcutaneous tissue

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate gently, measure surface diameter and depth when possible, and document location, number, color, surface change, firmness, mobility or fixation, tenderness, warmth, fluctuance, drainage, growth rate, duration, nearby lymph nodes, systemic symptoms, and skin-cancer risk.

Positive Finding

A discrete solid palpable lesion extends into the dermis or tissue beneath the skin and may lift the surface; it is generally deeper and often larger than a papule.

Mechanism

Localized cells, inflammatory material, fibrous tissue, fat, keratin, or another substance accumulates in deeper skin or subcutaneous tissue and forms a three-dimensional lump.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Nodule finding raises concern for focal inflammation, infection, cellular growth, deposited material, cyst formation, or neoplasia extending into the dermis or subcutaneous tissue because localized cells, inflammatory material, fibrous tissue, fat, keratin, or another substance accumulates in deeper skin or subcutaneous tissue and forms a three-dimensional lump. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nodule does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Size cutoffs overlap, deep nodules may have little visible surface change, and firmness, mobility, tenderness, or appearance cannot establish histology without cause-directed evaluation and sometimes biopsy. Therefore, Nodule cannot confirm or exclude focal inflammation, infection, cellular growth, deposited material, cyst formation, or neoplasia extending into the dermis or subcutaneous tissue by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cyst

  • lipoma

  • abscess

  • enlarged lymph node

  • skin cancer

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cyst, lipoma, abscess, enlarged lymph node, skin cancer.

False Negative Situations

Nodule may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of focal inflammation, infection, cellular growth, deposited material, cyst formation, or neoplasia extending into the dermis or subcutaneous tissue.

Modern Status

Current descriptive morphology, not a diagnosis; many nodules are benign, but a growing, fixed, ulcerated, bleeding, or nonhealing nodule can represent infection or malignancy and needs timely evaluation. Observe Nodule with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect and palpate gently, measure surface diameter and depth when possible, and document location, number, color, surface change, firmness, mobility or fixation, tenderness, warmth, fluctuance, drainage, growth rate, duration, nearby lymph nodes, systemic symptoms, and skin-cancer risk.
