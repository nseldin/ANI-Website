Class

Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody TNF-alpha inhibitor available in subcutaneous and intravenous products

Used To Treat

Subcutaneous SIMPONI treats label-specific rheumatoid arthritis with methotrexate, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and ulcerative colitis including current weight-specified pediatric use. Intravenous SIMPONI ARIA has a different rheumatologic indication and schedule. The products and routes are not interchangeable by milligram.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: serious infections and malignancy. Fatal TB, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal, viral, and other opportunistic infections have occurred. Test for latent TB, treat it before therapy, and monitor for active TB even after a negative baseline test. Discontinue for serious infection or sepsis. Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal, have been reported in children and adolescents receiving TNF blockers. Current labeling also requires hepatitis B review and avoidance of live vaccines; infants exposed in utero require product-specific live-vaccine timing review.

Mechanism

Golimumab binds bioactive soluble and membrane-associated human TNF-alpha, preventing interaction with TNFR1 and TNFR2. This lowers NF-kappaB/MAPK inflammatory transcription, endothelial adhesion, leukocyte recruitment, cytokines, metalloproteinases, and acute-phase responses. Binding transmembrane TNF plus an IgG1 Fc may produce reverse signaling and immune effector effects. TNF blockade reduces inflammation but destabilizes granuloma-mediated containment of mycobacteria and fungi and can permit hepatitis B reactivation. Subcutaneous absorption produces prolonged monthly exposure; IV dosing creates a controlled infusion peak. Drug and antidrug-antibody concentrations vary with body size, inflammation, immunogenicity, and concomitant methotrexate.

Nursing Essentials

  • Reconcile SIMPONI versus SIMPONI ARIA, route, concentration, weight, loading/maintenance schedule, and whether methotrexate is required for the prescribed rheumatoid regimen.

  • Monitor injection or infusion reaction, infection, neurologic change, edema/dyspnea, jaundice, bruising, skin lesions, and objective disease response.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity and serious active infection preclude administration until resolved; confirm the exact SC or IV product and indication.

  • Clarify TB/HBV, fungal exposure, malignancy, heart failure, demyelination, blood dyscrasia, vaccines, wounds, and pediatric weight.

Interactions

  • Avoid abatacept, anakinra, other TNF blockers, other biologics, and JAK inhibitors because additive serious infection can outweigh benefit.

  • Avoid live vaccines and therapeutic infectious agents. Methotrexate can affect immunogenicity and is part of the labeled SC rheumatoid regimen.

Key Labs

  • TB assessment, hepatitis B serology, CBC/liver context, infection evaluation, pediatric weight where applicable, and disease activity

  • Vital signs around IV infusion, heart-failure and neurologic findings, skin/malignancy surveillance, and injection reaction

Nclex Traps

  • SIMPONI and SIMPONI ARIA contain golimumab but have different routes, schedules, and indications; do not convert them by brand familiarity.

  • Monthly dosing does not mean monthly infection screening is enough; symptoms between doses still require action.

Description

Golimumab is a fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds soluble and transmembrane TNF-alpha and prevents receptor activation. It can be delivered as a monthly subcutaneous product or a weight-based intravenous infusion under the SIMPONI ARIA formulation, but route, dose, indication, and administration schedule differ. In rheumatoid arthritis, subcutaneous use is paired with methotrexate under current labeling. Blocking TNF reduces synovial, bowel, skin, and spinal inflammation while impairing granuloma maintenance and increasing serious infection risk. An intact Fc region permits Fc-receptor and complement effects. The practical high-yield distinction is not merely 'another TNF blocker'; it is exact product reconciliation, monthly versus infusion timing, and indication-specific dosing.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, injection-site reaction, infusion reaction, headache, rash, and transaminase elevation

  • TB/HBV reactivation, opportunistic infection, malignancy, heart-failure worsening, demyelination, cytopenia, liver injury, lupus-like syndrome, and severe hypersensitivity
