Used To Treat

Early treatment of uncomplicated influenza in patients at least seven years old and prophylaxis in patients at least five years old under current U.S. labeling. It is not recommended for asthma or COPD because inhaled powder can provoke bronchospasm.

Mechanism

Zanamivir structurally resembles the transition state of N-acetylneuraminic acid. It binds the neuraminidase catalytic pocket and blocks hydrolysis of terminal sialic-acid residues from host glycoproteins and budding virions. Hemagglutinin can still mediate entry, but progeny virions cannot efficiently detach and spread. Because the active drug is inhaled without metabolic activation, exposure is concentrated in the respiratory tract and absorbed drug is excreted unchanged by the kidney. The dry powder contains lactose carrying residual milk proteins. Bronchospasm results from local airway irritation and hyperresponsiveness rather than the neuraminidase target itself, which is why route and airway history can outweigh an otherwise suitable antiviral mechanism.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious and sometimes fatal bronchospasm has occurred, especially in asthma or COPD; zanamivir is not recommended in underlying airway disease. If used despite exceptional circumstances, have a rapid bronchodilator available and stop for wheeze or respiratory decline. The powder contains lactose with milk proteins and is contraindicated in severe milk-protein allergy. Do not place the formulation into a nebulizer or ventilator circuit.

Contraindications

  • History of serious hypersensitivity to zanamivir, lactose, or milk protein.

  • Underlying asthma or COPD is a major route-related reason to select another antiviral; inability to use the Diskhaler correctly also makes treatment unreliable.

Nursing Essentials

  • Demonstrate Diskhaler loading and inhalation, then use teach-back. Do not confuse the device with a rescue inhaler, DPI capsule, nebulizer, or spacer.

  • Assess wheeze and peak respiratory status before and after initial use. Influenza-related dyspnea can progress independently, so any decline needs urgent evaluation.

Interactions

  • Separate from live attenuated intranasal influenza vaccine according to current timing guidance because antiviral activity can reduce vaccine-virus replication.

  • Systemic interactions are limited by low absorption, but other bronchospasm-provoking exposures and inability to coordinate inhalation matter clinically.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level; assess oxygenation, respiratory history, inspiratory ability, renal function in unusual systemic contexts, and influenza severity

  • Bronchospasm, correct device technique, symptom-onset time, secondary pneumonia, neurobehavioral change, and clinical response

Nclex Traps

  • Zanamivir is inhaled powder and can cause bronchospasm; oseltamivir is usually safer for asthma or COPD.

  • Relenza blisters must be used only in the Diskhaler, never dissolved for nebulization.

Description

Zanamivir is a sialic-acid analogue delivered as an inhaled dry powder to inhibit influenza A and B neuraminidase at the respiratory surface. By preventing cleavage of sialic acid, it keeps newly formed virions attached to infected cells and mucus, reducing spread to adjacent epithelium. Inhalation limits systemic exposure but creates a decisive route-specific hazard: people with reactive or obstructed airways can develop severe bronchospasm, and the Diskhaler requires enough inspiratory flow and coordination to deliver powder. It is not the same as a nebulizer solution and should never be reconstituted or aerosolized through mechanical ventilation equipment.

Adverse Effects

  • Nasal/throat irritation, cough, sinusitis, dizziness, headache, and ear/nose/throat symptoms

  • Bronchospasm, serious allergic or skin reaction, and transient neuropsychiatric events
