Class

IV broad carbapenem plus cyclic-boronate KPC beta-lactamase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Adults with susceptible complicated urinary infection including pyelonephritis under the U.S. label, and an important current-guidance option for selected KPC-producing Enterobacterales infections when susceptibility and site support it.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Vabomere does not cover MBL or OXA-48-like carbapenemases. Meropenem can markedly lower valproate and can cause concentration-related encephalopathy or seizure.

Mechanism

Vaborbactam forms a reversible covalent complex with KPC and selected class A/C serine enzymes. Protected meropenem crosses the outer membrane and acylates PBPs, with fT>MIC driving killing. A three-hour infusion supports exposure, while vaborbactam efficacy correlates with unbound AUC relative to MIC. Renal elimination of both components requires coordinated adjustment. Porin loss, efflux, altered PBPs, increased KPC expression, a class B metallo-beta-lactamase such as NDM, or an OXA-48-like enzyme can still produce resistance because vaborbactam does not neutralize those catalytic pathways.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify KPC or supported susceptibility, renal function, valproate, CNS history, dose, and full three-hour infusion because correct enzyme matching without correct exposure can still fail.

  • Repeat cultures and reassess source in persistent infection because KPC mutation, permeability change, or absent drainage can emerge during therapy.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to meropenem, vaborbactam, carbapenems, or relevant beta-lactams requires avoidance.

  • Avoid with valproate when a clinically effective alternative exists because vaborbactam does not prevent the carbapenem interaction.

Interactions

  • Meropenem profoundly lowers valproate/divalproex; probenecid and OAT3 competition can raise component exposure.

  • Nephrotoxic co-therapy narrows the safety margin by reducing renal clearance.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC and carbapenemase testing, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, mental status, and valproate level if exposure occurs.

  • A generic CRE result is insufficient; distinguish KPC from OXA-48-like and MBL enzymes because vaborbactam activity differs.

Nclex Traps

  • Vaborbactam protects meropenem from KPC; meropenem performs the PBP killing.

  • Vabomere is not an MBL or OXA-48-like solution.

Description

Meropenem/vaborbactam, branded Vabomere, combines a broad PBP-binding carbapenem with a cyclic boronate inhibitor designed especially for KPC carbapenemases. Vaborbactam mimics the transition state of serine beta-lactam hydrolysis and protects meropenem from KPC, restoring wall-active killing in susceptible Enterobacterales. Vaborbactam has no direct antibacterial effect and does not reliably inhibit class B NDM/VIM/IMP or class D OXA-48-like carbapenemases. Meropenem retains its own spectrum limits, renal neurotoxicity, and profound valproate interaction.

Meropenem Vaborbactam

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, phlebitis, diarrhea, nausea, liver-test elevation, candidiasis, allergy, cytopenias, and CDI.

  • Confusion, myoclonus, nonconvulsive status, seizure, interstitial nephritis, and breakthrough seizure from valproate depletion.

Class Pathway

  • Vaborbactam inhibits KPC

  • Meropenem reaches multiple PBPs

  • Three-hour infusion preserves time-dependent exposure
