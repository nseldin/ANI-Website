Class Example Names

  • Ursodiol

Used To Treat

Primary biliary cholangitis with current ursodiol therapy; Obeticholic acid is retained as a former U.S. therapy and safety-history card after withdrawal of its accelerated approval in November 2025.

Description

Bile-acid therapies can change either the composition of the bile-acid pool or the signaling that controls bile-acid synthesis. Ursodiol is a hydrophilic bile acid that displaces more hydrophobic, detergent-like bile acids, promotes bile flow, and protects cholangiocytes. Obeticholic acid was an FXR agonist that suppressed bile-acid synthesis through intestinal-hepatic feedback, but its U.S. PBC approval was withdrawn; its mechanism and toxicity remain clinically relevant even though it is not a current routine option.

Mechanism

Bile acids are detergents and endocrine signals. Retained hydrophobic bile acids injure cholangiocytes and hepatocytes in cholestasis. Ursodiol makes the pool less hydrophobic and stimulates hepatobiliary secretion. FXR activation induces intestinal FGF19 and hepatic small-heterodimer-partner signaling, reducing CYP7A1-mediated bile-acid synthesis while increasing transport and disposal pathways.

Boxed Warning

Current-status warning: Ursodiol has no U.S. boxed warning, but requires biochemical response and liver-disease monitoring. Obeticholic acid formerly carried a boxed warning for hepatic decompensation and failure in patients with advanced cirrhosis and its U.S. accelerated approval for PBC was withdrawn on November 24, 2025. Do not teach a withdrawn option as active routine therapy.

Adverse Effects

  • Ursodiol: diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, weight gain and occasional hypersensitivity

  • Obeticholic acid historical profile: severe pruritus, fatigue, abdominal symptoms, lipid changes and potentially fatal hepatic decompensation

  • Progressive cholestasis despite therapy requires response reassessment rather than automatic dose continuation

Contraindications

  • Complete biliary obstruction or inability to deliver bile into the intestine

  • Obeticholic acid historical contraindications included decompensated cirrhosis, prior decompensation, or compensated cirrhosis with portal hypertension

  • Clarify drug-binding resins, aluminum antacids, worsening bilirubin/INR/albumin, ascites, variceal bleeding or encephalopathy

Nursing Essentials

  • Separate current U.S. treatment from former therapy in every response.

  • Trend cholestatic liver tests, bilirubin, synthetic function, symptoms and clinical decompensation; treatment is judged by both biochemical response and patient state.

Interactions

  • Bile-acid sequestrants and aluminum-containing antacids can reduce ursodiol absorption; separate administration.

  • Historical Obeticholic acid interactions included bile-acid resins and changes in warfarin INR; current prescribing is not appropriate after withdrawal.

Key Labs

  • Alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, AST/ALT, albumin and INR

  • Platelets and clinical portal-hypertension/decompensation assessment

  • Pruritus burden, adherence, imaging or specialist evaluation when obstruction or progression is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Ursodiol does not mechanically open a completely obstructed bile duct.

  • Obeticholic acid is a recognition and safety-history card, not a current U.S. PBC recommendation.

  • A lower alkaline phosphatase does not cancel new ascites, encephalopathy, bleeding or jaundice.
