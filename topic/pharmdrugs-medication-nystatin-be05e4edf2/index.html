Used To Treat

Local mucocutaneous Candida infections such as oral candidiasis and selected cutaneous or vaginal candidiasis using formulation-specific products. It is not effective for candidemia or systemic mycoses because oral drug is negligibly absorbed.

Mechanism

The polyene portion associates with ergosterol and forms membrane-spanning channels, disrupting electrochemical gradients and cell viability. Contact time drives local efficacy: retaining suspension in the mouth before swallowing keeps drug against plaques and mucosa. Topical products work at skin surfaces, and vaginal products work locally. Because systemic exposure is negligible at conventional oral doses, the drug cannot deliver the tissue concentration needed for esophageal-invasive or bloodstream disease. Continued local therapy after symptom improvement helps eradicate residual Candida and reduce early relapse.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to nystatin or formulation components.

  • Do not use oral suspension as treatment for candidemia, invasive candidiasis, cryptococcosis, or deep esophageal disease.

Nursing Essentials

  • Shake suspension, measure accurately, distribute around the mouth, and retain as long as possible before swallowing if prescribed. Avoid food or drink immediately afterward when product instructions advise contact time.

  • Assess dentures, inhaled corticosteroid technique, diabetes, antibiotics, immune status, feeding devices, and esophageal symptoms. Clean dentures and rinse after inhaled steroids because removing the driver prevents recurrence.

Interactions

  • Systemic interactions are minimal because absorption is negligible. Formulation excipients such as sucrose matter in diabetes and dental risk.

  • Do not assume combination creams are interchangeable with plain nystatin; a topical corticosteroid can mask progression, thin skin, and change the indication.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level or organ laboratory monitoring for ordinary local therapy. KOH/culture and host evaluation are useful for refractory or recurrent disease.

  • Failure after an adequate course should prompt confirmation of Candida, species/resistance, adherence/contact time, and alternative diagnoses.

Nclex Traps

  • Nystatin is a polyene, not a statin: it binds ergosterol rather than inhibiting cholesterol synthesis.

  • Swish-and-swallow remains local pharmacology; swallowing does not create systemic absorption.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: The critical safety warning is indication error: nystatin is not for systemic mycoses. Persistent or recurrent symptoms require microbiologic and diagnostic reassessment rather than endless empiric courses.

Class Pathway

  • Bind Candida membrane ergosterol

  • Increase membrane permeability

  • Local oral/topical/vaginal action with negligible GI absorption

Description

Nystatin is a polyene antifungal obtained from Streptomyces noursei that binds ergosterol in susceptible Candida membranes, increasing permeability so intracellular ions and metabolites leak out. Oral suspension acts by direct contact with mouth and gastrointestinal mucosa; most swallowed drug passes unchanged in stool, so it cannot reach blood or deep organs. This poor absorption is why nystatin has few systemic interactions and is useful for local thrush, but it is also why using it for candidemia would be a dangerous category error. Nystatin has no relationship to cholesterol-lowering statins and does not inhibit HMG-CoA reductase.

Adverse Effects

  • Unpleasant taste, mouth irritation, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, and topical burning or contact dermatitis.

  • Rare hypersensitivity, angioedema, facial swelling, bronchospasm, or severe skin reaction.
