Name

Urine specific gravity

Range

Urine specific gravity is a unitless estimate of urine concentration. A general example is 1.005-1.030, but method, sample timing, fluid intake, medicines, and the reporting laboratory change interpretation. A dipstick gives a rough estimate; laboratory testing and urine osmolality are more precise, and glucose, protein, dextran, sucrose, or recent contrast can distort the reading.

Why

An abnormal urine-specific-gravity result signals a urine concentration or dilution pattern, not a diagnosis. Persistently dilute urine together with large-volume urine output, excessive thirst, rising serum sodium, and high serum osmolality raises concern for arginine vasopressin deficiency or resistance (diabetes insipidus); concentrated urine can fit dehydration, while in hypotonic hyponatremia it may support concern for SIADH only after other causes are excluded. Promptly assess intake and output, measured urine volume, thirst and access to water, weight, hydration and perfusion, neurologic status, medicines and IV fluids, glucose, and recent contrast; cause-directed evaluation can include same-time serum sodium and osmolality, urine osmolality and sodium, glucose, and kidney function, with clinician-supervised dynamic testing when indicated. Escalate emergently for high urine output with hypernatremia, severe dehydration, reduced consciousness, or rapid deterioration, and for seizure or reduced consciousness with hyponatremia. One urine-specific-gravity result does not diagnose SIADH, diabetes insipidus, dehydration, or kidney failure; interpret the trend, method, interfering solutes, urine volume, paired serum and urine results, and full clinical context.

High Causes

  • Concentrated urine from dehydration or reduced effective circulating volume

  • SIADH pattern only when hypotonic hyponatremia and the full diagnostic evaluation fit

  • Glucose or protein in urine

  • Dextran, sucrose, or recent contrast interference

Low Causes

  • Arginine vasopressin deficiency or resistance (diabetes insipidus) when polyuria and paired serum findings fit

  • High water intake or excess hypotonic fluid

  • Impaired kidney concentrating ability

Critical Concerns

  • High-volume dilute urine with hypernatremia, severe dehydration, reduced consciousness, or rapid deterioration can represent decompensated AVP deficiency and needs emergency assessment.

  • Seizure or reduced consciousness with hyponatremia requires emergency escalation regardless of the specific-gravity result.

Nursing Considerations

  • Measure and trend intake, urine volume, weight, hydration, neurologic status, serum sodium, and paired serum and urine osmolality when ordered.

  • Review medicines, IV fluids, glucose or protein in urine, recent contrast, and the collection method before interpreting a surprising value.

  • Do not initiate water-deprivation testing independently; dynamic testing requires a clinician-supervised protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not diagnose SIADH or diabetes insipidus from urine specific gravity alone.

  • A high value can reflect heavy solutes or contrast rather than appropriate water conservation.

  • A dipstick result is a rough estimate; urine osmolality is more specific for particle concentration.
