Summary

PT (prothrombin time) is a high-yield Hematology diagnostic reference. Coagulation test tied to extrinsic pathway and warfarin monitoring through INR context.

Quick Answer

PT (prothrombin time): Coagulation test tied to extrinsic pathway and warfarin monitoring through INR context. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Prolonged

    Meaning

    Extrinsic/common pathway clotting is delayed, seen with warfarin effect, vitamin K deficiency, liver dysfunction, DIC, or factor deficiency.

  • Label

    Normal

    Meaning

    PT pathway timing is not prolonged, but platelet problems, aPTT pathway issues, or anticoagulants can still matter.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Use PT with INR, bleeding signs, liver function, medication history, and procedure risk.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Coagulation test tied to extrinsic pathway and warfarin monitoring through INR context.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
