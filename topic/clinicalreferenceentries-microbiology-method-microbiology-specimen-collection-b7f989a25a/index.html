    Principle

  • Content

    Specimen collection connects the clinical question to a valid laboratory test. The requested test, suspected agent, body site, illness stage, antimicrobial exposure, and sample volume all affect what collection is useful.

    Id

    purpose

    Title

    Purpose

  • Content

    Verify patient identity, source, collection time, required container, test request, and relevant exposure or therapy. Obtain ordered specimens before antimicrobial therapy when doing so is safe and does not delay urgent treatment.

    Id

    specimen-preparation

    Title

    Specimen and preparation

  • Content

    Follow the approved local collection protocol for the exact specimen and use aseptic technique. Label the anatomic source precisely; vague labels such as 'wound' can prevent meaningful interpretation.

    Id

    procedure

    Title

    Method

  • Content

    A high-quality specimen increases confidence that a detected organism reflects infection rather than contamination or colonization. Results must still be interpreted with symptoms and source quality.

    Id

    interpretation

    Title

    Interpretation

  • Content

    A negative result may reflect timing, prior therapy, inadequate volume, wrong site, contamination, or an assay that does not detect the suspected agent. Recollection may be safer than overinterpreting a compromised specimen.

    Id

    limitations

    Title

    Limitations

  • Content

    Use Standard Precautions and the collection device approved for the ordered test. Never place staff at avoidable sharps, splash, or aerosol risk merely to obtain a specimen.
