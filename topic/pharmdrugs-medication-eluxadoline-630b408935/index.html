Class

Mixed mu/kappa opioid agonist and delta opioid antagonist GI drug

Used To Treat

Irritable bowel syndrome with diarrhea in adults who have a gallbladder and no listed pancreatitis, biliary, alcohol, hepatic, or obstructive contraindication.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for eluxadoline in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Mu and kappa opioid-receptor agonism reduces propulsive motility, secretion, and visceral pain signaling, while delta antagonism may limit excessive mu-mediated constipation. Opioid effects at the sphincter of Oddi can obstruct bile/pancreatic flow and trigger pancreatitis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm gallbladder presence and screen biliary obstruction/sphincter disease, pancreatitis history, pancreatic duct disease, more than three alcoholic drinks daily, severe liver disease, severe constipation, or mechanical obstruction.

  • Stop immediately for new epigastric/RUQ pain radiating to back/shoulder, vomiting, pancreatic/liver enzyme elevation, or severe constipation.

Key Labs

  • Lipase/amylase and liver tests when symptomatic, hepatic/renal function for dose selection, and bowel/pain response.

Nclex Traps

  • No gallbladder means no eluxadoline. This contraindication is absolute because pancreatitis can occur after the first doses.

Description

Eluxadoline acts locally on enteric opioid receptors to slow intestinal transit and reduce visceral pain in IBS-D. Its defining safety rule is anatomical: clients without a gallbladder can develop sphincter of Oddi spasm and fatal pancreatitis after only one or two doses, so gallbladder status must be known before the first tablet.
