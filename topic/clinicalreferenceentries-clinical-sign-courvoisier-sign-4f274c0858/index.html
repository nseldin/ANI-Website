Definition

Courvoisier sign is a palpated abdominal sign. It is a clue to malignant distal biliary obstruction. A positive finding means a nontender enlarged gallbladder is palpable.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Courvoisier sign means a nontender enlarged gallbladder is palpable.

Why It Matters

The observed Courvoisier sign finding can signal malignant distal biliary obstruction. Gradual distal obstruction distends a previously compliant gallbladder, whereas chronically stone-damaged gallbladders often cannot expand. Courvoisier sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • malignant distal biliary obstruction

Assessment Method

Palpate the right upper abdomen in a jaundiced patient.

Positive Finding

A nontender enlarged gallbladder is palpable.

Mechanism

Gradual distal obstruction distends a previously compliant gallbladder, whereas chronically stone-damaged gallbladders often cannot expand.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Courvoisier sign finding raises concern for malignant distal biliary obstruction because gradual distal obstruction distends a previously compliant gallbladder, whereas chronically stone-damaged gallbladders often cannot expand. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Courvoisier sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Stones can occasionally distend the gallbladder and malignancy may not. Therefore, Courvoisier sign cannot confirm or exclude malignant distal biliary obstruction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as gallbladder hydrops, hepatomegaly, abdominal mass.

False Negative Situations

Courvoisier sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of malignant distal biliary obstruction.
