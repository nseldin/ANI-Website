Used To Treat

Rheumatoid arthritis inadequately responding to salicylates or other NSAIDs and ulcerative colitis treatment/maintenance, with formulation- and age-specific labeling. It is not a rapid analgesic, and delayed-release tablets must be swallowed intact.

Mechanism

Colonic microbiota reduce sulfasalazine's azo bond, releasing sulfapyridine and mesalamine. Sulfapyridine is absorbed and acetylated in the liver; slow acetylators can develop higher exposure and more nausea, headache, or toxicity. In rheumatoid disease, supported actions include reduced NF-kappaB activation, inflammatory cytokine and eicosanoid signaling, lymphocyte and neutrophil activity, but the complete mechanism remains uncertain. Colonic 5-ASA activates PPAR-gamma and reduces cyclooxygenase/lipoxygenase and NF-kappaB-related inflammatory signaling locally. Sulfasalazine also impairs intestinal folate absorption. Low solubility and renal excretion help explain crystalluria risk, while oxidative stress explains greater hemolysis concern in G6PD deficiency.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Deaths have occurred from hypersensitivity, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, other blood dyscrasias, liver or kidney injury, severe skin reactions, pneumonitis, and serious infection. Fever, sore throat, pallor, purpura, jaundice, mucosal lesions, dyspnea, or systemic rash requires immediate interruption and evaluation. Maintain hydration to reduce crystalluria and stone risk, and monitor CBC, liver tests, urinalysis, and renal function on the label schedule.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to sulfasalazine, sulfonamides, or salicylates; intestinal or urinary obstruction; and porphyria are label contraindications.

  • Clarify G6PD deficiency, severe allergy/asthma, liver/kidney disease, blood dyscrasia, infection, fertility goals, folate status, and ability to maintain hydration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain CBC/differential and liver tests before therapy, every two weeks for three months, monthly for the next three months, then every three months or as indicated; monitor urinalysis and renal function.

  • Give divided doses after meals, swallow enteric-coated tablets whole, encourage appropriate fluid intake, and investigate an apparently intact tablet in stool rather than assuming absorption.

Interactions

  • Reduces folic-acid and digoxin absorption; combined methotrexate increases GI intolerance and can add liver/marrow risk.

  • Can interfere with urinary normetanephrine and NAD(H)/NADP(H)-based laboratory assays, creating false results rather than true biochemical disease.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, liver tests, urinalysis, creatinine/eGFR, hemolysis markers when indicated, folate status, and disease response

  • Fever, sore throat, pallor, purpura, jaundice, rash/mucosa, respiratory symptoms, hydration, urine, and fertility counseling

Nclex Traps

  • Sulfasalazine is not simply a sulfa antibiotic; bacteria split it into systemic sulfapyridine and local 5-ASA with different teaching implications.

  • Orange-yellow urine can be expected, but jaundice, pallor, dark hemolytic urine, or reduced output is not a harmless color change.

Description

Sulfasalazine is an azo-linked prodrug that colonic bacteria cleave into absorbed sulfapyridine, which provides much of the systemic rheumatoid DMARD effect, and locally active 5-aminosalicylic acid, which suppresses colonic inflammation. Most intact drug reaches the colon, where bacterial azoreductases split the bond. Sulfapyridine is strongly associated with many concentration-related adverse effects. 5-ASA remains more local and suppresses inflammatory mediators in ulcerative colitis. The exact rheumatoid mechanism is not one proven receptor, but effects on NF-kappaB, cytokines, prostaglandins, lymphocytes, and folate handling contribute. This split-molecule design explains why one tablet can serve joint and bowel disease yet produce sulfonamide hypersensitivity, reversible male infertility, folate reduction, hemolysis, crystalluria, and assay interference.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, dyspepsia, headache, rash, orange-yellow urine or skin, folate deficiency, and reversible oligospermia

  • Agranulocytosis/aplastic anemia, hemolysis, hepatotoxicity, nephritis/crystalluria, SJS/TEN/DRESS, pneumonitis/fibrosing alveolitis, and fatal infection
