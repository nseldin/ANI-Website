Plain Meaning

The protein-based pulling force, especially from albumin, that helps hold water inside blood vessels.

Pathology

Oncotic pressure is the inward pull created by plasma proteins, mainly albumin. When albumin is low or protein leaks out of vessels, less water is held intravascularly, so fluid shifts into tissues or body spaces as edema, ascites, pleural effusion, or third spacing.

Pathophysiology

Starling forces balance hydrostatic pressure pushing fluid out against oncotic pressure pulling fluid in. Low albumin from liver failure, nephrotic syndrome, malnutrition, inflammation, burns, or protein-losing enteropathy weakens the inward pull; capillary leak can also move protein into tissues and worsen edema.

Etiology

Hypoalbuminemia, cirrhosis/liver synthetic failure, nephrotic syndrome, severe inflammation/sepsis, burns, malnutrition, protein-losing enteropathy, dilution, and capillary leak.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dependent edema, ascites, pleural effusion, pulmonary edema risk when fluid shifts, poor wound healing, low effective circulating volume despite total-body fluid excess

Diagnostics

  • Serum albumin/total protein

  • Urine protein or albumin-creatinine ratio

  • Liver synthetic markers

  • Renal function

  • Daily weight, intake/output, edema, lung sounds, ascites/effusion imaging when indicated

Treatments

  • Treat the driver of low albumin or protein leak

  • Nutrition support when appropriate

  • Diuresis, sodium/fluid strategy, albumin infusion, paracentesis, or disease-specific therapy only when ordered and clinically indicated

Nursing Priorities

  • Connect low albumin to edema and medication binding

  • Assess edema with perfusion because the client may be swollen but intravascularly depleted

  • Trend lungs, weight, urine output, albumin, renal/liver status, skin integrity, and response to therapy

Complications

  • Third spacing

  • Ascites

  • Pleural effusion

  • Pulmonary edema risk

  • Skin breakdown

  • Poor wound healing

  • Altered protein-bound drug effect

Patient Education

  • Report rapid swelling, shortness of breath, abdominal distention, low urine output, or sudden weight gain.

Nclex Traps

  • Hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out; oncotic pressure pulls fluid in.

  • Low albumin can create edema even when total body fluid is not low.

  • Edema does not prove strong circulating volume.
