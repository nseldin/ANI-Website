Name

Parathyroid hormone

Range

Intact parathyroid hormone (PTH) is an assay-specific blood measurement reported with the performing laboratory's units and adult reference interval; second- and third-generation methods do not create one interchangeable universal range. Interpret the result with a concurrent albumin-adjusted total or ionized calcium result plus phosphate, magnesium, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, and kidney function because a PTH value inside the printed interval can still be inappropriate when calcium is high. For adults with CKD G3a-G5 not receiving dialysis, KDIGO states that the optimal PTH level is unknown and recommends evaluating a progressively rising or persistently assay-high trend for modifiable factors rather than treating one elevated value as a target or mandate.

Why

An abnormal Parathyroid hormone result signals an altered calcium-phosphate regulatory response; its clinical meaning comes from whether PTH is appropriately suppressed or increased for the concurrent calcium level. Elevated or inappropriately normal PTH with confirmed hypercalcemia is associated with primary hyperparathyroidism, while elevated PTH with low or normal calcium suggests a secondary response such as vitamin-D deficiency, low calcium intake or absorption, or chronic kidney disease. Low or inappropriately low PTH with hypocalcemia suggests impaired parathyroid secretion, including after neck surgery, but magnesium disturbance and critical illness can change the pattern. Promptly assess perioral or finger tingling, cramps, tetany, weakness, thirst and polyuria, kidney-stone symptoms, confusion, seizure, breathing or rhythm change, recent neck surgery, kidney disease, diet, medicines, and calcium or vitamin-D supplements. Cause-directed evaluation can include repeat concurrent PTH and adjusted or ionized calcium, phosphate, magnesium, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, creatinine/eGFR, and, after a biochemical primary-hyperparathyroidism pattern is established, clinician-selected urine-calcium, skeletal, and renal testing; parathyroid imaging localizes tissue for a procedure and does not establish the biochemical diagnosis. Escalate immediately for laryngospasm, respiratory distress, seizure, altered consciousness, serious dysrhythmia, or rapid deterioration; the urgency comes from the symptomatic calcium and clinical pattern, not a PTH number alone. One PTH result does not diagnose hyperparathyroidism, hypoparathyroidism, or CKD-mineral and bone disorder and does not by itself authorize imaging, surgery, vitamin-D or calcium therapy, a calcimimetic, or another treatment.

High Causes

  • Primary hyperparathyroidism when PTH is elevated or inappropriately unsuppressed with confirmed hypercalcemia

  • Secondary hyperparathyroid response to vitamin-D deficiency, low calcium intake or absorption, chronic kidney disease, or hyperphosphatemia

  • Tertiary hyperparathyroidism after longstanding secondary stimulation in the appropriate kidney or malabsorption context

  • Lithium-associated altered calcium-PTH regulation

  • Assay, specimen, or biologic variation requiring confirmation with the paired mineral pattern

Low Causes

  • Hypoparathyroidism after thyroid, parathyroid, or other neck surgery or after neck radiation

  • Autoimmune or genetic parathyroid dysfunction

  • Appropriate PTH suppression during non-PTH-mediated hypercalcemia

  • Severe magnesium depletion that impairs PTH secretion or action

  • Assay, specimen, or acute-illness effects that require clinical correlation

Critical Concerns

  • New tingling, cramps, tetany, laryngospasm, seizure, serious dysrhythmia, or breathing difficulty after neck surgery raises urgent concern for symptomatic hypocalcemia.

  • Confusion, marked dehydration, severe weakness, repeated vomiting, serious rhythm change, or rapid deterioration with hypercalcemia requires prompt clinical evaluation.

  • There is no PTH-only emergency threshold; act on the calcium, symptoms, ECG, and stability pattern.

Nursing Considerations

  • Verify that PTH and calcium were collected and interpreted together, review the assay and laboratory interval, and trend phosphate, magnesium, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, creatinine/eGFR, symptoms, and prior values.

  • Assess recent neck surgery or radiation, kidney disease, stones, fractures or bone pain, diet or malabsorption, lithium and other medicines, and calcium or vitamin-D supplements.

  • Notify the treating clinician promptly for symptomatic calcium disturbance or a discordant pattern and follow the ordered evaluation; do not independently start, stop, or select mineral or parathyroid therapy from one PTH value.

Nclex Traps

  • High calcium should suppress PTH; a PTH value that looks numerically normal can therefore be inappropriately unsuppressed.

  • High PTH with low or normal calcium is not automatically primary hyperparathyroidism; evaluate kidney function, vitamin D, calcium intake or absorption, phosphate, and magnesium.

  • In CKD, follow serial calcium-phosphate-PTH trends and modifiable factors rather than treating one PTH result as a universal target.
