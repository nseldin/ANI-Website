Used To Treat

Short-term inotropic support for acute decompensated heart failure, cardiogenic shock, or low-cardiac-output states when contractility is the problem. It may also be used in stress testing protocols.

Contraindications

  • Idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis or obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is a classic clarify-before-giving condition.

  • Clarify uncorrected tachydysrhythmias, severe hypovolemia, or active ischemic chest pain.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use an infusion pump and monitor ECG, heart rate, blood pressure, chest pain, dyspnea, lung sounds, urine output, and perfusion.

  • Watch for increased myocardial oxygen demand: tachycardia, ectopy, angina, or ST changes.

  • Dobutamine improves contractility; it is not the first fix for pure vasodilatory hypotension without a pump problem.

Interactions

  • Beta blockers can blunt dobutamine response.

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, other catecholamines, and volatile anesthetics can increase cardiovascular risk.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG and blood pressure.

  • Potassium and magnesium when dysrhythmia risk is present.

  • Urine output, lactate/perfusion markers, and renal function in shock.

Nclex Traps

  • Dobutamine is an inotrope, not a pure vasopressor. If MAP is falling, reassess volume status and vasopressor need instead of assuming more dobutamine is safer.

Boxed Warning

High-alert safety focus: tachycardia, ectopy, dysrhythmias, myocardial ischemia, hypotension, and worsening obstruction in hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy.

Class

Inotrope; beta-1 adrenergic agonist with mild beta-2 vasodilating effect

Mechanism

Primarily stimulates beta-1 receptors in the heart to increase contractility and stroke volume. Mild beta-2 vasodilation can lower systemic vascular resistance, so blood pressure may fall if the client is volume depleted or vasodilated.

Description

Dobutamine is a beta-1 adrenergic agonist inotrope: it stimulates the heart to increase contractility, stroke volume, and cardiac output more than it raises vascular tone. Because beta-1 stimulation can provoke tachycardia, ischemia, and dysrhythmias, the first bedside read is continuous ECG/BP monitoring plus perfusion response.
