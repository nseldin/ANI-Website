Definition

Charcot triad is a clinical constellation. It is a clue to acute ascending cholangitis. A positive finding means all three features occur together.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Charcot triad means all three features occur together.

Why It Matters

The Charcot triad pattern can signal acute ascending cholangitis. Obstructed infected bile ducts cause local pain, bilirubin retention, and systemic inflammatory fever. Charcot triad cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • acute ascending cholangitis

Assessment Method

Assess for fever, right-upper-quadrant pain, and jaundice.

Positive Finding

All three features occur together.

Mechanism

Obstructed infected bile ducts cause local pain, bilirubin retention, and systemic inflammatory fever.

Clinical Interpretation

The Charcot triad pattern raises concern for acute ascending cholangitis because obstructed infected bile ducts cause local pain, bilirubin retention, and systemic inflammatory fever. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Charcot triad does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Charcot triad to focus evaluation of acute ascending cholangitis and the reviewed alternatives: viral hepatitis, liver abscess, pancreatitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess for fever, right-upper-quadrant pain, and jaundice. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Charcot triad as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The full triad is absent in many cases, especially older or immunocompromised patients. Therefore, Charcot triad cannot confirm or exclude acute ascending cholangitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • viral hepatitis

  • liver abscess

  • pancreatitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by viral hepatitis, liver abscess, pancreatitis without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Charcot triad may be absent even when acute ascending cholangitis is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status

Current classic clue with limited sensitivity. When performed as described, Charcot triad can adjust concern for acute ascending cholangitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Charcot triad: The full triad is absent in many cases, especially older or immunocompromised patients.

Safety Precautions

Observe Charcot triad with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Assess for fever, right-upper-quadrant pain, and jaundice. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not wait for all three before antibiotics, drainage planning, and sepsis care.
