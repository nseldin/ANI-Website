  • Content

    Antimicrobial resistance mechanisms are biological changes that reduce or eliminate an antimicrobial's activity against a microorganism.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    True resistance mechanisms include drug inactivation, altered targets, reduced entry, active efflux, metabolic bypass, and target protection. Resistance may be intrinsic or acquired through mutation or gene transfer. Biofilm growth can instead produce tolerance or persistence by slowing growth and limiting exposure; that state is clinically important but is not automatically an inherited or acquired resistance mechanism.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Beta-lactamase production, altered penicillin-binding proteins, ribosomal target modification, and efflux pumps illustrate different ways susceptibility can be lost.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Mechanism awareness helps clinicians interpret susceptibility results and avoid assuming that one drug represents an entire class. Treatment still depends on the infection site, organism, patient factors, and current tested phenotype.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    A detected resistance gene may not predict every phenotype, while a resistant phenotype may involve an untested mechanism. Do not infer individual susceptibility from species reputation alone.

    Title

    Limitations

  • Content

    WHO and CDC describe resistance as a biologic process accelerated by antimicrobial pressure and spread, requiring surveillance, stewardship, and infection prevention.
