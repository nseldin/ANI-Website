Definition

Slump test is a seated neural-tension maneuver. It is a clue to lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity. A positive finding means concordant neural pain changes with sensitizing or relieving movements.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Slump test means concordant neural pain changes with sensitizing or relieving movements.

Why It Matters

A positive Slump test response can signal lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity. Combined spinal and limb position increases mechanical load along the neural axis. Slump test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity

Assessment Method

Sequentially slump thoracic and cervical spine, extend the knee, and dorsiflex ankle with symptom-relieving release steps.

Positive Finding

Concordant neural pain changes with sensitizing or relieving movements.

Mechanism

Combined spinal and limb position increases mechanical load along the neural axis.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Slump test response raises concern for lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity because combined spinal and limb position increases mechanical load along the neural axis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Slump test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Slump test to focus evaluation of lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity and the reviewed alternatives: meningeal irritation, hip disease, functional pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Sequentially slump thoracic and cervical spine, extend the knee, and dorsiflex ankle with symptom-relieving release steps. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Slump test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal stretch discomfort is common and sensitivity maneuvers are subjective. Therefore, Slump test cannot confirm or exclude lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • meningeal irritation

  • hip disease

  • functional pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from meningeal irritation, hip disease, functional pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Slump test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; technique-dependent. When performed as described, Slump test can adjust concern for lumbosacral neural mechanosensitivity, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Slump test: Normal stretch discomfort is common and sensitivity maneuvers are subjective.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Slump test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Sequentially slump thoracic and cervical spine, extend the knee, and dorsiflex ankle with symptom-relieving release steps. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use after acute spinal trauma or force cervical flexion.
