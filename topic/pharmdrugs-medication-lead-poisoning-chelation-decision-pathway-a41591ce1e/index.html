Used To Treat

Confirmed lead exposure or poisoning using immediate source removal, public-health intervention, developmental and nutritional care, and specialist selection among succimer, calcium disodium EDTA, and dimercaprol. Chelation is not a substitute for eliminating exposure.

Description

Lead binds sulfhydryl groups, disrupts heme synthesis, impairs calcium-dependent neurotransmission, injures kidney and peripheral nerves, and harms the developing brain. Blood lead reflects recent and mobilizable burden but can rebound as lead redistributes from bone or when exposure continues. The first treatment is therefore to stop the source and coordinate environmental and public-health action. Chelator choice depends on a confirmed venous blood lead concentration, symptoms, encephalopathy, ability to take oral therapy, kidney and liver status, and current CDC or specialist protocol. Succimer is an oral option for selected patients without encephalopathy. Severe symptomatic poisoning or encephalopathy requires inpatient parenteral therapy, often dimercaprol with calcium disodium EDTA under expert direction.

Mechanism

Succimer uses sulfhydryl groups to form water-soluble lead complexes excreted in urine. Calcium disodium EDTA exchanges its calcium for lead and increases urinary excretion. Dimercaprol also binds lead through sulfhydryl groups and can be paired with calcium disodium EDTA in severe central nervous system toxicity. These drugs lower circulating and exchangeable lead but cannot reverse established neurodevelopmental injury or make an unsafe home safe. Bone stores and ongoing exposure explain post-treatment rebound and the need for serial testing.

Administration Timing

  • Remove the patient from exposure now, confirm an elevated screening capillary result with a venous sample on the CDC schedule, assess symptoms and development, and contact the regional Pediatric Environmental Health Specialty Unit, Poison Help, public health, or a medical toxicologist.

  • Use current specialist criteria to select no chelator, oral succimer, or inpatient dimercaprol plus calcium disodium EDTA. Lead encephalopathy is an emergency; do not delay parenteral planning for a routine outpatient appointment.

  • Complete the exact FDA-labeled or expert regimen with CBC, hepatic, renal, and urinalysis monitoring, then repeat blood lead testing because redistribution and re-exposure can cause rebound.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask about paint and renovation dust, imported remedies or cosmetics, pottery, spices, ammunition, batteries, occupations, hobbies, water, siblings, and pica. Treat the household source because dosing a chelator while exposure continues is an avoidable failure.

  • Verify that the EDTA product is calcium disodium edetate, never edetate disodium, because substitution can cause fatal hypocalcemia. Check the full generic name, vial, dose, route, renal status, and urine output independently so that the wrong salt or developing nephrotoxicity is detected before further dosing.

  • Coordinate developmental screening, nutrition including iron and calcium status, environmental inspection, public-health reporting, and follow-up. A falling number does not erase cognitive risk or prove the source is gone.

Key Labs

  • Confirmed venous blood lead concentration, CBC and indices, iron studies, renal and hepatic function, urinalysis, and symptom-directed abdominal imaging for ingested radiopaque objects or paint chips. Zinc protoporphyrin can support chronic exposure assessment but is not a substitute for blood lead.

  • During chelation, trend CBC, AST, ALT, creatinine, urinalysis, urine output, and repeat blood lead according to protocol. Evaluate abdominal pain, neuropathy, hypertension, encephalopathy, and developmental or behavioral effects.

Adverse Effects

  • Lead can cause abdominal colic, constipation, anemia, kidney injury, hypertension, neuropathy, cognitive and behavioral harm, seizures, cerebral edema, coma, and death.

  • Succimer can cause GI upset, rash, neutropenia, and hepatic enzyme elevation. Calcium disodium EDTA can cause nephrotoxicity, proteinuria, zinc depletion, fever, and infusion reactions. Dimercaprol can cause hypertension, tachycardia, nausea, pain, and hemolysis risk in G6PD deficiency.

Contraindications

  • Do not chelate solely because exposure is feared without confirming and stratifying the blood lead level; chelators have toxicity and can redistribute metals.

  • Do not use oral succimer alone for lead encephalopathy, and never substitute edetate disodium for calcium disodium EDTA. Do not discharge to the same uncontrolled source without a concrete exposure plan.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Encephalopathy, seizure, coma, severe abdominal symptoms, rapidly rising or very high confirmed level, retained lead foreign body, kidney injury, or inability to stop exposure requires emergency toxicology, ICU, public-health, and sometimes surgical or GI escalation. Rebound requires renewed source investigation before simply repeating chelation.

Interactions

  • Iron deficiency and pica can increase lead absorption; nutritional correction supports prevention but does not replace chelation when indicated.

  • Other nephrotoxins increase calcium-disodium-EDTA risk. Mineral supplements and polyvalent cations can alter oral chelator or co-medication absorption and should be reconciled with the treatment protocol.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Infants and children

    Note

    Because lead can injure the developing brain before a child looks ill, continue developmental surveillance, household investigation, and sibling testing so that subtle effects and shared exposure are identified early.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Maternal bone lead can mobilize and fetal neurodevelopment is vulnerable; chelation is specialized because both untreated toxicity and chelator exposure carry risk.

  • Population

    Workers and household contacts

    Note

    Take-home dust can expose children and partners; occupational hygiene and public-health controls are part of treatment.

Evidence Limitations

  • CDC thresholds and follow-up schedules can change, and lowering a blood lead concentration has not been shown to reverse established neurodevelopmental injury. Chelation decisions at severe levels require regional expert and public-health context.

Nclex Traps

  • Because a capillary screen can be contaminated by lead on the skin, confirm an elevated result with a timely venous sample so that treatment decisions reflect circulating blood lead.

  • Succimer is not adequate monotherapy for lead encephalopathy.

  • Calcium disodium EDTA and edetate disodium are different drugs; substitution can cause fatal hypocalcemia.

Regulatory Status

Succimer and calcium disodium EDTA are FDA-approved lead chelators for defined uses; dimercaprol labeling includes selected lead treatment in combination. Exact thresholds and combinations follow current CDC, toxicology, and public-health protocols. Edetate disodium is not interchangeable.

Boxed Warning

LEAD EMERGENCY WARNING: seizure, encephalopathy, coma, severe symptoms, or a very high confirmed venous lead level requires emergency toxicology care. Remove the source, never substitute edetate disodium for calcium disodium EDTA, and do not use chelation as permission to return a child to ongoing exposure.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning names the emergency, explains why delay or a wrong rescue choice is dangerous, and separates proven antidotes from supportive or uncertain interventions.
