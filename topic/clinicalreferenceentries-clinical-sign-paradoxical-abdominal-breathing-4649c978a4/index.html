Definition

Paradoxical abdominal breathing is an observed breathing sign. It is a clue to diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue. A positive finding means the abdomen moves inward during inspiration instead of outward.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Paradoxical abdominal breathing means the abdomen moves inward during inspiration instead of outward.

Why It Matters

The observed Paradoxical abdominal breathing finding can signal diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue. Negative intrathoracic pressure pulls a flaccid diaphragm cranially and draws the abdominal wall inward. Paradoxical abdominal breathing cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue

Assessment Method

Observe chest and abdominal movement supine and upright.

Positive Finding

The abdomen moves inward during inspiration instead of outward.

Mechanism

Negative intrathoracic pressure pulls a flaccid diaphragm cranially and draws the abdominal wall inward.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Paradoxical abdominal breathing finding raises concern for diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue because negative intrathoracic pressure pulls a flaccid diaphragm cranially and draws the abdominal wall inward. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Paradoxical abdominal breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Paradoxical abdominal breathing to focus evaluation of diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue and the reviewed alternatives: flail chest, upper-airway obstruction, normal infant pattern. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe chest and abdominal movement supine and upright. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Paradoxical abdominal breathing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity, postoperative splinting, and chest-abdominal asynchrony can confuse it. Therefore, Paradoxical abdominal breathing cannot confirm or exclude diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • flail chest

  • upper-airway obstruction

  • normal infant pattern

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as flail chest, upper-airway obstruction, normal infant pattern.

False Negative Situations

Paradoxical abdominal breathing may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of diaphragmatic weakness or fatigue.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Escalate new paradox with dyspnea or falling vital capacity.
