Class

PARP1/2/3 inhibitor and PARP-trapping antineoplastic

Used To Treat

Maintenance of recurrent BRCA-mutated ovarian/fallopian-tube/primary-peritoneal cancer responding to platinum therapy and treatment of BRCA-mutated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer after androgen-receptor-directed therapy.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Obtain CBC at baseline and monthly, interrupt for prolonged hematologic toxicity, and investigate failure to recover within 4 weeks for MDS/AML with marrow and cytogenetics. Prevent fetal exposure and escalate infection or bleeding promptly.

Mechanism

Rucaparib blocks PARP catalytic activity and increases PARP-DNA complexes, allowing replication-associated DNA damage to accumulate. BRCA/HRR-deficient cells cannot accurately repair resulting double-strand breaks and undergo apoptosis; transporter inhibition can raise serum creatinine without proportionate loss of filtration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify BRCA companion-diagnostic eligibility and cancer-specific maintenance or treatment setting.

  • Monitor fatigue, nausea, cytopenias, infection/bleeding, photosensitivity, liver tests, and renal trends; distinguish isolated transporter-related creatinine elevation from true AKI using the full clinical picture.

Key Labs

  • CBC monthly and more often for cytopenia, creatinine/eGFR, ALT/AST/bilirubin, pregnancy testing when applicable, and marrow/cytogenetics for suspected MDS/AML.

Nclex Traps

  • A serum-creatinine rise can reflect inhibited tubular secretion, but true renal injury must still be excluded.

  • Current ovarian use is BRCA-mutated maintenance after platinum response, not a blanket later-line ovarian-cancer indication.

Description

Rucaparib is an oral PARP1/2/3 inhibitor that impairs DNA-break repair and traps PARP-DNA complexes, exploiting BRCA-associated homologous-recombination failure in selected ovarian and prostate cancers. Myelosuppression and therapy-related MDS/AML are the central long-term hazards; nausea, fatigue, photosensitivity, liver-enzyme elevation, and a benign creatinine rise from transporter inhibition can complicate interpretation.
