Used To Treat

Depression and anxiety disorders; duloxetine additionally treats diabetic neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia and chronic musculoskeletal pain, while product indications differ.

Description

SNRIs raise serotonin and norepinephrine by blocking both transporters. Venlafaxine is more serotonergic at lower exposure and recruits more norepinephrine effect as dose rises, which helps explain dose-related blood pressure elevation and prominent withdrawal when its short half-life is exposed by missed doses. Duloxetine produces clinically relevant SERT/NET inhibition across its dosing range and strengthens descending brainstem-to-spinal inhibitory pain pathways, so it can reduce neuropathic pain even when depression is not the pain's cause.

Mechanism

SERT/NET blockade increases monoamines in mood circuits and in descending projections from brainstem nuclei to the dorsal horn. Norepinephrine acting at spinal alpha-2 receptors and serotonin at multiple receptors reduce nociceptive transmission, which explains duloxetine's pain indications. Peripheral norepinephrine also raises vascular tone and pulse; abrupt transporter release after a short-half-life drug is stopped produces dizziness, electric-shock sensations, nausea, anxiety and disequilibrium. Duloxetine hepatic metabolism and venlafaxine renal elimination of active metabolites create different organ-dose constraints.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Control and monitor blood pressure. Duloxetine can cause severe liver injury and should generally be avoided with substantial alcohol use or chronic liver disease; venlafaxine has particularly prominent discontinuation risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, sweating, insomnia, sexual dysfunction and increased blood pressure

  • Discontinuation syndrome, especially venlafaxine

  • Duloxetine hepatotoxicity and urinary hesitation/retention

  • Hyponatremia, bleeding, serotonin syndrome and mania activation

Contraindications

  • MAOI use within prohibited intervals

  • Duloxetine: substantial alcohol use, chronic liver disease or severe renal impairment under labeling

  • Control hypertension and assess glaucoma, urinary retention and bleeding risk

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask about missed doses when a venlafaxine patient reports sudden dizziness or electric sensations.

  • Explain that duloxetine treats pain by strengthening descending inhibition, not by sedating the patient or repairing the injured nerve.

Interactions

  • MAOIs and other serotonergic agents increase serotonin syndrome risk.

  • CYP1A2 inhibitors raise duloxetine; CYP2D6 interactions affect both drugs.

  • NSAIDs/anticoagulants add bleeding; alcohol increases duloxetine liver risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure and pulse

  • Liver function for duloxetine risk

  • Renal function, sodium and bleeding assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Duloxetine analgesia does not prove pain is psychological.

  • Venlafaxine withdrawal can begin after missed doses and should not be mistaken automatically for relapse.

  • SNRI norepinephrine effects make blood-pressure monitoring mechanistic, not ceremonial.
