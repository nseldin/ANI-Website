Mechanism

Magnesium competes with calcium at voltage-gated channels and presynaptic terminals, reduces acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction, stabilizes excitable membranes, and physiologically blocks NMDA-receptor channels in a voltage-dependent manner. In eclampsia, anticonvulsant benefit likely combines reduced excitatory neurotransmission, improved cerebral vascular stability, and lower neuromuscular propagation; the exact dominant mechanism is not fully resolved. In torsades, magnesium suppresses early afterdepolarizations and triggered activity that arise during prolonged repolarization. At high concentrations, progressive inhibition appears as nausea and warmth, then diminished deep-tendon reflexes, weakness, respiratory depression, conduction block, and cardiac arrest. Renal excretion is the main escape route, so oliguria is an early pharmacokinetic warning.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Current U.S. labeling warns that continuous administration in pregnancy beyond 5 to 7 days can cause fetal hypocalcemia, skeletal demineralization, osteopenia, and neonatal fracture; it is not approved for preterm-labor maintenance. Maternal accumulation can cause loss of reflexes, respiratory depression, acute renal failure, pulmonary edema, heart block, coma, and cardiac arrest. Monitor respiratory rate, deep-tendon reflexes, urine output, renal function, and clinical indication. Stop the infusion and prepare IV calcium as the physiologic antagonist when serious toxicity develops.

Contraindications

  • Heart block or myocardial damage, myasthenia gravis, significant hypermagnesemia, and product-specific contraindications; severe renal impairment requires major caution and dose/monitoring modification.

  • Do not infer that recurrent severe hypertension is controlled because seizures are prevented; magnesium is not the primary antihypertensive and delivery planning remains cause-directed.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before and during infusion, assess respiratory rate, oxygenation, deep-tendon reflexes, level of consciousness, urine output, renal function, blood pressure, lung sounds, and infusion pump settings. Keep calcium gluconate or the protocol-specified calcium salt accessible.

  • State which role is intended: maternal seizure prophylaxis/treatment, fetal neuroprotection, electrolyte replacement, or torsades. Each has a different dose, duration, endpoint, and co-monitoring plan.

Interactions

  • Neuromuscular blockers can have potentiated and prolonged effect; sedatives and other respiratory depressants reduce the margin before hypoventilation.

  • Nifedipine and other vasodilators can add hypotension or weakness, while calcium-channel physiology explains why IV calcium reverses major magnesium toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR, urine output, serum magnesium when toxicity, renal impairment, prolonged infusion, or electrolyte replacement warrants it; calcium, potassium, ECG and preeclampsia labs by indication

  • Respiratory rate, reflexes, mental status, blood pressure, lung sounds, fetal status, seizure activity, infusion duration, and neonatal tone/respiration after maternal exposure

Nclex Traps

  • Magnesium prevents eclamptic seizures but does not directly correct severe hypertension; an antihypertensive and definitive obstetric plan may still be required.

  • Loss of patellar reflexes is an early bedside sign of excessive neuromuscular blockade, while respiratory depression signals more advanced toxicity.

Antidote

Calcium gluconate is the classic NCLEX antidote. Calcium chloride may be used in critical-care settings per protocol.

Used To Treat

Prevention of eclampsia in preeclampsia, treatment and prevention of recurrent eclamptic seizures, fetal neuroprotection before anticipated early preterm birth under protocol, severe hypomagnesemia, and torsades de pointes; it is not FDA-approved as a maintenance treatment for preterm labor.

Class Pathway

  • Electrolyte and critical-care medication

  • Magnesium replacement and obstetric anticonvulsant

  • Calcium-entry, NMDA, and acetylcholine-release modulation

Description

Magnesium sulfate increases ionized magnesium to stabilize excitable membranes, reduce acetylcholine-driven neuromuscular transmission, and suppress seizure-prone or torsades-related electrical instability. In obstetrics it prevents or treats eclamptic seizures and, when early preterm delivery is imminent, can reduce the risk of cerebral palsy in surviving infants. In cardiology it stabilizes repolarization in torsades even when the serum magnesium is not profoundly low. It is not simply a uterine relaxant and should not be continued for days to stop preterm labor. The drug is cleared by the kidneys, so falling urine output converts a standard infusion into accumulating neuromuscular and respiratory toxicity.

Adverse Effects

  • Flushing, warmth, sweating, nausea, headache, blurred vision, lethargy, muscle weakness, and infusion-site discomfort

  • Areflexia, respiratory paralysis, hypotension, pulmonary edema, heart block, cardiac arrest, acute kidney injury, neonatal hypotonia or respiratory depression, and fetal bone injury with prolonged pregnancy exposure
