Definition

Hemiplegic circumduction gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity. A positive finding means the stiff extended leg swings outward in a semicircle.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hemiplegic circumduction gait means the stiff extended leg swings outward in a semicircle.

Why It Matters

The observed Hemiplegic circumduction gait finding can signal unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity. Reduced hip and knee flexion plus plantarflexor spasticity make the limb functionally long during swing. Hemiplegic circumduction gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity

Assessment Method

Observe leg trajectory, arm posture, and foot clearance during walking.

Positive Finding

The stiff extended leg swings outward in a semicircle.

Mechanism

Reduced hip and knee flexion plus plantarflexor spasticity make the limb functionally long during swing.

The observed Hemiplegic circumduction gait finding raises concern for unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity because reduced hip and knee flexion plus plantarflexor spasticity make the limb functionally long during swing. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hemiplegic circumduction gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Orthoses, joint fusion, and pain can create similar circumduction. Therefore, Hemiplegic circumduction gait cannot confirm or exclude unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as leg-length discrepancy, hip ankylosis, foot drop.

False Negative Situations

Hemiplegic circumduction gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of unilateral upper motor neuron weakness and spasticity.
