Definition

Fissure means a narrow, usually linear split in the skin that passes through the epidermis (outer skin layer) and can reach the dermis (the deeper supportive skin layer).

Plain Language

A fissure is a crack in the skin.

Why It Matters

Fissures can hurt, bleed, and break the skin barrier, allowing infection. Their location, depth, surrounding inflammation, circulation, sensation, and healing determine how urgently they need evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • a linear split in skin caused when dry, inflamed, thickened, macerated, or repeatedly stressed skin loses flexibility and separates

Assessment Method

Inspect in neutral light without forcing the split open, and document location, length, depth, bleeding, pain, surrounding scale, redness, warmth, swelling, drainage, moisture or friction exposure, recurrence, sensation, perfusion, and healing.

Positive Finding

A clearly defined narrow linear crack or cleavage is present in the skin and may extend from the epidermis into the dermis.

Mechanism

Loss of moisture, inflammation, thickening, or repeated mechanical tension reduces skin flexibility until the surface barrier splits along a line.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Fissure finding raises concern for a linear split in skin caused when dry, inflamed, thickened, macerated, or repeatedly stressed skin loses flexibility and separates because loss of moisture, inflammation, thickening, or repeated mechanical tension reduces skin flexibility until the surface barrier splits along a line. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Fissure does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Fissure to focus evaluation of a linear split in skin caused when dry, inflamed, thickened, macerated, or repeatedly stressed skin loses flexibility and separates and the reviewed alternatives: erosion, linear laceration, excoriation, ulcer. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect in neutral light without forcing the split open, and document location, length, depth, bleeding, pain, surrounding scale, redness, warmth, swelling, drainage, moisture or friction exposure, recurrence, sensation, perfusion, and healing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Fissure as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Depth and cause cannot be determined from appearance alone, flexural or interdigital fissures can be concealed, and the word fissure must not be confused with an anatomic groove or the separate diagnosis of anal fissure. Therefore, Fissure cannot confirm or exclude a linear split in skin caused when dry, inflamed, thickened, macerated, or repeatedly stressed skin loses flexibility and separates by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • erosion

  • linear laceration

  • excoriation

  • ulcer

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as erosion, linear laceration, excoriation, ulcer.

False Negative Situations

Fissure may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of a linear split in skin caused when dry, inflamed, thickened, macerated, or repeatedly stressed skin loses flexibility and separates.

Modern Status

Current secondary morphology, not a diagnosis; a fissure can be painful, bleed, and create an opening for infection, while its location and surrounding findings help identify dryness, dermatitis, friction, pressure, or another cause. Interpret Fissure in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Depth and cause cannot be determined from appearance alone, flexural or interdigital fissures can be concealed, and the word fissure must not be confused with an anatomic groove or the separate diagnosis of anal fissure.

Safety Precautions

Observe Fissure with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect in neutral light without forcing the split open, and document location, length, depth, bleeding, pain, surrounding scale, redness, warmth, swelling, drainage, moisture or friction exposure, recurrence, sensation, perfusion, and healing. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not pry, probe, or forcibly separate a fissure; promptly evaluate spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, fever, severe or rapidly worsening pain, impaired perfusion or sensation, significant bleeding, or failure to heal, especially on a foot with diabetes, neuropathy, or poor circulation.
