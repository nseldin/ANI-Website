Contraindications

  • Confirmed gastroparesis

  • Hypoglycemia unawareness or inability to follow required insulin/glucose adjustments

  • Do not mix pramlintide and insulin in the same syringe and do not use when a major meal is skipped

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the patient is eating a major meal, has reduced the mealtime insulin as ordered, and can monitor frequently; pramlintide is omitted when the meal does not meet the prescribed threshold.

  • Inject separately from insulin at a different site; persistent nausea means titration should pause rather than advance.

Interactions

  • Insulin is the central interaction because mismatched timing causes severe hypoglycemia.

  • Pramlintide delays absorption of time-critical oral drugs; administer those at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after under labeling.

  • Other drugs that slow GI motility or alter nutrient absorption can magnify delay.

Key Labs

  • Premeal and frequent postmeal glucose/CGM, especially during initiation

  • A1c and weight

  • Meal size, insulin dose/timing, hypoglycemia awareness, and gastric-emptying symptoms

Nclex Traps

  • Pramlintide is amylin, not insulin; basal and mealtime insulin remain necessary as prescribed.

  • Do not mix it in the insulin syringe.

  • The 50 percent initial mealtime-insulin reduction is meant to prevent a timing mismatch, not because pramlintide independently drives glucose into cells.

Class

Synthetic amylin analog used with mealtime insulin

Used To Treat

Adjunctive therapy for selected patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who use mealtime insulin and have not reached desired control despite optimized insulin. It is given before major meals, never replaces insulin, and requires intensive glucose self-management.

Mechanism

Pramlintide activates amylin receptors formed by the calcitonin receptor plus receptor-activity-modifying proteins in hindbrain and peripheral vagal pathways. It slows gastric delivery of nutrients, suppresses meal-stimulated glucagon without blocking the glucagon response to hypoglycemia, and promotes satiety. It does not activate insulin receptors or move glucose into cells directly. When mealtime insulin is unchanged, insulin action can peak before delayed carbohydrate absorption, producing severe hypoglycemia within roughly 3 hours. Separate injection is required because mixing with insulin changes the pharmacokinetics of both products.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Pramlintide used with insulin increases severe hypoglycemia risk, particularly in type 1 diabetes; episodes often occur within 3 hours and can cause injury while driving or operating machinery. Appropriate selection, careful instruction, frequent glucose monitoring, and reduction of mealtime insulin are essential. At initiation, current labeling directs a 50 percent reduction in mealtime insulin followed by individualized titration.

Description

Pramlintide is a synthetic amylin-receptor agonist used with mealtime insulin that activates hindbrain and vagal amylin receptors to slow gastric emptying, suppress postmeal glucagon, and increase satiety. It replaces actions of amylin, a hormone normally cosecreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells, so the postmeal glucose rise is smaller. In insulin-deficient diabetes, food then enters the blood more slowly while injected insulin may still act quickly. That timing mismatch explains the boxed warning for severe hypoglycemia and why mealtime insulin is initially reduced by 50 percent under labeling.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, reduced appetite, vomiting, headache, abdominal pain, and fatigue

  • Severe insulin-associated hypoglycemia

  • Injection-site reactions and weight loss; delayed oral-drug absorption
