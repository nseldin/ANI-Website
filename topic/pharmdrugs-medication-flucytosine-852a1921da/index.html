Class

Oral fluorinated pyrimidine antimetabolite antifungal

Used To Treat

Combination induction therapy for cryptococcal meningitis/disseminated cryptococcosis and selected severe Candida infections, almost always with another antifungal because resistance emerges rapidly with monotherapy.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - TOXICITY WITH RENAL IMPAIRMENT: Use extreme caution when renal function is impaired. Close hematologic, renal, and hepatic monitoring is essential because accumulation can cause life-threatening leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anemia, and hepatic injury.

Mechanism

After fungal uptake, 5-FC becomes 5-FU, then fluorouridine triphosphate that is falsely incorporated into RNA and fluorodeoxyuridine monophosphate that inhibits thymidylate synthase, depleting thymidine needed for DNA. Mutations in cytosine permease, cytosine deaminase, or downstream enzymes rapidly create resistance, explaining why monotherapy is unsafe for cryptococcosis. Minimal protein binding and small size support CSF penetration. Nearly complete renal elimination means half-life can expand dramatically in kidney failure; dose interval and therapeutic levels protect marrow and liver while maintaining antifungal exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline and frequent CBC with differential/platelets, creatinine/eGFR, liver tests, and correctly timed peak/trough levels under protocol. Recalculate promptly when urine output or creatinine changes.

  • Separate multiple capsules across the day exactly as ordered; nausea may improve by dividing each dose over a short period. Escalate fever, bleeding, bruising, mouth ulcers, jaundice, or severe diarrhea.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity and inability to provide renal, CBC, hepatic, and level monitoring are major barriers. Adjust dose/interval for renal impairment.

  • Do not use alone for cryptococcosis because resistance can emerge during therapy.

Interactions

  • Amphotericin improves antifungal efficacy but can injure kidneys; reduced flucytosine clearance then raises marrow toxicity, linking the combination's benefit and monitoring burden.

  • Other marrow suppressants, nephrotoxins, and hepatotoxins compound harm. Cytarabine can antagonize antifungal activity in vitro and complicate marrow toxicity.

Key Labs

  • CBC/differential/platelets, creatinine/eGFR, AST/ALT/bilirubin, flucytosine level when available, CSF/blood culture response, and electrolytes during amphotericin combination.

  • A rising creatinine should trigger immediate exposure reassessment before cytopenias appear because accumulation precedes marrow injury.

Nclex Traps

  • Flucytosine is not fluconazole and is not ordinary chemotherapy, although fungi convert it to a 5-FU-like antimetabolite.

  • Amphotericin plus flucytosine is synergistic; flucytosine monotherapy selects resistance.

Description

Flucytosine is an oral fluorinated cytosine analog that susceptible fungi import through cytosine permease and convert with cytosine deaminase into 5-fluorouracil. Fungal metabolites are incorporated into RNA and inhibit thymidylate synthase, disrupting protein and DNA synthesis. Humans lack cytosine deaminase, creating selectivity, but gut microbes and high systemic exposure can still generate toxic metabolites. The drug is cleared by the kidneys, so declining GFR causes accumulation, severe marrow suppression, hepatic injury, and GI toxicity. It is paired with amphotericin for cryptococcal induction because the combination clears CSF faster and prevents rapid selection of flucytosine-resistant mutants.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, rash, headache, and liver-test elevation.

  • Leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia/aplastic anemia, severe hepatotoxicity, enterocolitis, confusion, and cardiopulmonary toxicity at excessive exposure.
