Definition

Thomas test is a bedside hip-flexion contracture maneuver. It is a clue to hip flexor contracture. A positive finding means the opposite thigh rises or the knee extends depending on the tight structure.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Thomas test means the opposite thigh rises or the knee extends depending on the tight structure.

Why It Matters

A positive Thomas test response can signal hip flexor contracture. Short iliopsoas or rectus femoris cannot lengthen when lumbar compensation is removed. Thomas test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hip flexor contracture

Assessment Method

Flex one hip to flatten lumbar lordosis while the other leg rests on the table.

Positive Finding

The opposite thigh rises or the knee extends depending on the tight structure.

Mechanism

Short iliopsoas or rectus femoris cannot lengthen when lumbar compensation is removed.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Thomas test response raises concern for hip flexor contracture because short iliopsoas or rectus femoris cannot lengthen when lumbar compensation is removed. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thomas test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thomas test to focus evaluation of hip flexor contracture and the reviewed alternatives: fixed hip deformity, lumbar lordosis, pain guarding. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex one hip to flatten lumbar lordosis while the other leg rests on the table. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thomas test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pelvic tilt and lumbar motion create false results. Therefore, Thomas test cannot confirm or exclude hip flexor contracture by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • fixed hip deformity

  • lumbar lordosis

  • pain guarding

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from fixed hip deformity, lumbar lordosis, pain guarding, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Thomas test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current range-of-motion adjunct. When performed as described, Thomas test can adjust concern for hip flexor contracture, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Thomas test: Pelvic tilt and lumbar motion create false results.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Thomas test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex one hip to flatten lumbar lordosis while the other leg rests on the table. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Support the back and do not force the flexed hip against the abdomen.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Thomas test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The opposite thigh rises or the knee extends depending on the tight structure. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex one hip to flatten lumbar lordosis while the other leg rests on the table. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, hip flexor contracture, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
