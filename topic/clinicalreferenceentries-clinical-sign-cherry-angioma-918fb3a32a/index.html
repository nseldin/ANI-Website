Definition

Cherry angioma is a common benign, meaning noncancerous, vascular growth. It usually appears as a small, clearly outlined red-to-purple raised spot caused by proliferating and widened capillaries near the skin surface.

Plain Language

A cherry angioma is a small harmless red or purple blood-vessel spot that becomes more common with age.

Why It Matters

A typical stable cherry angioma has no malignant potential and usually needs no treatment. Rapid change, repeated bleeding, ulceration, an unusual appearance, or uncertainty about the diagnosis requires evaluation rather than assuming every red papule is harmless.

Associated Conditions

  • common acquired benign proliferation of small skin blood vessels that increases with age and most often appears on the trunk or proximal limbs

Assessment Method

Inspect in neutral light and document number, size, color, dome-shaped or flat contour, trunk and limb distribution, blanching, bleeding, tenderness, growth, sudden eruption, and change over time; use dermoscopy or biopsy when a clinician cannot confidently distinguish an atypical lesion.

Positive Finding

One or more sharply outlined 1-to-5-millimeter bright red to violaceous papules are present, usually on the trunk or proximal extremities; most blanch with pressure, although older fibrotic lesions may not blanch completely.

Mechanism

New capillaries proliferate and later dilate within the papillary dermis, forming a small vascular papule.

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pyogenic granuloma, amelanotic melanoma, petechiae, spider angioma.
