Definition

Tullio phenomenon is a mixed provoked-symptom and observed eye-movement sign. It is a clue to third-window inner-ear disorder. A positive finding means loud sound triggers vertigo, oscillopsia, or eye movement.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tullio phenomenon means loud sound triggers vertigo, oscillopsia, or eye movement.

Why It Matters

The provoked Tullio phenomenon symptom can signal third-window inner-ear disorder. An abnormal mobile window lets acoustic energy stimulate vestibular receptors. Tullio phenomenon cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • third-window inner-ear disorder

Assessment Method

In a controlled specialist setting, ask about vertigo or oscillopsia and observe for nystagmus caused by ordinary diagnostic sound.

Positive Finding

Loud sound triggers vertigo, oscillopsia, or eye movement.

Mechanism

An abnormal mobile window lets acoustic energy stimulate vestibular receptors.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Tullio phenomenon symptom raises concern for third-window inner-ear disorder because an abnormal mobile window lets acoustic energy stimulate vestibular receptors. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tullio phenomenon does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tullio phenomenon to focus evaluation of third-window inner-ear disorder and the reviewed alternatives: hyperacusis, panic, middle-ear myoclonus. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: In a controlled specialist setting, ask about vertigo or oscillopsia and observe for nystagmus caused by ordinary diagnostic sound. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tullio phenomenon as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Migraine and startle can cause sound intolerance without vestibular activation. Therefore, Tullio phenomenon cannot confirm or exclude third-window inner-ear disorder by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hyperacusis

  • panic

  • middle-ear myoclonus

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as hyperacusis, panic, middle-ear myoclonus, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Tullio phenomenon may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not expose the patient to dangerously loud sound to provoke symptoms.
