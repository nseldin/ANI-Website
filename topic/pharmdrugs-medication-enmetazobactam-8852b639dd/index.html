Class

Penicillanic-acid-sulfone inhibitor designed to protect cefepime against selected ESBLs

Used To Treat

Used with cefepime in Exblifep for adult complicated urinary tract infection, including pyelonephritis, caused by designated susceptible Gram-negative organisms under the U.S. label.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cefepime neurotoxicity remains a defining safety concern. Enmetazobactam protects against selected enzymes; it does not protect the brain from accumulated cefepime.

Mechanism

Enmetazobactam forms covalent inhibitory complexes with susceptible serine beta-lactamases. Cefepime then traverses Gram-negative porins and binds PBPs, with fT>MIC driving efficacy. Both components are cleared renally, and Exblifep changes infusion duration at augmented renal clearance because fast elimination can reduce exposure. In renal impairment, coordinated dose reduction prevents cefepime accumulation and neurotoxicity. This explains why renal function is checked at least daily when changing: both underexposure and toxic overexposure are plausible.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check current eGFR at least daily when renal function is changing because Exblifep dosing and infusion duration depend on clearance.

  • Assess attention, speech, myoclonus, and level of consciousness because early cefepime neurotoxicity can precede overt seizure.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to cefepime, enmetazobactam, or other beta-lactams is a contraindication under the label.

  • Do not use enmetazobactam alone or assume activity against an unsupported carbapenemase because it lacks dependable standalone bactericidal activity and its enzyme spectrum does not include every KPC, OXA, or metallo-beta-lactamase.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycoside and potent diuretic co-therapy can increase renal risk or require compatibility planning.

  • Urine glucose tests based on copper reduction can yield false positives with some cephalosporins; use glucose oxidase methods when applicable.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, AST/ALT/bilirubin, coagulation when risk exists, and neurologic assessment.

  • A reported ESBL does not by itself prove Exblifep susceptibility; use the final combination MIC and labeled context.

Nclex Traps

  • Enmetazobactam is primarily an ESBL shield for cefepime, not an MBL inhibitor.

  • Augmented renal clearance can require a longer infusion, while renal impairment requires dose reduction; both rules follow exposure.

Display Name

Enmetazobactam

Description

Enmetazobactam is a beta-lactamase inhibitor related to tazobactam that is optimized to protect cefepime from selected class A ESBLs, including important CTX-M enzymes. It has little direct antibacterial activity. Cefepime supplies broad PBP-mediated killing and relative stability against AmpC, while enmetazobactam blocks susceptible ESBL hydrolysis. The combination is not a general carbapenemase drug: it does not reliably solve KPC, OXA-48-like, or metallo-beta-lactamase resistance and should follow the labeled organism and susceptibility framework.

Class Pathway

  • Inhibit selected class A ESBLs

  • Protect cefepime

  • Cefepime binds PBPs and retains AmpC stability

Adverse Effects

  • In trials, increased transaminases or bilirubin, headache, and phlebitis/infusion-site reactions were common important findings.

  • Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity, cefepime-associated encephalopathy/seizure, CDI, prolonged prothrombin time, and positive direct Coombs tests can occur.
