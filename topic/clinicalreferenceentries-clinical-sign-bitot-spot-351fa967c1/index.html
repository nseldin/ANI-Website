Definition

Bitot spot is an observed conjunctival sign. It is a clue to vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia. A positive finding means foamy triangular keratinized patches are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bitot spot means foamy triangular keratinized patches are present.

Why It Matters

The observed Bitot spot finding can signal vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia. Loss of goblet cells and squamous metaplasia allow keratin and debris to accumulate. Bitot spot cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia

Assessment Method

Inspect temporal bulbar conjunctiva.

Positive Finding

Foamy triangular keratinized patches are present.

Mechanism

Loss of goblet cells and squamous metaplasia allow keratin and debris to accumulate.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bitot spot finding raises concern for vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia because loss of goblet cells and squamous metaplasia allow keratin and debris to accumulate. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bitot spot does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bitot spot to focus evaluation of vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia and the reviewed alternatives: pinguecula, ocular-surface keratinization, foreign material. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect temporal bulbar conjunctiva. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bitot spot as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Similar lesions can occur without systemic deficiency. Therefore, Bitot spot cannot confirm or exclude vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pinguecula, ocular-surface keratinization, foreign material.

False Negative Situations

Bitot spot may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia.
