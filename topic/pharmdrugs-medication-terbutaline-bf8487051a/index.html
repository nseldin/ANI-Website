Contraindications

  • Prolonged tocolysis, outpatient or home maintenance tocolysis, serious hypersensitivity, and any patient in whom sympathomimetic cardiac stress is unacceptable.

  • Use extreme caution with structural heart disease, tachyarrhythmia, poorly controlled hyperthyroidism or diabetes, severe preeclampsia, hypokalemia, or concurrent sympathomimetics.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before and after an acute dose, monitor maternal pulse, blood pressure, respiratory status, lung sounds, oxygen saturation, glucose, potassium and fluid balance as indicated, plus fetal rate and uterine activity.

  • Stop and escalate for chest pain, marked tachycardia, arrhythmia, dyspnea, crackles, falling oxygenation, or neurologic symptoms. Do not normalize them as expected beta effects.

Interactions

  • Other sympathomimetics, stimulants, thyroid hormone excess, and MAO inhibitors or tricyclic antidepressants can magnify cardiovascular responses.

  • Non-potassium-sparing diuretics and corticosteroid-related metabolic effects can worsen hypokalemia or hyperglycemia; beta blockers antagonize bronchodilation and may provoke bronchospasm.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, glucose, ECG and cardiac biomarkers when symptomatic, renal function and fluid balance, plus oxygenation and chest imaging if pulmonary edema is suspected

  • Maternal pulse and blood pressure, respiratory exam, contractions, fetal heart rate, treatment duration, fluid input, and neonatal glucose after meaningful peripartum exposure

Nclex Traps

  • A low serum potassium after beta-2 agonism often reflects intracellular shift, so blindly replacing without reassessment can overshoot when the shift reverses.

  • The 48-72 hour boxed limit is not an arbitrary clock: cumulative adrenergic and cardiopulmonary toxicity rises while maintenance tocolysis has not shown enough benefit to justify it.

Used To Treat

Prevention and reversal of bronchospasm in selected patients with asthma or reversible airway disease; limited acute obstetric use can briefly relax the uterus under close inpatient supervision, but prolonged or outpatient maintenance tocolysis is unsafe and not approved.

Mechanism

Terbutaline binds beta-2 adrenergic receptors, which couple to Gs and activate adenylyl cyclase. Rising cAMP activates protein kinase A, which phosphorylates targets that reduce myosin-light-chain kinase activity and calcium sensitivity, relaxing airway and myometrial smooth muscle. In skeletal muscle, beta-2 signaling stimulates sodium-potassium ATPase and moves potassium intracellularly, producing serum hypokalemia without removing total-body potassium. Hepatic and muscular signaling increases glucose production; pancreatic and metabolic effects can later affect neonatal glucose. At higher exposure or in susceptible patients, beta-1 stimulation, reflex tachycardia, and cAMP-driven myocardial demand can provoke dysrhythmia or ischemia. Fluid loading and increased cardiac stress contribute to pulmonary edema in obstetric use.

Boxed Warning

Terbutaline sulfate has a boxed warning against prolonged tocolysis beyond 48 to 72 hours and against outpatient or home maintenance tocolysis. Serious reactions, including maternal death, have occurred. Reported maternal toxicities include tachycardia, transient hyperglycemia, hypokalemia, cardiac arrhythmias, pulmonary edema, and myocardial ischemia; fetal tachycardia and neonatal hypoglycemia can occur. Acute specialist use must have a defined indication, dose, monitoring plan, and stop point. It should never be continued simply because contractions recur after the temporary effect wears off.

Description

Terbutaline is a beta-2 adrenergic agonist that raises cAMP to relax bronchial and uterine smooth muscle. In the airway this opens bronchi; in obstetrics a closely monitored dose can briefly reduce uterine activity in selected acute situations. It is not a safe long-term preterm-labor maintenance drug. Beta-2 receptors also shift potassium into cells and stimulate glycogenolysis, while imperfect selectivity and reflex responses accelerate the heart. The resulting tachycardia, arrhythmia, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, myocardial ischemia, and pulmonary edema explain the boxed time limit. Fetal tachycardia and neonatal hypoglycemia reflect placental transfer and shared adrenergic-metabolic physiology.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor, nervousness, headache, dizziness, palpitations, tachycardia, nausea, hyperglycemia, and hypokalemia

  • Supraventricular or ventricular arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, severe hypertension or hypotension, pulmonary edema, seizure, maternal death, fetal tachycardia, and neonatal hypoglycemia
