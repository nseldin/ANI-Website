Definition

Tropheryma whipplei is a clinically important bacterium associated with classic Whipple disease, malabsorption and weight loss, seronegative migratory arthralgia, and neurologic or cardiac Whipple disease. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Tropheryma whipplei is a Gram-positive bacillus with a unknown oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and facultative intracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

  • Content

    Tropheryma whipplei is associated with classic Whipple disease, malabsorption and weight loss, seronegative migratory arthralgia, and neurologic or cardiac Whipple disease. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
