Class

CYP17 androgen-biosynthesis inhibitor used with corticosteroid therapy

Description

Abiraterone acetate is converted to abiraterone, which suppresses androgen production in the testes, adrenal glands, and prostate-tumor tissue. This matters because metastatic prostate cancers can continue using low concentrations of androgen even after testicular testosterone is suppressed. Blocking CYP17 lowers that remaining androgen signal, but it also diverts steroid precursors toward mineralocorticoids. The same mechanism that slows the cancer can therefore cause hypertension, hypokalemia, and fluid retention, which is why prednisone and close monitoring are part of therapy rather than optional add-ons.

Mechanism

Abiraterone irreversibly inhibits CYP17A1, including 17-alpha-hydroxylase and C17,20-lyase activity. These reactions are required to make androgen precursors. Reduced androgen synthesis weakens androgen-receptor signaling in prostate-cancer cells. Upstream steroid precursors then accumulate and increase mineralocorticoid activity; glucocorticoid feedback also falls. Prednisone replaces part of that glucocorticoid signal, reduces ACTH drive, and limits mineralocorticoid excess. Sudden interruption of corticosteroid support during stress or infection can expose adrenocortical insufficiency.

Used To Treat

In combination with prednisone, metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer and metastatic high-risk castration-sensitive prostate cancer. The patient must also continue a gonadotropin-releasing hormone analog unless bilateral orchiectomy has already removed the testicular androgen source.

Boxed Warning

Abiraterone has no boxed warning in the referenced U.S. label, but mineralocorticoid excess, adrenocortical insufficiency, hepatotoxicity, embryo-fetal toxicity, severe hypoglycemia with selected diabetes drugs, and increased fractures and mortality with radium Ra 223 are major label hazards.

Contraindications

  • The referenced generic label lists no formal contraindication, but baseline severe hepatic impairment is a do-not-use condition because exposure and hepatotoxicity risk are excessive.

  • This card covers the Zytiga-type abiraterone acetate regimen with prednisone. Do not apply its milligram, food, or corticosteroid instructions to Yonsa, which has different formulation-specific labeling. Pregnancy exposure and handling by a pregnant person can harm a fetus.

Adverse Effects

  • Fatigue, joint discomfort, hypertension, edema, hot flushes, diarrhea, cough, nausea, and urinary symptoms.

  • Hypokalemia, fluid retention, arrhythmia or heart-failure worsening, adrenocortical insufficiency, hepatotoxicity, severe hypoglycemia, fracture, and fetal harm.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inducers can lower abiraterone exposure; avoid them or follow the exact label adjustment when no alternative exists.

  • Abiraterone inhibits CYP2D6 and CYP2C8. Narrow-therapeutic-index substrates and diabetes drugs such as pioglitazone or repaglinide require review because exposure or hypoglycemia can increase.

  • Do not combine routinely with radium Ra 223 because clinical studies found more fractures and deaths.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify that the product uses the Zytiga-type abiraterone acetate regimen, then confirm its food instruction, prednisone regimen, and ongoing medical or surgical castration before the first dose. Do not inherit these instructions into Yonsa therapy.

  • Correct hypokalemia and control blood pressure before treatment. Monitor blood pressure, potassium, edema, weight, heart-failure symptoms, and rhythm risk because mineralocorticoid excess can destabilize cardiovascular disease.

  • Assess liver tests before treatment and at label-defined intervals. Hold and escalate for jaundice, marked aminotransferase elevation, severe weakness, hypotension, or symptoms of adrenal crisis.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, blood pressure, weight and edema assessment

  • ALT, AST, bilirubin before and during therapy

  • Glucose in patients using glucose-lowering medicines

  • PSA and disease-directed imaging or clinical response

Administration

  • Administer once daily with the formulation-specific food instruction and prescribed prednisone. Swallow tablets whole.

  • Continue the GnRH analog unless the patient has had bilateral orchiectomy. Do not stop prednisone abruptly without oncology direction.

Patient Education

  • Report muscle weakness, palpitations, rapid weight gain, edema, shortness of breath, jaundice, dark urine, severe fatigue, dizziness, or symptoms of low glucose promptly.

  • Use contraception and follow handling precautions. Keep every oncology, blood-pressure, potassium, and liver-monitoring appointment.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not give abiraterone as isolated androgen blockade: verify the ordered prednisone and continued GnRH suppression unless orchiectomy is documented.

  • Do not transfer this Zytiga-type food, dose, or prednisone teaching to Yonsa; product-specific instructions differ.

Routes

  • Oral tablet; food instructions are formulation-specific and this card covers the Zytiga-type regimen.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Severe hepatotoxicity or liver failure.

  • Adrenocortical insufficiency, severe hypokalemia, ventricular arrhythmia, or heart-failure decompensation from mineralocorticoid excess.

Toxicity Management

  • There is no specific antidote. Hold further doses, obtain urgent oncology or poison-control guidance, and provide supportive monitoring of rhythm, blood pressure, potassium, glucose, fluid status, and liver injury.

Special Populations

  • Embryo-fetal harm is expected from androgen-pathway inhibition; it is not indicated for women, pregnant handlers should avoid direct contact with damaged tablets, and treated males need label-directed contraception.

  • Avoid baseline severe hepatic impairment and use label-directed reduction and intensified liver monitoring in moderate impairment; renal impairment usually does not require an adjustment, but cardiovascular and electrolyte vulnerability is greater in frail older adults.

  • Safety and effectiveness are not established in pediatric patients.
