Class

Topical all-trans retinoic acid; pan-RAR comedolytic

Used To Treat

Acne vulgaris; selected formulation-specific products are used with comprehensive photoprotection for fine facial wrinkling and mottled hyperpigmentation.

Description

Topical tretinoin is an all-trans retinoic acid retinoid that binds nuclear RARs in keratinocytes to normalize follicular epithelial turnover and clear microcomedones. It decreases cohesion of keratinocytes in the follicular opening, increases extrusion of existing comedones, and changes RAR-controlled transcription. It must be separated conceptually from oral tretinoin used for acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL): the same molecule has radically different exposure, indication, and life-threatening systemic toxicities by route.

Mechanism

Tretinoin diffuses into keratinocytes and binds RAR-alpha, beta, and gamma. RAR-RXR complexes bind DNA response elements and regulate genes controlling proliferation and differentiation. In acne, reduced follicular-cell cohesion and accelerated turnover open the microcomedo and prevent new plugs; anti-inflammatory effects reduce lesion progression. Conventional tretinoin is photolabile and can be oxidized by benzoyl peroxide, so formulation and administration timing matter.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Topical tretinoin commonly causes erythema, peeling, dryness, burning and marked photosensitivity. Avoid eczematous, sunburned or abraded skin, protect from sunlight, and avoid simultaneous harsh keratolytics. Pregnancy requires clinician review; do not confuse topical counseling with the systemic APL drug's boxed-warning profile.

Adverse Effects

  • Retinoid dermatitis with erythema, peeling, burning, stinging, dryness and pruritus

  • Photosensitivity and exaggerated sunburn

  • Temporary hyperpigmentation or hypopigmentation and an early acne flare

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to tretinoin or vehicle components

  • Avoid application to eyes, lips, mucosa, eczema, abrasions or sunburn

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation and concurrent photosensitizing or highly irritating therapy

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a pea-sized thin film to dry acne-prone skin after gentle cleansing; wet skin can increase penetration and irritation.

  • Teach that benefit takes weeks and that maintenance prevents new microcomedones rather than acting as an overnight spot treatment.

Interactions

  • Conventional tretinoin can be oxidized by benzoyl peroxide; separate morning/evening unless the specific formulation is compatible.

  • Sulfur, resorcinol, salicylic acid, abrasive cleansers, alcohol/astringents and other retinoids add irritation.

  • Photosensitizing drugs and UV therapy increase burn risk.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level

  • Pregnancy assessment when clinically relevant

  • Serial acne morphology, irritation, pigmentation and photoprotection assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Topical tretinoin for acne is not oral ATRA for leukemia.

  • Dryness is expected, but escalating the dose through severe dermatitis damages the barrier and worsens adherence.

  • Benzoyl peroxide timing depends on the tretinoin formulation.
