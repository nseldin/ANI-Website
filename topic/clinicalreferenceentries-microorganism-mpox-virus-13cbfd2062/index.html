Definition

Mpox virus is a clinically important virus associated with mpox, mucocutaneous lesions, proctitis, and ocular or neurologic complications. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Avoiding direct lesion and body-fluid contact, healthcare precautions, contact tracing, and risk-based vaccination under current guidance reduce transmission. The reviewed route description is contact transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 3 to 17 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Mpox virus enters through skin, respiratory mucosa, or other mucosa, spreads to regional lymphatic tissue, and can produce systemic and mucocutaneous disease. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Mpox virus is associated with mpox, mucocutaneous lesions, proctitis, and ocular or neurologic complications. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
