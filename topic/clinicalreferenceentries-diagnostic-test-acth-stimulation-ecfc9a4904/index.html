Summary

ACTH stimulation (adrenocorticotropic hormone stimulation test) is a high-yield Endocrine diagnostic reference. Dynamic test assessing adrenal cortisol response to ACTH.

Quick Answer

ACTH stimulation (adrenocorticotropic hormone stimulation test): Dynamic test assessing adrenal cortisol response to ACTH. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Adequate cortisol rise

    Meaning

    The adrenal glands respond to ACTH, making clinically significant primary adrenal insufficiency less likely.

  • Label

    Blunted cortisol rise

    Meaning

    Inadequate cortisol response supports adrenal insufficiency and requires urgent context if the client is hypotensive, hyperkalemic, hyponatremic, or acutely ill.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Timing and baseline steroid exposure matter. Do not delay emergency steroid treatment for adrenal crisis when unstable.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Dynamic test assessing adrenal cortisol response to ACTH.

    • Before / preparation

    • Check timing, fasting status, current endocrine medications, steroid use, pregnancy status when relevant, and whether dynamic testing requires serial specimens.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect the result to symptoms, glucose safety, thyroid/adrenal crisis risk, and medication teaching.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Severe hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, thyroid storm/myxedema cues, adrenal crisis, altered mental status, dehydration, or unstable vital signs.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Used for adrenal insufficiency patterns; timing and steroid medication history matter.
