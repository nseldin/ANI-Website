Definition

Seidel test is a named fluorescein eye test. It is a clue to aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound. A positive finding means clear fluid dilutes the orange dye into a bright green streaming plume.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Seidel test means clear fluid dilutes the orange dye into a bright green streaming plume.

Why It Matters

A positive Seidel test result can signal aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound. Escaping aqueous humor washes concentrated fluorescein away from the wound. Seidel test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound

Assessment Method

Apply fluorescein without pressure and examine with cobalt-blue light.

Positive Finding

Clear fluid dilutes the orange dye into a bright green streaming plume.

Mechanism

Escaping aqueous humor washes concentrated fluorescein away from the wound.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Seidel test result raises concern for aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound because escaping aqueous humor washes concentrated fluorescein away from the wound. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Seidel test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Seidel test to focus evaluation of aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound and the reviewed alternatives: tear flow, contact-lens fluid, conjunctival laceration. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply fluorescein without pressure and examine with cobalt-blue light. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Seidel test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Small self-sealed wounds and tissue plugging can be negative. Therefore, Seidel test cannot confirm or exclude aqueous humor leakage through a corneal or scleral wound by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tear flow

  • contact-lens fluid

  • conjunctival laceration

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as tear flow, contact-lens fluid, conjunctival laceration.

False Negative Situations

Seidel test may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.

Modern Status

Current ocular-emergency test; false negatives occur. Interpret Seidel test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Small self-sealed wounds and tissue plugging can be negative.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Seidel test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply fluorescein without pressure and examine with cobalt-blue light. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not press the eye, measure pressure, or manipulate a suspected open globe; shield and escalate.
