Definition

Thessaly test is a weight-bearing meniscal maneuver. It is a clue to meniscal tear. A positive finding means joint-line pain, catching, or locking is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Thessaly test means joint-line pain, catching, or locking is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Thessaly test response can signal meniscal tear. Body weight compresses the meniscus while tibiofemoral rotation displaces an unstable torn segment between the articular surfaces, producing pain or catching. Thessaly test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • meniscal tear

Assessment Method

With support, have the patient stand on one leg with the knee slightly flexed and rotate body and knee.

Positive Finding

Joint-line pain, catching, or locking is reproduced.

Mechanism

Body weight compresses the meniscus while tibiofemoral rotation displaces an unstable torn segment between the articular surfaces, producing pain or catching.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Thessaly test response raises concern for meniscal tear because body weight compresses the meniscus while tibiofemoral rotation displaces an unstable torn segment between the articular surfaces, producing pain or catching. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thessaly test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thessaly test to focus evaluation of meniscal tear and the reviewed alternatives: patellofemoral pain, MCL injury, loose body. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With support, have the patient stand on one leg with the knee slightly flexed and rotate body and knee. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thessaly test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, balance, arthritis, and fear cause false positives. Therefore, Thessaly test cannot confirm or exclude meniscal tear by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • patellofemoral pain

  • MCL injury

  • loose body

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from patellofemoral pain, MCL injury, loose body, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Thessaly test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; often impractical in acute injury. When performed as described, Thessaly test can adjust concern for meniscal tear, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Thessaly test: Pain, balance, arthritis, and fear cause false positives.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Thessaly test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With support, have the patient stand on one leg with the knee slightly flexed and rotate body and knee. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Provide hands-on support and avoid when weight bearing is unsafe.
