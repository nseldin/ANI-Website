Definition

Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea is an observed drainage sign. It is a clue to cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak. A positive finding means a gush of clear fluid follows positional change.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea means a gush of clear fluid follows positional change.

Why It Matters

The observed Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea finding can signal cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak. CSF pools in a sinus or nasal cavity while supine and drains when gravity changes. Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak

Assessment Method

Observe whether clear unilateral nasal drainage increases after sitting up or leaning forward, without deliberate provocation.

Positive Finding

A gush of clear fluid follows positional change.

Mechanism

CSF pools in a sinus or nasal cavity while supine and drains when gravity changes.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea finding raises concern for cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak because cSF pools in a sinus or nasal cavity while supine and drains when gravity changes. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Watery rhinitis can behave similarly and leaks may be intermittent. Therefore, Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea cannot confirm or exclude cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tears, saline irrigation, vasomotor rhinitis.

False Negative Situations

Reservoir sign of CSF rhinorrhea may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of cranial cerebrospinal fluid leak.
