Used To Treat

Serious infections caused by penicillinase-producing, methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus and other susceptible staphylococci, including bacteremia, endocarditis, osteoarticular, and deep infection when the site and patient fit.

Mechanism

The drug's side chain blocks access of many staphylococcal beta-lactamases to the ring while leaving the carbonyl available to PBPs. Once bound, transpeptidation stops and active wall remodeling causes lysis. High protein binding and nonrenal clearance shape exposure. Nafcillin also induces hepatic drug-metabolizing pathways, which can lower warfarin effect and concentrations of selected co-medications. Biliary elimination explains why hepatic injury or cholestasis can matter even when creatinine is normal; sodium content and frequent IV dosing explain fluid and electrolyte burden in vulnerable patients.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity, cholestatic/hepatocellular injury, neutropenia, interstitial nephritis, hypokalemia, phlebitis, and sodium load require active monitoring during prolonged high-dose therapy.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to nafcillin or related beta-lactams requires phenotype-specific avoidance or specialist management.

  • Do not continue nafcillin for MRSA or an isolate reported oxacillin resistant because the resistance usually reflects PBP2a, not penicillinase.

Nursing Essentials

  • For MSSA bacteremia, repeat cultures until clearance and investigate line, valve, bone, joint, spine, and implanted-device sources because correct beta-lactam choice cannot compensate for an infected focus.

  • Trend liver tests, CBC, potassium, renal function, sodium/fluid status, and IV sites during prolonged therapy because toxicity often develops after the opening doses.

Interactions

  • Nafcillin can induce warfarin metabolism and lower INR, with anticoagulant requirements changing again after nafcillin stops; trend rather than making one permanent dose change.

  • Do not physically mix aminoglycosides with nafcillin unless compatibility is validated because beta-lactams can inactivate aminoglycosides in solution.

Key Labs

  • Blood or source cultures, CBC with differential, AST/ALT/alkaline phosphatase/bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, sodium and fluid status, and serial cultures for invasive S. aureus.

  • A persistently positive blood culture is evidence of uncontrolled source, metastatic infection, inadequate exposure, or resistance and requires immediate reassessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Nafcillin treats MSSA, not MRSA.

  • Predominantly nonrenal elimination does not mean no monitoring; liver injury, sodium, potassium, cytopenias, and mixed organ failure still matter.

Class Pathway

  • Bulky side-chain beta-lactamase resistance

  • High-affinity MSSA PBP binding

  • Narrow definitive IV therapy

Description

Nafcillin is a narrow IV, penicillinase-resistant beta-lactam antibiotic that binds native staphylococcal PBPs and blocks cell-wall cross-linking in susceptible MSSA; its bulky naphthyl side chain protects the beta-lactam ring from staphylococcal penicillinase. This combination of enzyme stability and native-PBP binding makes nafcillin a definitive therapy for serious MSSA infection. It does not cover MRSA because mecA/mecC produces low-affinity PBP2a; preventing beta-lactamase hydrolysis cannot fix an altered target. Nafcillin undergoes substantial hepatic/biliary elimination, so isolated renal failure often requires less adjustment than many beta-lactams, but combined liver/kidney dysfunction, high sodium exposure, and prolonged treatment still require close review.

Adverse Effects

  • Rash, anaphylaxis, diarrhea/CDI, phlebitis, nausea, transaminase or bilirubin elevation, cholestatic hepatitis, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and hemolytic anemia.

  • Interstitial nephritis, rising creatinine, hypokalemia from renal potassium wasting, sodium/volume burden, and neurotoxicity at excessive exposure can occur.
