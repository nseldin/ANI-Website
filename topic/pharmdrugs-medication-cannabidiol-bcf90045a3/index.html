Class

Purified plant-derived cannabinoid antiseizure medication

Used To Treat

Seizures associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome, or tuberous sclerosis complex in appropriately aged clients.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for cannabidiol in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Cannabidiol's human antiseizure mechanism is not established. Proposed effects include modulation of intracellular calcium, adenosine signaling, and excitatory/inhibitory transmission, but current labeling specifically notes that cannabinoid-receptor activation does not appear to explain the anticonvulsant effect.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline and scheduled ALT/AST/bilirubin, with closer surveillance when valproate or clobazam is used; jaundice, dark urine, RUQ pain, vomiting, or profound fatigue require evaluation.

  • Give consistently with respect to food because high-fat meals can multiply exposure; measure with the supplied oral syringe and monitor sedation, diarrhea, appetite/weight, suicidality, and seizure change.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, total bilirubin, antiseizure drug/interactions as indicated, weight/nutrition, and seizure diary.

Nclex Traps

  • Cannabidiol raises the active clobazam metabolite through CYP2C19 inhibition and liver risk rises sharply with valproate.

Description

Prescription cannabidiol is a purified antiseizure oral solution that reduces seizures through a still-unresolved mechanism that does not appear to depend on CB1 or CB2 cannabinoid-receptor activation; it is not interchangeable with retail CBD products. Proposed effects involve intracellular calcium, adenosine, and excitatory-inhibitory signaling, while practical mastery centers on dose-related liver injury, major food effects on exposure, sedation, and interactions with valproate and clobazam.
