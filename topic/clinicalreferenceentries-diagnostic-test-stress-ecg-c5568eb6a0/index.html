Summary

Stress ECG is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Assesses cardiac response to exercise or medication stress for ischemia or exertional symptoms.

Quick Answer

Stress ECG: Assesses cardiac response to exercise or medication stress for ischemia or exertional symptoms. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    No ischemic ECG changes or concerning symptoms occurred at the achieved workload/target; this lowers suspicion but depends on test adequacy.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    Reproducible chest pain, ischemic ST changes, hypotension, serious dysrhythmia, or poor exercise tolerance suggests inducible ischemia or unsafe cardiac response.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    A nondiagnostic or submaximal test is not the same as a normal test. Symptoms during or after testing need immediate assessment.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Assesses cardiac response to exercise or medication stress for ischemia or exertional symptoms.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess chest pain, perfusion, rhythm, oxygenation, allergies, anticoagulant status, kidney function if contrast is possible, and baseline vital signs.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect cardiac test results to perfusion, ischemia, dysrhythmia, heart failure, and immediate safety priorities.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Chest pain with ECG changes, rising cardiac biomarkers, unstable dysrhythmia, hypotension, syncope, pulmonary edema, or post-procedure bleeding.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Treat the client and the trend, not one pretty tracing or one isolated number.
