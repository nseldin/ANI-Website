Contraindications

  • For the reproductive regimen: ectopic pregnancy, chronic adrenal failure, long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy, bleeding disorder or concurrent anticoagulant when prohibited, inherited porphyria, IUD in place until removed, and inability to access emergency care.

  • For KORLYM: pregnancy, use of drugs essential for survival that have narrow ranges and are CYP3A substrates, long-term corticosteroid need, unexplained vaginal bleeding or endometrial pathology, and strong CYP3A interaction contexts.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify product and indication before every dose. Document gestational age and intrauterine location assessment when indicated, misoprostol plan, emergency contact, bleeding thresholds and follow-up confirmation.

  • For KORLYM, titrate to glucose and clinical cortisol effects rather than serum cortisol. Monitor potassium, blood pressure, edema, fatigue, nausea, hypoglycemia after diabetes-drug continuation, and signs of excessive glucocorticoid blockade.

Interactions

  • Mifepristone is a CYP3A substrate and a strong inhibitor of CYP3A with additional CYP2C8/2C9 effects; interactions can persist because its half-life is long.

  • It antagonizes corticosteroids, can raise concentrations of narrow-therapeutic-index CYP3A substrates, and adds QT risk with hypokalemia or QT-prolonging drugs. Anticoagulants magnify reproductive bleeding risk.

Key Labs

  • Reproductive use: pregnancy dating/location and hemoglobin/Rh evaluation as indicated plus follow-up confirmation; KORLYM: potassium, glucose, renal/liver function, ECG risk and endometrial assessment

  • Bleeding amount, pain trajectory, fever/malaise/syncope, pregnancy symptoms; or during chronic use, blood pressure, edema, adrenal-blockade symptoms, glucose, thyroid function and abnormal uterine bleeding

Nclex Traps

  • Mifepristone blocks progesterone support; misoprostol supplies the prostaglandin contraction. Neither treats ectopic pregnancy.

  • In KORLYM treatment, cortisol may remain high because the receptor, not cortisol synthesis, is blocked; assess clinical response and toxicity rather than chasing a cortisol level.

Used To Treat

With misoprostol, medication termination of an intrauterine pregnancy through 70 days gestation using the 200-mg reproductive product; the distinct 300-mg KORLYM product controls hyperglycemia secondary to hypercortisolism in selected adults with endogenous Cushing syndrome who have type 2 diabetes or glucose intolerance and failed or cannot undergo surgery.

Mechanism

Mifepristone binds progesterone receptors with high affinity and prevents progesterone-dependent transcription. Decidual capillaries and tissue lose hormonal support, endogenous prostaglandin release rises, the cervix remodels, and myometrium becomes more responsive to prostaglandins. Buccal misoprostol 24 to 48 hours later activates EP receptors and produces cervical opening and uterine contraction. Mifepristone also competitively antagonizes glucocorticoid receptors. In Cushing syndrome, this blocks cortisol signaling downstream, reducing hepatic glucose output and insulin resistance despite persistent or rising serum cortisol and ACTH; therefore cortisol levels cannot be used to titrate clinical response. Excess glucocorticoid blockade can cause adrenal-insufficiency physiology without a low cortisol measurement, and progesterone blockade thickens endometrium or causes bleeding during chronic KORLYM use.

Boxed Warning

Mifepristone 200-mg reproductive labeling carries a boxed warning for rare serious and sometimes fatal infection or bleeding. Sepsis can present atypically without fever, bacteremia, or striking pelvic findings; maintain suspicion when severe abdominal pain, persistent malaise, weakness, nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea continues more than 24 hours after misoprostol. Heavy prolonged bleeding can require urgent medical or surgical treatment. The product is distributed through the Mifepristone REMS Program, and follow-up must confirm completion. KORLYM has a separate boxed warning that its progesterone antagonism will terminate pregnancy; pregnancy must be excluded before treatment and prevented with nonhormonal contraception.

Class Pathway

  • Reproductive and endocrine medication

  • Progesterone/glucocorticoid receptor antagonist

  • Decidual destabilization or cortisol-receptor blockade by regimen

Description

Mifepristone blocks progesterone receptors, withdrawing hormonal support from decidual tissue and sensitizing the cervix and myometrium to prostaglandins; at higher chronic exposure it also blocks glucocorticoid receptors and reduces cortisol signaling in selected patients with Cushing syndrome. In the 200-mg reproductive regimen, decidual destabilization and cervical softening prepare the uterus for misoprostol-driven contraction and evacuation. In the distinct KORLYM regimen, glucocorticoid-receptor blockade can improve cortisol-driven hyperglycemia even though measured cortisol remains high because cortisol production itself is not inhibited. The products, doses, distribution rules, interactions and monitoring are not interchangeable. Mifepristone does not treat ectopic pregnancy. After reproductive use, prolonged heavy bleeding, syncope, persistent severe pain, fever or malaise may signal hemorrhage, incomplete abortion, ectopic pregnancy or serious infection and requires a defined emergency pathway.

Adverse Effects

  • Cramping, bleeding, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, dizziness, fatigue, endometrial thickening, and altered potassium

  • Hemorrhage, incomplete or ongoing pregnancy, serious infection/sepsis, adrenal-insufficiency physiology, severe hypokalemia, QT prolongation, endometrial hyperplasia/bleeding, and major CYP3A interaction toxicity
