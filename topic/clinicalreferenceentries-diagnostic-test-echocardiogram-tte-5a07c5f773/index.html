Summary

Echocardiogram (TTE) (transthoracic echocardiogram) is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Ultrasound of the heart that evaluates valves, ejection fraction, wall motion, and pericardial fluid.

Quick Answer

Echocardiogram (TTE) (transthoracic echocardiogram): Ultrasound of the heart that evaluates valves, ejection fraction, wall motion, and pericardial fluid. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Ultrasound of the heart that evaluates valves, ejection fraction, wall motion, and pericardial fluid.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess chest pain, perfusion, rhythm, oxygenation, allergies, anticoagulant status, kidney function if contrast is possible, and baseline vital signs.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect cardiac test results to perfusion, ischemia, dysrhythmia, heart failure, and immediate safety priorities.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Chest pain with ECG changes, rising cardiac biomarkers, unstable dysrhythmia, hypotension, syncope, pulmonary edema, or post-procedure bleeding.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Treat the client and the trend, not one pretty tracing or one isolated number.
