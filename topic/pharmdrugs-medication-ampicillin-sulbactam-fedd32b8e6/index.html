Used To Treat

Susceptible polymicrobial skin and skin-structure, intra-abdominal, and gynecologic infections under current labeling, with broader specialist use guided by site, cultures, local susceptibility, source control, and renal function. It provides gram-positive, selected gram-negative, and anaerobic activity but is not a dependable MRSA or Pseudomonas regimen and should not be treated as universally broad.

Mechanism

Ampicillin crosses accessible bacterial envelopes and covalently acylates PBPs, preventing transpeptidase cross-linking of peptidoglycan. Continued autolysis then weakens the wall and causes bactericidal lysis during active growth. Sulbactam acts as a mechanism-based beta-lactamase inhibitor: selected serine enzymes attack its beta-lactam ring and become trapped in inactive complexes, preserving ampicillin long enough to reach PBPs. Sulbactam itself binds selected Acinetobacter PBPs, especially PBP1 and PBP3, which explains why it can contribute direct activity in that organism. Neither component overcomes every ESBL, AmpC, carbapenemase, porin, efflux, or altered-PBP mechanism.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious or fatal beta-lactam anaphylaxis and severe cutaneous reactions can occur. Previous ampicillin-sulbactam-associated cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction is a contraindication. Clostridioides difficile diarrhea can emerge during treatment or more than two months later. Both components accumulate in renal impairment; excessive beta-lactam CNS exposure can cause encephalopathy, myoclonus or convulsions, so changing kidney function requires prompt interval reassessment rather than waiting for a scheduled review.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to ampicillin, sulbactam, penicillins, cephalosporins, or another relevant beta-lactam, including anaphylaxis or severe cutaneous reaction.

  • Previous cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction associated with ampicillin-sulbactam; renal impairment is not an absolute contraindication but requires exact interval adjustment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain cultures and assess source control, allergy phenotype, renal trajectory, liver history, CBC, and infection severity before therapy when feasible. Recalculate renal dosing when creatinine changes because the half-life of both components rises together.

  • Verify the total combination dose and component ratio: a 3-g dose contains 2 g ampicillin plus 1 g sulbactam. Inspect the prepared solution, follow route-specific stability, and administer aminoglycosides separately because aminopenicillins can inactivate them in the same solution.

Interactions

  • Probenecid reduces renal tubular secretion and prolongs both components; allopurinol with ampicillin increases rash frequency, and oral anticoagulant effect can shift during infection or antibiotic exposure.

  • Do not reconstitute or co-mix with an aminoglycoside because direct contact can inactivate the aminoglycoside. Separate preparation and administration preserve the activity of both ordered drugs.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, serum creatinine or calculated creatinine clearance, CBC, AST/ALT, bilirubin, treatment-response markers, and neurologic assessment when renal function is impaired.

  • For new diarrhea, cytopenia, jaundice, rash, or mental-status change, assess C. difficile, hemolysis or Coombs context, hepatic injury, renal accumulation, and other neurotoxic medications.

Nclex Traps

  • Sulbactam protects ampicillin and has special Acinetobacter PBP activity, but Unasyn does not automatically cover every resistant Acinetobacter, MRSA, or Pseudomonas isolate.

  • The vial strength is the combined weight, not the ampicillin amount alone. Renal impairment usually lengthens the interval, and aminoglycosides must be prepared separately.

Description

Ampicillin sulbactam is a parenteral two-part beta-lactam antibiotic in which ampicillin binds PBPs and blocks bacterial cell-wall cross-linking while sulbactam irreversibly inhibits selected beta-lactamases that would otherwise destroy ampicillin. Sulbactam also has organism-specific direct PBP activity against Acinetobacter, but modern resistant Acinetobacter treatment depends on susceptibility, optimized sulbactam exposure, and current specialist guidance rather than the drug name alone. Both components are cleared largely by the kidney, so falling renal function lengthens exposure and raises beta-lactam neurotoxicity risk unless the interval is adjusted.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, rash, injection-site pain, thrombophlebitis, candidal overgrowth, transient liver-test elevation, and positive direct Coombs testing can occur.

  • Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN/AGEP, C. difficile colitis, cholestatic hepatitis or jaundice, cytopenias, interstitial nephritis, seizures or encephalopathy with accumulation, and severe line or infusion reactions.
