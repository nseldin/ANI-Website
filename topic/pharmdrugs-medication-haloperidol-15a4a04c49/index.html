Used To Treat

Psychotic disorders and severe agitation; oral labeling also includes Tourette motor/vocal tics and narrowly defined severe pediatric behavior indications after other approaches fail. Long-acting decanoate is maintenance therapy after oral tolerability, not rapid agitation control.

Mechanism

Competitive D2 antagonism in the mesolimbic pathway lowers pathologically excessive dopamine signaling and reduces positive psychotic symptoms. The same D2 blockade in nigrostriatal neurons removes dopamine's inhibition of striatal acetylcholine, producing dystonia, akathisia and parkinsonism; chronic receptor adaptation contributes to tardive dyskinesia. Tuberoinfundibular blockade removes dopamine's tonic brake on prolactin. Haloperidol also blocks cardiac hERG potassium current at clinically important exposure, delaying repolarization and explaining QT prolongation and torsades risk, especially with IV use, high doses, low potassium/magnesium or interacting drugs.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Haloperidol is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. QT prolongation, torsades and sudden death have occurred, especially with IV administration or excessive doses; IV use is not FDA-approved. Fever, severe rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability suggest NMS and require immediate cessation/escalation.

Contraindications

  • Severe toxic CNS depression or coma, Parkinson disease and product hypersensitivity

  • Avoid or tightly control major QT risk, low potassium/magnesium and prohibited QT combinations

  • Do not use decanoate until tolerability and a maintenance strategy are established

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline AIMS/EPS status and repeat it; ask about inner restlessness because akathisia is often mislabeled anxiety or agitation.

  • For acute dystonia, protect the airway and give ordered diphenhydramine or benztropine promptly; for suspected NMS, stop dopamine blockers and escalate immediately.

Interactions

  • Metoclopramide and other D2 blockers add EPS/NMS risk.

  • QT-prolonging drugs, hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia add torsades risk.

  • CNS depressants and antihypertensives increase sedation/hypotension; CYP3A4/2D6 modifiers can alter exposure.

Key Labs

  • AIMS and focused EPS examination

  • ECG plus potassium and magnesium when risk warrants

  • Prolactin symptoms, CK/renal function in suspected NMS and metabolic baseline

Nclex Traps

  • Pacing can be akathisia, not psychotic agitation.

  • High potency predicts more EPS/prolactin and fewer anticholinergic effects than chlorpromazine, not zero orthostasis or sedation.

  • Haloperidol decanoate cannot replace rapid-acting treatment in an emergency.

Description

Haloperidol is a high-potency first-generation antipsychotic that suppresses hallucinations, delusions and severe behavioral disorganization through strong D2-receptor blockade. Because it has less H1, muscarinic and alpha-1 activity than low-potency chlorpromazine, sedation and orthostasis may be less prominent, but acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, hyperprolactinemia, tardive dyskinesia and neuroleptic malignant syndrome are more central risks. Potency means fewer milligrams are needed for D2 occupancy; it does not mean better final efficacy.

Adverse Effects

  • Acute dystonia, akathisia, drug-induced parkinsonism and tardive dyskinesia

  • Hyperprolactinemia with amenorrhea, galactorrhea, sexual dysfunction or bone effects

  • QT prolongation, torsades, hypotension and sudden death

  • NMS, seizures, sedation and temperature dysregulation
