Pronunciation

is-KEE-mee-uh

Plain Meaning

Inadequate blood flow to tissue, causing reduced oxygen delivery, reduced nutrient delivery, and impaired waste removal.

Word Parts

  • Root

    isch-

    Meaning

    to hold back or restrain

  • Root

    -emia

    Meaning

    blood condition

Word Origin

Ischemia literally points toward blood being held back. Clinically, it is a perfusion problem, not just a low oxygen number.

Pathology

Ischemia deprives tissue of oxygen and metabolic substrate while allowing acids and waste products to accumulate. ATP falls, ion pumps fail, cells swell, lactate rises, and membrane injury progresses if perfusion is not restored. Reversible ischemia can recover; prolonged ischemia causes infarction and necrosis.

Etiology

Causes include arterial thrombosis, embolus, severe atherosclerotic narrowing, vasospasm, shock, compartment syndrome, strangulated bowel, torsion, severe anemia with supply-demand mismatch, and pressure-related vessel compression.

Signs Symptoms

  • Pain, pallor, coolness, weak or absent pulses, paresthesia, paralysis, or delayed capillary refill in limb ischemia

  • Chest pressure, ECG changes, dyspnea, diaphoresis, or troponin rise in myocardial ischemia

  • Neurologic deficit in cerebral ischemia

  • Abdominal pain out of proportion can suggest mesenteric ischemia

Diagnostics

  • Assess perfusion: pulses, capillary refill, skin temperature/color, pain, sensation, motor function, urine output, mental status, and lactate when systemic

  • ECG/troponin, vascular ultrasound, CT angiography, ankle-brachial index, or neuroimaging are selected by organ system

  • Rising lactate can signal anaerobic metabolism but must be interpreted with the whole patient

Treatments

  • Restore perfusion when possible: oxygenation support, fluids or vasopressors for shock as ordered, antithrombotic therapy, thrombolysis, revascularization, fasciotomy, or surgery depending on cause

  • Reduce demand, protect tissue, control pain, and treat rhythm, blood pressure, glucose, or oxygen problems

Nursing Priorities

  • Treat ischemia as time-sensitive because reversible injury can become infarction

  • Escalate sudden neuro deficit, chest pain with ischemic signs, acute limb changes, severe abdominal pain, or falling urine output with shock cues

  • Avoid heat or massage over suspected acute limb ischemia or DVT

Complications

  • Infarction

  • Necrosis

  • Arrhythmia

  • Stroke

  • Acute kidney injury

  • Bowel necrosis

  • Limb loss

  • Reperfusion injury

Patient Education

  • Seek emergency care for stroke signs, crushing chest discomfort, sudden cold/pale painful limb, or severe abdominal pain.

  • Control smoking, diabetes, hypertension, lipids, and prescribed antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Ischemia is not only hypoxia; it is inadequate blood flow plus impaired delivery and waste removal.

  • Pain out of proportion is a major warning sign in compartment syndrome and mesenteric ischemia.

  • Late loss of pulses or paralysis suggests advanced injury.
