Definition

Intercostal retraction is an observed respiratory sign. It is a clue to marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance. A positive finding means soft tissue pulls inward between ribs on inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Intercostal retraction means soft tissue pulls inward between ribs on inspiration.

Why It Matters

The observed Intercostal retraction finding can signal marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance. High inspiratory pressure is transmitted to compliant chest-wall tissues when airflow or lung expansion is impaired. Intercostal retraction cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance

Assessment Method

Inspect intercostal spaces during quiet breathing.

Positive Finding

Soft tissue pulls inward between ribs on inspiration.

Mechanism

High inspiratory pressure is transmitted to compliant chest-wall tissues when airflow or lung expansion is impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Intercostal retraction finding raises concern for marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance because high inspiratory pressure is transmitted to compliant chest-wall tissues when airflow or lung expansion is impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Intercostal retraction does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Intercostal retraction to focus evaluation of marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance and the reviewed alternatives: pectus deformity, paradoxical motion, normal infant breathing. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect intercostal spaces during quiet breathing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Intercostal retraction as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Thin children show motion more easily; severe fatigue may make retractions disappear. Therefore, Intercostal retraction cannot confirm or exclude marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pectus deformity

  • paradoxical motion

  • normal infant breathing

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pectus deformity, paradoxical motion, normal infant breathing.

False Negative Situations

Intercostal retraction may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of marked negative intrathoracic pressure from airway obstruction or low compliance.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Loss of retractions with declining effort can signal exhaustion rather than improvement.
