Definition

Roth spots is an ophthalmoscopic sign. It is a clue to retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes. A positive finding means a retinal hemorrhage has a white or pale center.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Roth spots means a retinal hemorrhage has a white or pale center.

Why It Matters

The observed Roth spots finding can signal retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes. A capillary rupture surrounds fibrin, platelet material, inflammatory cells, or ischemic retina. Roth spots cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes

Assessment Method

Examine the retina through appropriate ophthalmoscopy.

Positive Finding

A retinal hemorrhage has a white or pale center.

Mechanism

A capillary rupture surrounds fibrin, platelet material, inflammatory cells, or ischemic retina.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Roth spots finding raises concern for retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes because a capillary rupture surrounds fibrin, platelet material, inflammatory cells, or ischemic retina. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Roth spots does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Roth spots to focus evaluation of retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes and the reviewed alternatives: cotton-wool spots, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine the retina through appropriate ophthalmoscopy. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Roth spots as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

They are neither sensitive nor specific for endocarditis. Therefore, Roth spots cannot confirm or exclude retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cotton-wool spots

  • diabetic retinopathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cotton-wool spots, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion.

False Negative Situations

Roth spots may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of retinal hemorrhage with a pale center from varied systemic causes.
