Definition

Lhermitte sign is a provoked neurologic symptom-sign. It is a clue to cervical dorsal-column irritation. A positive finding means a reproducible shock-like sensation travels caudally with neck flexion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lhermitte sign means a reproducible shock-like sensation travels caudally with neck flexion.

Why It Matters

The provoked Lhermitte sign symptom can signal cervical dorsal-column irritation. Flexion stretches electrically unstable or demyelinated cervical dorsal-column fibers and produces ectopic impulses. Lhermitte sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cervical dorsal-column irritation

Assessment Method

Ask whether neck flexion produces a brief electric-shock sensation down the spine or limbs; do not repeatedly force flexion.

Positive Finding

A reproducible shock-like sensation travels caudally with neck flexion.

Mechanism

Flexion stretches electrically unstable or demyelinated cervical dorsal-column fibers and produces ectopic impulses.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Lhermitte sign symptom raises concern for cervical dorsal-column irritation because flexion stretches electrically unstable or demyelinated cervical dorsal-column fibers and produces ectopic impulses. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lhermitte sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lhermitte sign to focus evaluation of cervical dorsal-column irritation and the reviewed alternatives: cervical radiculopathy, anxiety-related paresthesia, spinal tumor. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask whether neck flexion produces a brief electric-shock sensation down the spine or limbs; do not repeatedly force flexion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lhermitte sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is patient reported and not diagnostic of multiple sclerosis; absence does not exclude cord disease. Therefore, Lhermitte sign cannot confirm or exclude cervical dorsal-column irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cervical radiculopathy

  • anxiety-related paresthesia

  • spinal tumor

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as cervical radiculopathy, anxiety-related paresthesia, spinal tumor, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Lhermitte sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Current clue with low etiologic specificity. Interpret Lhermitte sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: It is patient reported and not diagnostic of multiple sclerosis; absence does not exclude cord disease.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Lhermitte sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask whether neck flexion produces a brief electric-shock sensation down the spine or limbs; do not repeatedly force flexion. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not provoke aggressively in trauma or suspected instability, and classify it as a symptom elicited by movement.
