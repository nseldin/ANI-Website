Definition

Blastomyces dermatitidis is a clinically important fungus associated with pulmonary blastomycosis, cutaneous blastomycosis, and disseminated blastomycosis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Routine person-to-person spread is not expected; reduce high-burden soil or decaying-wood exposure when feasible and evaluate persistent pulmonary or skin findings early. The reviewed route description is environmental inhalation. The reviewed incubation estimate is 3 to 12 weeks; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Inhaled environmental propagules convert to tissue yeast, causing pulmonary inflammation with possible spread to skin, bone, genitourinary tract, or CNS. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Blastomyces dermatitidis is associated with pulmonary blastomycosis, cutaneous blastomycosis, and disseminated blastomycosis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
