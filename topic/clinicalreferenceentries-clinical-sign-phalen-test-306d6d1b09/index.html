Definition

Phalen test is a bedside nerve-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel. A positive finding means median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Phalen test means median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Phalen test response can signal median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel. Wrist flexion raises carpal-tunnel pressure around the median nerve. Phalen test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel

Assessment Method

Hold wrists in maximal comfortable flexion with hands opposed for up to 60 seconds.

Positive Finding

Median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced.

Mechanism

Wrist flexion raises carpal-tunnel pressure around the median nerve.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Phalen test response raises concern for median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel because wrist flexion raises carpal-tunnel pressure around the median nerve. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Phalen test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Phalen test to focus evaluation of median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel and the reviewed alternatives: pronator syndrome, ulnar neuropathy, Raynaud phenomenon. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Hold wrists in maximal comfortable flexion with hands opposed for up to 60 seconds. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Phalen test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Healthy people can develop tingling and severe neuropathy may be numb rather than provocative. Therefore, Phalen test cannot confirm or exclude median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pronator syndrome

  • ulnar neuropathy

  • Raynaud phenomenon

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from pronator syndrome, ulnar neuropathy, Raynaud phenomenon, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Phalen test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; symptoms and electrodiagnostics determine context. When performed as described, Phalen test can adjust concern for median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Phalen test: Healthy people can develop tingling and severe neuropathy may be numb rather than provocative.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Phalen test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Hold wrists in maximal comfortable flexion with hands opposed for up to 60 seconds. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Stop early for severe pain and avoid forced flexion after wrist injury.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Phalen test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Median-distribution paresthesia is reproduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Hold wrists in maximal comfortable flexion with hands opposed for up to 60 seconds. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, median neuropathy at the carpal tunnel, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Phalen test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Stop early for severe pain and avoid forced flexion after wrist injury. Base escalation for Phalen test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
