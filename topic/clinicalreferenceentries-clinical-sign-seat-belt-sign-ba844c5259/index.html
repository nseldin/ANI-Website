Definition

Seat-belt sign is an observed trauma sign. It is a clue to blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading. A positive finding means linear bruising or abrasion follows the seat-belt path.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Seat-belt sign means linear bruising or abrasion follows the seat-belt path.

Why It Matters

The observed Seat-belt sign finding can signal blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading. Rapid deceleration concentrates restraint force across skin and deeper viscera or spine. Seat-belt sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading

Assessment Method

Inspect the chest, abdomen, and neck after a motor-vehicle crash.

Positive Finding

Linear bruising or abrasion follows the seat-belt path.

Mechanism

Rapid deceleration concentrates restraint force across skin and deeper viscera or spine.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Seat-belt sign finding raises concern for blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading because rapid deceleration concentrates restraint force across skin and deeper viscera or spine. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Seat-belt sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Seat-belt sign to focus evaluation of blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading and the reviewed alternatives: direct impact bruise, clothing abrasion, airbag injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the chest, abdomen, and neck after a motor-vehicle crash. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Seat-belt sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Serious internal injury can occur without a mark and superficial bruising may occur alone. Therefore, Seat-belt sign cannot confirm or exclude blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • direct impact bruise

  • clothing abrasion

  • airbag injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as direct impact bruise, clothing abrasion, airbag injury.

False Negative Situations

Seat-belt sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of blunt thoracoabdominal injury from restraint loading.

Modern Status

Current high-risk mechanism marker; does not prove internal injury. Interpret Seat-belt sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Serious internal injury can occur without a mark and superficial bruising may occur alone.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Maintain trauma precautions and use mechanism, tenderness, vitals, and imaging rather than reassuring from appearance.
