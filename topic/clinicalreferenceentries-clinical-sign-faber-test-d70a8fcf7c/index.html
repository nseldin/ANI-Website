Definition

FABER test is a bedside hip and sacroiliac maneuver. It is a clue to hip or sacroiliac pathology. A positive finding means groin pain suggests hip pathology; posterior pain may implicate the SI region.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive FABER test means groin pain suggests hip pathology; posterior pain may implicate the SI region.

Why It Matters

A positive FABER test response can signal hip or sacroiliac pathology. The figure-four position loads the hip capsule and transmits stress through the pelvis. FABER test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hip or sacroiliac pathology

Assessment Method

Place the hip in flexion, abduction, and external rotation with the ankle over the opposite knee, then apply gentle downward pressure.

Positive Finding

Groin pain suggests hip pathology; posterior pain may implicate the SI region.

Mechanism

The figure-four position loads the hip capsule and transmits stress through the pelvis.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive FABER test response raises concern for hip or sacroiliac pathology because the figure-four position loads the hip capsule and transmits stress through the pelvis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; FABER test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use FABER test to focus evaluation of hip or sacroiliac pathology and the reviewed alternatives: lumbar radiculopathy, adductor strain, greater trochanteric pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place the hip in flexion, abduction, and external rotation with the ankle over the opposite knee, then apply gentle downward pressure. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating FABER test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Multiple structures are stressed and a positive result is not a single diagnosis. Therefore, FABER test cannot confirm or exclude hip or sacroiliac pathology by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lumbar radiculopathy

  • adductor strain

  • greater trochanteric pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from lumbar radiculopathy, adductor strain, greater trochanteric pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

FABER test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current localization adjunct; pain location matters. When performed as described, FABER test can adjust concern for hip or sacroiliac pathology, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for FABER test: Multiple structures are stressed and a positive result is not a single diagnosis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing FABER test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place the hip in flexion, abduction, and external rotation with the ankle over the opposite knee, then apply gentle downward pressure. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force the knee down or compare range without stabilizing the opposite pelvis.
