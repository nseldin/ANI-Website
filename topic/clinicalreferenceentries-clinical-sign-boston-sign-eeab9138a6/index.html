Definition

Boston sign is an observed eyelid movement sign. It is a clue to thyroid eye disease. A positive finding means the upper lid descends irregularly or in a jerky manner.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Boston sign means the upper lid descends irregularly or in a jerky manner.

Why It Matters

A positive Boston sign response can signal thyroid eye disease. Restrictive lid-retractor dysfunction produces uneven relaxation during downgaze. Boston sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid eye disease

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to follow a target downward.

Positive Finding

The upper lid descends irregularly or in a jerky manner.

Mechanism

Restrictive lid-retractor dysfunction produces uneven relaxation during downgaze.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Boston sign response raises concern for thyroid eye disease because restrictive lid-retractor dysfunction produces uneven relaxation during downgaze. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Boston sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Boston sign to focus evaluation of thyroid eye disease and the reviewed alternatives: von Graefe sign, blepharospasm, lid scar. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to follow a target downward. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Boston sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Definitions and reproducibility are limited. Therefore, Boston sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • von Graefe sign

  • blepharospasm

  • lid scar

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from von Graefe sign, blepharospasm, lid scar, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Boston sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and poorly standardized. Boston sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease. Apply the reviewed limitation for Boston sign: Definitions and reproducibility are limited.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Boston sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to follow a target downward. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Describe the actual lid motion rather than relying on the eponym.
