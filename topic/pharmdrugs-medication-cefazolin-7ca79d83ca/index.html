Used To Treat

Susceptible MSSA and streptococcal skin, bone, joint, bloodstream, and endocardial infections, selected urinary or biliary infections, and perioperative prophylaxis when the expected flora and procedure match its spectrum.

Mechanism

Cefazolin enters susceptible bacteria and binds PBPs, preventing peptidoglycan transpeptidation and wall cross-linking. Growing cells continue autolysis and rupture. Its first-generation side-chain structure favors gram-positive activity and stability against selected staphylococcal penicillinases, but it does not bind MRSA PBP2a effectively. Predominant renal elimination makes exposure track creatinine clearance. When accumulation raises CSF concentrations, beta-lactam antagonism of inhibitory GABA-A signaling can produce myoclonus, encephalopathy, or seizures. Surgical redosing is based on procedure length, blood loss, and drug half-life because tissue concentration must remain above the organism MIC during contamination.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cefazolin has no current U.S. boxed warning. Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity, C. difficile colitis, hemolytic or other cytopenic reactions, and seizures from unadjusted renal exposure are major hazards. Perioperative prophylaxis should usually stop on the protocol timeline because extending antibiotics without infection does not improve a clean operation and instead increases adverse effects, C. difficile, and resistance pressure.

Contraindications

  • Known serious immediate hypersensitivity to cefazolin or relevant cephalosporins; severe delayed beta-lactam reactions require careful avoidance.

  • Do not rely on cefazolin for meningitis, MRSA, Enterococcus, Listeria, Pseudomonas, atypicals, or an infection with resistant gram-negative organisms.

Nursing Essentials

  • For prophylaxis, confirm procedure, weight-based dose, allergy phenotype, infusion completion before incision, redosing trigger, blood loss, and stop time.

  • For treatment, obtain cultures when feasible, calculate renal function, assess source control, and narrow from empiric broad therapy when MSSA or streptococcal susceptibility is established.

Interactions

  • Probenecid can reduce renal secretion and increase exposure; concurrent nephrotoxins make an acute clearance decline more consequential.

  • Warfarin effect or vitamin-K-dependent coagulation can change during prolonged antibiotic therapy, poor intake, or illness; monitor high-risk patients rather than assuming a direct universal interaction.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, renal function and calculated clearance, CBC during prolonged use, liver tests when indicated, stool assessment, and neurologic status in renal impairment.

  • For surgical prophylaxis, administration time, incision time, redosing, blood loss, procedure duration, and discontinuation are clinical quality measures.

Nclex Traps

  • Cefazolin is the parenteral first-generation MSSA/surgery drug; cephalexin is the familiar oral first-generation counterpart.

  • Broader is not better after MSSA is identified. Cefazolin's focused efficacy can be preferable to unnecessary antipseudomonal therapy.

Description

Cefazolin is an IV first-generation cephalosporin that binds bacterial penicillin-binding proteins and blocks cell-wall cross-linking, with especially useful activity against susceptible MSSA and streptococci and a major surgical-prophylaxis role. A pre-incision dose creates active drug levels in blood and tissues at the moment bacteria are introduced; giving it after closure misses the preventive mechanism. It has selected activity against organisms such as E. coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Proteus mirabilis when susceptible, but it is not a Pseudomonas, Enterococcus, Listeria, MRSA, atypical, or broad anaerobic drug. Its good tolerability and MSSA activity often make it preferable to a broader cephalosporin when cultures support narrowing.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site pain or phlebitis, diarrhea, nausea, rash, pruritus, candidiasis, and transient liver-test elevation.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, neutropenia or hemolytic anemia, interstitial nephritis, encephalopathy, myoclonus, and seizures.
