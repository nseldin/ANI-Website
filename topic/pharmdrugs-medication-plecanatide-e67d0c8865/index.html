Class

Intestinal guanylate cyclase-C agonist secretagogue

Used To Treat

Chronic idiopathic constipation and irritable bowel syndrome with constipation in adults.

Boxed Warning

Risk of serious dehydration in pediatric patients: contraindicated under age 6 because juvenile animals died from dehydration; avoid use from age 6 through 17 because safety and effectiveness are not established.

Mechanism

GC-C activation increases intracellular and extracellular cyclic GMP. Intracellular cGMP activates CFTR-mediated chloride/bicarbonate secretion and inhibits sodium absorption, drawing water into the lumen; extracellular cGMP may reduce visceral pain-fiber activity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Exclude mechanical obstruction and confirm adult age before therapy; monitor stool frequency, severe diarrhea, dizziness, hypotension, dehydration, renal risk, and electrolyte loss.

  • Teach stopping the drug and contacting the prescriber for severe diarrhea; tablets may be crushed/mixed according to labeled administration instructions.

Key Labs

  • Hydration, BP, renal function and electrolytes when diarrhea is severe or the client is vulnerable; bowel-pattern response.

Nclex Traps

  • Plecanatide is locally acting but can still cause systemic harm through intestinal water loss.

Description

Plecanatide is a minimally absorbed uroguanylin-like guanylate cyclase-C agonist secretagogue that raises cGMP so CFTR secretes chloride and bicarbonate and water follows into the bowel to soften stool and accelerate transit. That same fluid shift explains severe diarrhea and dangerous pediatric dehydration.
