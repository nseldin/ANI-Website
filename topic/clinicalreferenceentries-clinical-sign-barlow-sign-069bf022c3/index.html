Definition

Barlow sign is an infant hip-dislocation maneuver. It is a clue to dislocatable developmental hip instability. A positive finding means the femoral head slips posteriorly out of the acetabulum with a clunk.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Barlow sign means the femoral head slips posteriorly out of the acetabulum with a clunk.

Why It Matters

A positive Barlow sign response can signal dislocatable developmental hip instability. Capsular laxity permits a located femoral head to cross the posterior acetabular rim. Barlow sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dislocatable developmental hip instability

Assessment Method

With hips flexed and adducted, apply gentle posterior pressure along the femur.

Positive Finding

The femoral head slips posteriorly out of the acetabulum with a clunk.

Mechanism

Capsular laxity permits a located femoral head to cross the posterior acetabular rim.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Barlow sign response raises concern for dislocatable developmental hip instability because capsular laxity permits a located femoral head to cross the posterior acetabular rim. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Barlow sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Barlow sign to focus evaluation of dislocatable developmental hip instability and the reviewed alternatives: normal click, femoral fracture, soft-tissue snap. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With hips flexed and adducted, apply gentle posterior pressure along the femur. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Barlow sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

False positives and harm increase with excessive force or repeated testing. Therefore, Barlow sign cannot confirm or exclude dislocatable developmental hip instability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal click

  • femoral fracture

  • soft-tissue snap

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal click, femoral fracture, soft-tissue snap, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Barlow sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current newborn screening maneuver requiring training. Interpret Barlow sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: False positives and harm increase with excessive force or repeated testing.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Barlow sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With hips flexed and adducted, apply gentle posterior pressure along the femur. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never force the hip or repeatedly attempt dislocation.
