Definition

COVID-19 pneumonia develops when SARS-CoV-2 infects respiratory epithelium and can trigger diffuse inflammatory lung injury, endothelial dysfunction, and thrombosis. The characteristic pattern is fever, cough, dyspnea, fatigue, hypoxemia, and bilateral infiltrates with severity strongly shaped by age, immunity, and comorbidity. Confirm or stage it with viral nucleic-acid or antigen testing interpreted by timing and pretest probability; distinguish it from influenza, bacterial pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, heart failure, and other viral disease by testing, imaging, biomarkers, and clinical course. The priority is to start an indicated antiviral early for eligible high-risk disease because acute respiratory distress syndrome can follow if the process advances.

Mechanistically, SARS-CoV-2 infects respiratory epithelium and can trigger diffuse inflammatory lung injury, endothelial dysfunction, and thrombosis.

Pathophysiology

Inflammation is present, often from infection, autoimmune injury, obstruction, or tissue damage depending on the condition.

Signs Symptoms

  • fever may be present but older adults/newborns may be atypical

  • localized pain/swelling/drainage when tissue infection is present

Diagnostics

  • viral nucleic-acid or antigen testing interpreted by timing and pretest probability

  • rest and exertional oxygenation, respiratory effort, and organ-function assessment

  • chest imaging and thromboembolic or bacterial evaluation when severity or course warrants it

  • Distinguish COVID-19 pneumonia from influenza, bacterial pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, heart failure, and other viral disease by testing, imaging, biomarkers, and clinical course.

Labs

  • PaO2 or SpO2 may fall with gas-exchange failure

  • PaCO2 may rise with ventilatory failure

  • WBC may rise with infection

  • lactate may rise with severe hypoxia or sepsis

Treatments

  • start an indicated antiviral early for eligible high-risk disease

  • provide oxygen and anti-inflammatory inpatient therapy according to severity

  • use thrombosis prevention, lung-protective support, and infection-control precautions

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate for rapidly rising oxygen need, respiratory fatigue, cyanosis, new confusion, hemodynamic instability, chest pain, unilateral swelling, hemoptysis, or suspected pneumothorax because SARS-CoV-2 infects respiratory epithelium and can trigger diffuse inflammatory lung injury, endothelial dysfunction, and thrombosis.

  • Use current transmission-based precautions and assess symptom onset, risk factors, work of breathing, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, mental status, and perfusion because deterioration may be faster than the patient's subjective breathlessness suggests.

  • Titrate prescribed oxygen to the ordered target, verify probe accuracy, and trend oxygen need during rest, movement, and position changes because a rising requirement is a key marker of worsening gas exchange.

  • Use upright, lateral, or awake-prone positioning when appropriate and tolerated, encourage measured mobility and airway clearance for retained secretions, and avoid exhausting a patient with high work of breathing because SARS-CoV-2 infects respiratory epithelium and can trigger diffuse inflammatory lung injury, endothelial dysfunction, and thrombosis.

  • Administer antiviral, corticosteroid, immunomodulator, and thromboprophylaxis therapy only for the patient's severity and contraindications; monitor glucose, liver, renal, infection, and bleeding effects because SARS-CoV-2 infects respiratory epithelium and can trigger diffuse inflammatory lung injury, endothelial dysfunction, and thrombosis.

  • Assess hydration, nutrition, venous-thromboembolism symptoms, secondary bacterial infection, delirium, and skin pressure from oxygen devices because both disease and prolonged isolation create complications.

Complications

  • acute respiratory distress syndrome

  • thrombosis can complicate COVID-19 pneumonia.

  • cardiac or kidney injury

  • secondary infection can complicate COVID-19 pneumonia.

  • post-COVID disability can complicate COVID-19 pneumonia.

Red Flags

  • Rapidly increasing oxygen requirement, severe work of breathing, exhaustion, or cyanosis

  • New confusion, hypotension, cool skin, oliguria, or other organ dysfunction

  • Sudden pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis, unilateral leg swelling, or abrupt desaturation

  • New focal chest findings, purulent sputum, recurrent fever, or suspected secondary infection

Patient Education

  • Teach correct use of the pulse oximeter and the clinician's personal thresholds, while explaining that breathing effort, confusion, chest pain, and trend matter more than one isolated number.

  • Use prescribed medicines rather than leftover antibiotics or steroids, pace recovery gradually, and seek urgent help for worsening breathlessness, blue lips, confusion, fainting, chest pain, or low oxygen that does not recover.

Nclex Traps

  • A comfortable appearance can coexist with significant hypoxemia

  • Antibiotics do not treat uncomplicated viral pneumonia
