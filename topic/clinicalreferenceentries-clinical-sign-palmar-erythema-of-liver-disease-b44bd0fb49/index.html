Definition

Palmar erythema of liver disease is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to chronic liver disease or other hyperdynamic states. A positive finding means symmetric blanching redness is present while the central palm may be spared.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Palmar erythema of liver disease means symmetric blanching redness is present while the central palm may be spared.

Why It Matters

The observed Palmar erythema of liver disease finding can signal chronic liver disease or other hyperdynamic states. Altered sex-hormone metabolism and vasodilator signaling increase superficial palmar blood flow. Palmar erythema of liver disease cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic liver disease or other hyperdynamic states

Assessment Method

Inspect both palms, especially thenar and hypothenar areas.

Positive Finding

Symmetric blanching redness is present while the central palm may be spared.

Mechanism

Altered sex-hormone metabolism and vasodilator signaling increase superficial palmar blood flow.

Evidence Limitations

It is common in benign states and does not measure liver function. Therefore, Palmar erythema of liver disease cannot confirm or exclude chronic liver disease or other hyperdynamic states by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as thyrotoxicosis, pregnancy, normal variation.

False Negative Situations

Palmar erythema of liver disease may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic liver disease or other hyperdynamic states.
