Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to dorzolamide or formulation components. Clarify severe renal impairment, sulfonamide reaction history, corneal endothelial disease and concurrent systemic carbonic-anhydrase inhibitor use.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess renal function, corneal history and sulfonamide reaction history. Stop and seek review for significant conjunctivitis/lid reaction, rash, breathing difficulty or new corneal haze/vision decline.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Oral acetazolamide or other carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors add systemic enzyme inhibition. High-dose salicylates can interact with systemic CA inhibition. Separate topical agents by at least 5 minutes.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic nerve and visual fields

  • Corneal examination/endothelial risk

  • Renal function, bicarbonate and electrolytes when systemic accumulation or combined CA inhibition is possible

Nclex Traps

  • Dorzolamide reduces aqueous production; it does not pull fluid from the eye by simple osmosis.

  • A bitter taste after an eye drop reflects nasolacrimal drainage.

  • Topical sulfonamide exposure can still cause systemic hypersensitivity.

Used To Treat

Elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension, alone or combined with other pathways.

Mechanism

Carbonic anhydrase II normally hydrates carbon dioxide to generate bicarbonate. In ciliary epithelium, bicarbonate supports sodium and fluid transport into aqueous humor. Enzyme inhibition lowers bicarbonate formation, sodium movement and aqueous production. Nasolacrimal absorption and red-cell binding explain prolonged systemic presence despite topical dosing.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: As a sulfonamide, dorzolamide can cause serious systemic hypersensitivity despite topical use. Use caution with low corneal endothelial cell reserve because irreversible corneal edema/decompensation has occurred. Severe renal impairment increases systemic accumulation; oral and topical carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors together can amplify acid-base and electrolyte effects.

Description

Dorzolamide is a topical sulfonamide carbonic-anhydrase-II inhibitor that lowers pressure by reducing bicarbonate-dependent aqueous-humor secretion in the ciliary processes. Unlike systemic acetazolamide, most effect is local, but the drug and metabolite enter red cells and are renally cleared. Burning, bitter taste, sulfonamide hypersensitivity and corneal-endothelial decompensation in susceptible eyes are the important practical issues.

Adverse Effects

  • Ocular burning/stinging, bitter taste, blurred vision, superficial punctate keratitis, allergic conjunctivitis/lid reaction, corneal edema, headache and rare systemic sulfonamide reaction or metabolic acidosis.
