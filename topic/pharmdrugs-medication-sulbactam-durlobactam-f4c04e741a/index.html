Class

IV Acinetobacter-active beta-lactam plus broad class A/C/D serine beta-lactamase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Adults with hospital-acquired or ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia caused by susceptible Acinetobacter baumannii-calcoaceticus complex under the U.S. label. It is a preferred sulbactam-based component for selected carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii infection according to current guidance and susceptibility.

Description

Sulbactam/durlobactam, branded Xacduro, is a two-component IV beta-lactam antibiotic regimen for susceptible Acinetobacter: sulbactam binds PBP1 and PBP3 to disrupt bacterial cell-wall synthesis, while durlobactam inhibits class A, C, and D serine beta-lactamases, including many ADC and OXA-type enzymes, that would otherwise hydrolyze sulbactam. Sulbactam is therefore the direct antibacterial component and durlobactam is its resistance-protecting partner. Durlobactam alone does not kill the organism, and it does not inhibit class B metallo-beta-lactamases. The combination is labeled for susceptible Acinetobacter HABP/VABP, not for generic pneumonia or every carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative infection.

Mechanism

Sulbactam fT>MIC drives PBP-mediated killing. Durlobactam reversibly carbamylates susceptible serine enzymes, and its unbound AUC relative to MIC predicts protection. Co-packaged dosing maintains both pharmacodynamic targets. Resistance can arise from an MBL, PBP modification, efflux, porin loss, or enzyme changes outside durlobactam coverage. Severe CRAB pneumonia also involves ventilator biofilm, host injury, high inoculum, and source control, which is why current practice may combine a sulbactam backbone with another active agent in important infection rather than assuming one novel product solves the full syndrome.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity can occur. Durlobactam does not inhibit MBLs, and Xacduro is not a universal CRAB or Gram-negative product without susceptible Acinetobacter evidence. Impaired or augmented renal clearance changes the regimen, hemodialysis dosing follows dialysis, and OAT1 inhibitors such as probenecid can raise sulbactam exposure and should not be coadministered.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the organism is susceptible Acinetobacter baumannii-calcoaceticus complex, then check renal function, co-packaged preparation, infusion, partner strategy, and pneumonia source because the name carbapenem resistant is not enough.

  • Trend oxygenation, ventilator parameters, hemodynamics, cultures, liver tests, CBC, potassium, and creatinine because response and toxicity can diverge.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, resistance mechanism when available, creatinine/eGFR, AST/ALT/bilirubin, CBC, potassium, oxygenation, and respiratory source assessment.

  • An MBL result or rising MIC explains why durlobactam protection may be insufficient despite broad class D activity.

Nclex Traps

  • Sulbactam is the direct Acinetobacter antibiotic; durlobactam is the enzyme shield.

  • Xacduro is labeled for susceptible Acinetobacter HABP/VABP, not every hospital pneumonia.

Adverse Effects

  • Liver-test abnormalities, diarrhea, anemia, hypokalemia, infusion reactions, candidiasis, and CDI.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, cytopenias, renal accumulation, and treatment failure from unsupported resistance mechanisms or uncontrolled source.

Contraindications

  • Known severe hypersensitivity to sulbactam, durlobactam, or other beta-lactams is a labeled contraindication.

  • Do not use durlobactam as standalone therapy or substitute arbitrary separate vials because the co-packaged regimen and exposure relationship were specifically studied.

Interactions

  • No direct interaction among durlobactam, sulbactam, imipenem, and cilastatin was observed in a healthy-volunteer study, but the complete treatment regimen can still compound organ toxicity.

  • Nephrotoxic co-agents can reduce clearance and complicate interpretation of kidney injury.
