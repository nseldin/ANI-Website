Definition

Cytomegalovirus is a clinically important virus associated with congenital CMV, mononucleosis-like illness, retinitis, and organ-invasive CMV. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Hand hygiene around saliva and urine, blood and transplant safety, pregnancy counseling, and risk-based monitoring reduce exposure or severe disease. The reviewed route description is body fluid transmission. Initial acquisition is often asymptomatic and clinical latency varies by congenital, transfusion-associated, transplant, and community contexts. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    CMV establishes lifelong latency after body-fluid transmission; fetal infection and reactivation during marked immunosuppression can injure multiple organs. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Cytomegalovirus is associated with congenital CMV, mononucleosis-like illness, retinitis, and organ-invasive CMV. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.

    Test choice depends on syndrome: nucleic-acid testing, serology, newborn testing, or tissue assessment may be needed, and viral detection alone may not prove disease. A positive result can represent infection, colonization, past exposure, residual material, or contamination depending on the method and source; a negative result can reflect timing, specimen quality, prior therapy, or an untested target.
