Definition

Altered mental status is an observed clinical finding. It is a clue to an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness. A positive finding means the patient is newly less alert, inattentive, disoriented, unusually agitated, confused, difficult to arouse, or otherwise meaningfully different from baseline.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Altered mental status means the patient is newly less alert, inattentive, disoriented, unusually agitated, confused, difficult to arouse, or otherwise meaningfully different from baseline.

Why It Matters

The observed Altered mental status finding can signal an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness. Normal awareness depends on intact brain networks plus adequate oxygen, glucose, perfusion, electrolytes, temperature, and freedom from toxins; disruption anywhere in those systems can change mental status. Altered mental status cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness

Assessment Method

Compare the patient's current alertness, attention, orientation, speech, behavior, and ability to follow commands with the known baseline; immediately assess airway, breathing, circulation, oxygenation, bedside glucose, vital signs, pupils, and focal neurologic findings.

Use Altered mental status to focus evaluation of an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness and the reviewed alternatives: delirium, dementia, aphasia, intoxication, postictal state. Evidence Limitations

The phrase is broad and does not identify the cause; baseline cognition, language, hearing, medications, sleep, and the examiner's observations affect interpretation. Therefore, Altered mental status cannot confirm or exclude an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as delirium, dementia, aphasia, intoxication, postictal state.

False Negative Situations

Altered mental status may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of an acute or subacute change in alertness, attention, awareness, cognition, or behavior caused by neurologic, metabolic, toxic, infectious, cardiopulmonary, or traumatic illness.
