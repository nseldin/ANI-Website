Definition

Jolt accentuation of headache is a bedside symptom-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache. A positive finding means baseline headache worsens with the movement.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Jolt accentuation of headache means baseline headache worsens with the movement.

Why It Matters

The provoked Jolt accentuation of headache symptom can signal possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache. Head motion may increase traction on pain-sensitive inflamed meninges. Jolt accentuation of headache cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache

Assessment Method

Ask an appropriate stable patient to rotate the head horizontally about two to three times per second.

Positive Finding

Baseline headache worsens with the movement.

Mechanism

Head motion may increase traction on pain-sensitive inflamed meninges.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Jolt accentuation of headache symptom raises concern for possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache because head motion may increase traction on pain-sensitive inflamed meninges. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Jolt accentuation of headache does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Jolt accentuation of headache to focus evaluation of possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache and the reviewed alternatives: migraine, vestibular disorder, neck strain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask an appropriate stable patient to rotate the head horizontally about two to three times per second. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Jolt accentuation of headache as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Evidence is heterogeneous and a negative result cannot safely exclude meningitis. Therefore, Jolt accentuation of headache cannot confirm or exclude possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • migraine

  • vestibular disorder

  • neck strain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as migraine, vestibular disorder, neck strain, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Jolt accentuation of headache may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct; cannot rule out meningitis. When performed as described, Jolt accentuation of headache can adjust concern for possible meningeal irritation in an alert patient with headache, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Jolt accentuation of headache: Evidence is heterogeneous and a negative result cannot safely exclude meningitis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Jolt accentuation of headache, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask an appropriate stable patient to rotate the head horizontally about two to three times per second. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use in unstable patients, cervical injury, severe vertigo, or when movement is unsafe.
