Mechanism

Primarily stimulates alpha-1 receptors to increase vascular tone and systemic vascular resistance, raising MAP. Beta-1 activity can support contractility, but reflex bradycardia may occur.

Boxed Warning

High-alert safety focus: tissue ischemia, severe hypertension, dysrhythmias, extravasation injury, and giving a vasopressor before correcting major hypovolemia.

Contraindications

  • Do not use as a substitute for adequate volume resuscitation when severe hypovolemia is uncorrected unless emergency support is needed while volume is being restored.

  • Clarify mesenteric or peripheral thrombosis, severe hypoxia/hypercarbia, or high-risk extravasation access issues.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use an infusion pump and titrate to the ordered MAP goal; central access is preferred when available.

  • Monitor MAP, ECG, peripheral pulses, skin temperature/color, capillary refill, mentation, urine output, lactate trend, and IV site.

  • Treat suspected extravasation immediately per protocol, commonly with phentolamine infiltration if ordered/available.

Interactions

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, other vasopressors, and sympathomimetics can exaggerate pressor or dysrhythmia risk.

  • Alpha or beta blockers can change the hemodynamic response.

Key Labs

  • Lactate trend.

  • Creatinine/eGFR and urine output.

  • Potassium and acid-base status as ordered.

  • Continuous blood pressure and ECG monitoring.

Nclex Traps

  • A higher blood pressure is not enough; reassess perfusion, urine output, mentation, and lactate.

  • Norepinephrine supports vascular tone but does not replace fluids, antibiotics/source control, oxygenation, or treatment of the cause of shock.

Used To Treat

Hemodynamic support for septic, vasodilatory, or distributive shock with hypotension after appropriate fluid resuscitation, and selected severe hypotension protocols.

Description

Norepinephrine is a catecholamine vasopressor that primarily causes alpha-1 vasoconstriction to raise systemic vascular resistance and MAP, with beta-1 activity that can support contractility. The first-read nursing frame is perfusion rescue versus excessive vasoconstriction: continuous BP/MAP, ECG, IV access integrity, urine output, lactate/perfusion trend, and ischemic skin/limb changes.
