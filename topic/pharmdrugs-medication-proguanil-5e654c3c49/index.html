Class

Antimalarial antifolate

Used To Treat

Malaria prophylaxis or treatment when paired with atovaquone.

Mechanism

Proguanil works by mimicking ubiquinone and inhibiting the protozoal mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex. That blocks electron transport needed for pyrimidine synthesis, so absorption with food, vomiting/diarrhea, and rifampin/rifabutin interactions matter because low exposure can fail treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess travel history, pregnancy status, renal function, adherence, and symptoms of malaria.

  • Teach daily adherence when prescribed for prophylaxis and to take combination therapy with food.

Contraindications

  • Severe renal impairment is a major caution for atovaquone-proguanil prophylaxis.

Interactions

  • Warfarin effect may change. Rifampin or rifabutin can reduce partner atovaquone exposure.

Key Labs

  • Renal function before use when clinically relevant. Monitor fever pattern and malaria symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not treat malaria prophylaxis like a one-time dose. Timing before, during, and after travel matters.

Description

Proguanil is an antimalarial antifolate: it works by mimicking ubiquinone and inhibiting the protozoal mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex. It is used for malaria prophylaxis or treatment when paired with atovaquone.
