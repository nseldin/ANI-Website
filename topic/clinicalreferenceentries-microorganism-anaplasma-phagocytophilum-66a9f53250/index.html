Definition

Anaplasma phagocytophilum is a clinically important bacterium associated with human granulocytic anaplasmosis, febrile cytopenic illness, and severe respiratory or neurologic complications. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Anaplasma phagocytophilum is a Gram-negative coccobacillus with a unknown oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and obligate intracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Ixodes-transmitted organisms replicate within neutrophil vacuoles, disrupt innate immune function, and cause systemic inflammation with leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and transaminitis. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Anaplasma phagocytophilum is associated with human granulocytic anaplasmosis, febrile cytopenic illness, and severe respiratory or neurologic complications. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Whole-blood PCR is most useful early; paired serology supports confirmation and smear-visible granulocyte morulae are neither sensitive nor definitive. A positive result can represent infection, colonization, past exposure, residual material, or contamination depending on the method and source; a negative result can reflect timing, specimen quality, prior therapy, or an untested target.
