Class

CGRP receptor antagonist fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody

Used To Treat

Preventive treatment of migraine in adults by monthly subcutaneous injection.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current AIMOVIG labeling has no boxed warning. Monitor serious or delayed hypersensitivity, constipation with severe abdominal pain/distention/vomiting or inability to pass stool, new/worsening hypertension and Raynaud symptoms. Review other constipating drugs and discontinue/evaluate clinically significant vascular effects.

Mechanism

Erenumab occupies the CLR/RAMP1 calcitonin-gene-related-peptide receptor complex and prevents CGRP from activating Gs, adenylyl cyclase, and downstream cAMP signaling in trigeminovascular pain pathways. Unlike fremanezumab, galcanezumab, and eptinezumab, it blocks the receptor rather than the ligand. Proteolytic catabolism and an effective half-life of about 28 days explain monthly dosing, few conventional CYP interactions, and why an adverse effect cannot be rapidly washed out.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify 70 mg or 140 mg subcutaneously once monthly, baseline monthly migraine days, bowel pattern, blood pressure, digital perfusion, allergy history, pregnancy plans, injection technique, storage, and sharps disposal.

  • Escalate severe or progressive abdominal pain, distention, vomiting, or inability to pass stool or gas because constipation can progress to obstruction. Check new hypertension and painful pale, blue, cold, or numb digits rather than waiting for the next monthly visit.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to erenumab or an excipient. Reactions can occur within hours or be delayed for more than a week, so timing does not exclude the drug.

Interactions

  • CYP interactions are not expected because erenumab is catabolized as a protein rather than metabolized by hepatic CYP enzymes.

  • Other constipating drugs can increase the clinical burden of reduced bowel motility; review opioids, anticholinergics, iron, and other contributors before and during treatment.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug concentration, CBC, liver panel, TB screen, or hepatitis screen is required solely for erenumab. Monitor clinical response, bowel function, blood pressure, digital perfusion, and hypersensitivity.

Nclex Traps

  • Erenumab targets the CGRP receptor; the other major preventive CGRP antibodies target the ligand.

  • It prevents migraine and does not replace an acute rescue plan.

  • A long half-life makes monthly dosing convenient but also means stopping today does not remove yesterday's dose.

Description

Erenumab is a fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody that binds the CGRP receptor rather than the CGRP ligand, providing long-acting adult migraine prevention. It is not acute rescue. Receptor blockade can produce severe constipation with complications requiring hospitalization or surgery, as well as new or worsening hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon; these are mechanistically and clinically important, not minor footnotes.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reactions and constipation are common. Muscle cramps or spasms can occur.

  • Serious concerns are hypersensitivity, severe constipation with ileus or obstruction requiring hospitalization or surgery, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon.
