Definition

Silk-glove sign is a palpation sign. It is a clue to patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia. A positive finding means thickened cord structures create a silky rubbing sensation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Silk-glove sign means thickened cord structures create a silky rubbing sensation.

Why It Matters

A positive Silk-glove sign response can signal patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia. A persistent peritoneal sac adds layered tissue around the cord. Silk-glove sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia

Assessment Method

Roll the spermatic cord gently over the pubic tubercle in a trained pediatric examination.

Positive Finding

Thickened cord structures create a silky rubbing sensation.

Mechanism

A persistent peritoneal sac adds layered tissue around the cord.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Silk-glove sign response raises concern for patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia because a persistent peritoneal sac adds layered tissue around the cord. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Silk-glove sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Silk-glove sign to focus evaluation of patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia and the reviewed alternatives: varicocele, undescended testis, lymph node. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Roll the spermatic cord gently over the pubic tubercle in a trained pediatric examination. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Silk-glove sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Examiner experience and normal cord structures limit reliability. Therefore, Silk-glove sign cannot confirm or exclude patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • varicocele

  • undescended testis

  • lymph node

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from varicocele, undescended testis, lymph node, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Silk-glove sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical pediatric adjunct. Silk-glove sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude patent processus vaginalis or pediatric inguinal hernia. Apply the reviewed limitation for Silk-glove sign: Examiner experience and normal cord structures limit reliability.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Silk-glove sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Roll the spermatic cord gently over the pubic tubercle in a trained pediatric examination. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not repeatedly manipulate a painful or irreducible groin mass.
