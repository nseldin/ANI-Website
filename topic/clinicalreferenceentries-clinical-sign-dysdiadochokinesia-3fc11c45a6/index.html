Definition

Dysdiadochokinesia is a bedside coordination sign. It is a clue to cerebellar dysfunction. A positive finding means alternating movements are irregular, slow, or break down.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dysdiadochokinesia means alternating movements are irregular, slow, or break down.

Why It Matters

The observed Dysdiadochokinesia finding can signal cerebellar dysfunction. The cerebellum cannot time reciprocal agonist-antagonist activation accurately. Dysdiadochokinesia cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cerebellar dysfunction

Assessment Method

Ask for rapid alternating hand pronation and supination or rapid foot tapping.

Positive Finding

Alternating movements are irregular, slow, or break down.

Mechanism

The cerebellum cannot time reciprocal agonist-antagonist activation accurately.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Dysdiadochokinesia finding raises concern for cerebellar dysfunction because the cerebellum cannot time reciprocal agonist-antagonist activation accurately. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dysdiadochokinesia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dysdiadochokinesia to focus evaluation of cerebellar dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: Parkinson disease, hemiparesis, apraxia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask for rapid alternating hand pronation and supination or rapid foot tapping. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dysdiadochokinesia as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Arthritis, weakness, age, and unfamiliarity reduce speed. Therefore, Dysdiadochokinesia cannot confirm or exclude cerebellar dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Parkinson disease

  • hemiparesis

  • apraxia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Parkinson disease, hemiparesis, apraxia.

False Negative Situations

Dysdiadochokinesia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of cerebellar dysfunction.
