Definition

Mitmachen is a bedside motor sign. It is a clue to catatonic passive cooperation. A positive finding means the patient passively permits or participates in any movement.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mitmachen means the patient passively permits or participates in any movement.

Why It Matters

The observed Mitmachen finding can signal catatonic passive cooperation. Pathologic automatic cooperation disrupts goal-directed voluntary control. Mitmachen cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonic passive cooperation

Assessment Method

Move a limb while instructing the patient not to assist.

Positive Finding

The patient passively permits or participates in any movement.

Mechanism

Pathologic automatic cooperation disrupts goal-directed voluntary control.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mitmachen finding raises concern for catatonic passive cooperation because pathologic automatic cooperation disrupts goal-directed voluntary control. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mitmachen does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mitmachen to focus evaluation of catatonic passive cooperation and the reviewed alternatives: waxy flexibility, apathy, poor comprehension. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Move a limb while instructing the patient not to assist. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Mitmachen as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The definition overlaps with cooperation and is difficult to standardize. Therefore, Mitmachen cannot confirm or exclude catatonic passive cooperation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • waxy flexibility

  • apathy

  • poor comprehension

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as waxy flexibility, apathy, poor comprehension.

False Negative Situations

Mitmachen may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonic passive cooperation.

Modern Status

Current specialist catatonia sign. Interpret Mitmachen in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: The definition overlaps with cooperation and is difficult to standardize.

Safety Precautions

Observe Mitmachen with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Move a limb while instructing the patient not to assist. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Interpret only within a structured catatonia examination.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Mitmachen observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The patient passively permits or participates in any movement. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Move a limb while instructing the patient not to assist. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, catatonic passive cooperation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
