Definition

Tendon xanthomas is an observed and palpated sign. It is a clue to longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia. A positive finding means firm nodular or fusiform tendon thickening is present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tendon xanthomas means firm nodular or fusiform tendon thickening is present.

Why It Matters

The observed Tendon xanthomas finding can signal longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia. LDL-derived cholesterol accumulates in tendon macrophages and extracellular tissue. Tendon xanthomas cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate Achilles and hand extensor tendons.

Positive Finding

Firm nodular or fusiform tendon thickening is present.

Mechanism

LDL-derived cholesterol accumulates in tendon macrophages and extracellular tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Tendon xanthomas finding raises concern for longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia because lDL-derived cholesterol accumulates in tendon macrophages and extracellular tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tendon xanthomas does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tendon xanthomas to focus evaluation of longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia and the reviewed alternatives: Achilles tendinosis, gouty tophus, rheumatoid nodule. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect and palpate Achilles and hand extensor tendons. Evidence Limitations

Tendon thickening is not always lipid deposition and xanthomas may be absent. Therefore, Tendon xanthomas cannot confirm or exclude longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Achilles tendinosis, gouty tophus, rheumatoid nodule.

False Negative Situations

Tendon xanthomas may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of longstanding LDL elevation, especially familial hypercholesterolemia.
