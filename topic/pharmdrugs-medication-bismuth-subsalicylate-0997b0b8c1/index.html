Class

Antidiarrheal / salicylate-containing GI agent

Used To Treat

Occasional diarrhea, indigestion, nausea, and upset stomach when appropriate.

Mechanism

Bismuth subsalicylate works by coating GI mucosa, binding toxins, and providing salicylate-mediated anti-inflammatory/antisecretory effects. Black stools/tongue can occur, and salicylate allergy/anticoagulants/children with viral illness matter.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask about aspirin allergy, anticoagulant use, GI bleeding, tinnitus, and child/teen viral illness before use.

  • Teach that black tongue or dark stool can occur, but true GI bleeding symptoms need evaluation.

Contraindications

  • Avoid in children or teenagers with influenza, varicella, or viral illness unless specifically directed by a provider.

  • Clarify salicylate allergy, active bleeding, or anticoagulant therapy.

Interactions

  • Warfarin, antiplatelets, NSAIDs, and other salicylates increase bleeding or toxicity risk.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level for casual use. Monitor bleeding signs if high risk.

  • Salicylate toxicity can cause tinnitus, hyperventilation, metabolic changes, and CNS symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Pepto-Bismol contains a salicylate. That matters for Reye syndrome teaching and bleeding risk.

  • Do not dismiss black stool automatically. Ask whether it is expected bismuth discoloration or possible melena.

Description

Bismuth subsalicylate is an antidiarrheal / salicylate-containing GI agent: it works by coating GI mucosa, binding toxins, and providing salicylate-mediated anti-inflammatory/antisecretory effects. It is used for occasional diarrhea, indigestion, nausea, and upset stomach when appropriate.
