Class

Oxazolidinone 50S ribosomal inhibitor antibiotic

Used To Treat

Acute bacterial skin and skin-structure infections caused by susceptible gram-positive organisms, including MRSA, using oral or IV therapy.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for tedizolid in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Tedizolid binds 23S rRNA in the 50S ribosomal subunit and prevents assembly of the 70S initiation complex, stopping bacterial protein synthesis. It is generally bacteriostatic against staphylococci, streptococci, and enterococci.

Nursing Essentials

  • Review serotonergic drugs and monitor clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity, agitation, fever, and autonomic instability.

  • Trend CBC for extended/off-label courses, assess neuropathy/visual symptoms, diarrhea, liver tests, infection response, and neutrophil count.

Key Labs

  • CBC with platelets, liver tests when indicated, cultures, and clinical skin/infection response.

Nclex Traps

  • Tedizolid is not interaction-free linezolid. Serotonin syndrome remains a current warning, and severe neutropenia may reduce antibacterial effectiveness.

Description

Tedizolid is an oxazolidinone prodrug converted to active tedizolid, which blocks formation of the bacterial 70S initiation complex. It is related to linezolid but is typically given once daily for a shorter labeled skin-infection course; serotonin toxicity, cytopenia risk with longer exposure, neuropathy concerns, and reduced efficacy in profound neutropenia still matter.
