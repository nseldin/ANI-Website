Summary

Pregnancy expectations are organized by gestational age: first trimester organogenesis and baseline screening, second trimester fetal anatomy/growth screening, third trimester surveillance for fetal movement, hypertensive disease, infection, and labor readiness.

Quick Answer

**First trimester (0-13 weeks):** dating, baseline labs, organogenesis, nausea/fatigue, ectopic/miscarriage red flags. **Second trimester (14-27 weeks):** quickening, anatomy scan around 18-22 weeks, fundal height after about 20 weeks, glucose screen usually 24-28 weeks. **Third trimester (28 weeks-birth):** fetal movement, preeclampsia signs, GBS screen around 36-37 weeks, labor/ROM teaching, presentation and surveillance when indicated.

Sections

    • First trimester: 0-13 weeks

    • Focus on pregnancy confirmation, dating, baseline prenatal labs, blood type/Rh and antibody screen, STI screening, medication/teratogen review, folic acid, nausea/vomiting hydration, and danger signs such as unilateral pelvic pain, shoulder pain, heavy bleeding, syncope, persistent low-grade fever or higher fever, or severe dehydration.

    • Second trimester: 14-27 weeks

    • Expect fetal movement/quickening, anatomy ultrasound commonly **18-22 weeks**, maternal physiologic changes, fundal height roughly tracks weeks after about **20 weeks**, and gestational diabetes screening commonly **24-28 weeks**.

    • Third trimester: 28 weeks-birth

    • Priorities are fetal movement/kick awareness, hypertension/preeclampsia screening, signs of preterm labor, rupture of membranes/infection cues, fetal presentation, nonstress test/biophysical profile if indicated, Rh immune globulin around **28 weeks** for eligible Rh-negative clients, and GBS screening around **36-37 weeks**.

    • NCLEX danger cues

    • **Headache, vision changes, RUQ/epigastric pain, severe swelling, hypertension, decreased fetal movement, vaginal bleeding, persistent low-grade fever or higher fever, leaking fluid, severe abdominal pain, or regular contractions before term** are not casual complaints.

    • NCLEX trap

    • Do not treat pregnancy as one long generic state. Gestational age decides what is expected, what test is due, and what finding is dangerous.
