Definition

Allen test is a bedside hand-circulation maneuver. It is a clue to radial and ulnar collateral perfusion. A positive finding means delayed or absent palmar reperfusion follows release.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Allen test means delayed or absent palmar reperfusion follows release.

Why It Matters

A positive Allen test response can signal radial and ulnar collateral perfusion. The released artery cannot supply the palmar arch adequately when flow or collateral connections are insufficient. Allen test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • radial and ulnar collateral perfusion

Assessment Method

Compress both arteries while the hand blanches, then release one and observe refill.

Positive Finding

Delayed or absent palmar reperfusion follows release.

Mechanism

The released artery cannot supply the palmar arch adequately when flow or collateral connections are insufficient.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Allen test response raises concern for radial and ulnar collateral perfusion because the released artery cannot supply the palmar arch adequately when flow or collateral connections are insufficient. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Allen test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Allen test to focus evaluation of radial and ulnar collateral perfusion and the reviewed alternatives: Raynaud phenomenon, low output, arterial spasm. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compress both arteries while the hand blanches, then release one and observe refill. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Allen test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Technique, pressure, temperature, and subjective refill limit reliability. Therefore, Allen test cannot confirm or exclude radial and ulnar collateral perfusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Raynaud phenomenon

  • low output

  • arterial spasm

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from Raynaud phenomenon, low output, arterial spasm, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Allen test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current preprocedure adjunct; limited predictive accuracy. When performed as described, Allen test can adjust concern for radial and ulnar collateral perfusion, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Allen test: Technique, pressure, temperature, and subjective refill limit reliability.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Allen test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Compress both arteries while the hand blanches, then release one and observe refill. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid prolonged occlusion and use objective assessment when radial harvest or cannulation risk matters.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Allen test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Delayed or absent palmar reperfusion follows release. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Compress both arteries while the hand blanches, then release one and observe refill. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, radial and ulnar collateral perfusion, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Allen test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Avoid prolonged occlusion and use objective assessment when radial harvest or cannulation risk matters. Base escalation for Allen test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
