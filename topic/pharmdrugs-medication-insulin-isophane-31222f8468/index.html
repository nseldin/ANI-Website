Boxed Warning

No universal boxed warning for all insulin products; hypoglycemia and medication errors are major high-alert risks.

Mechanism

Insulin Isophane works by activating insulin receptors so glucose moves into cells and potassium shifts intracellularly. Onset, peak, and meal timing determine the hypoglycemia risk window.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check glucose, meal status, insulin type/onset/peak, potassium when IV insulin/DKA.

  • Treat hypoglycemia promptly.

  • Independent double-check per policy.

Contraindications

  • Hypoglycemia

  • hypersensitivity to product

Interactions

  • Beta blockers can mask hypoglycemia.

  • Steroids raise glucose.

  • Alcohol increases hypoglycemia risk.

Description

Insulin Isophane is an intermediate-acting NPH insulin: it works by activating insulin receptors so glucose moves into cells and potassium shifts intracellularly. It is used for type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes when insulin is needed, gestational diabetes when indicated, diabetic ketoacidosis/HHS protocols, and hyperkalemia shifting therapy with glucose.
