Used To Treat

Diagnostic testing of hypothalamic-pituitary ACTH reserve and specialist-directed reduction of cortisol production in selected endogenous Cushing syndrome contexts.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for metyrapone in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Metyrapone inhibits CYP11B1, reducing conversion of 11-deoxycortisol to cortisol, and also affects corticosterone synthesis. Loss of cortisol feedback raises ACTH, so 11-deoxycortisol accumulates if the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis can respond.

Nursing Essentials

  • For diagnostic testing, follow exact timing of doses and morning cortisol/11-deoxycortisol/ACTH collection; glucocorticoids and other drugs can invalidate results.

  • Monitor adrenal-insufficiency symptoms, hypotension, nausea/vomiting, hyponatremia/hypoglycemia, plus hypertension, hypokalemia, edema, acne, or hirsutism from precursor accumulation.

Key Labs

  • Cortisol, 11-deoxycortisol, ACTH, sodium, potassium, glucose, blood pressure, and pregnancy/adrenal context.

Nclex Traps

  • Metyrapone testing asks whether ACTH rises when cortisol synthesis is blocked; treatment asks whether cortisol falls without causing adrenal crisis.

Description

Metyrapone blocks the final 11-beta-hydroxylation steps that produce cortisol and corticosterone. In a diagnostic test, falling cortisol should release pituitary feedback and raise ACTH plus 11-deoxycortisol; therapeutically, the same blockade lowers cortisol but can cause adrenal insufficiency and shunt precursors toward androgen and mineralocorticoid effects.
