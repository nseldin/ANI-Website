Class

Fluoroquinolone antibiotic

Used To Treat

Selected susceptible bacterial infections such as complicated UTI/pyelonephritis, pneumonia, GI infections, prostatitis, or anthrax/plague exposure when benefits outweigh risks.

Boxed Warning

Class boxed warnings include disabling tendon/neuropathy/CNS effects, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, myasthenia gravis exacerbation, and aortic aneurysm/dissection risk in selected patients.

Mechanism

Pradofloxacin works by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase II and topoisomerase IV, blocking DNA supercoiling, replication, and chromosome separation. This bactericidal effect is useful but high-risk: tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, dysglycemia, QT prolongation, aortic warnings, myasthenia gravis worsening, and stewardship matter.

Nursing Essentials

  • Avoid with myasthenia gravis unless no alternatives.

  • Report tendon pain, neuropathy, severe mood/CNS effects.

  • Separate from antacids/iron/calcium.

Contraindications

  • History of serious fluoroquinolone reaction

  • myasthenia gravis warning requires strong caution

Interactions

  • Antacids/iron/calcium reduce absorption.

  • QT-prolonging drugs increase dysrhythmia risk.

  • Warfarin effect can increase.
