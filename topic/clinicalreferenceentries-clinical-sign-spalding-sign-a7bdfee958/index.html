Definition

Spalding sign is an imaging sign. It is a clue to intrauterine fetal demise of some duration. A positive finding means cranial bones overlap abnormally.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Spalding sign means cranial bones overlap abnormally.

Why It Matters

The Spalding sign imaging pattern can signal intrauterine fetal demise of some duration. After fetal death, loss of intracranial pressure permits skull plates to collapse and overlap. Spalding sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • intrauterine fetal demise of some duration

Assessment Method

Review prenatal imaging for fetal skull-bone overlap in the appropriate context.

Positive Finding

Cranial bones overlap abnormally.

Mechanism

After fetal death, loss of intracranial pressure permits skull plates to collapse and overlap.

Clinical Interpretation

The Spalding sign imaging pattern raises concern for intrauterine fetal demise of some duration because after fetal death, loss of intracranial pressure permits skull plates to collapse and overlap. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Spalding sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Spalding sign to focus evaluation of intrauterine fetal demise of some duration and the reviewed alternatives: labor molding, craniosynostosis, imaging projection. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review prenatal imaging for fetal skull-bone overlap in the appropriate context. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Spalding sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The sign develops after time and is neither early nor exclusive. Therefore, Spalding sign cannot confirm or exclude intrauterine fetal demise of some duration by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • labor molding

  • craniosynostosis

  • imaging projection

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with labor molding, craniosynostosis, imaging projection.

False Negative Situations

Spalding sign may be missed when the associated process is early or subtle, the relevant region is not included, or acquisition timing and resolution are inadequate.

Modern Status

Historical imaging sign; modern ultrasound confirms demise directly. Spalding sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude intrauterine fetal demise of some duration. Apply the reviewed limitation for Spalding sign: The sign develops after time and is neither early nor exclusive.
