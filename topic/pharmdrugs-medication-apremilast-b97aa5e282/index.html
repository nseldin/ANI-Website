Class

Oral phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor and targeted intracellular immunomodulator

Used To Treat

Active psoriatic arthritis and systemic-therapy-eligible plaque psoriasis in adults and label-defined pediatric patients, plus oral ulcers of Behcet disease in adults. Immediate-release Otezla and once-daily Otezla XR have specific switching, weight, and renal rules; the five-day starter titration is used to reduce early gastrointestinal intolerance.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis and angioedema can occur. Severe diarrhea, nausea, or vomiting may cause volume depletion and hospitalization, especially early, in older adults, or with diuretics/antihypertensives. Monitor for new or worsening depression, suicidal thoughts, and other mood changes. Check weight regularly and evaluate clinically important loss. Strong CYP enzyme inducers can cause treatment failure. Adjust for severe renal impairment and do not crush, split, or chew either formulation.

Mechanism

PDE4 hydrolyzes cyclic AMP to inactive 5-prime AMP in monocytes, dendritic cells, neutrophils, keratinocytes, and other cells. Apremilast competitively inhibits PDE4, increasing cAMP. cAMP activates protein kinase A, which phosphorylates CREB and modulates NF-kappaB-linked transcription, generally increasing regulatory mediators such as IL-10 while reducing TNF, IL-23, and selected chemokines. The result is a network-level reduction rather than blockade of one extracellular cytokine. Apremilast is metabolized through multiple routes including CYP3A4, but strong enzyme induction is the clinically important interaction because it lowers exposure and efficacy. Severe renal impairment raises active-drug exposure, so immediate-release dosing is reduced and XR is not recommended. The five-day titration limits peak early gastrointestinal signaling effects; it is not required because the immune mechanism needs loading.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use the labeled five-day titration to reduce early GI effects; monitor hydration, blood pressure, weight, mood, and pediatric height/weight rather than dismissing diarrhea as merely expected.

  • Verify immediate-release versus XR, body weight, indication, and renal function. Tablets are swallowed whole; XR is not recommended in severe renal impairment.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to apremilast or formulation excipients.

  • Clarify depression or suicidal history, low body weight or poor growth, severe renal impairment, recurrent dehydration, older age, pregnancy, and strong enzyme-inducing medicines.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP enzyme inducers such as rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, or phenobarbital markedly reduce exposure and can make treatment ineffective.

  • Diuretics, antihypertensives, and other causes of volume depletion can magnify complications of severe vomiting or diarrhea even without a direct metabolic interaction.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and interval renal function when risk is present; weight, BMI or pediatric growth trajectory; hydration and blood pressure during GI symptoms

  • Mood and suicidality assessment, objective joint/skin/oral-ulcer response, and medication review for enzyme inducers

Nclex Traps

  • Apremilast is a PDE4 inhibitor, not a biologic cytokine antibody and not a JAK inhibitor; routine TB screening is not its defining safety step.

  • Titration prevents early GI intolerance; it does not mean the maintenance dose should stay at a starter-pack level.

Description

Apremilast is an oral, nonbiologic anti-inflammatory drug that inhibits phosphodiesterase-4, the enzyme that normally degrades cyclic AMP inside immune cells. Raising intracellular cAMP activates protein kinase A and CREB-linked regulatory programs while reducing production of TNF, IL-23, and other inflammatory mediators. This intracellular reset can improve psoriatic skin, joints, and Behcet oral ulcers without broadly depleting lymphocytes or carrying the boxed infection warnings used for TNF or JAK inhibitors. Its characteristic problems follow from the same signaling shift: early diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting can cause dehydration; appetite and weight can fall; and depression or suicidal thinking requires active surveillance. Titration does not create the therapeutic effect slowly; it gives the gut time to adapt and reduces early discontinuation.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, headache, upper-respiratory infection, decreased appetite, fatigue, and weight loss

  • Severe volume depletion or hypotension from GI loss, depression or suicidal behavior, angioedema, anaphylaxis, and clinically important growth/weight suppression
