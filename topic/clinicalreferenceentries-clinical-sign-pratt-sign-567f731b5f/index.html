Definition

Pratt sign is an observed venous sign. It is a clue to possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described. A positive finding means dilated superficial pretibial veins are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pratt sign means dilated superficial pretibial veins are present.

Why It Matters

The observed Pratt sign finding can signal possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described. Deep venous obstruction may redirect blood into superficial collaterals. Pratt sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described

Assessment Method

Inspect for prominent pretibial veins and calf findings without provocative squeezing.

Positive Finding

Dilated superficial pretibial veins are present.

Mechanism

Deep venous obstruction may redirect blood into superficial collaterals.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pratt sign finding raises concern for possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described because deep venous obstruction may redirect blood into superficial collaterals. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pratt sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pratt sign to focus evaluation of possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described and the reviewed alternatives: varicose veins, post-thrombotic syndrome, pregnancy veins. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect for prominent pretibial veins and calf findings without provocative squeezing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pratt sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Low specificity and sensitivity make objective DVT pathways essential. Therefore, Pratt sign cannot confirm or exclude possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • varicose veins

  • post-thrombotic syndrome

  • pregnancy veins

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as varicose veins, post-thrombotic syndrome, pregnancy veins.

False Negative Situations

Pratt sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable for DVT. Pratt sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described. Apply the reviewed limitation for Pratt sign: Low specificity and sensitivity make objective DVT pathways essential.

Safety Precautions

Observe Pratt sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect for prominent pretibial veins and calf findings without provocative squeezing. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not diagnose or exclude DVT from visible veins.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Pratt sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Dilated superficial pretibial veins are present. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Inspect for prominent pretibial veins and calf findings without provocative squeezing. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, possible deep venous thrombosis, historically described, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Pratt sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not diagnose or exclude DVT from visible veins. Base escalation for Pratt sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
