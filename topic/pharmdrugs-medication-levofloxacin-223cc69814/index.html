Used To Treat

Selected susceptible bacterial infections such as complicated UTI/pyelonephritis, pneumonia, GI infections, prostatitis, or anthrax/plague exposure when benefits outweigh risks.

Mechanism

Levofloxacin works by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase II and topoisomerase IV, blocking DNA supercoiling, replication, and chromosome separation. This bactericidal effect is useful but high-risk: tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, dysglycemia, QT prolongation, aortic warnings, myasthenia gravis worsening, and stewardship matter.

Boxed Warning

Class boxed warnings include disabling tendon/neuropathy/CNS effects, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, myasthenia gravis exacerbation, and aortic aneurysm/dissection risk in selected patients.

Contraindications

  • History of serious fluoroquinolone reaction

  • myasthenia gravis warning requires strong caution

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

  • Clarify myasthenia gravis, tendon disorder, significant QT risk, pregnancy/pediatric concerns, and aortic aneurysm risk factors.

Nursing Essentials

  • Avoid with myasthenia gravis unless no alternatives.

  • Report tendon pain, neuropathy, severe mood/CNS effects.

  • Separate from antacids/iron/calcium.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Levofloxacin is categorized here as Fluoroquinolone antibiotic; DNA gyrase/topoisomerase inhibitor. Pathway: Anti-infective medication > Fluoroquinolone DNA-gyrase/topoisomerase inhibitor > Respiratory fluoroquinolone. Interactions

  • Antacids/iron/calcium reduce absorption.

  • QT-prolonging drugs increase dysrhythmia risk.

  • Warfarin effect can increase.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • renal function

  • ECG/QT risk

  • glucose in diabetics

  • Allergies, cultures before first dose when ordered, renal function for renally cleared drugs, liver function for hepatotoxic drugs, CBC, diarrhea/C. difficile cues, and therapeutic levels for aminoglycosides/vancomycin when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Tendon pain on therapy is a stop-and-call cue, not a stretch more cue.

  • Do not delay urgent antibiotics for sepsis, but obtain cultures first if ordered and it does not delay care.

Description

Levofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic and DNA gyrase/topoisomerase inhibitor: it works by inhibiting bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase II and topoisomerase IV, blocking DNA supercoiling, replication, and chromosome separation. It is used for cancer treatment or immune/autoimmune protocols depending on the agent, regimen, tumor biology, and dose schedule.
