Definition

Cerebellar gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction. A positive finding means the gait is broad-based, irregular, veering, and unstable even with eyes open.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cerebellar gait means the gait is broad-based, irregular, veering, and unstable even with eyes open.

Why It Matters

The observed Cerebellar gait finding can signal midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction. Cerebellar timing and error-correction failure destabilize axial and multijoint coordination. Cerebellar gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe base width, path, tandem walking, turning, and truncal control with fall protection.

Positive Finding

The gait is broad-based, irregular, veering, and unstable even with eyes open.

Mechanism

Cerebellar timing and error-correction failure destabilize axial and multijoint coordination.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Cerebellar gait finding raises concern for midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction because cerebellar timing and error-correction failure destabilize axial and multijoint coordination. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cerebellar gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cerebellar gait to focus evaluation of midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: sensory ataxia, vestibular neuritis, drug intoxication. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe base width, path, tandem walking, turning, and truncal control with fall protection. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cerebellar gait as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sedatives, weakness, vestibular disease, and severe sensory loss can resemble it. Therefore, Cerebellar gait cannot confirm or exclude midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • sensory ataxia

  • vestibular neuritis

  • drug intoxication

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as sensory ataxia, vestibular neuritis, drug intoxication.

False Negative Situations

Cerebellar gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of midline or hemispheric cerebellar dysfunction.
