Used To Treat

Susceptible bacterial infections including UTI, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prophylaxis/treatment, some MRSA skin infections, and selected inflammatory conditions depending on the agent.

Mechanism

Silver Sulfadiazine works by blocking sequential bacterial folate synthesis: sulfonamides inhibit dihydropteroate synthase and trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. Rash/SJS, sulfa allergy history, hyperkalemia, renal effects, photosensitivity, marrow suppression, and warfarin interaction matter.

Boxed Warning

Warnings include severe skin reactions, blood dyscrasias, hepatotoxicity, and fetal/neonatal risks for the specific product.

Contraindications

  • Sulfonamide hypersensitivity

  • infants under 2 months for TMP-SMX

  • significant hepatic damage/blood dyscrasias require clarification

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess sulfa allergy, rash, CBC risk, renal function, hydration.

  • Photosensitivity teaching.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Warfarin effect can increase.

  • ACE/ARB/spironolactone with TMP-SMX increases hyperkalemia risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • CBC

  • creatinine

  • potassium

  • LFTs

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • TMP-SMX + ACE inhibitor/spironolactone can quietly cause dangerous hyperkalemia.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.
