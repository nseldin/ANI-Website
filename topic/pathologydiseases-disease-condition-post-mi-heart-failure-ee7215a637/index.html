Definition

Post-MI heart failure develops when infarcted myocardium loses contractility or develops mechanical and electrical complications, raising filling pressure and reducing cardiac output. The characteristic pattern is new dyspnea, crackles, S3, cool extremities, oliguria, hypotension, or recurrent chest pain after myocardial infarction. Confirm or stage it with urgent electrocardiogram and serial troponin for recurrent ischemia; distinguish it from pneumonia, excess fluid, right-ventricular infarction, papillary-muscle rupture, ventricular septal defect, and recurrent ischemia by echo and hemodynamics. The priority is to reperfuse recurrent ischemia and treat mechanical complications urgently because cardiogenic shock and pulmonary edema can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Chest pain, dyspnea, syncope, palpitations, hypotension, weak/absent pulses, cool skin, altered mentation, or low urine output depending on the perfusion problem.

  • Worsening trend matters: new ECG change, rising troponin/BNP, escalating oxygen need, or falling blood pressure changes priority.

Diagnostics

  • urgent electrocardiogram and serial troponin for recurrent ischemia

  • echocardiography for ejection fraction, wall motion, mitral regurgitation, septal rupture, or free-wall complications

  • serial perfusion, congestion, kidney, electrolyte, and rhythm assessment

  • Distinguish Post-MI heart failure from pneumonia, excess fluid, right-ventricular infarction, papillary-muscle rupture, ventricular septal defect, and recurrent ischemia by echo and hemodynamics.

Labs

  • troponin may rise with myocardial injury

  • BNP may rise with heart failure strain

  • potassium and magnesium shifts increase dysrhythmia risk

  • creatinine matters before contrast and medication dosing

Treatments

  • reperfuse recurrent ischemia and treat mechanical complications urgently

  • relieve congestion while preserving perfusion

  • start evidence-based post-infarct and heart-failure therapy as blood pressure and kidney function allow

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate for recurrent ischemic pain, new ST change, acute pulmonary edema, hypotension, cool mottled skin, oliguria, sustained ventricular rhythm, syncope, or a new harsh murmur because reinfarction, cardiogenic shock, or papillary, septal, or free-wall rupture may be occurring.

  • Assess infarct location and timing, dyspnea, orthopnea, chest pain, fatigue, cool skin, crackles, jugular venous pressure, edema, new murmur, and urine output because necrotic myocardium may fail as a pump or develop a mechanical complication after infarction.

  • Trend rhythm, blood pressure, oxygenation, lung sounds, daily weight, intake and output, kidney function, potassium, magnesium, troponin trend, and echocardiographic function because recurrent ischemia, dysrhythmia, congestion, and low output can overlap.

  • Administer prescribed diuretic, beta-blocker, renin-angiotensin system, mineralocorticoid, SGLT2, antiplatelet, and lipid therapy while monitoring pressure, pulse, renal function, electrolytes, volume, and bleeding because post-infarct remodeling prevention must not worsen shock or kidney injury.

  • Coordinate gradual cardiac rehabilitation, energy conservation, nutrition, medication access, and daily symptom tracking because supervised recovery improves function while avoiding excessive demand during vulnerable remodeling.

Complications

  • cardiogenic shock and pulmonary edema

  • thrombus can complicate Post-MI heart failure.

  • reinfarction can complicate Post-MI heart failure.

  • adverse remodeling can complicate Post-MI heart failure.

Red Flags

  • Recurrent chest pressure with new electrocardiographic change or rising biomarkers

  • Acute crackles, severe dyspnea, hypoxemia, or pink frothy sputum

  • Hypotension, cool mottled skin, confusion, narrow pulse pressure, or oliguria

  • New murmur, abrupt pulmonary edema, syncope, tamponade signs, or sustained ventricular dysrhythmia

Patient Education

  • Teach daily weight, breathing and swelling checks with the exact call threshold because early congestion can be treated before it requires hospitalization.

  • Explain that symptom improvement does not mean the injured ventricle has fully healed; cardiac rehabilitation and disease-modifying medicines reduce remodeling and another event.

Nclex Traps

  • A new murmur with shock after infarction is a mechanical emergency

  • Do not treat hypotension reflexively with fluid before distinguishing right- from left-sided failure and rupture
