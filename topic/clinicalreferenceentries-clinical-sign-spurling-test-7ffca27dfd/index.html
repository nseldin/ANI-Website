Definition

Spurling test is a bedside cervical provocation maneuver. It is a clue to cervical radiculopathy. A positive finding means typical arm radicular pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Spurling test means typical arm radicular pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Spurling test response can signal cervical radiculopathy. The position narrows the neural foramen and loads an irritated cervical root. Spurling test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cervical radiculopathy

Assessment Method

Extend and rotate the neck toward symptoms and apply gentle axial load only when safe.

Positive Finding

Typical arm radicular pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

The position narrows the neural foramen and loads an irritated cervical root.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Spurling test response raises concern for cervical radiculopathy because the position narrows the neural foramen and loads an irritated cervical root. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Spurling test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Spurling test to focus evaluation of cervical radiculopathy and the reviewed alternatives: facet pain, myelopathy, peripheral neuropathy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Extend and rotate the neck toward symptoms and apply gentle axial load only when safe. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Spurling test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A negative test does not exclude radiculopathy and neck pain alone is nonspecific. Therefore, Spurling test cannot confirm or exclude cervical radiculopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • facet pain

  • myelopathy

  • peripheral neuropathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from facet pain, myelopathy, peripheral neuropathy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Spurling test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current relatively specific adjunct; not a screening test after trauma. When performed as described, Spurling test can adjust concern for cervical radiculopathy, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Spurling test: A negative test does not exclude radiculopathy and neck pain alone is nonspecific.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Spurling test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Extend and rotate the neck toward symptoms and apply gentle axial load only when safe. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never perform with trauma, instability, severe myelopathy, or vascular concern.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Spurling test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Typical arm radicular pain is reproduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Extend and rotate the neck toward symptoms and apply gentle axial load only when safe. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, cervical radiculopathy, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Spurling test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Never perform with trauma, instability, severe myelopathy, or vascular concern. Base escalation for Spurling test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
