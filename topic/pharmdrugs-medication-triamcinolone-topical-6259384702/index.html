Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to product components

  • Avoid untreated bacterial, fungal, viral or parasitic infection unless appropriate anti-infective therapy is present

  • Avoid unplanned use on face, eyelids, groin, axilla, diaper occlusion or large chronic fields

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify concentration and vehicle and map the body site; ointment under occlusion can be far more potent than a casual '0.1%' reading suggests.

  • Use the smallest effective amount for the shortest planned duration and reassess diagnosis if not improving.

Interactions

  • Other topical/systemic corticosteroids add HPA and skin toxicity.

  • Occlusive dressings, diapers and plastic wraps markedly increase absorption.

  • CYP3A inhibitors can increase systemic steroid exposure when topical absorption is extensive.

Key Labs

  • Morning cortisol, urinary free cortisol or ACTH stimulation when HPA suppression is suspected

  • Glucose and electrolytes if systemic glucocorticoid effects occur

  • Skin infection evaluation, growth in children and ocular assessment near eyes

Nclex Traps

  • Topical does not mean local-only when potency, BSA, duration and occlusion are high.

  • A rash that improves while infection spreads is steroid masking, not successful treatment.

  • The face, folds and diaper area absorb more and require a different potency strategy than thick extensor plaques.

Used To Treat

Inflammatory and pruritic corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses such as selected eczema, contact dermatitis and psoriasis, using site-, potency-, vehicle- and duration-specific plans.

Mechanism

The steroid-GR complex enters the nucleus, binds glucocorticoid response elements and induces anti-inflammatory proteins such as annexin A1 while transrepressing NF-kappa-B and AP-1. Reduced phospholipase-A2 activity lowers arachidonic-acid-derived prostaglandins/leukotrienes; reduced cytokines and adhesion molecules decrease immune recruitment and capillary permeability. Vasoconstriction rapidly reduces erythema, but prolonged collagen/fibroblast suppression causes atrophy and striae.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Large doses, potent strengths, large BSA, prolonged use, damaged skin or occlusion can suppress the HPA axis and cause Cushing syndrome, hyperglycemia or glucosuria. Local harms include atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, glaucoma/cataract exposure and masked or worsened infection.

Description

Topical triamcinolone acetonide is a glucocorticoid anti-inflammatory that binds cytosolic glucocorticoid receptors and changes transcription to suppress cytokines, eicosanoids, edema and pruritus. Its real potency depends on concentration, vehicle, body site, barrier integrity and occlusion. It is not a disease-specific cure: the same broad immune suppression can mask infection, thin skin and, over large or occluded areas, suppress the systemic hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis.

Adverse Effects

  • Burning, itching, irritation, dryness and folliculitis

  • Skin atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, purpura, hypopigmentation and steroid acne

  • Perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis and secondary infection

  • HPA suppression, Cushing syndrome, hyperglycemia and pediatric growth effects
