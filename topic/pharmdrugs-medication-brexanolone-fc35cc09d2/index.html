IV allopregnanolone GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator

Used To Treat

Postpartum depression in patients 15 years of age and older through a continuous 60-hour monitored IV infusion in a certified facility.

Boxed Warning

Excessive sedation and sudden loss of consciousness can occur during administration. Use only through the ZULRESSO REMS in a certified facility; continuously monitor pulse oximetry, assess sedation at least every 2 hours during planned nonsleep periods, ensure a caregiver accompanies all infant interactions, and stop immediately for hypoxia or loss of consciousness until symptoms resolve.

Mechanism

Brexanolone binds neurosteroid allosteric sites on GABA-A receptors and potentiates GABA-mediated chloride currents across receptor assemblies including alpha1-beta2-gamma2 and extrasynaptic alpha4-beta3-delta channels. The precise PPD mechanism remains incompletely understood; modulation may help restore inhibitory-network adaptation after the sharp peripartum fall in neuroactive steroids.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before infusion assess suicidality, bipolar/psychosis symptoms, CNS depressants, respiratory disease, falls, IV access, caregiver/child-safety plan and end-stage renal disease, in which the solubilizing excipient can accumulate.

  • Maintain continuous observation and pulse oximetry; distinguish sleep from unresponsiveness, protect airway and infant, stop rather than merely slow for hypoxia or loss of consciousness, and reassess before resumption.

Key Labs

  • Continuous oxygen saturation and serial sedation/vital-sign assessment; renal status, pregnancy context, depression severity and suicide-risk evaluation. No therapeutic serum concentration is used.

Nclex Traps

  • Brexanolone is a 60-hour monitored infusion, not a home PRN anxiolytic.

  • A patient must not be the sole caregiver during the infusion even if she feels alert at the moment.

Description

Brexanolone is an IV formulation of allopregnanolone, an endogenous progesterone-derived neuroactive steroid, that positively modulates synaptic and extrasynaptic GABA-A receptors during a 60-hour infusion for postpartum depression. It can improve depressive symptoms rapidly, but broad enhancement of inhibitory signaling can abruptly produce profound sedation or loss of consciousness even after a patient previously appeared awake.
