Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity

  • Not a substitute for emergency membrane stabilization and potassium shifting

  • Use caution/avoid in severe GI motility disorders and bowel obstruction

Nursing Essentials

  • Mix with room-temperature liquid or approved soft food; never take dry and do not heat.

  • Trend potassium after dose, diet, kidney function, dialysis and RAAS-inhibitor changes; replace magnesium when indicated.

Interactions

  • Give most oral drugs at least 3 hours before or after patiromer unless the label lists no clinically important interaction.

  • Other potassium-lowering therapies can cause hypokalemia.

  • Magnesium supplements may be needed but should be timed thoughtfully because the polymer binds cations.

Key Labs

  • Serum potassium

  • Magnesium

  • Renal function, bicarbonate and ECG when hyperkalemia is urgent

Nclex Traps

  • Patiromer removes potassium through stool; insulin only shifts it into cells.

  • Calcium exchange distinguishes it from sodium polystyrene sulfonate and avoids a sodium load.

  • A delayed binder never replaces IV calcium for an unstable hyperkalemic ECG.

Used To Treat

Hyperkalemia in adults and pediatric patients age 12 years and older; useful for ongoing control but not emergency treatment of life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Mechanism

Carboxylate groups on cross-linked beads carry calcium counterions. In the high-potassium colonic lumen, potassium replaces calcium on the polymer and the complex is excreted. This is actual body removal, unlike insulin/beta-2 agonists that temporarily redistribute potassium. Slow GI transit and exchange explain delayed onset; nonspecific cation/drug binding explains magnesium loss and interactions.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not use as emergency monotherapy for life-threatening hyperkalemia because onset is delayed. Separate most oral medications by at least 3 hours unless specifically shown not to interact. Avoid in severe constipation, bowel obstruction or major motility disorder. Monitor magnesium as well as potassium.

Description

Patiromer is a nonabsorbed polymer that exchanges calcium for potassium, mainly in the colon, so captured potassium leaves in stool and whole-body potassium falls. The effect is delayed, making it unsuitable as sole emergency therapy when ECG instability demands IV calcium and rapid intracellular shifting. The polymer can also bind magnesium and oral medications, explaining hypomagnesemia and 3-hour dose separation.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, diarrhea, nausea, abdominal discomfort and flatulence

  • Hypomagnesemia

  • Hypokalemia after excessive dosing or changing renal/RAAS context

  • GI intolerance or impaction concern in severe dysmotility
