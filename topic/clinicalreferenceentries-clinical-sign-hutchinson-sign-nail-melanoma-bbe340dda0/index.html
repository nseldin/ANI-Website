Definition

Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) is an observed nail-pigment sign. It is a clue to subungual melanoma. A positive finding means pigment extends from nail bed or matrix onto the proximal or lateral nail fold.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) means pigment extends from nail bed or matrix onto the proximal or lateral nail fold.

Why It Matters

The observed Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) finding can signal subungual melanoma. Malignant melanocytes spread beyond the nail unit into adjacent skin. Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subungual melanoma

Assessment Method

Inspect pigmented nail bands and periungual skin.

Positive Finding

Pigment extends from nail bed or matrix onto the proximal or lateral nail fold.

Mechanism

Malignant melanocytes spread beyond the nail unit into adjacent skin.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) finding raises concern for subungual melanoma because malignant melanocytes spread beyond the nail unit into adjacent skin. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) to focus evaluation of subungual melanoma and the reviewed alternatives: trauma, ethnic melanonychia, drug pigmentation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect pigmented nail bands and periungual skin. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Benign pseudo-Hutchinson appearance and skin tone can mimic extension. Therefore, Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) cannot confirm or exclude subungual melanoma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as trauma, ethnic melanonychia, drug pigmentation.

False Negative Situations

Hutchinson sign (nail melanoma) may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of subungual melanoma.
