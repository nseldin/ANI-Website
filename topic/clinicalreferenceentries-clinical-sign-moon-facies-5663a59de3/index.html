Definition

Moon facies is an observed facial sign. It is a clue to chronic glucocorticoid excess. A positive finding means the face becomes rounded with temporal and cheek fat accumulation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Moon facies means the face becomes rounded with temporal and cheek fat accumulation.

Why It Matters

The observed Moon facies finding can signal chronic glucocorticoid excess. Cortisol excess redistributes adipose tissue and promotes fluid-related fullness. Moon facies cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic glucocorticoid excess

Assessment Method

Observe facial contour over time.

Positive Finding

The face becomes rounded with temporal and cheek fat accumulation.

Mechanism

Cortisol excess redistributes adipose tissue and promotes fluid-related fullness.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Moon facies finding raises concern for chronic glucocorticoid excess because cortisol excess redistributes adipose tissue and promotes fluid-related fullness. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Moon facies does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Moon facies to focus evaluation of chronic glucocorticoid excess and the reviewed alternatives: nephrotic edema, hypothyroidism, normal facial shape. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe facial contour over time. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Moon facies as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Weight gain and edema can produce similar contour. Therefore, Moon facies cannot confirm or exclude chronic glucocorticoid excess by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • nephrotic edema

  • hypothyroidism

  • normal facial shape

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as nephrotic edema, hypothyroidism, normal facial shape.

False Negative Situations

Moon facies may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic glucocorticoid excess.

Observe Moon facies with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe facial contour over time. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Review prescribed steroid exposure without shaming and do not stop steroids abruptly.
