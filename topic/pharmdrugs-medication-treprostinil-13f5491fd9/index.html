Class

Prostacyclin analog pulmonary vasodilator

Used To Treat

Pulmonary arterial hypertension by subcutaneous, IV, inhaled, or oral formulation; inhaled therapy also has selected pulmonary-hypertension indications associated with interstitial lung disease.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current treprostinil products have no boxed warning. Continuous REMODULIN must not be stopped abruptly because sudden loss of prostacyclin support can worsen PAH. IV delivery through an external central catheter can cause fatal bloodstream infection, so subcutaneous infusion is preferred when feasible. TYVASO can cause bronchospasm, ORENITRAM abrupt discontinuation can worsen PAH, and every formulation can cause hypotension and bleeding.

Mechanism

Treprostinil activates prostacyclin IP receptors on vascular smooth muscle and platelets. Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, raises cAMP, and engages protein kinase A, reducing calcium-dependent contraction and platelet activation. Pulmonary resistance and right-ventricular afterload fall, while systemic signaling explains hypotension, flushing, headache, jaw pain, diarrhea, and bleeding. Treprostinil lasts longer than epoprostenol, but abrupt infusion withdrawal or a large reduction still removes pathway support and can worsen PAH.

Nursing Essentials

  • Read the brand and route before the dose. For infusion verify concentration, ng/kg/min, pump, site or dedicated line, cassette, backup equipment, and interruption plan. For TYVASO verify the correct device and four waking-day sessions. For ORENITRAM verify intact extended-release tablets, food, BID/TID spacing, and titration plan.

  • Do not stop continuous therapy while troubleshooting. Preserve delivery through the established backup system and seek urgent PAH guidance for missed oral doses, inhalation interruption, or any route transition.

  • Monitor blood pressure, syncope, bleeding, jaw pain, GI effects, SQ site, central-line infection, cough or bronchospasm, oxygenation, right-heart status, adherence, and functional response.

Contraindications

  • REMODULIN and TYVASO list no formal contraindication, but low systemic pressure, bleeding risk, hepatic impairment, and device or airway suitability can still make treatment unsafe without adjustment.

  • ORENITRAM should not be used in severe hepatic impairment and is avoided in moderate hepatic impairment; exact product labeling governs other organ-function limits.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2C8 inhibition can increase treprostinil exposure and adverse effects, while CYP2C8 induction can reduce exposure and efficacy; recommendations differ by formulation.

  • Anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs add bleeding risk, and other vasodilators add hypotension. Never duplicate treprostinil products or convert routes without a supervised transition calculation.

Key Labs

  • Hemodynamics and oxygenation; CBC and bleeding assessment; liver function for exposure and dose context; infection evaluation for central access; and BNP/NT-proBNP, functional class, six-minute-walk distance, echo, or right-heart measures for response.

Nclex Traps

  • REMODULIN, TYVASO, TYVASO DPI, and ORENITRAM contain treprostinil but have different routes, devices, schedules, strengths, and transition rules.

  • Treprostinil lasts longer than epoprostenol but continuous infusion still must not be abruptly stopped.

  • Inhaled treprostinil can treat PH associated with interstitial lung disease; that does not make every PAH drug appropriate for WHO Group 3 PH.

Class Pathway

PAH therapy > Prostacyclin analog > IP receptor-Gs-cAMP > IV/SQ, inhaled, or oral formulation

Description

Treprostinil is a longer-duration prostacyclin analog that activates IP receptors, raises cAMP, dilates pulmonary arteries, inhibits platelet activation, and reduces right-ventricular afterload in pulmonary hypertension. The same molecule is delivered as continuous subcutaneous or IV REMODULIN, inhaled TYVASO or TYVASO DPI, or oral extended-release ORENITRAM, but those products are not interchangeable. Infusion therapy carries interruption, line, and severe site-pain hazards; inhaled therapy adds cough and bronchospasm; oral therapy requires intact tablets with food and careful titration. Inhaled treprostinil also has a specific PH-ILD indication beyond WHO Group 1 PAH.

Adverse Effects

  • Mechanism-related effects include headache, flushing, jaw pain, nausea, diarrhea, dizziness, edema, hypotension, syncope, and bleeding.

  • SQ infusion often causes severe site pain and reaction; IV therapy adds catheter infection and sepsis; inhaled products cause cough, throat irritation, pharyngolaryngeal pain, and bronchospasm; oral therapy adds GI intolerance.
