Used To Treat

Additional LDL-C reduction as monotherapy when a statin is unsuitable or, more commonly, in combination with a maximally tolerated statin when greater receptor-mediated clearance is needed.

Description

Ezetimibe is the prototype cholesterol-absorption inhibitor. It blocks Niemann-Pick C1-like 1 (NPC1L1) at the small-intestinal brush border, reducing uptake of both dietary cholesterol and the much larger pool of biliary cholesterol continually secreted into the gut. Less cholesterol reaches the liver in chylomicron remnants, so hepatocyte cholesterol falls and LDL-receptor expression rises. The liver partly compensates by increasing synthesis, which is why combining ezetimibe with a statin is mechanistically stronger than either alone: one blocks incoming cholesterol and the other blocks new synthesis. Ezetimibe does not block triglyceride or fat-soluble-vitamin absorption like a general malabsorption drug, and its value is not limited to people who eat a high-cholesterol diet because biliary recycling continues even during a low-cholesterol diet.

Mechanism

NPC1L1 cycles between intracellular compartments and the apical enterocyte membrane to internalize free cholesterol from bile-salt micelles. Ezetimibe and its active glucuronide bind the NPC1L1 pathway and prevent this endocytic uptake. Reduced chylomicron cholesterol delivery lowers hepatic cholesterol, activating SREBP2 and increasing LDL receptors. Ezetimibe undergoes enterohepatic recirculation, producing repeated intestinal exposure despite a low oral dose. Bile-acid sequestrants can trap drug or active metabolite in the gut and reduce effective exposure, which explains the instruction to give ezetimibe at least two hours before or four hours after the sequestrant. It lowers LDL modestly alone and additively with statins; it does not neutralize PCSK9 or directly inhibit HMG-CoA reductase.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: When ezetimibe is combined with a statin, the statin's liver, muscle, pregnancy, and interaction warnings still apply. Unexplained muscle symptoms or significant hepatic abnormalities require evaluation rather than assuming the nonstatin component is harmless.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, fatigue, arthralgia, upper-respiratory symptoms, and mild enzyme elevations can occur. Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis are uncommon but reported, especially with a statin or fibrate.

  • Gallbladder disease risk can rise when used with a fibrate because cholesterol delivery to bile changes and fibrates make bile more lithogenic.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity is a product contraindication. When combined with a statin, apply the contraindications of that statin.

  • Do not use a bile-acid sequestrant at the same time without spacing; physical binding can reduce absorption and produce an apparently ineffective regimen.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm whether ezetimibe is replacing or adding to a statin, expected LDL reduction, timing relative to sequestrants, muscle or liver symptoms, and the follow-up lipid date.

  • Teach that it blocks intestinal cholesterol uptake, not all dietary fat. A low-fat, cardioprotective dietary pattern remains useful for total risk even though biliary cholesterol is also a major substrate.

Interactions

  • Give at least two hours before or four hours after cholestyramine, colestipol, or colesevelam. Cyclosporine can increase ezetimibe exposure and requires monitoring.

  • Fibrates can increase biliary cholesterol and gallstone risk. Gemfibrozil combinations require special interaction review; product and guideline recommendations should be followed.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel to verify incremental LDL lowering; liver tests when combined with a statin or when symptoms arise; CK, creatinine, and urinalysis for significant muscle symptoms; gallbladder assessment for biliary pain.

  • No serum ezetimibe concentration is routinely needed. A small or absent LDL response usually prompts adherence, timing, secondary-cause, and medication-reconciliation review.

Nclex Traps

  • Ezetimibe inhibits NPC1L1; it is not a bile-acid sequestrant and is systemically absorbed and glucuronidated.

  • Taking it with cholestyramine can reduce its effect. Spacing is part of the mechanism, not a convenience suggestion.
