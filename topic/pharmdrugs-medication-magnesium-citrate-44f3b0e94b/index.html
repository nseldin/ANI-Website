Used To Treat

Nausea and vomiting due to surgery/anesthesia, chemotherapy, migraine, vestibular disorders, pregnancy, gastroparesis, or GI illness depending on the agent.

Mechanism

Magnesium citrate works by acting as an osmotic saline laxative: poorly absorbed magnesium ions hold water in the intestinal lumen, distending bowel and triggering peristalsis. The bedside issue is stool output versus dehydration, diarrhea, hypermagnesemia risk in renal impairment, and avoiding use when obstruction or acute abdomen is suspected.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • QT risk, sedation, EPS, anticholinergic effects, bowel obstruction concern, hydration, and serotonin syndrome risk when relevant.

Nclex Traps

  • Different antiemetics have different dangers: ondansetron/QT, metoclopramide/EPS-tardive dyskinesia, promethazine/sedation-tissue injury.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for Magnesium citrate in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

Magnesium citrate is an osmotic saline laxative and magnesium salt: it works by acting as an osmotic saline laxative: poorly absorbed magnesium ions hold water in the intestinal lumen, distending bowel and triggering peristalsis. It is used for short-term constipation relief or bowel evacuation/preparation when an osmotic saline laxative is appropriate and bowel obstruction, severe dehydration, or renal-risk context has been considered.
