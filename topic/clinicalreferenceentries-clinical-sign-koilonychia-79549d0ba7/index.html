Definition

Koilonychia is an observed nail change in which one or more nails become thin and concave, creating a spoon-shaped surface. It is a clinical clue, not a diagnosis of anemia.

Plain Language

The middle of the nail curves inward like a spoon while the edges turn upward.

Why It Matters

Acquired spoon nails should prompt evaluation for iron deficiency and why it developed, including bleeding, poor intake, or malabsorption. Koilonychia also has non-iron causes, so iron should not be started from appearance alone.

Associated Conditions

  • classically chronic iron deficiency, but also hereditary or physiologic variants, repeated trauma or chemical exposure, dermatologic disease, and selected systemic disorders

Assessment Method

Inspect fingernails and toenails from above and the side for concavity, thinning, brittleness, symmetry, number of nails involved, periungual disease, and change from baseline; ask about infancy or family history, trauma and occupational exposure, fatigue, dyspnea, pica, bleeding, diet, gastrointestinal symptoms, and medications, then obtain cause-directed blood testing when indicated.

Positive Finding

One or more nail plates are abnormally thin and concave from side to side with raised edges, producing a spoon-shaped contour.

Mechanism

The exact pathophysiology is not settled; nail-matrix or nail-bed changes, thinning and softening of the plate, impaired tissue support or blood flow, and repeated mechanical force can contribute depending on the cause.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Koilonychia finding raises concern for classically chronic iron deficiency, but also hereditary or physiologic variants, repeated trauma or chemical exposure, dermatologic disease, and selected systemic disorders because the exact pathophysiology is not settled; nail-matrix or nail-bed changes, thinning and softening of the plate, impaired tissue support or blood flow, and repeated mechanical force can contribute depending on the cause. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Koilonychia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

The finding is neither sensitive nor specific for iron deficiency, subtle curvature is difficult to judge, the water-drop test can be falsely positive and is not well validated, normal infant nails or familial forms occur, and nail recovery lags because nails grow slowly. Therefore, Koilonychia cannot confirm or exclude classically chronic iron deficiency, but also hereditary or physiologic variants, repeated trauma or chemical exposure, dermatologic disease, and selected systemic disorders by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • familial koilonychia

  • normal thin infant nails

  • traumatic nail dystrophy

  • onychomycosis

  • nail psoriasis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as familial koilonychia, normal thin infant nails, traumatic nail dystrophy, onychomycosis, nail psoriasis.

False Negative Situations

Koilonychia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of classically chronic iron deficiency, but also hereditary or physiologic variants, repeated trauma or chemical exposure, dermatologic disease, and selected systemic disorders.
