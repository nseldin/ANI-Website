Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Serious hypersensitivity

  • Dose reduction is required in severe renal impairment, moderate hepatic impairment and with strong CYP3A4 inhibition under product labeling

Nursing Essentials

  • Review ECG/QT history and CYP2D6/CYP3A4 interactions in addition to routine anticholinergic screening.

  • ER capsules are swallowed whole; reassess residual volume, cognition, bowel function and dry-mouth dental risk.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors increase exposure, especially when CYP2D6 metabolism is poor.

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs and class IA/III antiarrhythmics increase repolarization concern.

  • Other anticholinergics add retention, constipation and cognitive toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Renal and hepatic function

  • Post-void residual and bowel/cognitive assessment

  • ECG/electrolytes when QT risk is clinically meaningful

Nclex Traps

  • Fesoterodine is converted directly to 5-HMT; tolterodine requires CYP2D6 for that pathway.

  • Bladder functional selectivity does not eliminate systemic anticholinergic effects.

  • A normal QT at baseline does not erase interaction-driven exposure risk.

Used To Treat

Overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge urinary incontinence in adults.

Mechanism

Parent tolterodine and 5-HMT competitively block muscarinic receptors, reducing M3-driven IP3-calcium contraction during bladder filling. Extensive CYP2D6 metabolizers generate active 5-HMT; poor metabolizers retain more parent drug, but total unbound antimuscarinic activity can remain clinically similar. Excess exposure can lengthen cardiac repolarization, so QT concerns become more important with poor metabolism, interacting drugs and congenital/acquired QT risk.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with urinary retention, gastric retention or uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma. Angioedema can compromise the airway. Use caution with congenital/acquired QT prolongation, antiarrhythmics, CYP inhibitors and combined anticholinergic burden.

Description

Tolterodine is a competitive muscarinic antagonist whose parent drug and active 5-hydroxymethyl metabolite reduce involuntary detrusor contraction. CYP2D6 converts tolterodine to 5-HMT; poor CYP2D6 metabolizers rely more on CYP3A4 and have higher parent-drug exposure. That metabolic split matters when strong inhibitors, hepatic/renal impairment or QT risk are present.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, headache, abdominal pain and blurred vision

  • Urinary retention

  • Somnolence, dizziness, confusion or hallucinations

  • QT prolongation/tachycardia at increased exposure and rare angioedema
