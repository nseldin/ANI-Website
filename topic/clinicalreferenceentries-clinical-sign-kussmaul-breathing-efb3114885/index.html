Definition

Kussmaul breathing is a sustained pattern of unusually deep, generally regular breathing used to compensate for severe metabolic acidosis. It is distinct from the cardiovascular Kussmaul jugular-venous-pressure sign.

Plain Language

The person takes repeated deep, steady breaths to blow off carbon dioxide when the blood has become dangerously acidic.

Why It Matters

Kussmaul breathing can signal diabetic ketoacidosis, kidney failure, lactic acidosis, or another severe metabolic disturbance. It requires prompt glucose, perfusion, acid-base, electrolyte, and cause assessment; not every fast breath is Kussmaul breathing.

Assessment Method

Observe depth, rate, effort, and mental status.

Positive Finding

Breathing is sustained, deep, and often rapid without cyclic pauses.

Mechanism

Acidemia stimulates chemoreceptors to increase alveolar ventilation and lower carbon dioxide.

Clinical Interpretation

The Kussmaul breathing pattern raises concern for severe metabolic acidosis because acidemia stimulates chemoreceptors to increase alveolar ventilation and lower carbon dioxide. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Kussmaul breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

False Negative Situations
