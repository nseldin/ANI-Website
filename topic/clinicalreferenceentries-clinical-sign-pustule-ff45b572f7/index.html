Definition

Pustule means a raised skin lesion filled with pus, which is opaque white or yellow inflammatory material. A pustule can form in a hair follicle or elsewhere and does not by itself prove bacterial infection.

Plain Language

A pustule is a pus-filled skin bump; some are infectious, while others contain sterile inflammation.

Why It Matters

The number, distribution, follicular pattern, medication timing, and systemic symptoms help distinguish acne or localized infection from inflammatory disease, a medication reaction, or a contagious generalized rash. Fever, rapid spread, toxicity, or mucosal involvement requires prompt evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • neutrophils and inflammatory debris collecting within the epidermis or a hair follicle because of bacterial infection, acne, sterile inflammation, medication reaction, or another inflammatory or infectious process

Assessment Method

Inspect without squeezing and document size, number, follicular or nonfollicular location, distribution, surrounding redness, warmth, pain, fluctuance, drainage, stage and synchrony, mucosal or palm and sole involvement, medications, exposures, fever, toxicity, and immune status.

Positive Finding

A circumscribed raised lesion contains opaque white, yellow, or cream-colored purulent material rather than clear fluid.

Mechanism

Neutrophils, cellular debris, and sometimes microorganisms accumulate in an epidermal or follicular cavity, producing opaque material and lifting the skin surface.

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as vesicle, papule, follicular plug, small abscess, inflamed cyst.

False Negative Situations

Pustule may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of neutrophils and inflammatory debris collecting within the epidermis or a hair follicle because of bacterial infection, acne, sterile inflammation, medication reaction, or another inflammatory or infectious process.

Modern Status

Current descriptive morphology, not proof of bacterial infection; localized acneiform lesions and sterile inflammatory pustules differ from an acute generalized febrile pustular eruption that may require urgent infection-control or medication-reaction evaluation.
