Used To Treat

Selected acute ischemic stroke, acute myocardial infarction, and acute massive pulmonary embolism under indication-specific protocols after hemorrhage risk, timing, anatomy, and alternatives are evaluated.

Description

Alteplase can restore blood flow only when the problem is a fibrin-rich thrombus and the potential benefit exceeds the bleeding risk. It binds fibrin within a clot and converts nearby plasminogen to plasmin, which cuts the fibrin mesh. The same fibrinolytic system cannot distinguish a harmful thrombus from every hemostatic plug, so recent surgery, active bleeding, uncontrolled pressure, intracranial pathology, and coagulation abnormalities matter. In stroke, noncontrast head imaging must exclude hemorrhage because a bleeding stroke can mimic an ischemic stroke and alteplase would amplify that bleeding. Time matters because salvageable penumbra shrinks while infarcted, injured microvasculature becomes more prone to hemorrhagic transformation.

Mechanism

Alteplase is recombinant human tissue-type plasminogen activator. Fibrin binding increases its local catalytic conversion of plasminogen to plasmin. Plasmin cleaves cross-linked fibrin and fibrinogen, dissolving clot structure and consuming some circulating coagulation substrate. The drug has a short plasma half-life, but clot breakdown and impaired hemostasis continue after infusion. Reperfusion can rescue threatened tissue, yet it can also force blood through damaged capillaries, explaining hemorrhagic transformation and the need for strict blood-pressure and neurologic surveillance. Angioedema can occur, particularly with concurrent ACE-inhibitor exposure, because plasmin and kallikrein pathways can increase bradykinin.

Boxed Warning

Alteplase does not have a U.S. boxed warning, but severe internal and intracranial bleeding is its defining hazard. Acute ischemic stroke treatment requires rapid expert evaluation, hemorrhage-excluding brain imaging, exact last-known-well or protocol-defined timing, blood-pressure control, contraindication review, weight-based preparation, and access to emergency hemorrhage management. Indications use different doses and exclusion rules; a Cathflo catheter-clearance dose is not an Activase systemic-thrombolysis regimen.

Adverse Effects

  • Bleeding or oozing from puncture sites, nausea, fever, hypotension, and reperfusion dysrhythmias in myocardial infarction.

  • Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, retroperitoneal or GI bleeding, hemopericardium, cholesterol embolization, orolingual angioedema, anaphylactoid reaction, and death.

Contraindications

  • For acute ischemic stroke, current intracranial hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, active internal bleeding, recent intracranial or intraspinal surgery or serious head trauma, selected intracranial lesions, bleeding diathesis, and severe uncontrolled hypertension are major label contraindications.

  • AMI and PE have overlapping but not identical exclusions; use the indication-specific label and protocol instead of transferring a stroke checklist unchanged.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record exact weight, last-known-well, baseline NIHSS or indication-specific severity, blood pressure, glucose, imaging result, anticoagulant/antiplatelet history, recent procedures, bleeding history, and all puncture sites before infusion.

  • Perform frequent neurologic, airway, blood-pressure, and bleeding checks. Stop infusion and activate the hemorrhage pathway for new severe headache, vomiting, acute hypertension, neurologic decline, or major bleeding.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, and other drugs that impair hemostasis increase bleeding risk; timing around stroke thrombolysis follows a strict protocol.

  • ACE inhibitors can increase orolingual angioedema risk through bradykinin-related pathways, so tongue or lip swelling after alteplase is an airway emergency.

Key Labs

  • Glucose, CBC and platelets, PT/INR, aPTT, renal function, pregnancy testing when relevant, blood type and screen when bleeding risk is high, plus indication-specific imaging.

  • Neurologic status, blood pressure, airway, puncture sites, urine/stool/emesis, hemoglobin trend, and post-treatment imaging according to protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Alteplase lyses fibrin; it does not mechanically remove a large-vessel clot and does not replace thrombectomy evaluation.

  • Do not give aspirin reflexively immediately after stroke alteplase. Post-thrombolysis antithrombotic timing depends on protocol and follow-up hemorrhage assessment.
