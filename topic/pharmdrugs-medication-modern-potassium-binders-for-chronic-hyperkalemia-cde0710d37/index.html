Used To Treat

Ongoing hyperkalemia management when GI potassium removal can support continuation of indicated therapy; not a substitute for immediate membrane stabilization and intracellular shifting in life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Description

Patiromer and sodium zirconium cyclosilicate remove potassium through the stool without systemic absorption, but they exchange different counterions and have different adverse effects. Patiromer exchanges calcium, mainly in the colon, and can bind magnesium. Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate exchanges sodium/hydrogen throughout the GI tract and can cause sodium-related edema. Neither should delay IV calcium, insulin/glucose or other emergency treatment when ECG instability exists.

Mechanism

Patiromer's cross-linked polymer presents calcium counterions that exchange for luminal potassium; increased fecal potassium shifts whole-body balance over hours to days. Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate uses size-selective micropores that trap potassium/ammonium in exchange for sodium and hydrogen. These agents remove potassium from the body, unlike insulin or beta-2 agonists, which temporarily shift potassium into cells, and IV calcium, which stabilizes myocardium without lowering potassium.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not use as sole emergency treatment for life-threatening hyperkalemia because onset is delayed. Patiromer can cause hypomagnesemia and binds other oral medications, generally requiring 3-hour separation. Avoid or use cautiously in severe GI motility disorders.

Adverse Effects

  • Patiromer: constipation, hypomagnesemia, diarrhea, nausea and flatulence

  • Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate: edema and hypokalemia

  • GI intolerance or obstruction risk in severe motility disease

Contraindications

  • No routine use as emergency monotherapy

  • Clarify bowel obstruction/major GI dysmotility

  • Match sodium/edema risk versus magnesium/constipation and interaction risk

Nursing Essentials

  • If severe hyperkalemia or ECG change exists, prioritize cardiac stabilization and rapid shifting while definitive removal proceeds.

  • Trend potassium after RAAS-inhibitor, diet, kidney-function and binder changes; do not chase one value without context.

Interactions

  • Patiromer generally requires 3-hour separation from other oral drugs unless specifically tested.

  • Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate can transiently alter gastric pH and affects selected oral-drug exposure.

  • Laxatives or severe diarrhea can unpredictably change potassium balance.

Key Labs

  • Potassium and ECG when urgent

  • Magnesium with patiromer

  • Renal function, bicarbonate and volume/edema status

Nclex Traps

  • Binding removes potassium; insulin only redistributes it.

  • IV calcium protects the heart but does not lower potassium.

  • Patiromer exchanges calcium, unlike sodium polystyrene sulfonate's sodium exchange.
