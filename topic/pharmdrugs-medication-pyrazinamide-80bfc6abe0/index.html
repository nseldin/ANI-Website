Class

First-line antitubercular medication

Used To Treat

Active tuberculosis as part of initial RIPE therapy when the organism and client factors support use.

Mechanism

Pyrazinamide is converted by mycobacterial pyrazinamidase to pyrazinoic acid. In acidic conditions, pyrazinoic acid accumulates and disrupts membrane transport/energy handling and persistence biology, helping shorten TB therapy when used in combination.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor liver injury symptoms and joint pain.

  • Teach adherence with the full TB regimen and to report jaundice, dark urine, severe fatigue, or severe arthralgia.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe liver disease, acute gout, or severe prior hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Alcohol and other hepatotoxic drugs increase liver injury concern.

Key Labs

  • Liver function tests when indicated.

  • Uric acid may rise and can trigger gout-like symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Joint pain during pyrazinamide therapy can be hyperuricemia, not just normal aches.

  • Liver symptoms during TB therapy are priority reporting cues.

Description

Pyrazinamide is a first-line antitubercular prodrug activated by mycobacterial pyrazinamidase into pyrazinoic acid, which works best in acidic intracellular TB lesions and helps shorten active TB therapy; hepatotoxicity and hyperuricemia/gout are first-read safety anchors. Think of it as the RIPE drug tied to liver injury plus painful joints/purines.
