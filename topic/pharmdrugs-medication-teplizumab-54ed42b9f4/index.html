Class

CD3-directed T-cell-modulating disease-modifying type-1-diabetes monoclonal antibody

Used To Treat

Delay onset of stage 3 type 1 diabetes in adults and children age 8 years and older with stage 2 disease; current labeling also includes delaying beta-cell-function decline in children age 8 to 17 years with recently diagnosed stage 3 type 1 diabetes using the product-specific course schedule.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current TZIELD labeling has no boxed warning. Screen for active EBV/CMV and other serious infection; viral reactivation can be life-threatening during treatment and for at least two months afterward. Cytokine-release syndrome, liver-test elevation, severe/prolonged lymphopenia, serious infection and hypersensitivity require protocol monitoring or discontinuation. Live vaccines are avoided from 8 weeks before through up to 52 weeks after the final course.

Mechanism

Teplizumab binds CD3 on CD4 and CD8 T lymphocytes, partially signals through the T-cell receptor complex and internalizes the CD3-antibody complex. The resulting program can deactivate pancreatic-beta-cell-autoreactive T cells, increase regulatory T-cell proportion and increase exhausted CD8 phenotypes. This changes immune pressure on beta cells while transiently causing lymphopenia and cytokine release.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm diabetes stage with autoantibodies and dysglycemia or recent stage-3 criteria, age, infection/vaccine status, CBC, liver tests and pregnancy plan before treatment; premedicate as ordered.

  • Monitor temperature, blood pressure, symptoms, CBC and liver tests during the course. Teach that hyperglycemia symptoms still require urgent testing and that delayed progression is not immunity from future diabetes.

Key Labs

  • Islet autoantibodies, glucose/OGTT and A1c or C-peptide as appropriate, CBC with lymphocytes/neutrophils, AST/ALT/bilirubin, EBV/CMV testing, infection screening and pregnancy assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Teplizumab preserves beta-cell function by modifying T cells; it is not insulin and does not directly lower acute glucose.

  • Transient lymphocyte-count reduction reflects redistribution/depletion biology but prolonged severe lymphopenia is a stop signal, not an expected result to ignore.

Description

Teplizumab-mzwv is an IV anti-CD3 monoclonal antibody that modifies autoreactive T-cell behavior rather than replacing insulin. In stage 2 type 1 diabetes it delays progression to symptomatic stage 3 disease; current 2026 labeling also covers preservation of beta-cell function in selected children with newly diagnosed stage 3 disease. It does not cure autoimmunity or guarantee that insulin will never be needed. Why do vaccine restrictions extend far beyond the 14-day infusion course? Teplizumab changes T-cell activation and commonly causes transient lymphopenia, so it can weaken vaccine response; live-vaccine safety after treatment has not been established. Therefore current labeling separates vaccine types and uses conservative windows: complete age-appropriate vaccination first, avoid inactivated or mRNA vaccines during the specified shorter interval, and avoid live vaccines from eight weeks before treatment through up to 52 weeks afterward. The long window is an immune-safety rule, not evidence that every patient remains profoundly lymphopenic for a year.
