Class

Synthetic progesterone-receptor agonist available as long-acting depot contraception and oral progestin

Used To Treat

Injectable contraception, endometriosis-associated pain with the subcutaneous depot product, and formulation-specific treatment of secondary amenorrhea or abnormal uterine bleeding; oral medroxyprogesterone can protect the endometrium when systemic estrogen is used in a patient with a uterus.

Boxed Warning

DEPO-PROVERA contraceptive labeling carries a boxed warning for significant loss of bone mineral density. Loss is greater with increasing duration and may not be completely reversible; the effect during adolescence or early adulthood may reduce peak bone mass. DMPA is not recommended as a long-term method beyond two years unless other options are considered inadequate. The warning is formulation-specific: oral medroxyprogesterone used with systemic estrogen has a different benefit-risk frame. The two-year statement is not automatic discontinuation for every patient, but it requires documented reassessment of alternatives, pregnancy goals, bone risks, and ongoing benefit rather than silent indefinite renewal.

Mechanism

Medroxyprogesterone binds intracellular progesterone receptors A and B. The ligand-receptor complex enters the nucleus and changes transcription in hypothalamus, pituitary, cervix, endometrium, breast, and bone-related endocrine pathways. Sustained DMPA exposure slows hypothalamic GnRH pulses, suppresses pituitary FSH/LH and prevents the midcycle LH surge, so ovulation does not occur. Cervical glands secrete thick, low-volume mucus that impedes sperm, while endometrial glands become inactive and thin. Lower ovarian estradiol reduces endometrial proliferation but also shifts bone remodeling toward resorption and reduces expected adolescent bone accrual. Depot crystals dissolve slowly and apparent half-life is prolonged, so stopping future injections does not immediately remove circulating progestin or restore the HPO axis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record product, route, site, date, due window, and lot; IM and subcutaneous products differ in dose and administration. Give a clear late-injection and backup-contraception plan.

  • Explain expected irregular bleeding and delayed fertility return before injection. Reassess calcium/vitamin D intake, weight-bearing activity, smoking, glucocorticoids, eating disorder, fracture history, and other bone risks during continued use.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected pregnancy, current or past breast cancer or another progestin-sensitive malignancy, active significant liver disease, unexplained vaginal bleeding, and serious thromboembolic disease according to the exact product label.

  • Before repeat depot dosing, verify timing, pregnancy risk, bleeding history, mood, bone risk, interacting drugs, and whether another method better fits future fertility goals.

Interactions

  • Aminoglutethimide can reduce medroxyprogesterone concentrations; broad CYP3A-inducer effects and product-specific evidence should be reviewed when contraceptive reliability is critical.

  • Systemic estrogen changes thrombotic and endometrial risk; the combined regimen must be evaluated as a regimen rather than assuming progestin neutralizes every estrogen effect.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy assessment as indicated; bone-density testing is individualized rather than routine for every user, while liver, metabolic, or bleeding evaluation follows risk and symptoms

  • Bleeding pattern, injection schedule, mood, weight, bone and fracture risk, pregnancy symptoms, VTE symptoms, and return-of-fertility expectations

Nclex Traps

  • DMPA's fertility delay reflects slow depot release and prolonged HPO-axis suppression; it does not mean permanent infertility.

  • Why limit long-term use? The same suppression that prevents ovulation lowers estradiol, reducing bone formation and peak bone accrual while the nonremovable depot prolongs exposure.

Description

Medroxyprogesterone acetate is a synthetic progestin whose clinical behavior depends on formulation. Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate, or DMPA, forms a slowly dissolving intramuscular or subcutaneous reservoir that suppresses ovulation for months, thickens cervical mucus, and makes the endometrium atrophic. Oral medroxyprogesterone provides shorter progestin exposure for cycle control, endometrial protection, or other gynecologic indications. DMPA is highly effective without daily adherence, but the depot cannot be removed after injection. Drug release can delay ovulation and fertility return for months after the last scheduled dose, and sustained hypoestrogenism reduces bone mineral accrual and density. That pharmacokinetic tail explains both convenience and the two-year warning.

Adverse Effects

  • Irregular bleeding or amenorrhea, weight change, headache, mood change, breast tenderness, abdominal discomfort, acne, and injection-site reaction

  • Bone-density loss, delayed return of ovulation, severe depression, thrombosis in susceptible settings, liver dysfunction, ectopic pregnancy if method failure occurs, anaphylaxis, and rare injection-site necrosis
