Contraindications

  • Marked marrow suppression below product thresholds and serious hypersensitivity

  • Pregnancy should be avoided; reproductive and handling precautions apply to patients and caregivers

  • Do not interchange product strengths or scored tablets/capsules without verifying indication and dose

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend CBC with differential, platelets, reticulocytes, renal/liver function, and dose holds. A falling reticulocyte count can reveal marrow suppression before hemoglobin collapses.

  • Teach daily adherence and delayed benefit. MCV and HbF can support adherence assessment, but clinical crisis frequency and toxicity determine success.

Interactions

  • Other marrow suppressants, radiation, and selected antiretrovirals can increase cytopenia or pancreatitis/hepatotoxicity.

  • Live vaccines may pose infection risk during immunosuppression; renal impairment increases exposure.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, platelets, reticulocytes, MCV, HbF/hemoglobin fraction, renal and liver function

  • Crisis/acute-chest frequency, transfusions, ulcers/skin changes, pregnancy status, and infection/bleeding signs

Nclex Traps

  • Hydroxyurea prevents sickle complications over time; it does not treat acute pain immediately.

  • Macrocytosis is an expected pharmacodynamic clue, but CBC suppression still requires holds and dose changes.

Used To Treat

Product-specific reduction of painful crises and acute-chest complications in sickle-cell anemia and cytoreduction in selected myeloproliferative or malignant disorders. Siklos, Droxia, and Hydrea indications, strengths, and tablet/capsule handling differ. Hydroxyurea prevents complications over time; it is not an immediate analgesic for a vaso-occlusive crisis.

Mechanism

Ribonucleotide reductase converts ribonucleotides into deoxyribonucleotides needed for DNA replication. Hydroxyurea quenches the catalytic tyrosyl radical in the RRM2 subunit, reversibly inhibiting the enzyme and arresting susceptible cells in S phase. In sickle-cell disease, the complete HbF induction mechanism remains uncertain, but stress erythropoiesis and gene-regulatory effects increase gamma-globin and HbF. HbF dilutes intracellular HbS and disrupts nucleation and elongation of deoxy-HbS polymers. Hydroxyurea also lowers neutrophils and reticulocytes, increases red-cell water and mean cell volume, improves deformability, and reduces endothelial adhesion and inflammation. Renal elimination makes dose reduction important when kidney function falls.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hydroxyurea can cause severe myelosuppression and is carcinogenic/mutagenic with long-term secondary-malignancy concern. It can cause embryo-fetal harm; use effective contraception and safe handling. Hold and adjust for neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, or severe anemia according to the exact product algorithm. Macrocytosis is expected and is not caused by B12 deficiency, but it can mask a true deficiency.

Description

Hydroxyurea is an antimetabolite that inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, limiting deoxyribonucleotide supply for DNA synthesis. In sickle-cell disease it increases fetal hemoglobin, lowers neutrophil burden, improves red-cell hydration and deformability, and reduces cellular adhesion. HbF does not join HbS polymers efficiently, so more HbF interrupts polymer growth when oxygen falls. These effects reduce vaso-occlusion but take weeks to months. The same DNA-synthesis inhibition can suppress neutrophils, platelets, and reticulocytes and can harm an embryo, so CBC-guided dosing and reproductive precautions are central.

Adverse Effects

  • Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia/reticulocytopenia, infection, or bleeding

  • Macrocytosis, GI upset, skin/nail hyperpigmentation, leg ulcers, mucositis, or alopecia

  • Embryo-fetal toxicity, impaired fertility, cutaneous malignancy, or secondary leukemia with long-term exposure/context
