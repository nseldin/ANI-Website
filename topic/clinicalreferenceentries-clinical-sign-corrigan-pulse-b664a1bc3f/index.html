Definition

Corrigan pulse is a palpated pulse sign. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means the pulse rises abruptly and collapses rapidly.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Corrigan pulse means the pulse rises abruptly and collapses rapidly.

Why It Matters

The observed Corrigan pulse finding can signal severe aortic regurgitation. Large systolic ejection followed by diastolic runoff back through the incompetent aortic valve creates a wide pulse pressure and rapid collapse. Corrigan pulse cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Palpate the radial or brachial pulse with the arm elevated.

Positive Finding

The pulse rises abruptly and collapses rapidly.

Mechanism

Large systolic ejection followed by diastolic runoff back through the incompetent aortic valve creates a wide pulse pressure and rapid collapse.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Corrigan pulse finding raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation because large systolic ejection followed by diastolic runoff back through the incompetent aortic valve creates a wide pulse pressure and rapid collapse. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Corrigan pulse does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Corrigan pulse to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: thyrotoxicosis, anemia, arteriovenous fistula. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate the radial or brachial pulse with the arm elevated. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Corrigan pulse as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is not specific for aortic regurgitation and mild disease may lack the finding. Therefore, Corrigan pulse cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • thyrotoxicosis

  • anemia

  • arteriovenous fistula

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as thyrotoxicosis, anemia, arteriovenous fistula.

False Negative Situations

Corrigan pulse may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe aortic regurgitation.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; severity and context matter. When performed as described, Corrigan pulse can adjust concern for severe aortic regurgitation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Corrigan pulse: It is not specific for aortic regurgitation and mild disease may lack the finding.

Safety Precautions

Observe Corrigan pulse with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Palpate the radial or brachial pulse with the arm elevated. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Elevating the arm accentuates the pulse but do not infer valve severity without echocardiography.
