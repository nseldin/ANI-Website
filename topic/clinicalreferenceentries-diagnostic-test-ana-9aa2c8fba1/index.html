Summary

ANA (antinuclear antibody) is a high-yield Hematology/Immunology diagnostic reference. Antinuclear antibody screening test used in autoimmune disease evaluation, especially lupus patterns.

Quick Answer

ANA (antinuclear antibody): Antinuclear antibody screening test used in autoimmune disease evaluation, especially lupus patterns. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    Systemic autoimmune disease such as lupus is less likely, though not impossible when clinical suspicion is strong.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    Antinuclear antibodies are present. This supports autoimmune evaluation but can occur in healthy people, infections, medications, or other autoimmune disease.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Titer, pattern, symptoms, urinalysis, complements, anti-dsDNA/ENA, CBC, and organ findings determine meaning.

Sections

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
