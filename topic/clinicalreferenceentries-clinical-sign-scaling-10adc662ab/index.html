Definition

Scaling means keratin from the stratum corneum (the outermost layer of dead skin cells) builds up or sheds in visible flakes or plates.

Plain Language

Scaling is dry or greasy flaking from the outer layer of skin.

Why It Matters

The distribution, border, thickness, associated plaques, scalp or nail changes, and symptoms help distinguish psoriasis, dermatitis, fungal infection, and other causes. Widespread tender peeling with fever, mucosal involvement, or a new medication can indicate a severe reaction.

Associated Conditions

  • abnormal accumulation and shedding of stratum corneum caused by altered keratinization or epidermal turnover in inflammatory, infectious, genetic, environmental, or medication-related skin disease

Assessment Method

Inspect in neutral light and document location, distribution, border, thickness, color, adherence, dry or greasy quality, associated plaques or redness, scalp and nail findings, itch, pain, fissuring, hair loss, exposures, medications, duration, and systemic symptoms.

Positive Finding

Visible dry or greasy flakes or plate-like fragments of outer keratin accumulate on or shed from the skin surface.

Mechanism

Epidermal turnover, maturation, or cohesion is altered, so excess outer keratin builds up and separates in visible flakes or plates.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Scaling finding raises concern for abnormal accumulation and shedding of stratum corneum caused by altered keratinization or epidermal turnover in inflammatory, infectious, genetic, environmental, or medication-related skin disease because epidermal turnover, maturation, or cohesion is altered, so excess outer keratin builds up and separates in visible flakes or plates. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Scaling does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Scaling to focus evaluation of abnormal accumulation and shedding of stratum corneum caused by altered keratinization or epidermal turnover in inflammatory, infectious, genetic, environmental, or medication-related skin disease and the reviewed alternatives: crust, xerosis, desquamation, hyperkeratosis, dried exudate. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect in neutral light and document location, distribution, border, thickness, color, adherence, dry or greasy quality, associated plaques or redness, scalp and nail findings, itch, pain, fissuring, hair loss, exposures, medications, duration, and systemic symptoms. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Scaling as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Washing, emollients, scratching, and treatment alter scale, visibility differs across skin tones, and appearance alone cannot distinguish inflammatory disease from infection or malignancy. Therefore, Scaling cannot confirm or exclude abnormal accumulation and shedding of stratum corneum caused by altered keratinization or epidermal turnover in inflammatory, infectious, genetic, environmental, or medication-related skin disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • crust

  • xerosis

  • hyperkeratosis

  • dried exudate

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as crust, xerosis, desquamation, hyperkeratosis, dried exudate.

False Negative Situations

Scaling may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of abnormal accumulation and shedding of stratum corneum caused by altered keratinization or epidermal turnover in inflammatory, infectious, genetic, environmental, or medication-related skin disease.

Modern Status

Current secondary morphology, not a diagnosis; its pattern can support psoriasis, dermatitis, or dermatophyte infection, but widespread peeling with pain, fever, mucosal involvement, or recent medication exposure may indicate a severe reaction. Interpret Scaling in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Washing, emollients, scratching, and treatment alter scale, visibility differs across skin tones, and appearance alone cannot distinguish inflammatory disease from infection or malignancy.

Safety Precautions

Observe Scaling with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect in neutral light and document location, distribution, border, thickness, color, adherence, dry or greasy quality, associated plaques or redness, scalp and nail findings, itch, pain, fissuring, hair loss, exposures, medications, duration, and systemic symptoms. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not assume every scaly lesion is psoriasis or fungal infection and do not use topical corticosteroids blindly when infection is possible; urgently assess widespread tender peeling, fever, mucosal or eye involvement, a new medication, or systemic illness, and evaluate a persistent changing or nonhealing scaly lesion.
