Used To Treat

Topical ketoconazole treats susceptible superficial fungal and Malassezia-associated skin disease. Oral tablets are reserved for selected serious systemic fungal infections only when effective alternatives are unavailable or not tolerated; oral tablets are not indicated for onychomycosis, cutaneous dermatophytes, or Candida infections.

Mechanism

Fungal CYP51 inhibition depletes ergosterol. At systemic concentrations, ketoconazole also inhibits human CYP enzymes needed for cortisol and androgen synthesis, including cholesterol side-chain cleavage and 17-alpha-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase pathways; this explains adrenal insufficiency, gynecomastia, reduced libido, and reproductive effects. Strong CYP3A4 inhibition can sharply raise partner drugs and provoke torsades or other toxicity. Oral absorption requires gastric acidity, so acid suppression lowers exposure. Topical use minimizes these systemic mechanisms because little drug reaches blood.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - ORAL KETOCONAZOLE: Serious hepatotoxicity, including death and liver transplantation, can occur without obvious risk factors. Tablets are not indicated for onychomycosis, cutaneous dermatophyte infection, or Candida infection and should be used only when safer effective antifungal therapy is unavailable or not tolerated. Multiple CYP3A/QT interactions are contraindicated because life-threatening ventricular dysrhythmia can result.

Contraindications

  • Active liver disease, serious hypersensitivity, and numerous CYP3A/QT-dependent drugs are major oral contraindications. Follow the current label interaction list.

  • Do not use oral tablets for common superficial fungal disease when topical or safer systemic therapy exists.

Nursing Essentials

  • Always identify route. For oral therapy, confirm specialist rationale, baseline liver tests, weekly ALT under labeling, medication list, adrenal risk, and acid-suppressant use. Stop and escalate jaundice, dark urine, anorexia, nausea, fatigue, or right-upper-quadrant pain.

  • For topical therapy, keep away from eyes, use for the prescribed duration, and reassess if the rash worsens because dermatitis can mimic persistent fungus.

Interactions

  • Potent CYP3A inhibition raises many antiarrhythmics, statins, sedatives, opioids, immunosuppressants, and oncology drugs; several are contraindicated.

  • PPIs/H2 blockers/antacids lower oral absorption. Other hepatotoxins and alcohol increase concern; exogenous or stress-dose steroids may be required if adrenal reserve is impaired.

Key Labs

  • For oral use: baseline and frequent ALT/AST/bilirubin/alkaline phosphatase/INR as indicated, cortisol when adrenal insufficiency is suspected, potassium/magnesium and ECG for QT risk, and drug-specific interaction levels.

  • Routine systemic laboratory monitoring is not needed for ordinary topical use because absorption is low, but local response and irritation still require assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Topical ketoconazole remains useful; the severe boxed warning concerns systemic oral tablets.

  • Adrenal suppression is mechanistically linked to human steroidogenic CYP inhibition, not simply 'liver toxicity.'

Description

Ketoconazole is an imidazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51 and ergosterol synthesis. Topical shampoo or cream creates local skin exposure with limited systemic absorption and remains useful for seborrheic dermatitis, tinea, and selected superficial infection. Oral ketoconazole is a different safety problem: it can cause fatal hepatotoxicity or liver transplantation, strongly inhibits CYP3A4, prolongs QT through interacting substrates, and suppresses adrenal and gonadal steroid synthesis. Because safer systemic azoles exist, the oral label restricts use to serious mycoses when alternatives are unavailable or not tolerated. Saying only 'azole antifungal' hides the decisive route distinction.

Adverse Effects

  • Topical burning, irritation, dryness, or contact dermatitis. Oral nausea, abdominal pain, headache, pruritus, and liver-test elevation.

  • Fatal hepatic failure, adrenal insufficiency, gynecomastia/sexual dysfunction, QT/torsades through interactions, anaphylaxis, and severe skin reaction.
