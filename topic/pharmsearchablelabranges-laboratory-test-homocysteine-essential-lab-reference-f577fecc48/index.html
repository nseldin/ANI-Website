Range

Total homocysteine is reported in micromol/L. An adult upper reference limit near 15 micromol/L is common, but the performing laboratory's method and interval are the authority; age, sex, kidney function, smoking, vitamin status, medicines, supplements, and pregnancy or other population context can change interpretation. Fasting for 8-12 hours may be requested, but it is not universal, so follow the exact collection instructions and do not stop medicines or supplements unless directed.

Why

An elevated total homocysteine result signals altered methionine metabolism and is associated with vitamin B12, folate, or vitamin B6 deficiency, kidney dysfunction, hypothyroidism, or the rare inherited disorder homocystinuria, depending on the clinical context. Promptly assess fatigue, pallor, mouth or tongue changes, paresthesias (tingling or numbness), gait or cognitive change, diet and malabsorption risk, bariatric or bowel surgery, alcohol, medicines and B-vitamin supplements, kidney and thyroid disease, premature thrombosis, lens or vision problems, skeletal features, and family or newborn-screen history. Cause-directed evaluation can include CBC and MCV, vitamin B12, folate, methylmalonic acid, creatinine/eGFR, TSH, and, when homocystinuria is suspected, plasma amino acids including methionine plus metabolic-genetics review and molecular testing. Escalate promptly when suspected megaloblastic anemia accompanies ataxia or another new neurologic deficit because clinician-directed vitamin B12 replacement may need to begin without waiting for confirmatory results; an acute vascular event in suspected or known homocystinuria requires emergency evaluation. One high result does not diagnose a B-vitamin deficiency or homocystinuria, prove the cause of a clot or cardiovascular event, or by itself justify vitamin treatment. Routine cardiovascular screening is not recommended for everyone, and lowering homocysteine in the general population has not reliably lowered myocardial-infarction or stroke risk.

High Causes

  • Vitamin B12 deficiency

  • Folate deficiency

  • Vitamin B6 deficiency

  • Chronic kidney disease

  • Hypothyroidism

  • Classic homocystinuria or another inherited homocysteine-remethylation disorder

  • Older age, smoking, malnutrition, alcohol use, or a medicine or supplement effect, depending on context

Low Causes

  • A low result is usually not the clinical priority; interpret any unexpected value with the performing laboratory and collection context.

Critical Concerns

  • Suspected megaloblastic anemia with ataxia, gait change, or another new neurologic deficit needs prompt clinician review; vitamin B12 treatment may need to start before confirmatory testing is complete.

  • Suspected or known homocystinuria with an acute thromboembolic event requires emergency vascular evaluation.

  • Do not diagnose vitamin deficiency, homocystinuria, or a cardiovascular cause from homocysteine alone, and do not assume that lowering a nonspecific elevation prevents myocardial infarction or stroke.

Nursing Considerations

  • Verify fasting and medication or supplement instructions, document B-vitamin use without independently stopping it, and review the collection method and laboratory interval.

  • Assess anemia and neurologic findings, nutrition and malabsorption risks, kidney and thyroid function, smoking and alcohol, thrombosis or premature vascular history, eye and skeletal features, and family or newborn-screen history.

  • Coordinate CBC/MCV, vitamin B12, folate, methylmalonic acid, creatinine/eGFR, TSH, and specialist metabolic testing when indicated; trend both symptoms and cause-specific markers because a lower homocysteine value does not by itself prove clinical recovery.

Nclex Traps

  • Homocysteine is a supportive metabolic clue, not a stand-alone diagnosis; methylmalonic acid is more specific to vitamin B12-related metabolism than homocysteine.

  • Do not give folate or other vitamins solely to normalize one result without evaluating vitamin B12 and the cause, and do not promise general cardiovascular-event prevention from homocysteine lowering.
