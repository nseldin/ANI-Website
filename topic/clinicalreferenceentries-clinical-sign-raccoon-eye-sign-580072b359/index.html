Definition

Raccoon-eye sign is an observed trauma sign. It is a clue to anterior basilar skull fracture. A positive finding means periorbital ecchymosis develops, often bilateral and without direct eye impact.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Raccoon-eye sign means periorbital ecchymosis develops, often bilateral and without direct eye impact.

Why It Matters

The observed Raccoon-eye sign finding can signal anterior basilar skull fracture. Blood tracks from the anterior cranial fossa into loose eyelid tissues. Raccoon-eye sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • anterior basilar skull fracture

Assessment Method

Inspect both periorbital regions after trauma.

Positive Finding

Periorbital ecchymosis develops, often bilateral and without direct eye impact.

Mechanism

Blood tracks from the anterior cranial fossa into loose eyelid tissues.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Raccoon-eye sign finding raises concern for anterior basilar skull fracture because blood tracks from the anterior cranial fossa into loose eyelid tissues. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Raccoon-eye sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Raccoon-eye sign to focus evaluation of anterior basilar skull fracture and the reviewed alternatives: nasal fracture, allergic shiners, bleeding disorder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect both periorbital regions after trauma. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Raccoon-eye sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Direct facial injury and coagulopathy mimic it; absence does not exclude fracture. Therefore, Raccoon-eye sign cannot confirm or exclude anterior basilar skull fracture by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • nasal fracture

  • allergic shiners

  • bleeding disorder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as nasal fracture, allergic shiners, bleeding disorder.

False Negative Situations

Raccoon-eye sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of anterior basilar skull fracture.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Protect the eye and cervical spine and urgently assess vision, CSF leak, and neurologic status.
