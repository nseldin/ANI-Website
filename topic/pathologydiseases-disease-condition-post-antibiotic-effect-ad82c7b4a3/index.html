Plain Meaning

Post-antibiotic effect is persistent suppression of bacterial growth after antibiotic concentration has fallen below the MIC.

Pathology

Some antibiotics keep bacteria suppressed after brief exposure because recovery from drug-induced injury takes time, the drug remains bound at the target, or bacteria must resynthesize damaged enzymes/proteins. This effect supports longer dosing intervals for selected antibiotics, especially when balanced with toxicity monitoring.

Pathophysiology

PAE is organism- and drug-specific. Protein-synthesis inhibitors, DNA/RNA-active agents, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, and some carbapenems can show meaningful PAE in selected contexts. It does not eliminate the need for source control or correct dosing.

Signs Symptoms

  • Sustained clinical response despite concentrations dipping below MIC for part of the interval

  • Therapeutic failure if clinicians overestimate PAE or source control is inadequate

Diagnostics

  • Culture/MIC, infection site, severity, renal function, drug levels when required, and response trend

  • Do not infer PAE at the bedside without knowing the drug/organism context

Treatments

  • Use dosing intervals supported by the specific antibiotic and protocol

  • Maintain monitoring for toxicity and clinical response

Nursing Priorities

  • Understand why some antibiotics are not dosed simply to stay above MIC all day

  • Still give on schedule because PAE is planned pharmacology, not a reason to delay doses

  • Connect extended-interval aminoglycoside dosing with peak effect, PAE, and trough safety

Complications

  • Underdosing if PAE is misapplied

  • Toxicity if exposure is excessive

  • Resistance selection

  • Treatment failure

Patient Education

  • Follow the prescribed schedule even if doses are spaced farther apart than expected.

  • Report worsening infection signs or medication-specific toxicity symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • PAE is not the same as a long half-life.

  • PAE does not mean skipped doses are safe.

  • The drug, organism, site, and patient condition determine whether PAE helps.
