Definition

Bradypnea is an observed respiratory-rate sign in which breathing is slower than the expected resting range for the patient's age and situation. In a typical resting adult, it commonly means fewer than 12 breaths per minute.

Plain Language

Bradypnea means breathing more slowly than expected.

Why It Matters

New bradypnea can signal opioid or sedative effect, brain injury, hypothermia, severe metabolic illness, respiratory-muscle fatigue, or failing ventilation. Sleep and athletic conditioning can also lower the rate, so alertness, breath depth, airflow, oxygenation, carbon dioxide, medications, and baseline determine urgency.

Assessment Method

Count respirations for a full minute and assess depth, rhythm, airflow, responsiveness, pupils, oxygenation, carbon dioxide when available, temperature, recent opioids or sedatives, neurologic findings, and the patient's baseline; verify monitor readings directly.

Positive Finding

The measured respiratory rate is below the expected range for age and situation; in a typical resting adult this is commonly fewer than 12 breaths per minute.

Mechanism

Depressed brainstem drive, medication effect, neurologic injury, low temperature, or exhausted respiratory muscles reduces the frequency of inspiratory signals, which can lower minute ventilation when breath depth does not compensate.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bradypnea finding raises concern for a respiratory rate below the age- and context-appropriate range from sleep or athletic conditioning, opioid or sedative effect, central nervous system injury, hypothermia, severe metabolic disturbance, respiratory muscle fatigue, or impending ventilatory failure because depressed brainstem drive, medication effect, neurologic injury, low temperature, or exhausted respiratory muscles reduces the frequency of inspiratory signals, which can lower minute ventilation when breath depth does not compensate. Evidence Limitations

A low rate does not by itself prove hypoventilation, and a normal oxygen saturation can lag or remain deceptively preserved with supplemental oxygen; sleep, trained-athlete baseline, device artifact, and deep compensating breaths affect interpretation. Therefore, Bradypnea cannot confirm or exclude a respiratory rate below the age- and context-appropriate range from sleep or athletic conditioning, opioid or sedative effect, central nervous system injury, hypothermia, severe metabolic disturbance, respiratory muscle fatigue, or impending ventilatory failure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as physiologic sleep breathing, athletic resting pattern, breath holding, monitor artifact.

False Negative Situations

Bradypnea may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of a respiratory rate below the age- and context-appropriate range from sleep or athletic conditioning, opioid or sedative effect, central nervous system injury, hypothermia, severe metabolic disturbance, respiratory muscle fatigue, or impending ventilatory failure.

Modern Status

Current vital-sign abnormality; a low rate may be physiologic during sleep or conditioning, but new bradypnea with reduced alertness is a high-priority warning sign.
