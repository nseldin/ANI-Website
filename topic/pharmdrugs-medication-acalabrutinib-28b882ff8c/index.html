Used To Treat

Adult chronic lymphocytic leukemia or small lymphocytic lymphoma and labeled mantle-cell lymphoma settings, including combination or prior-therapy contexts specified in the current product label.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Acalabrutinib has no current U.S. boxed warning. Serious or opportunistic infection, major hemorrhage, cytopenias, second primary malignancy, atrial fibrillation/flutter and ventricular arrhythmia, hypertension, and drug-induced liver injury require active surveillance. Review anticoagulants, antiplatelets, CYP3A drugs, procedures, and hepatitis B risk. Hold or modify therapy only through the current product-specific oncology plan because covalent target inhibition and interaction rules make casual schedule changes unsafe.

Mechanism

Acalabrutinib and its active metabolite bind covalently to cysteine 481 in the BTK active site, producing prolonged enzyme inhibition after plasma concentration falls. BTK normally phosphorylates PLC-gamma2, generating IP3 and diacylglycerol, intracellular calcium signaling, PKC activation, and NF-kappaB/MAPK survival programs. Blocking that cascade interrupts antigen-driven growth and chemokine-mediated tissue retention, so malignant lymphocytes can transiently redistribute from lymph nodes into blood before declining. BTK-related signaling in platelets helps explain bleeding, while impaired B-cell and innate immune function explains opportunistic infection. CYP3A controls exposure, so inhibitors can raise toxicity and inducers can produce treatment failure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before treatment, assess CBC, liver function, infection and hepatitis risk, bleeding history, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, rhythm and cardiovascular history, pregnancy potential, skin surveillance, and complete medication list.

  • During therapy, ask specifically about fever, cough, bruising, melena, hematuria, headache after trauma, palpitations, syncope, dyspnea, jaundice, dark urine, and new skin lesions; 'check interactions' must mean a documented CYP3A and bleeding review.

Contraindications

  • The current U.S. tablet label lists no absolute contraindication, but serious hypersensitivity, severe hepatic impairment, uncontrolled infection, major active bleeding, and high-risk interactions require oncology review.

  • Do not start or continue through an invasive procedure, severe cytopenia, arrhythmia, or hepatotoxicity without the label- and protocol-specific hold plan.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors and inducers under current tablet labeling; reduce dose with moderate inhibitors as directed because exposure changes can cause toxicity or loss of cancer control.

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs, fish-oil or herbal bleeding products, and invasive procedures add hemorrhage risk; acid-reducer instructions differ by tablet versus older capsule formulation, so verify the exact product.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and platelets, AST/ALT/bilirubin, infection and hepatitis assessment, blood pressure, ECG/rhythm evaluation when symptomatic or high risk, and pregnancy testing when relevant.

  • Trend disease burden and response; early lymphocytosis can reflect redistribution rather than progression, but interpretation belongs to the oncology protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Acalabrutinib covalently binds BTK Cys481; it is not a nonspecific cytotoxic and not a BCL-2 inhibitor like venetoclax.

  • Oral targeted therapy still carries major bleeding, infection, rhythm, and interaction risk. 'Just a pill' is the dangerous misconception.

Description

Acalabrutinib is an oral covalent inhibitor of Bruton's tyrosine kinase, or BTK, a signaling enzyme that B cells use downstream of the B-cell receptor, chemokine receptors, and adhesion pathways. By shutting down BTK, it reduces malignant B-cell survival, proliferation, trafficking, and protective lymph-node interactions. It is more kinase-selective than first-generation ibrutinib, but selective does not mean free of class toxicity: bleeding, infection, cytopenias, atrial or ventricular dysrhythmias, hypertension, second cancers, and liver injury still occur. It is targeted therapy because it attacks a dependency of the malignant cell, not because it spares all normal B-cell or platelet signaling.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, diarrhea, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, bruising, nausea, and upper or lower respiratory infection are common.

  • Major hemorrhage, opportunistic infection, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, atrial fibrillation/flutter, ventricular arrhythmia, hypertension, DILI, tumor lysis in high-burden disease, and second malignancy including skin cancer.
