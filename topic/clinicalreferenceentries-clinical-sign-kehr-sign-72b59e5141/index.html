Definition

Kehr sign is a patient-reported referred-pain symptom-sign. It is a clue to diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation. A positive finding means pain is felt at the shoulder despite no primary shoulder injury.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Kehr sign means pain is felt at the shoulder despite no primary shoulder injury.

Why It Matters

The provoked Kehr sign symptom can signal diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation. Phrenic afferents from the diaphragm converge with C3-C5 somatic pathways and refer pain to the shoulder. Kehr sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation

Assessment Method

Ask about shoulder-tip pain, often worse supine, while evaluating abdominal pathology.

Positive Finding

Pain is felt at the shoulder despite no primary shoulder injury.

Mechanism

Phrenic afferents from the diaphragm converge with C3-C5 somatic pathways and refer pain to the shoulder.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Kehr sign symptom raises concern for diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation because phrenic afferents from the diaphragm converge with C3-C5 somatic pathways and refer pain to the shoulder. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Kehr sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Kehr sign to focus evaluation of diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation and the reviewed alternatives: rotator cuff injury, pleurisy, myocardial ischemia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask about shoulder-tip pain, often worse supine, while evaluating abdominal pathology. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Kehr sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Shoulder pain is common and laterality is not absolute. Therefore, Kehr sign cannot confirm or exclude diaphragmatic irritation from intra-abdominal blood or inflammation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • rotator cuff injury

  • pleurisy

  • myocardial ischemia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as rotator cuff injury, pleurisy, myocardial ischemia, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Kehr sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: After trauma or pregnancy, shoulder-tip pain with instability needs urgent hemorrhage evaluation.
