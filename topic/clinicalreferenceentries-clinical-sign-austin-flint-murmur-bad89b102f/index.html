Definition

Austin Flint murmur is an auscultatory cardiac sign. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means a low-pitched mid-to-late diastolic rumble is heard without an opening snap.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Austin Flint murmur means a low-pitched mid-to-late diastolic rumble is heard without an opening snap.

Why It Matters

A positive Austin Flint murmur response can signal severe aortic regurgitation. The regurgitant aortic jet impinges on the anterior mitral leaflet and functionally obstructs inflow. Austin Flint murmur cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Auscultate the apex with the bell while the patient is in the left lateral position.

Positive Finding

A low-pitched mid-to-late diastolic rumble is heard without an opening snap.

Mechanism

The regurgitant aortic jet impinges on the anterior mitral leaflet and functionally obstructs inflow.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Austin Flint murmur response raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation because the regurgitant aortic jet impinges on the anterior mitral leaflet and functionally obstructs inflow. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Austin Flint murmur does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Austin Flint murmur to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: mitral stenosis, atrial myxoma, flow murmur. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate the apex with the bell while the patient is in the left lateral position. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Austin Flint murmur as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is difficult to hear and does not reliably quantify regurgitation. Therefore, Austin Flint murmur cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • mitral stenosis

  • atrial myxoma

  • flow murmur

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from mitral stenosis, atrial myxoma, flow murmur, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Austin Flint murmur may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Austin Flint murmur, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate the apex with the bell while the patient is in the left lateral position. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not diagnose mitral stenosis solely from an apical diastolic rumble.
