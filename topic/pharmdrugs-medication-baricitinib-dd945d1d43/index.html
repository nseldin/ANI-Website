Class

Oral targeted synthetic DMARD that inhibits Janus kinase 1 and Janus kinase 2

Used To Treat

Adults with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis after inadequate response to one or more TNF blockers, adults with severe alopecia areata, and label-defined hospitalized patients with COVID-19 who require supplemental oxygen, noninvasive or invasive ventilation, or ECMO. Dose and duration depend on indication, renal function, and interacting drugs.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Serious infections including tuberculosis and invasive opportunistic infections; increased mortality; malignancy; major adverse cardiovascular events; and thrombosis have occurred with JAK inhibitors. Avoid use during active serious infection, screen and monitor for TB and other infections, and assess cardiovascular, smoking, malignancy, and clot risk. The excess mortality, MACE, cancer, and thrombosis signal came from a postmarketing rheumatoid-arthritis safety study of another JAK inhibitor versus TNF blockers, but current labeling applies the class warning to baricitinib. Discontinue for thrombosis and interrupt for serious infection.

Mechanism

After cytokine binding, receptor-associated JAK1/JAK2 pairs phosphorylate receptor tails and STAT proteins; STAT dimers then enter the nucleus and alter inflammatory, antiviral, growth, and hematopoietic genes. Baricitinib reversibly inhibits the ATP-binding sites of JAK1 and JAK2, reducing STAT phosphorylation across IL-6, interferon, GM-CSF, and other pathways. The pattern is concentration-dependent and not perfectly isoform-exclusive. Most active drug leaves through the kidney, including transport by OAT3, so impaired renal clearance or a strong OAT3 inhibitor raises exposure. That pharmacology explains indication-specific renal cutoffs and dose reductions. It also explains why profound cytopenia or severe infection should pause therapy: the drug is suppressing pathways used for host defense and blood-cell production.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain TB/hepatitis assessment, CBC, liver tests, renal function, lipids, and vaccination review; verify renal eligibility because rheumatoid-arthritis, alopecia, and COVID-19 thresholds and durations differ.

  • Teach infection, zoster, clot, stroke/MI, and bowel-perforation warning symptoms and inspect scalp response objectively in alopecia rather than judging only early shedding.

Contraindications

  • Current U.S. labeling lists no formal contraindication; active serious infection and severe renal or hepatic dysfunction may make use inappropriate depending on indication.

  • Clarify age, smoking, cardiovascular/VTE/cancer risk, TB/hepatitis, recurrent zoster, diverticulitis, CBC abnormalities, renal/hepatic function, pregnancy, and vaccination.

Interactions

  • Avoid biologic DMARDs, other JAK inhibitors, and potent immunosuppressants; avoid live vaccines.

  • Strong OAT3 inhibitors such as probenecid increase baricitinib exposure and can require dose reduction; nephrotoxic illness or acute kidney injury can also change exposure quickly.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and hemoglobin, creatinine/eGFR, AST/ALT, fasting lipids, TB/hepatitis evaluation, and pregnancy context

  • Infection/zoster surveillance, cardiovascular and VTE assessment, skin/cancer review, abdominal symptoms, and indication-specific response

Nclex Traps

  • Baricitinib used for severe COVID-19 is an anti-inflammatory adjunct for selected hospitalized patients, not a drug that kills SARS-CoV-2.

  • Because renal transport clears active drug, probenecid and declining kidney function matter even though baricitinib is not primarily a CYP substrate.

Description

Baricitinib is an oral JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor that turns down cytokine signaling inside immune and hematopoietic cells. JAK1 participates in interferon, IL-6-family, and common-gamma-chain signaling; JAK2 transmits erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, GM-CSF, and several inflammatory signals. In rheumatoid arthritis this reduces synovial inflammatory transcription. In alopecia areata it interrupts interferon- and gamma-chain-cytokine signaling around the hair follicle, allowing immune-privilege recovery and regrowth in some patients. In selected severe COVID-19 it is used for a short course to reduce dysregulated inflammation, not as an antiviral. JAK2 involvement also makes CBC monitoring biologically important rather than a routine checkbox.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, nausea, herpes simplex or zoster, acne, folliculitis, headache, UTI, elevated lipids, creatine phosphokinase, and liver enzymes

  • Serious/opportunistic infection, TB, malignancy, MACE, venous/arterial thrombosis, GI perforation, anemia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, severe hypersensitivity, and liver injury
