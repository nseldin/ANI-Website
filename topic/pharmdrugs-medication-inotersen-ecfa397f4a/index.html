Class

Subcutaneous transthyretin-directed RNase-H1 antisense oligonucleotide

Used To Treat

Polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis in adults under the TEGSEDI REMS monitoring program.

Boxed Warning

Thrombocytopenia and glomerulonephritis: platelet loss can be sudden, unpredictable and fatal from hemorrhage; glomerulonephritis can require immunosuppression and progress to dialysis-dependent renal failure. Check platelets at least weekly when permitted by the label, renal function/urinalysis/UPCR every 2 weeks, continue specified monitoring for 8 weeks after stopping, and dispense only through the TEGSEDI REMS.

Mechanism

The DNA-like antisense strand hybridizes with a conserved TTR mRNA sequence. Nuclear RNase H1 recognizes the RNA-DNA duplex, cleaves the RNA, and permits the oligonucleotide to act again. Lower TTR reduces amyloid precursor and retinol-binding-protein transport, but unconjugated systemic exposure contributes to platelet, renal, hepatic and inflammatory toxicity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm REMS authorization and interpretable platelet count before every dose; assess bleeding, severe headache/neck stiffness, antiplatelet/anticoagulant drugs, creatinine/eGFR, hematuria, edema, urine output and UPCR.

  • Hold and urgently escalate thresholds exactly as labeled; investigate pseudothrombocytopenia when EDTA-related platelet clumping makes a result uninterpretable, but never give while the count is unknown.

Key Labs

  • Platelets weekly or more often by threshold, serum creatinine/eGFR, urinalysis and UPCR every 2 weeks, liver tests, inflammatory/immune assessment, vitamin A symptoms, and continued post-discontinuation monitoring for 8 weeks.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal count last week does not exclude abrupt severe thrombocytopenia this week.

  • Edema may be ATTR cardiomyopathy, nephrotic glomerulonephritis, or both; pair assessment with urine protein and renal trend.

Description

Inotersen is a weekly subcutaneous antisense oligonucleotide that binds both mutant and wild-type TTR mRNA and recruits RNase H1 to degrade the RNA, reducing hepatic transthyretin synthesis in adult hATTR polyneuropathy. Its identity is dominated by unpredictable immune thrombocytopenia and glomerulonephritis: unlike newer GalNAc eplontersen, it requires REMS-controlled platelet and renal surveillance.
