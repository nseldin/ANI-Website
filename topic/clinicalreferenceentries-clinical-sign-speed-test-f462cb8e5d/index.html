Definition

Speed test is a bedside resisted-shoulder maneuver. It is a clue to long-head biceps or superior labral pain. A positive finding means pain localizes to the bicipital groove.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Speed test means pain localizes to the bicipital groove.

Why It Matters

A positive Speed test response can signal long-head biceps or superior labral pain. Resisted shoulder flexion loads the long-head biceps tendon and anchor. Speed test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • long-head biceps or superior labral pain

Assessment Method

Flex the shoulder against resistance with elbow extended and palm up.

Positive Finding

Pain localizes to the bicipital groove.

Mechanism

Resisted shoulder flexion loads the long-head biceps tendon and anchor.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Speed test response raises concern for long-head biceps or superior labral pain because resisted shoulder flexion loads the long-head biceps tendon and anchor. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Speed test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Speed test to focus evaluation of long-head biceps or superior labral pain and the reviewed alternatives: subacromial pain, arthritis, pectoralis strain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex the shoulder against resistance with elbow extended and palm up. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Speed test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many anterior shoulder conditions are positive. Therefore, Speed test cannot confirm or exclude long-head biceps or superior labral pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • subacromial pain

  • arthritis

  • pectoralis strain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from subacromial pain, arthritis, pectoralis strain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Speed test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct with poor specificity. When performed as described, Speed test can adjust concern for long-head biceps or superior labral pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Speed test: Many anterior shoulder conditions are positive.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Speed test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex the shoulder against resistance with elbow extended and palm up. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not diagnose a labral tear from this maneuver alone.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Speed test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Pain localizes to the bicipital groove. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex the shoulder against resistance with elbow extended and palm up. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, long-head biceps or superior labral pain, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Speed test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not diagnose a labral tear from this maneuver alone. Base escalation for Speed test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
