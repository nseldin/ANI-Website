Class

Selective intestinal serotonin 5-HT3 receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Women with severe, chronic diarrhea-predominant IBS who have failed conventional therapy and whose GI anatomy/history does not create excess constipation or ischemic-colitis risk.

Boxed Warning

Serious GI adverse reactions: ischemic colitis and complications of constipation have caused hospitalization, surgery, transfusion, and death. Use only for indicated women with severe IBS-D; stop immediately for constipation, rectal bleeding, bloody diarrhea, or new/worsening abdominal pain, and never restart after ischemic colitis.

Mechanism

5-HT3 antagonism reduces ligand-gated cation-channel activation on enteric neurons, modulating sensory pain signals, secretion, and exaggerated motor responses. Colonic transit slows, which improves diarrhea but can compromise perfusion or produce obstruction when excessive.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen prior ischemic colitis, impaired intestinal circulation, obstruction/stricture/adhesions, severe constipation, toxic megacolon, diverticulitis, Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, and severe hepatic impairment.

  • Teach immediate discontinuation and urgent evaluation for constipation, rectal bleeding, bloody diarrhea, or new/worsening abdominal pain.

Key Labs

  • Clinical bowel pattern and abdominal/bleeding assessment; CBC, lactate, imaging, or colon evaluation when ischemia is suspected; liver function for eligibility.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not treat rectal bleeding or new pain as ordinary IBS while alosetron is on board; assume ischemic colitis until evaluated.

Description

Alosetron is a selective intestinal 5-HT3 receptor antagonist that blocks enteric serotonin signaling to slow colonic transit, reduce secretion, and damp visceral pain in severe IBS-D. The benefit is deliberately narrow because serious constipation complications and ischemic colitis can occur without warning and may require hospitalization, transfusion, surgery, or result in death.
