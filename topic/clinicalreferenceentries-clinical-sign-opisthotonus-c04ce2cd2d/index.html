Definition

Opisthotonus is an observed postural sign. It is a clue to severe extensor muscle spasm. A positive finding means the neck and back arch markedly with the body supported by head and heels.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Opisthotonus means the neck and back arch markedly with the body supported by head and heels.

Why It Matters

The observed Opisthotonus finding can signal severe extensor muscle spasm. Disinhibition or toxin-mediated overactivity of axial motor circuits causes sustained extensor contraction. Opisthotonus cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe extensor muscle spasm

Assessment Method

Observe the body posture during an episode while protecting airway and spine.

Positive Finding

The neck and back arch markedly with the body supported by head and heels.

Mechanism

Disinhibition or toxin-mediated overactivity of axial motor circuits causes sustained extensor contraction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Opisthotonus finding raises concern for severe extensor muscle spasm because disinhibition or toxin-mediated overactivity of axial motor circuits causes sustained extensor contraction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Opisthotonus does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Opisthotonus to focus evaluation of severe extensor muscle spasm and the reviewed alternatives: decerebrate posturing, dystonic crisis, seizure. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the body posture during an episode while protecting airway and spine. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Opisthotonus as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Seizure posturing and voluntary arching may resemble it. Therefore, Opisthotonus cannot confirm or exclude severe extensor muscle spasm by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • decerebrate posturing

  • dystonic crisis

  • seizure

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as decerebrate posturing, dystonic crisis, seizure.

False Negative Situations

Opisthotonus may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe extensor muscle spasm.
