Class

Rapid artemisinin-derived blood schizonticide and first-line IV therapy for severe malaria

Used To Treat

Initial treatment of severe malaria in adults and children. IV artesunate is given urgently, parasitemia and organ function are reassessed, and every patient then receives a complete oral antimalarial follow-on regimen once parasite density and oral tolerance permit.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Delayed post-artemisinin hemolytic anemia can occur about one to four weeks after treatment. Arrange weekly monitoring for four weeks with hemoglobin and hemolysis markers rather than ending follow-up when fever and parasitemia improve. Hypersensitivity and severe skin reactions can occur. Severe malaria itself can cause hemolysis, hypoglycemia, renal failure, acidosis, ARDS, shock, and cerebral disease, so trend the whole syndrome.

Mechanism

Esterases convert artesunate to dihydroartemisinin. Inside infected red cells, blood-stage Plasmodium concentrates and digests hemoglobin in a food vacuole. Ferrous iron and heme cleave the endoperoxide bridge, creating carbon-centered radicals and reactive intermediates that alkylate proteins and lipids, disrupt membranes, alter redox balance, and impair multiple essential parasite pathways. Ring-stage parasites are cleared particularly rapidly. The broad multi-target injury helps overcome high parasite burden but the active metabolite disappears quickly, so a longer-acting oral regimen must eliminate residual organisms. After splenic pitting removes dead parasites from erythrocytes, those once-infected cells have shortened survival; synchronized clearance later can produce delayed hemolysis even after smears become negative.

Nursing Essentials

  • Treat severe malaria immediately and involve critical care/infectious-disease expertise. Obtain thick and thin smears and parasite density, but do not wait for species confirmation to begin IV artesunate when severe criteria are met.

  • After the third dose, assess the four-hour parasite density and oral tolerance. Transition to a complete oral regimen within the recommended window and schedule weekly hemolysis surveillance for four weeks.

Contraindications

  • Known serious hypersensitivity, although life-threatening severe malaria requires immediate expert alternative planning rather than delay.

  • Do not stop after the initial IV series; verify a full oral regimen, species/resistance considerations, and radical cure when P. vivax or P. ovale is identified.

Interactions

  • Follow-on regimen interactions matter more than short artesunate exposure; avoid stacking QT or hepatotoxic risk without review.

  • Antimalarial previously used for prophylaxis should generally not be reused for treatment when alternatives exist because resistance or breakthrough exposure is possible.

Key Labs

  • Serial parasite density, CBC, glucose, creatinine, liver tests, lactate/acid-base status, coagulation and organ-specific monitoring

  • Weekly hemoglobin, reticulocyte count, bilirubin, LDH, and haptoglobin for four weeks after treatment; neurologic, respiratory, renal and hemodynamic status

Nclex Traps

  • Three IV artesunate doses are initial stabilization, not a complete malaria regimen.

  • A negative smear at discharge does not end surveillance because delayed hemolysis can appear weeks later.

Description

Artesunate is a water-soluble artemisinin prodrug rapidly converted to dihydroartemisinin, a potent blood-stage antimalarial. Parasite digestion of hemoglobin releases iron-containing heme that activates the drug's endoperoxide bridge, generating short-lived radicals that damage parasite proteins, membranes, and metabolic systems. This produces rapid parasite clearance and is why IV artesunate is preferred for severe malaria. The drug's short half-life is also why three initial IV doses are not a complete cure: surviving parasites can recrudesce unless a full oral partner regimen follows. A distinctive delayed risk is post-artemisinin hemolytic anemia, often appearing one to three weeks later as once-infected erythrocytes cleared of parasites are removed from circulation.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, transient bradycardia, and liver-test changes

  • Delayed hemolytic anemia, hypersensitivity, reticulocytopenia, neutropenia, QT effects, and severe malaria-related organ complications
