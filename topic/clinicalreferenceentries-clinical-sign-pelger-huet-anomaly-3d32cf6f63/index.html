Definition

Pelger-Huët anomaly is a microscopic leukocyte sign. It is a clue to inherited or acquired hyposegmentation of neutrophils. A positive finding means nuclei are bilobed or unsegmented with mature clumped chromatin.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pelger-Huët anomaly means nuclei are bilobed or unsegmented with mature clumped chromatin.

Why It Matters

The Pelger-Huët anomaly laboratory finding can signal inherited or acquired hyposegmentation of neutrophils. Lamin-B receptor alteration or dysplastic maturation prevents normal nuclear segmentation. Pelger-Huët anomaly cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • inherited or acquired hyposegmentation of neutrophils

Assessment Method

Review neutrophil nuclear morphology.

Positive Finding

Nuclei are bilobed or unsegmented with mature clumped chromatin.

Mechanism

Lamin-B receptor alteration or dysplastic maturation prevents normal nuclear segmentation.

Clinical Interpretation

The Pelger-Huët anomaly laboratory finding raises concern for inherited or acquired hyposegmentation of neutrophils because lamin-B receptor alteration or dysplastic maturation prevents normal nuclear segmentation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pelger-Huët anomaly does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Inherited forms are benign; acquired forms need clinical context. Therefore, Pelger-Huët anomaly cannot confirm or exclude inherited or acquired hyposegmentation of neutrophils by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with band neutrophils, left shift, smear artifact.

False Negative Situations

Pelger-Huët anomaly may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
