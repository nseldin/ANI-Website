Used To Treat

Rapid external skin decontamination after contact with specified chemical-warfare agents or T-2 toxin. It removes and chemically neutralizes agent on skin; it does not reverse agent already absorbed or replace airway support, atropine, pralidoxime, anticonvulsants, or emergency decontamination operations.

Description

RSDL is an FDA-cleared single-use skin decontamination device whose reactive lotion traps and chemically neutralizes specified chemical-warfare agents on skin before further absorption. It is intended for trained emergency or military use and works best when applied as soon as possible because continued skin contact allows additional absorption and local injury. The treated skin is wiped rather than scrubbed aggressively, which limits spread and mechanical injury. Eyes and wounds require their own irrigation and incident protocol. A person with secretions, bronchospasm, weakness, seizures, or collapse needs systemic nerve-agent rescue even if the skin was decontaminated perfectly.

Mechanism

The lotion contains Dekon 139 and a solvent system that lifts lipophilic chemical agent from the skin and promotes chemical breakdown into less toxic products. Physical removal lowers the amount available to cross the skin, while chemical neutralization lowers the hazard remaining on the surface. Speed matters because neutralizing agent after it has entered tissue cannot reverse acetylcholinesterase inhibition or established organ injury. This explains why RSDL complements, rather than replaces, personal protective equipment, clothing removal, gross decontamination, and systemic antidotes.

Administration Timing

  • Move to a safe decontamination area, protect responders, remove contaminated clothing according to incident procedure, and apply RSDL to exposed intact skin as soon as possible.

  • Use the packaged sponge and current device instructions; wipe the affected surface without spreading contamination to clean skin, eyes, mouth, or wounds.

  • Continue emergency assessment and toxin-specific treatment during decontamination because systemic toxicity can progress after surface agent is removed.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use appropriate PPE and control runoff, clothing, and waste because secondary contamination can injure staff and bystanders.

  • Record agent if known, exposure time, body area, decontamination steps, neurologic and respiratory findings, and antidotes because response depends on absorbed dose and delay.

  • Keep the product out of eyes and wounds and follow site-specific irrigation guidance because a skin-cleared device is not automatically safe for other tissues.

Key Labs

  • No laboratory test should delay decontamination; serial respiratory, neurologic, cholinergic, and skin findings guide emergency treatment.

  • Cholinesterase activity, blood gas, electrolytes, ECG, glucose, and organ studies may support nerve-agent care but normal early tests do not prove absence of exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Local irritation, redness, burning, or discomfort can occur, especially on damaged skin.

  • Operational harms include delayed systemic treatment, secondary contamination, spread into eyes or wounds, and false reassurance after incomplete decontamination.

Contraindications

  • Do not apply to eyes or use as a substitute for copious eye irrigation; follow the current device and incident instructions for wounds or damaged skin.

  • Do not delay rescue breathing, airway control, atropine, pralidoxime, benzodiazepines, or EMS activation while searching for RSDL.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Miosis with secretions, wheeze, bradycardia, fasciculations, weakness, confusion, seizures, apnea, or collapse requires immediate nerve-agent emergency treatment.

  • Persistent pain, blistering, tissue injury, eye symptoms, or uncertain agent requires poison-center, medical-toxicology, burn, and incident-command consultation.

Interactions

  • RSDL works on the external contamination compartment; atropine, pralidoxime, and benzodiazepines work on systemic muscarinic, nicotinic, and seizure consequences.

  • Do not mix improvised chemicals or household cleaners with RSDL because uncontrolled reactions can injure skin or create toxic vapor.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    A smaller body mass and thinner skin can increase absorbed dose, while fear and movement raise eye-spread risk; decontaminate with close airway and temperature support.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Rapid maternal decontamination and resuscitation protect both patients; avoid delaying systemic antidotes because maternal hypoxemia and shock also threaten fetal oxygen delivery.

  • Population

    Responders and caregivers

    Note

    Secondary exposure is a major risk, so PPE, scene control, and contaminated-waste handling are part of patient treatment.

Evidence Limitations

  • Clearance and performance are agent- and use-condition specific; RSDL is not a universal antidote for every industrial corrosive or toxic chemical.

  • Operational access varies and public instructions may differ during an incident, so users must follow emergency authorities and the current device labeling.

Nclex Traps

  • RSDL decontaminates skin; it does not reverse systemic cholinergic poisoning.

  • Apply early because absorbed agent cannot be wiped away later.

  • Protect responders and avoid spreading contamination to eyes, wounds, or clean skin because transfer increases ocular, wound, and secondary dermal exposure.

Regulatory Status

FDA-cleared medical device for removal or neutralization of specified chemical-warfare agents and T-2 toxin from skin; scope should not be generalized to every chemical exposure.

Boxed Warning

EMERGENCY DECONTAMINATION WARNING: protect responders because secondary contamination can disable the care team, and do not delay systemic antidotes or airway care because absorbed agent can continue causing life-threatening toxicity. RSDL is for specified external skin contamination and must not be placed in the eyes because ocular tissue can be injured. Successful surface decontamination also must not be treated as proof that no agent was absorbed because systemic toxicity can continue after skin agent is removed.
