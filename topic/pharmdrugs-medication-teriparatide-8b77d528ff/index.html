Contraindications

  • Avoid increased osteosarcoma-risk states and hypercalcemic disorders

  • Do not use for bone metastases or prior skeletal radiation

  • Clarify kidney stones, severe renal disease and digoxin before use

Nursing Essentials

  • Administer initial doses where the patient can sit or lie down; store/use the pen exactly as labeled and never share it.

  • Ensure adequate but not excessive calcium/vitamin D and arrange antiresorptive consolidation when the course ends.

Interactions

  • Hypercalcemia can increase digoxin toxicity.

  • Other calcium-raising drugs and high-dose supplements compound hypercalcemia.

  • Antiresorptive sequencing should be planned to preserve gains.

Key Labs

  • Calcium, 25-OH vitamin D and renal function

  • Bone density and fracture history

  • Urinary calcium/stone assessment when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Intermittent PTH builds bone; chronic PTH excess resorbs bone.

  • Teriparatide no longer has a boxed warning, but osteosarcoma-risk exclusions still matter.

  • More than 2 years is not automatically forbidden; it is reserved for persistent or recurrent high fracture risk.

Used To Treat

Osteoporosis in postmenopausal women and men at high fracture risk, and glucocorticoid-associated osteoporosis at high risk, according to current product labeling.

Mechanism

PTH1R activates Gs-cAMP-PKA and Gq signaling in osteoblast-lineage cells. Intermittent exposure increases osteoblast differentiation, reduces osteoblast apoptosis and increases remodeling with a positive formation balance. Sustained exposure increases RANKL relative to osteoprotegerin and favors resorption, explaining the dosing paradox. Formation gains fade after stopping unless osteoclast activity is subsequently restrained.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: The former U.S. osteosarcoma boxed warning was removed, but avoid use in patients with increased baseline osteosarcoma risk: open epiphyses, Paget disease/other high-turnover bone disease, bone metastases or skeletal malignancy, hereditary predisposition, or prior skeletal radiation. Lifetime use beyond 2 years is reserved for patients who remain or return to high fracture risk.

Description

Teriparatide is a recombinant parathyroid-hormone 1-34 analog that activates PTH1 receptors in brief daily pulses to build bone. Intermittent exposure favors osteoblast recruitment, survival and formation more than resorption. That is the opposite of continuously elevated PTH, which drives RANKL and osteoclast-mediated loss. Therapy is usually time-limited and should be followed by an antiresorptive so newly built bone is not remodeled away.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, dizziness, leg cramps and arthralgia

  • Transient orthostatic hypotension after initial doses

  • Transient hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria

  • Urolithiasis concern and rare calciphylaxis/cutaneous calcification reports
