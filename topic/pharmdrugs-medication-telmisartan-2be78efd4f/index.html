Class

Very long-acting, predominantly biliary-eliminated AT1-receptor-blocking ARB

Used To Treat

Hypertension and reduction of myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular death in selected patients age 55 or older at high cardiovascular risk who are unable to take ACE inhibitors according to labeling.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Telmisartan's long half-life and tissue binding do not change the instruction: stop promptly when pregnancy is detected.

Mechanism

Telmisartan selectively blocks AT1 receptors, preventing angiotensin-II-mediated vascular calcium signaling, aldosterone release, sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, and remodeling. It does not inhibit ACE or directly preserve bradykinin, so dry cough is much less characteristic. Efferent dilation reduces intraglomerular pressure and can raise creatinine in poor renal inflow; reduced aldosterone raises potassium. Telmisartan reaches peak concentrations within roughly an hour, shows nonlinear exposure, is more than 99.5% protein bound, and has a terminal half-life near 24 hours. More than 97% of radiolabeled dose is recovered through fecal-biliary elimination, with very little urine recovery. This explains both sustained effect and the importance of hepatic disease, while also explaining why hemodialysis is ineffective for removal.

Nursing Essentials

  • Connect once-daily coverage to the approximately 24-hour half-life and assess trough pressure, not only a post-dose reading.

  • For cardiovascular-risk use, confirm the patient fits the labeled high-risk ACE-intolerant population and continues antiplatelet, lipid, diabetes, smoking, and lifestyle therapy as indicated.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes.

  • Clarify severe biliary obstruction or hepatic impairment, dangerous potassium elevation, evolving AKI, or major volume depletion.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Interactions

  • Digoxin exposure can rise and may require monitoring; potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class.

  • Because CYP metabolism is not central, do not invent generic CYP interactions; focus on P-gp substrates, pressure, renal physiology, and hepatic handling.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure throughout the dosing interval, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, liver context, and digoxin concentration or toxicity signs when combined.

  • No routine telmisartan level is used, and hemodialysis does not remove it.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Telmisartan's long half-life explains sustained once-daily effect; it does not mean the drug is safer during hypotension.

  • Cardiovascular-risk labeling applies to selected high-risk patients unable to take ACE inhibitors, not every patient with hypertension.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Telmisartan is an active, very long-acting angiotensin-II AT1-receptor blocker (ARB) that prevents vasoconstrictor and aldosterone signaling across the dosing interval. Its approximately 24-hour terminal half-life and predominantly biliary-fecal elimination support once-daily coverage; it is not appreciably metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes and is not removed by hemodialysis. Besides hypertension, its current label includes cardiovascular-risk reduction in selected adults age 55 or older who are unable to take ACE inhibitors, while acknowledging that the evidence does not guarantee preservation of every ACE-inhibitor benefit. That nuance matters: telmisartan is a targeted alternative, not a universal superior replacement. Its long duration supports once-daily coverage but also means hypotension or hyperkalemia will not disappear quickly after a dose. Hepatic impairment can markedly increase bioavailability because biliary handling is central, whereas dialysis cannot meaningfully clear the highly protein-bound drug.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness, hypotension, back pain, diarrhea, potassium or creatinine increase, and fatigue can occur.

  • Hepatic impairment can increase exposure, and prolonged hypotension cannot be rapidly reversed by dialysis.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
