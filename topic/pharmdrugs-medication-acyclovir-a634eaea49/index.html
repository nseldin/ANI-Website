Used To Treat

HSV-1 and HSV-2 mucocutaneous or genital disease, herpes encephalitis, neonatal HSV, varicella, and herpes zoster according to route and severity. It suppresses active replication but does not eliminate latent virus from sensory ganglia.

Mechanism

HSV and VZV thymidine kinase phosphorylates acyclovir to acyclovir monophosphate. Host guanylate kinase and other cellular kinases create acyclovir triphosphate, which has much greater affinity for viral DNA polymerase than for host polymerases. It competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate, is incorporated into viral DNA, and lacks the normal 3-prime hydroxyl group needed to form the next phosphodiester bond. Polymerase stalls after incorporation, stopping genome replication. Viral thymidine-kinase loss or alteration reduces activation and can produce resistance; viral DNA-polymerase mutation can reduce binding. Acyclovir is filtered and secreted by the kidney and is poorly soluble in urine, so high tubular concentration during rapid IV infusion or dehydration can form crystals, obstruct flow, and reduce clearance further.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Renal failure and neurotoxicity can occur, especially with rapid IV infusion, inadequate hydration, pre-existing kidney dysfunction, older age, high exposure, or concurrent nephrotoxins. Infuse IV doses over the labeled interval, maintain hydration, and adjust for renal function. Confusion, hallucinations, tremor, myoclonus, agitation, dysarthria, or seizures can signal drug accumulation rather than worsening encephalitis alone.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity to acyclovir or valacyclovir.

  • Use an indication-, route-, weight-, and renal-function-specific dose; dehydration and reduced urine flow require correction rather than simply giving the standard dose.

Nursing Essentials

  • For IV therapy, verify dilution, infusion time, hydration, urine output, creatinine trend, and neurologic status before each dose. A sudden creatinine rise can increase acyclovir exposure and create a self-amplifying toxicity cycle.

  • Start promptly in suspected herpes encephalitis or neonatal HSV because ongoing viral replication destroys tissue; do not wait for every confirmatory result when clinical urgency warrants empiric therapy.

Interactions

  • Other nephrotoxins such as calcineurin inhibitors, amphotericin, or high-risk NSAID exposure can compound kidney injury.

  • Probenecid and cimetidine can reduce renal clearance; mycophenolate exposure interactions and additive neurotoxicity deserve review in transplant patients.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR, urine output, hydration and urinalysis when renal injury is suspected; CBC and liver tests during prolonged or severe-disease therapy

  • Neurologic examination, lesion progression, ocular involvement, immune status, treatment timing, adherence, and culture/PCR or resistance evaluation when response is poor

Nclex Traps

  • Acyclovir does not eradicate latent HSV or VZV; recurrence remains possible after successful treatment.

  • New confusion in a renally impaired patient receiving acyclovir can be drug neurotoxicity, viral CNS disease, or both; check exposure and kidney function urgently.

Description

Acyclovir is a guanosine nucleoside analogue that becomes concentrated pharmacologically inside HSV- or VZV-infected cells because viral thymidine kinase performs its first activation step. The active triphosphate competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates the growing viral DNA chain. Oral therapy treats or suppresses selected mucocutaneous disease; intravenous therapy provides reliable exposure for encephalitis, neonatal herpes, disseminated infection, severe varicella-zoster disease, or inability to absorb oral medication. Its selectivity explains why host DNA synthesis is less affected, while renal elimination explains why dehydration or unadjusted dosing can cause crystal nephropathy and neurotoxicity.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, headache, malaise, rash, and IV phlebitis

  • Crystal-associated acute kidney injury, neurotoxicity, severe cutaneous or hypersensitivity reaction, cytopenia, and liver-test abnormalities
