Definition

Mycobacterium tuberculosis is the acid-fast bacterial species responsible for most human tuberculosis. Inhaled bacilli usually enter the lungs, where infection may be contained without symptoms or may progress to active pulmonary or extrapulmonary disease. Infection and disease are therefore not interchangeable: latent tuberculosis infection means viable organisms are contained without clinical illness or contagiousness, while active tuberculosis disease reflects tissue injury and bacterial replication that requires prompt treatment and public-health action.

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    M. tuberculosis belongs to the M. tuberculosis complex, a group of closely related mycobacteria. The species name identifies the organism; TB names the disease, and an acid-fast bacillus on a smear is only a laboratory category because nontuberculous mycobacteria can also be acid fast. This distinction explains why a positive smear can trigger urgent precautions but still requires nucleic-acid testing, culture, and species identification.

    Id

    identity-classification

    Title

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    The organism is a slender, nonmotile, non-spore-forming bacillus with a lipid-rich cell envelope containing mycolic acids. That waxy envelope resists ordinary Gram-stain interpretation and retains acid-fast dyes after acid-alcohol decolorization. It also slows nutrient entry and contributes to slow growth, so culture and susceptibility results take longer than for many common bacteria. M. tuberculosis is aerobic and can survive within macrophages, allowing it to persist despite an intact extracellular immune response.

    Id

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Humans with infectious pulmonary or laryngeal TB are the principal reservoir for person-to-person spread. Coughing, speaking, or singing releases small airborne particles that can remain suspended and be inhaled by others; touching surfaces is not the usual route. Risk rises with prolonged indoor exposure, poor ventilation, and high organism burden. Once inhaled, infection may remain clinically silent for years, so there is no useful single incubation interval from exposure to active disease.

    Id

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Bacilli reaching alveoli are engulfed by macrophages but can inhibit intracellular killing. Cell-mediated immunity recruits macrophages and lymphocytes into granulomas that wall off infection. Containment limits replication but does not guarantee eradication, which is why immune suppression can permit later reactivation. When control fails, caseating inflammation and cavitation damage lung tissue, increase coughing and aerosol generation, and can seed lymph nodes, meninges, bone, kidneys, or other organs through blood or lymph.

    Id

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Pulmonary TB may cause a persistent cough, hemoptysis, fever, night sweats, fatigue, and weight loss, yet early disease can be subtle. Extrapulmonary disease produces site-specific findings such as lymphadenopathy, back pain, sterile pyuria, or neurologic symptoms. Suspected infectious TB requires immediate airborne controls and public-health coordination. Respiratory compromise, major hemoptysis, altered mental status, or suspected TB meningitis is an emergency rather than a routine outpatient teaching problem.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    A TB blood test or tuberculin skin test demonstrates immune sensitization but cannot by itself distinguish latent infection from active disease. Evaluation for disease combines symptoms, imaging, and bacteriologic specimens. Sputum acid-fast smears provide a rapid burden clue, a tuberculosis nucleic-acid amplification test can identify M. tuberculosis complex more quickly, culture confirms viable organisms, and susceptibility testing guides the multidrug regimen. A negative smear does not safely exclude active pulmonary TB when clinical suspicion remains.

    Id

    diagnostic-identification

    Title

    Diagnostic identification

  • Content

    Standard Precautions apply to all care, and suspected or confirmed infectious pulmonary or laryngeal TB requires Airborne Precautions, appropriate patient placement, source control during transport, and fit-tested respiratory protection according to current policy. Completing an effective multidrug regimen protects the patient and interrupts transmission. Treating latent infection can prevent progression. A single drug must not be used for active TB because bacterial subpopulations can select resistance and make disease harder to cure.
