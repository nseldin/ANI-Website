Definition

Hawkins-Kennedy sign is a bedside shoulder-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to subacromial pain. A positive finding means anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hawkins-Kennedy sign means anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Hawkins-Kennedy sign response can signal subacromial pain. Internal rotation under the acromion compresses rotator-cuff and bursal tissues. Hawkins-Kennedy sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subacromial pain

Assessment Method

Flex shoulder and elbow to 90 degrees, then gently internally rotate.

Positive Finding

Anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

Internal rotation under the acromion compresses rotator-cuff and bursal tissues.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Hawkins-Kennedy sign response raises concern for subacromial pain because internal rotation under the acromion compresses rotator-cuff and bursal tissues. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hawkins-Kennedy sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hawkins-Kennedy sign to focus evaluation of subacromial pain and the reviewed alternatives: arthritis, posterior capsule tightness, biceps tendinopathy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex shoulder and elbow to 90 degrees, then gently internally rotate. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hawkins-Kennedy sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain can arise from multiple intra-articular structures. Therefore, Hawkins-Kennedy sign cannot confirm or exclude subacromial pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • arthritis

  • posterior capsule tightness

  • biceps tendinopathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from arthritis, posterior capsule tightness, biceps tendinopathy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Hawkins-Kennedy sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current sensitive but nonspecific adjunct. When performed as described, Hawkins-Kennedy sign can adjust concern for subacromial pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Hawkins-Kennedy sign: Pain can arise from multiple intra-articular structures.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Hawkins-Kennedy sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex shoulder and elbow to 90 degrees, then gently internally rotate. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use controlled rotation and do not equate pain with a tendon tear.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Hawkins-Kennedy sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex shoulder and elbow to 90 degrees, then gently internally rotate. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, subacromial pain, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Hawkins-Kennedy sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
