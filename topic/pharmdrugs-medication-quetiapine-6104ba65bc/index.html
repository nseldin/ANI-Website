Used To Treat

Schizophrenia; acute bipolar mania; bipolar depression; bipolar maintenance adjunct; extended-release adjunctive treatment of major depression. Immediate- and extended-release products have different dosing and food instructions.

Mechanism

Parent quetiapine antagonizes D2 and 5-HT2A receptors; lower and transient striatal D2 occupancy reduces but does not eliminate EPS, tardive dyskinesia and NMS. H1 blockade causes immediate sedation and appetite increase, while alpha-1 blockade prevents standing vasoconstriction and causes orthostasis/tachycardia. CYP3A4 forms norquetiapine, which inhibits the norepinephrine transporter and partially stimulates 5-HT1A receptors, strengthening monoamine and anxiolytic pathways relevant to bipolar and major depression. This separation explains why a low bedtime dose that sedates is not automatically an evidence-based antidepressant dose.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Quetiapine is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. It also carries antidepressant suicidality warning context. Severe metabolic effects, orthostasis, somnolence, QT risk and thyroid changes require active surveillance.

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity

  • Avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitor/inducer combinations unless labeling-directed adjustment is made

  • Assess hypotension, fall, metabolic, QT, thyroid, cataract and suicide risk

Nursing Essentials

  • Check orthostatic vitals during initiation/titration and protect against falls; sedation can be profound before tolerance develops.

  • Separate sleep response from mood response and reassess target symptoms at an evidence-based dose and duration.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors markedly raise and inducers markedly lower exposure.

  • Alcohol, opioids, antihistamines and sedatives add CNS depression.

  • QT-prolonging drugs and electrolyte loss increase dysrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Weight/BMI, glucose/A1c, lipids and blood pressure

  • TSH/free T4 when clinically indicated

  • AIMS/EPS and ECG/electrolytes based on risk

Nclex Traps

  • Sedation at a low dose does not prove antipsychotic or antidepressant efficacy.

  • Low EPS risk is not no EPS/NMS/tardive risk.

  • IR and XR administration, food effects and titration are not interchangeable.

Description

Quetiapine is a second-generation antipsychotic with relatively transient D2 binding, strong 5-HT2A antagonism and prominent H1/alpha-1 blockade. This pattern produces lower average EPS and prolactin elevation than risperidone but marked somnolence and orthostatic hypotension, plus clinically important weight, glucose and lipid effects. Its active metabolite norquetiapine inhibits NET and partially agonizes 5-HT1A, helping explain antidepressant efficacy that is not simply a consequence of sedation.

Adverse Effects

  • Somnolence, dizziness, orthostatic hypotension and falls

  • Weight gain, hyperglycemia and dyslipidemia

  • Dry mouth, constipation and tachycardia

  • QT prolongation, hypothyroid changes, EPS/tardive dyskinesia and NMS
