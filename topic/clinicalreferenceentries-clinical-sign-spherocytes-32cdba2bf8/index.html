Definition

Spherocytes is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to reduced red-cell membrane surface area. A positive finding means small dense round erythrocytes without central pallor are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Spherocytes means small dense round erythrocytes without central pallor are present.

Why It Matters

The Spherocytes laboratory finding can signal reduced red-cell membrane surface area. Membrane loss forces cells toward a sphere, reducing deformability and increasing splenic clearance. Spherocytes cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • reduced red-cell membrane surface area

Assessment Method

Review peripheral smear for dense round cells lacking central pallor.

Positive Finding

Small dense round erythrocytes without central pallor are present.

Membrane loss forces cells toward a sphere, reducing deformability and increasing splenic clearance.

Evidence Limitations

Smear appearance alone cannot distinguish inherited from immune causes. Therefore, Spherocytes cannot confirm or exclude reduced red-cell membrane surface area by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Negative Situations

Spherocytes may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
