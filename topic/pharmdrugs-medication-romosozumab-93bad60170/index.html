Class

Sclerostin-neutralizing monoclonal antibody; dual bone-formation and antiresorptive therapy

Used To Treat

Osteoporosis in postmenopausal women at high fracture risk, including prior osteoporotic fracture, multiple risk factors, or failure/intolerance of other therapy; limited to 12 monthly doses.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Romosozumab may increase myocardial infarction, stroke and cardiovascular-death risk. Do not initiate in a patient with MI or stroke during the preceding year; weigh other cardiovascular risk factors, and discontinue if MI or stroke occurs during therapy.

Mechanism

Sclerostin binds LRP5/6 coreceptors and prevents canonical Wnt stabilization of beta-catenin. Antibody neutralization allows Wnt receptor signaling, osteoblast differentiation and matrix production while altering osteoclast-coupling signals. Formation markers peak early and then fall toward baseline despite continued dosing, providing the mechanistic reason for the 12-dose limit rather than an arbitrary calendar rule.

Nursing Essentials

  • A full 210 mg monthly dose is two consecutive 105 mg subcutaneous injections administered by a healthcare professional.

  • Track all 12 doses, cardiovascular symptoms, dental status and the planned antiresorptive that follows.

Contraindications

  • Hypocalcemia and serious hypersensitivity

  • Do not initiate within 1 year of MI or stroke

  • Correct calcium/vitamin-D deficiency and evaluate severe renal disease/dialysis

Interactions

  • Other calcium-lowering drugs increase hypocalcemia risk.

  • Other potent antiresorptives can add jaw/femur toxicity if overlapped without a plan.

  • No routine CYP interaction is expected for a monoclonal antibody.

Key Labs

  • Calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and 25-OH vitamin D

  • Cardiovascular history/risk assessment

  • Dental exam, thigh/groin symptoms and bone density/fracture response

Nclex Traps

  • The full dose requires two syringes.

  • The 12-month limit exists because anabolic effect wanes; it is not proof osteoporosis is cured.

  • Follow-on antiresorptive therapy preserves gains.

Description

Romosozumab is a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes sclerostin, an osteocyte protein that normally restrains Wnt signaling. Removing that brake rapidly increases osteoblast-mediated formation and transiently decreases resorption. The anabolic effect wanes after 12 monthly doses, so therapy stops at one year and is followed by an antiresorptive when osteoporosis treatment remains necessary.

Adverse Effects

  • Arthralgia, headache and injection-site reactions

  • Hypocalcemia, especially in severe renal impairment or dialysis

  • Osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fracture

  • Myocardial infarction, stroke and cardiovascular death concern
