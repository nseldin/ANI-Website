Used To Treat

Parkinson disease, restless legs syndrome, hyperprolactinemia, and selected endocrine/movement-disorder uses.

Mechanism

Bromocriptine works by stimulating dopamine receptors. In Parkinson disease this supports motor signaling; in hyperprolactinemia selected agents suppress prolactin. Orthostasis, hallucinations, sleep attacks, impulse-control behaviors, and nausea are high-yield risks.

Boxed Warning

Warnings vary; sudden sleep attacks, impulse-control behaviors, hypotension, hallucinations, and dyskinesias are key risks.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity

  • psychosis or severe cardiovascular instability may require clarification

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor orthostasis, hallucinations, dyskinesia, sleep attacks, impulse-control behaviors.

  • Do not stop many Parkinson meds abruptly.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Antipsychotics can oppose effect.

  • Antihypertensives increase orthostasis.

  • CNS depressants increase sedation.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • blood pressure

  • mental status

  • fall risk

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • Sudden sleep episodes while driving/standing are a real safety warning.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.
