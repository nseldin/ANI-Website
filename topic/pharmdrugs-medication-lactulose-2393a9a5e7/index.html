Used To Treat

Constipation and prevention/treatment of portal-systemic hepatic encephalopathy; in HE, dosing is titrated to mental status and a prescribed soft-stool target rather than uncontrolled diarrhea.

Mechanism

Lactic and acetic acids increase luminal osmoles and draw water into stool. Lower colonic pH shifts NH3 plus H+ toward NH4+, trapping nitrogen in the lumen; faster transit and altered bacterial metabolism further reduce ammonia generation and absorption. The clinically useful endpoint is improved cognition with controlled stool output, not maximal catharsis or normalization of a single ammonia result.

Contraindications

  • Requirement for a low-galactose diet; product hypersensitivity

  • Clarify bowel obstruction, severe acute abdominal pain, inability to protect the airway and profound dehydration

  • Account for product carbohydrate content in diabetes or galactosemia-related care

Nursing Essentials

  • For HE, document baseline orientation, behavior, asterixis, airway/gag reflex and bowel pattern; titrate to the ordered goal, often 2 to 3 soft stools daily.

  • Search for GI bleeding, infection, constipation, sedatives, renal failure, excess diuresis and electrolyte loss as precipitants.

Interactions

  • Other laxatives can confound the stool target and intensify dehydration.

  • Antacids that raise colonic pH may theoretically reduce acidification; follow product guidance.

  • Diuretics or other potassium-lowering therapies increase consequences of lactulose-induced diarrhea.

Key Labs

  • Mental status, asterixis and stool count/consistency

  • Sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, BUN/creatinine and glucose in vulnerable or aggressively treated patients

  • Ammonia only as contextual data; trend clinical response and precipitating illness

Nclex Traps

  • The HE goal is controlled soft stool and clearer mentation, not continuous watery diarrhea.

  • A high ammonia value does not by itself grade consciousness or airway safety.

  • Lactulose works in the colon; loose stool from another laxative is not mechanistically equivalent.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Excess dosing causes diarrhea, dehydration, hypokalemia and hypernatremia, which can worsen encephalopathy and precipitate dysrhythmia or kidney injury. Protect the airway in obtunded patients and use the prescribed rectal protocol when oral administration is unsafe. Other laxatives can create loose stool without proving adequate ammonia trapping.

Description

Lactulose is a nonabsorbed synthetic galactose-fructose disaccharide and osmotic laxative that reaches the colon, where bacterial fermentation produces acids that draw water into stool and trap diffusible ammonia as ammonium. In hepatic encephalopathy it is commonly titrated to 2 to 3 soft stools daily while mental status and hydration are followed.

Adverse Effects

  • Flatulence, belching, cramping, abdominal distention, nausea and vomiting

  • Diarrhea with dehydration, hypokalemia, hypernatremia and metabolic disturbance

  • Aspiration risk or skin injury during aggressive therapy in an impaired patient
