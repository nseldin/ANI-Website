Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder. Lower-dose use for insomnia is common but off-label; a sedating bedtime dose does not necessarily supply the antidepressant exposure used for major depression.

Mechanism

Trazodone and active m-chlorophenylpiperazine modify serotonin signaling through 5-HT2 antagonism and SERT inhibition, with repeated exposure producing antidepressant circuit adaptation. H1 antagonism reduces wake-promoting histamine signaling, while alpha-1 antagonism prevents compensatory vasoconstriction on standing and contributes to erectile vascular effects. These mechanisms explain why sleepiness and orthostasis can appear after the first dose even though antidepressant benefit takes weeks. Hepatic CYP3A4 metabolism makes strong inhibitors clinically important; higher exposure plus hERG/repolarization effects increases QT and arrhythmia risk.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Priapism is time-sensitive: a painful or persistent erection, especially approaching 4 hours, requires emergency evaluation to prevent ischemia and permanent erectile injury.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI use or serotonergic linezolid/methylene blue outside emergency guidance

  • Assess recent myocardial infarction, bradycardia, congenital QT, low potassium/magnesium and multiple QT drugs

  • Use caution with priapism predisposition, severe hypotension, hepatic impairment and heavy CNS-depressant burden

Nursing Essentials

  • Check orthostatic vitals and nighttime fall risk before treating sedation as automatically therapeutic.

  • Teach that priapism is an emergency rather than an embarrassing effect to wait out; delay can produce cavernosal necrosis and permanent dysfunction.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors raise trazodone and QT/sedation risk; inducers lower exposure.

  • Other serotonergic drugs add serotonin syndrome; NSAIDs/anticoagulants add bleeding.

  • Alcohol, opioids, sedatives, antihypertensives and QT-prolonging drugs add impairment, hypotension or dysrhythmia.

Key Labs

  • Orthostatic blood pressure, alertness and falls

  • ECG plus potassium/magnesium when cardiac risk is meaningful

  • Sodium, bleeding, mood/mania/suicide assessment and priapism teaching

Nclex Traps

  • A low bedtime dose used for sleep is not automatically an adequate antidepressant regimen.

  • Sedation begins quickly because of H1/alpha-1 blockade; antidepressant adaptation does not.

  • Priapism requires emergency care even if the medication was prescribed only for sleep.

Description

Trazodone is a serotonergic antidepressant that blocks 5-HT2A/2C receptors and more weakly inhibits SERT, while alpha-1 and H1 antagonism produces orthostasis and sedation. This receptor mix is why it is frequently used off-label for sleep, yet its FDA-labeled purpose is depression. The signature emergency is prolonged painful erection (priapism), which can cause ischemic tissue injury; the signature bedside hazards are falls, hypotension, oversedation and QT-related dysrhythmia in vulnerable patients.

Adverse Effects

  • Somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, dry mouth and headache

  • Orthostatic hypotension, syncope and falls

  • Priapism and sexual/genitourinary effects

  • QT prolongation/torsades, serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, bleeding and mania
