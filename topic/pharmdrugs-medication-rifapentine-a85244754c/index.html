Boxed Warning

High-alert warning focus: rifapentine can cause hepatotoxicity, serious hypersensitivity reactions, cytopenias, C. difficile-associated diarrhea, and major CYP/P-gp interaction problems. TB regimen eligibility and adherence must be verified because rifamycin monotherapy or partial therapy promotes resistance.

Class

Long-acting rifamycin antitubercular antibiotic; RNA polymerase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Latent TB regimens such as once-weekly isoniazid/rifapentine when eligible, and selected active TB regimens as part of combination therapy.

Description

Rifapentine is a long-acting rifamycin that inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, blocking mycobacterial RNA synthesis while enabling intermittent TB regimens in selected clients. Its first-read nursing identity is rifamycin TB therapy with hepatotoxicity, hypersensitivity, orange secretions, cytopenia, C. difficile risk, and strong interaction screening.

Mechanism

Rifapentine binds bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and prevents RNA chain formation. Its longer half-life supports intermittent TB dosing, but enzyme induction can lower many medication levels and can make hormonal contraception or anticoagulation unreliable without planning.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the exact TB regimen and schedule; many errors come from confusing daily rifampin with weekly rifapentine-based therapy.

  • Teach orange-red body fluids can occur and soft contact lenses may stain.

  • Monitor liver symptoms, rash/fever, bruising, severe diarrhea, and medication failures from interactions.

Contraindications

  • Rifamycin hypersensitivity. Clarify significant liver disease, severe interacting therapy, pregnancy questions, and active TB versus latent TB regimen appropriateness.

Interactions

  • Can reduce levels/effects of warfarin, hormonal contraceptives, some antiretrovirals, azoles, anticonvulsants, transplant drugs, and many CYP substrates.

Key Labs

  • Liver function tests when ordered or symptomatic.

  • CBC if symptoms or prolonged therapy.

  • TB testing, chest imaging, culture/susceptibility, and public-health follow-up context.

Nclex Traps

  • Rifapentine is not just rifampin misspelled; it is a longer-acting rifamycin often tied to 3HP latent-TB therapy.

  • Orange secretions are expected; jaundice, severe fatigue, fever/rash, or watery diarrhea are not.
