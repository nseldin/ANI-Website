Used To Treat

This is an adverse-effect mechanism card. It helps distinguish a bothersome persistent dry cough from potentially fatal bradykinin-mediated angioedema and explains why an ARB may be considered for cough but an ARNI is unsafe after relevant angioedema.

Description

ACE is not only an angiotensin-producing enzyme; it is also kininase II, a major bradykinin-degrading enzyme. ACE inhibition therefore raises bradykinin and related peptides such as substance P. In the respiratory tract these mediators sensitize sensory nerves and increase prostaglandin and nitric-oxide signaling, producing the characteristic persistent dry cough. In angioedema they increase endothelial permeability, allowing plasma to move into deep dermal and submucosal tissues. Swelling of lips, tongue, floor of mouth, pharynx, or larynx can obstruct the airway without urticaria or itching. Cough is uncomfortable but usually not immediately dangerous; tongue or throat swelling is an airway emergency. Angioedema may occur early or after months to years, so prior tolerance does not eliminate risk. The mechanism also explains why antihistamines alone may have limited effect when histamine is not the main mediator.

Mechanism

Bradykinin activates B2 receptors on endothelium, increasing nitric oxide, prostacyclin, and vascular permeability. Substance P activates neurokinin pathways and airway sensory C fibers. ACE normally degrades both peptides; inhibition prolongs their activity. Cough reflects sensory-neural and inflammatory signaling without tissue edema. Angioedema reflects leakage into submucosal tissue and can progress rapidly around the larynx. ARBs do not inhibit ACE, so they do not directly accumulate bradykinin and have much lower cough rates, although rare angioedema still occurs. Neprilysin also degrades vasoactive peptides; sacubitril inhibits neprilysin, which is why sacubitril/valsartan cannot overlap with an ACE inhibitor and is avoided in a history of angioedema. A 36-hour washout allows ACE-inhibitor effect to recede before neprilysin inhibition begins.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: The mechanism card has no boxed warning, but airway angioedema is immediately life threatening. Stop the ACE inhibitor, activate emergency airway assessment, and never assume absence of hives means safety. Do not restart an ACE inhibitor after ACE-inhibitor angioedema, and do not use an ARNI in a relevant angioedema history.

Adverse Effects

  • Persistent dry cough, throat tickle, sleep disruption, and medication discontinuation.

  • Lip, tongue, facial, pharyngeal, intestinal, or laryngeal angioedema; stridor, dysphonia, drooling, dysphagia, or respiratory distress signals airway danger.

Contraindications

  • Previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema and hereditary or idiopathic angioedema according to product labeling; active swelling requires emergency care.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan must not overlap an ACE inhibitor or begin within 36 hours, and ARNI use is contraindicated in a history of angioedema under current labeling/guidance.

Nursing Essentials

  • For cough, assess timing, lung disease, reflux, infection, heart failure, and whether the symptom resolves after a supervised medication change. Do not label the cough as an IgE allergy.

  • For swelling, assess voice, tongue, floor of mouth, swallowing, secretions, stridor, and respiratory effort immediately; prepare for early expert airway management rather than waiting for oxygen saturation to fall.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitor plus neprilysin inhibitor produces excessive overlap in peptide-degradation blockade, explaining the 36-hour separation rule.

  • DPP-4 inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, and other drugs have been associated with higher angioedema risk in some ACE-inhibitor contexts; review the whole medication list in a patient with swelling.

Key Labs

  • No lab rules out bradykinin angioedema. Airway anatomy and trajectory are the priority; tryptase or complement studies may help a specialist evaluate alternative or hereditary mechanisms later.

  • For the underlying ACE inhibitor, continue monitoring pressure, creatinine/eGFR, and potassium.

Nclex Traps

  • Dry cough and angioedema share a peptide pathway but do not share urgency. Cough prompts evaluation; tongue or throat swelling prompts emergency airway action.

  • Angioedema can occur after years of apparently safe use. A long medication history does not exclude the ACE inhibitor.
