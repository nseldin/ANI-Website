Summary

ABG (arterial blood gas) is a high-yield Respiratory diagnostic reference. Arterial blood gas measures pH, PaCO2, PaO2, bicarbonate, and oxygenation/ventilation status.

Quick Answer

ABG (arterial blood gas): Arterial blood gas measures pH, PaCO2, PaO2, bicarbonate, and oxygenation/ventilation status. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Oxygenation result

    Meaning

    PaO2 and oxygen saturation show arterial oxygen status. Low PaO2 means hypoxemia and must be paired with work of breathing, SpO2 trend, and oxygen delivery.

  • Label

    Ventilation result

    Meaning

    PaCO2 reflects ventilation. High PaCO2 suggests hypoventilation/ventilatory failure; low PaCO2 suggests hyperventilation or compensation.

  • Label

    Acid-base result

    Meaning

    pH and bicarbonate show acidemia/alkalemia and metabolic contribution. Interpretation starts with pH direction, PaCO2, HCO3, compensation, and clinical stability.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Arterial blood gas measures pH, PaCO2, PaO2, bicarbonate, and oxygenation/ventilation status.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess oxygenation, respiratory effort, bronchodilator/steroid use, anticoagulants if invasive, and whether the client can cooperate with the test.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Hold pressure longer for anticoagulated clients, monitor bleeding/hematoma, distal perfusion, and severe pain/numbness.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Hypoxemia, respiratory distress, hemoptysis, absent breath sounds, pneumothorax signs, or severe bronchospasm.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • PaCO2 reflects ventilation; PaO2 reflects oxygenation. Do not mix them up.
