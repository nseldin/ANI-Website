Definition

Ulnar drift sign is an observed hand-deformity sign. It is a clue to chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure. A positive finding means fingers deviate toward the ulna at MCP joints.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ulnar drift sign means fingers deviate toward the ulna at MCP joints.

Why It Matters

The observed Ulnar drift sign finding can signal chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure. Radial collateral and sagittal-band failure lets extensor tendons and phalanges shift ulnarly. Ulnar drift sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure

Assessment Method

Inspect fingers with hands resting and extended.

Positive Finding

Fingers deviate toward the ulna at MCP joints.

Mechanism

Radial collateral and sagittal-band failure lets extensor tendons and phalanges shift ulnarly.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Ulnar drift sign finding raises concern for chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure because radial collateral and sagittal-band failure lets extensor tendons and phalanges shift ulnarly. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ulnar drift sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Reducibility and pain vary; deviation is not exclusive to RA. Therefore, Ulnar drift sign cannot confirm or exclude chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as post-traumatic deformity, congenital deviation, neurologic tone.

False Negative Situations

Ulnar drift sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic MCP synovitis and ligament failure.
