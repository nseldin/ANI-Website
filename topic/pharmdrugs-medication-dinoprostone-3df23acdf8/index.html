Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to prostaglandins, fetal distress when delivery is not imminent, unexplained pregnancy bleeding, marked cephalopelvic disproportion, prior uterine surgery that threatens integrity, or any condition making induction or prolonged contraction unsafe.

  • Do not use concurrently with IV oxytocin. Product-specific parity restrictions and cautions such as glaucoma must be checked before insertion.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline Bishop score, fetal tracing, contractions, membrane status, presentation, uterine-scar history, bleeding, and indication. Keep the retrieval tape accessible.

  • After removal, visually confirm the slab is present because retained insert material continues releasing drug. Continue monitoring; removal does not instantly erase absorbed prostaglandin.

Interactions

  • Oxytocin and other uterotonics are pharmacodynamically additive. Remove CERVIDIL at least 30 minutes before starting IV oxytocin.

  • Agents or conditions that increase uterine tone, alter perfusion, or change vaginal release can modify response.

Key Labs

  • No serum level guides therapy; obtain CBC, type and screen, or other studies based on the induction indication and hemorrhage risk

  • Bishop score, contraction frequency/duration/resting tone, fetal heart-rate category, maternal vital signs, membrane status, labor progress, and complete insert removal

Nclex Traps

  • Dinoprostone primarily prepares the cervix but can still cause dangerous contractions and fetal compromise.

  • The removable insert is not equivalent to every dinoprostone formulation, and a 30-minute interval before IV oxytocin is a product-specific safety step.

Used To Treat

Initiation or continuation of cervical ripening at or near term when there is a medical or obstetric indication for induction of labor; product-specific formulations and administration methods are not interchangeable.

Mechanism

Dinoprostone activates PGE2 EP receptors in cervix and myometrium. Cervical EP2 and EP4 signaling increases cAMP-dependent remodeling, matrix metalloproteinase activity, collagen dispersion, glycosaminoglycan accumulation, and water content; inflammatory-cell and nitric-oxide pathways add further softening. Dinoprostone also increases local PGF2-alpha production and oxytocin-receptor responsiveness. Myometrial EP1/EP3 and downstream calcium signaling can produce contractions or tachysystole. Near term, changing receptor expression makes the cervix and uterus more responsive. After membrane rupture, vaginal pH can increase release from the CERVIDIL insert, which is why removal before amniotomy or after rupture is clinically important.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: CERVIDIL is for hospital use by trained obstetric personnel with facilities for continuous uterine and fetal monitoring and emergency care. Remove the insert after 12 hours, at active labor, before amniotomy, or immediately for tachysystole, hypertonicity, fetal distress, or other adverse response. It is contraindicated with concurrent IV oxytocics and should be removed at least 30 minutes before an oxytocic begins. Previous cesarean or major uterine surgery, unexplained bleeding, fetal distress without imminent delivery, marked cephalopelvic disproportion, or another contraindication to induction raises rupture and fetal danger.

Description

Dinoprostone activates prostaglandin E2 receptors in the cervix to increase matrix remodeling, collagen dispersion, hydration, effacement, and dilation while sensitizing myometrium to endogenous or infused oxytocin. This pharmaceutical PGE2 is used mainly to transform an unfavorable cervix before induction, but it can also stimulate contractions directly. The removable CERVIDIL vaginal insert releases drug gradually and can be retrieved if active labor, tachysystole, hypertonicity, membrane rupture, or fetal distress occurs. That removability is a meaningful safety property, not just a formulation detail. Cervical ripening must therefore be monitored as an active obstetric intervention rather than treated like passive local medication.

Adverse Effects

  • Uterine cramping, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fever, back pain, headache, and local vaginal discomfort

  • Tachysystole with or without fetal heart-rate changes, hypertonicity, fetal distress, uterine rupture, DIC, amniotic-fluid embolism syndrome, severe hypersensitivity, or emergency operative delivery
