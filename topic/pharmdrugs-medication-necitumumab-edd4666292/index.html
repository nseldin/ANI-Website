Class

Human IgG1 monoclonal antibody against extracellular EGFR; discontinued U.S. product

Used To Treat

Historically used with gemcitabine and cisplatin as first-line treatment of metastatic squamous non-small-cell lung cancer. The U.S. marketing end date recorded in DailyMed was May 27, 2026, so this entry is retained for prior-treatment history, delayed toxicity, examination knowledge, and medication reconciliation rather than as a currently marketed U.S. option.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNINGS in the historical U.S. label: cardiopulmonary arrest/sudden death and hypomagnesemia. Monitor magnesium, potassium, and calcium before every infusion and for at least 8 weeks after the final dose; replace deficits promptly and withhold for severe electrolyte toxicity. Closely assess cardiopulmonary disease and permanently discontinue for grade 3 or 4 infusion reaction or other label-defined life-threatening toxicity.

Mechanism

Necitumumab binds extracellular EGFR, blocks EGF-family ligand binding and receptor phosphorylation, and promotes receptor internalization and degradation. Suppression of RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT signaling can slow EGFR-dependent squamous tumor cells, while the IgG1 Fc can support ADCC. Normal distal-nephron EGF-EGFR signaling stimulates TRPM6 magnesium uptake; inhibiting that pathway wastes magnesium in urine, and magnesium depletion makes potassium and calcium abnormalities harder to correct. The combination of electrolyte instability, underlying lung cancer, and platinum-based therapy helps explain the boxed cardiopulmonary danger.

Nursing Essentials

  • For a patient with prior necitumumab exposure, retain the last-dose date and trend magnesium, potassium, and calcium through at least 8 weeks because antibody effect and renal wasting can outlast the infusion schedule.

  • Escalate palpitations, syncope, weakness, tetany, tremor, seizure, chest pain, dyspnea, or new edema immediately; these can be consequences of electrolyte or cardiopulmonary toxicity rather than routine fatigue.

  • Document the product as discontinued in the United States while preserving its brand and regimen aliases so historical medication lists remain searchable.

Contraindications

  • Do not present necitumumab as a currently marketed U.S. treatment after its May 27, 2026 marketing end date.

  • The former indication was squamous NSCLC; studies in non-squamous NSCLC did not establish benefit and exposed patients to added toxicity.

  • Severe uncorrected electrolyte loss, serious prior infusion reaction, or unstable cardiopulmonary disease requires urgent prescriber reassessment.

Interactions

  • Cisplatin can worsen renal electrolyte loss and kidney injury, while gemcitabine adds marrow suppression; the three-drug regimen therefore requires integrated rather than antibody-only monitoring.

  • Loop or thiazide diuretics, PPIs, aminoglycosides, amphotericin, and other magnesium-lowering exposures can compound depletion.

  • QT-prolonging drugs and digoxin become more dangerous when magnesium or potassium is low.

Key Labs

  • Magnesium, potassium, calcium, creatinine, and ECG when symptomatic before each historical dose and for at least 8 weeks after therapy

  • CBC and renal/liver tests for gemcitabine/cisplatin regimen

  • Vital signs, oxygenation, and cardiac assessment

  • Imaging and histology confirming squamous NSCLC in historical treatment review

Nclex Traps

  • The key Portrazza pairing was metastatic squamous NSCLC plus gemcitabine/cisplatin, not non-squamous lung cancer.

  • The 8-week electrolyte window exists because monoclonal-antibody exposure and renal magnesium wasting persist after the final infusion.

  • A discontinued product can remain clinically relevant when the patient was recently exposed or presents with a delayed adverse effect.

Population Risks

  • Patients with cardiac disease, baseline electrolyte loss, diarrhea, malnutrition, renal dysfunction, diuretic use, or platinum nephrotoxicity have amplified dysrhythmia risk.

  • Pregnancy exposure could harm a fetus; historical counseling followed product-specific contraception guidance.

  • People treated before discontinuation still need delayed-toxicity recognition and accurate medication reconciliation.

Description

Necitumumab is a human IgG1 EGFR-blocking antibody formerly added to gemcitabine/cisplatin for metastatic squamous NSCLC. It prevents extracellular ligand activation, promotes receptor internalization and degradation, and can recruit ADCC, but its modest regimen benefit came with unusually consequential electrolyte and cardiopulmonary risk. Severe renal magnesium wasting can drive hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, QT instability, dysrhythmia, seizure, or sudden death, which is why magnesium, potassium, and calcium monitoring continues for at least 8 weeks after the last dose.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, muscle weakness, seizure, QT instability, and dysrhythmia

  • Cardiopulmonary arrest, sudden death, and thromboembolic events

  • Acneiform rash, xerosis, fissures, paronychia, and skin infection

  • Infusion reactions

  • Gemcitabine/cisplatin-regimen cytopenias, nausea, vomiting, nephrotoxicity, fatigue, and infection
