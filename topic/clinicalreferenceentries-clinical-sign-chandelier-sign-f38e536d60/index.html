Definition

Chandelier sign is a provoked symptom-sign. It is a clue to pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation. A positive finding means marked cervical motion pain causes guarding or withdrawal.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chandelier sign means marked cervical motion pain causes guarding or withdrawal.

Why It Matters

The provoked Chandelier sign symptom can signal pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation. Movement tensions inflamed pelvic peritoneum, tubes, or adjacent structures. Chandelier sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation

Assessment Method

During an indicated bimanual examination, move the cervix gently with consent and ask about pain.

Positive Finding

Marked cervical motion pain causes guarding or withdrawal.

Mechanism

Movement tensions inflamed pelvic peritoneum, tubes, or adjacent structures.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Chandelier sign symptom raises concern for pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation because movement tensions inflamed pelvic peritoneum, tubes, or adjacent structures. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chandelier sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chandelier sign to focus evaluation of pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation and the reviewed alternatives: endometriosis, ovarian torsion, pelvic abscess. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During an indicated bimanual examination, move the cervix gently with consent and ask about pain. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chandelier sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sensitivity and specificity are limited and tenderness is subjective. Therefore, Chandelier sign cannot confirm or exclude pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • endometriosis

  • ovarian torsion

  • pelvic abscess

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as endometriosis, ovarian torsion, pelvic abscess, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Chandelier sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Current minimum-clinical-criteria component for PID; not specific. Interpret Chandelier sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Sensitivity and specificity are limited and tenderness is subjective.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Chandelier sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: During an indicated bimanual examination, move the cervix gently with consent and ask about pain. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Positive pregnancy test, shock, or severe unilateral pain requires emergency ectopic and torsion evaluation.

Nursing Relevance

Record the patient's description of Chandelier sign, the exact trigger, distribution, duration, and associated objective findings; the reviewed positive response is: Marked cervical motion pain causes guarding or withdrawal. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: During an indicated bimanual examination, move the cervix gently with consent and ask about pain. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, pelvic inflammatory disease or pelvic peritoneal irritation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Chandelier sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Positive pregnancy test, shock, or severe unilateral pain requires emergency ectopic and torsion evaluation. Base escalation for Chandelier sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
