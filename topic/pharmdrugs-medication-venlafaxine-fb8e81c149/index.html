Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, social anxiety disorder and panic disorder, with indication and dosing dependent on immediate- versus extended-release formulation.

Mechanism

Venlafaxine and active O-desmethylvenlafaxine inhibit SERT and NET, increasing serotonin and norepinephrine in mood, arousal and anxiety circuits; they have little direct muscarinic, H1 or alpha-1 antagonism. CYP2D6 converts venlafaxine to O-desmethylvenlafaxine, so genotype/inhibitors shift the parent-to-metabolite ratio even though both contribute to the active moiety. Repeated monoamine elevation produces slower autoreceptor and network-plasticity changes needed for clinical response. Noradrenergic vascular and cardiac stimulation explains dose/exposure-related hypertension and tachycardia; rapid concentration decline explains dizziness, electric-shock sensations, nausea, disequilibrium and anxiety after missed doses.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Monitor blood pressure before and during treatment and taper gradually; abrupt exposure loss can produce severe discontinuation symptoms and, rarely, destabilizing neurologic or psychiatric effects.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI use or serotonergic linezolid/methylene blue outside emergency guidance

  • Control hypertension and assess serious cardiac, seizure, bipolar and glaucoma risk

  • Clarify renal/hepatic dose adjustment before giving; reduced clearance can convert a routine dose into high exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask whether symptoms appear before the next dose or after a late dose; timing can identify interdose withdrawal rather than relapse.

  • Check blood pressure/pulse at baseline and after titration because increasing NET blockade can make cardiovascular effects emerge after earlier doses were tolerated.

Interactions

  • MAOIs and other serotonergic agents add serotonin syndrome risk.

  • CYP2D6 inhibitors alter parent/metabolite exposure; total active effect and adverse pattern still require monitoring.

  • NSAIDs/anticoagulants add bleeding; diuretics add hyponatremia; other activating or BP-raising drugs add cardiovascular burden.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure and pulse before and during titration

  • Sodium in high-risk/symptomatic patients and renal/hepatic function for dosing

  • Mood, suicidality/mania, withdrawal timing, sexual effects and bleeding

Nclex Traps

  • Effexor withdrawal after a missed dose is not proof of addiction; it reflects rapid pharmacokinetic adaptation and decline.

  • SNRI effects are not all-or-none: venlafaxine becomes more noradrenergic as exposure rises.

  • Extended release smooths peaks but does not eliminate withdrawal if stopped abruptly.

Description

Venlafaxine is an SNRI whose pharmacology changes across exposure: serotonin-transporter inhibition predominates at lower doses, while norepinephrine-transporter inhibition becomes more clinically important as dose rises. That helps explain why higher exposure can add energy or analgesic-network effects but also raise pulse and blood pressure. Its short parent half-life and relatively rapid decline make late or missed doses more likely to cause abrupt discontinuation symptoms than long-lived fluoxetine.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, sweating, insomnia/somnolence, dry mouth, constipation and sexual dysfunction

  • Sustained hypertension, tachycardia and palpitations

  • Prominent discontinuation syndrome after missed doses or rapid taper

  • Serotonin syndrome, SIADH/hyponatremia, bleeding, mania, seizures and angle-closure glaucoma
