Definition

Heel-to-ear sign is a neonatal tone maneuver. It is a clue to gestational maturity and passive flexor tone. A positive finding means greater resistance and a larger gap reflect greater maturity.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Heel-to-ear sign means greater resistance and a larger gap reflect greater maturity.

Why It Matters

A positive Heel-to-ear sign response can signal gestational maturity and passive flexor tone. Mature hip and knee flexor tone limits passive lower-limb excursion. Heel-to-ear sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gestational maturity and passive flexor tone

Assessment Method

Bring the infant's foot gently toward the ipsilateral ear while keeping the pelvis down.

Positive Finding

Greater resistance and a larger gap reflect greater maturity.

Mechanism

Mature hip and knee flexor tone limits passive lower-limb excursion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Heel-to-ear sign response raises concern for gestational maturity and passive flexor tone because mature hip and knee flexor tone limits passive lower-limb excursion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Heel-to-ear sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Heel-to-ear sign to focus evaluation of gestational maturity and passive flexor tone and the reviewed alternatives: joint laxity, arthrogryposis, femur injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Bring the infant's foot gently toward the ipsilateral ear while keeping the pelvis down. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Heel-to-ear sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Breech position and hip dysplasia can alter range. Therefore, Heel-to-ear sign cannot confirm or exclude gestational maturity and passive flexor tone by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • joint laxity

  • arthrogryposis

  • femur injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from joint laxity, arthrogryposis, femur injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Heel-to-ear sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current Ballard assessment component. Interpret Heel-to-ear sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Breech position and hip dysplasia can alter range.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Heel-to-ear sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Bring the infant's foot gently toward the ipsilateral ear while keeping the pelvis down. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid with hip instability, fracture concern, or pain.
