Class

Recombinant human leptin-receptor agonist replacement therapy

Used To Treat

Adjunct to diet for metabolic complications of leptin deficiency in congenital or acquired generalized lipodystrophy; it is not indicated for general obesity and is not established for HIV-related or most partial lipodystrophy.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - anti-metreleptin antibodies with neutralizing activity and lymphoma. Neutralizing antibodies can block both administered and endogenous leptin, causing severe infection or loss of metabolic control; test when either occurs. T-cell lymphoma has occurred, especially in acquired generalized lipodystrophy with hematologic abnormalities. MYALEPT is available through a REMS. Also monitor hypoglycemia with insulin/secretagogues, autoimmunity, hypersensitivity and pancreatitis risk during abrupt withdrawal.

Mechanism

Metreleptin binds the ObR leptin receptor, a class-I cytokine receptor that signals through JAK2/STAT pathways in hypothalamic and peripheral tissues. Central signaling communicates energy sufficiency and suppresses excessive intake; peripheral effects improve insulin sensitivity and lipid handling. Less caloric influx and better substrate partitioning reduce glucose, HbA1c and triglycerides in generalized lipodystrophy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm generalized lipodystrophy and REMS access; document CBC/hematologic abnormalities, infection history, triglycerides/pancreatitis, glucose therapy, liver disease and weight-based preparation before dosing.

  • Anticipate substantial insulin or secretagogue dose reduction as sensitivity improves. Never stop abruptly in severe hypertriglyceridemia without a taper/monitoring plan because pancreatitis risk can rebound.

Key Labs

  • Fasting glucose and HbA1c, triglycerides and full lipid panel, liver tests, CBC, renal function, amylase/lipase when symptomatic, anti-metreleptin neutralizing-antibody testing for severe infection or efficacy loss, and glucose during insulin adjustment.

Nclex Traps

  • Generalized lipodystrophy is a lack-of-functional-adipose/leptin disorder despite severe metabolic disease; metreleptin is replacement, not routine obesity treatment.

  • Severe infection or worsening control may signal neutralizing antibodies and requires testing, not automatic dose escalation.

Description

Metreleptin is recombinant methionyl-human leptin replacement for generalized lipodystrophy, where loss of adipose tissue causes profound leptin deficiency, hyperphagia, insulin resistance, severe hypertriglyceridemia and ectopic fat in liver and muscle. Restoring leptin signaling reduces food intake and improves metabolic control; it is not a conventional weight-loss drug.
