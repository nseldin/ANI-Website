Definition

Howell-Jolly bodies is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to absent or impaired splenic filtration. A positive finding means small round dense nuclear remnants persist within erythrocytes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Howell-Jolly bodies means small round dense nuclear remnants persist within erythrocytes.

Why It Matters

The Howell-Jolly bodies laboratory finding can signal absent or impaired splenic filtration. A functioning spleen normally removes residual erythroblast nuclear DNA. Howell-Jolly bodies cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • absent or impaired splenic filtration

Review peripheral smear.

A functioning spleen normally removes residual erythroblast nuclear DNA.

Evidence Limitations

Rare inclusions may occur with severe marrow stress and do not quantify splenic function. Therefore, Howell-Jolly bodies cannot confirm or exclude absent or impaired splenic filtration by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Pappenheimer bodies

  • basophilic stippling

  • parasites

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with Pappenheimer bodies, basophilic stippling, parasites.

False Negative Situations

Howell-Jolly bodies may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
