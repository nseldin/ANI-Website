Definition

Dependent rubor sign is an observed vascular sign. It is a clue to severe chronic arterial insufficiency. A positive finding means a pale elevated foot turns dusky red when lowered.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dependent rubor sign means a pale elevated foot turns dusky red when lowered.

Why It Matters

The observed Dependent rubor sign finding can signal severe chronic arterial insufficiency. Maximally dilated ischemic arterioles fill with deoxygenated blood under gravity. Dependent rubor sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe chronic arterial insufficiency

Assessment Method

Compare foot color elevated and dependent.

Positive Finding

A pale elevated foot turns dusky red when lowered.

Mechanism

Maximally dilated ischemic arterioles fill with deoxygenated blood under gravity.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Dependent rubor sign finding raises concern for severe chronic arterial insufficiency because maximally dilated ischemic arterioles fill with deoxygenated blood under gravity. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dependent rubor sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dependent rubor sign to focus evaluation of severe chronic arterial insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: cellulitis, gout, reactive hyperemia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compare foot color elevated and dependent. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dependent rubor sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Color is lighting and skin-tone dependent and can be absent. Therefore, Dependent rubor sign cannot confirm or exclude severe chronic arterial insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cellulitis

  • gout

  • reactive hyperemia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cellulitis, gout, reactive hyperemia.

False Negative Situations

Dependent rubor sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe chronic arterial insufficiency.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Observe Dependent rubor sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Compare foot color elevated and dependent. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not mistake rubor for cellulitis without temperature, pulses, pain, and distribution.
