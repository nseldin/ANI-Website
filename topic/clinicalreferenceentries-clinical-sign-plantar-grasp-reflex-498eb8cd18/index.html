Definition

Plantar grasp reflex is an infant primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to normal early motor development. A positive finding means the toes flex around the stimulus.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Plantar grasp reflex means the toes flex around the stimulus.

Why It Matters

A positive Plantar grasp reflex response can signal normal early motor development. Plantar tactile afferents activate a primitive flexor grasp circuit. Plantar grasp reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • normal early motor development

Assessment Method

Press gently at the base of the toes.

Positive Finding

The toes flex around the stimulus.

Mechanism

Plantar tactile afferents activate a primitive flexor grasp circuit.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Plantar grasp reflex response raises concern for normal early motor development because plantar tactile afferents activate a primitive flexor grasp circuit. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Plantar grasp reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Plantar grasp reflex to focus evaluation of normal early motor development and the reviewed alternatives: Babinski response, ticklish withdrawal, clubfoot. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Press gently at the base of the toes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Plantar grasp reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The response changes with age and foot stimulation can trigger withdrawal. Therefore, Plantar grasp reflex cannot confirm or exclude normal early motor development by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Babinski response

  • ticklish withdrawal

  • clubfoot

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from Babinski response, ticklish withdrawal, clubfoot, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Plantar grasp reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
