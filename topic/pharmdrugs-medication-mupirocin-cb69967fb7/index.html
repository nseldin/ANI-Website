Used To Treat

Short-course topical treatment of impetigo caused by susceptible Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus pyogenes; intranasal products and other infection uses have separate labeling.

Mechanism

Mupirocin resembles the isoleucyl-adenylate intermediate and competitively blocks IleRS, leaving tRNA-Ile uncharged and stopping translation when ribosomes encounter isoleucine codons. Human cytoplasmic synthetases bind it poorly, creating selective toxicity. The unusual target limits cross-resistance with beta-lactams, macrolides and aminoglycosides, but plasmid-mediated high-level mupirocin resistance and target mutations emerge when use is widespread.

Contraindications

  • Known mupirocin or vehicle hypersensitivity

  • Avoid ointment in eyes, nose, mouth or vagina and avoid large open wounds with significant renal impairment

  • Clarify abscess, spreading cellulitis, systemic illness, recurrent infection and treatment failure

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a small amount with clean cotton swab/gauze three times daily for up to 10 days as labeled; reassess if no response in 3-5 days.

  • Use contact precautions/hygiene and obtain culture for recurrence, outbreak, MRSA risk or nonresponse.

Interactions

  • Do not mix with other creams/ointments because dilution can reduce antibacterial activity and alter stability.

  • No major systemic CYP interaction is expected from limited intact-skin use.

  • Avoid prolonged repeated decolonization courses without a protocol because resistance develops.

Key Labs

  • Culture and susceptibility for nonresponse, recurrent impetigo or outbreak

  • Renal function when large damaged areas could absorb polyethylene glycol

  • Serial erythema, drainage, fever and spread assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Mupirocin blocks isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase, not the 50S ribosome.

  • A fluctuant abscess needs drainage; ointment does not provide source control.

  • Mupirocin ointment is not interchangeable with the nasal product.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Ointment is not for ophthalmic, intranasal or other mucosal use; a separate nasal product exists. Stop severe local irritation or systemic allergy. Polyethylene glycol from ointment can be absorbed through large open wounds and is renally eliminated, so avoid extensive damaged-skin use in moderate-to-severe renal impairment.

Description

Mupirocin is a topical antibacterial with a distinctive target: bacterial isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase. By preventing isoleucine from being attached to its transfer RNA, it halts bacterial protein synthesis; low concentrations are bacteriostatic and high local concentrations are bactericidal. Ointment is labeled for impetigo, not as a universal wound cream. Deep infection, abscess, cellulitis, systemic symptoms or nonresponse requires culture, drainage and/or systemic therapy rather than indefinite topical extension.

Adverse Effects

  • Application-site burning, stinging, pain, pruritus, rash and contact dermatitis

  • Dryness, erythema and nausea

  • Severe hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis

  • C. difficile-associated diarrhea is rare but antibacterial overgrowth and resistance can occur
