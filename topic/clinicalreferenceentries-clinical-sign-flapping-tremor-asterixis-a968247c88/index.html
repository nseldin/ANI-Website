Definition

Flapping tremor asterixis is an observed motor sign. It is a clue to toxic-metabolic encephalopathy. A positive finding means brief irregular lapses of posture produce flapping movements.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Flapping tremor asterixis means brief irregular lapses of posture produce flapping movements.

Why It Matters

The observed Flapping tremor asterixis finding can signal toxic-metabolic encephalopathy. Intermittent failure of sustained muscle contraction reflects diffuse cortical-subcortical metabolic dysfunction. Flapping tremor asterixis cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • toxic-metabolic encephalopathy

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to extend arms, dorsiflex wrists, and spread fingers.

Positive Finding

Brief irregular lapses of posture produce flapping movements.

Mechanism

Intermittent failure of sustained muscle contraction reflects diffuse cortical-subcortical metabolic dysfunction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Flapping tremor asterixis finding raises concern for toxic-metabolic encephalopathy because intermittent failure of sustained muscle contraction reflects diffuse cortical-subcortical metabolic dysfunction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Flapping tremor asterixis does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Flapping tremor asterixis to focus evaluation of toxic-metabolic encephalopathy and the reviewed alternatives: essential tremor, clonus, drug-induced myoclonus. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to extend arms, dorsiflex wrists, and spread fingers. Evidence Limitations

Weakness and tremor can mimic it; absence does not exclude encephalopathy. Therefore, Flapping tremor asterixis cannot confirm or exclude toxic-metabolic encephalopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • essential tremor

  • clonus

  • drug-induced myoclonus

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as essential tremor, clonus, drug-induced myoclonus.

False Negative Situations

Flapping tremor asterixis may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of toxic-metabolic encephalopathy.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Check both sides and escalate new asterixis with altered mental status.
