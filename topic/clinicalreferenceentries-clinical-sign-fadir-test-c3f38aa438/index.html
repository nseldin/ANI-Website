Definition

FADIR test is a bedside hip-impingement maneuver. It is a clue to femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain. A positive finding means familiar anterior groin pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive FADIR test means familiar anterior groin pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive FADIR test response can signal femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain. The position approximates the femoral neck and acetabular rim and stresses anterior labral tissue. FADIR test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain

Assessment Method

Flex, adduct, and internally rotate the hip gently.

Positive Finding

Familiar anterior groin pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

The position approximates the femoral neck and acetabular rim and stresses anterior labral tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive FADIR test response raises concern for femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain because the position approximates the femoral neck and acetabular rim and stresses anterior labral tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; FADIR test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use FADIR test to focus evaluation of femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain and the reviewed alternatives: iliopsoas pain, adductor strain, lumbar referral. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex, adduct, and internally rotate the hip gently. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating FADIR test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many asymptomatic hips have morphology and the test is not specific. Therefore, FADIR test cannot confirm or exclude femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • iliopsoas pain

  • adductor strain

  • lumbar referral

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from iliopsoas pain, adductor strain, lumbar referral, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

FADIR test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current sensitive but nonspecific provocation adjunct. When performed as described, FADIR test can adjust concern for femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for FADIR test: Many asymptomatic hips have morphology and the test is not specific.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing FADIR test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex, adduct, and internally rotate the hip gently. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force range or treat a positive test as proof that imaging morphology causes pain.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact FADIR test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Familiar anterior groin pain is reproduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Flex, adduct, and internally rotate the hip gently. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, femoroacetabular impingement or intra-articular hip pain, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For FADIR test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not force range or treat a positive test as proof that imaging morphology causes pain. Base escalation for FADIR test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
