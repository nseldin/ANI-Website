Contraindications

  • Current pregnancy for contraceptive initiation, unexplained uterine bleeding, active significant liver disease or progestin-sensitive cancer, plus IUD-specific pelvic infection, uterine distortion, and postpartum timing restrictions.

  • Emergency contraception does not require proof of infertility and should not be withheld for the purpose of a pregnancy test when current guidance supports prompt use, but it cannot treat an established or ectopic pregnancy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask which product. For emergency contraception, teach prompt use, what to do after vomiting, when to resume/start ongoing contraception, backup timing, and pregnancy testing. For IUDs, document product and removal date.

  • Teach expected bleeding changes and warning symptoms without claiming amenorrhea is proof of pregnancy or device failure. Check strings and evaluate pain, fever, abnormal discharge, pregnancy symptoms, or expulsion concern.

Interactions

  • CYP3A inducers can reduce emergency-pill and systemic contraceptive exposure; discuss a copper IUD or guideline-supported alternative emergency method when interaction risk is high.

  • Ulipristal and progestins can antagonize each other's intended receptor effects, so starting or resuming hormonal contraception after ulipristal follows current CDC timing guidance.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy assessment as indicated; STI screening can be performed according to risk without unnecessary delay of IUD placement, and no routine hormone level is needed

  • Bleeding pattern, menses timing after emergency dosing, pelvic pain, fever/discharge, IUD strings and expulsion, medication inducers, and pregnancy/ectopic symptoms

Nclex Traps

  • Plan B primarily delays ovulation and does not disrupt an implanted pregnancy; calling it an abortifacient is scientifically inaccurate.

  • MIRENA's contraceptive duration and heavy-bleeding-treatment duration are different: up to eight years versus up to five years under current labeling.

Used To Treat

Emergency contraception after unprotected intercourse or contraceptive failure; long-acting intrauterine contraception with duration depending on product; treatment of heavy menstrual bleeding with selected 52-mg systems; and routine contraception in combination products.

Mechanism

Levonorgestrel binds nuclear progesterone receptors and alters transcription. Before the LH surge, emergency dosing suppresses or delays the follicular LH signal and ovulation, so sperm present after intercourse encounter no released oocyte; FDA labeling does not support post-fertilization disruption of implantation as its mechanism. In an IUD, high local uterine exposure makes cervical mucus viscous, reduces sperm motility and function, and creates a thin glandular endometrium; ovulation often continues because systemic exposure is low. In combined oral products, systemic progestin plus estrogen suppresses GnRH, FSH and LH more consistently. CYP3A induction can lower oral/systemic exposure, while an intrauterine system's strong local effect makes interaction consequences formulation-specific rather than identical.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Levonorgestrel products have no shared current U.S. boxed warning, but warnings are formulation-specific. Emergency levonorgestrel does not protect intercourse later in the cycle, does not end an existing pregnancy, and should prompt pregnancy testing if the next menses is substantially delayed. With an IUD, perforation, expulsion, pelvic infection around insertion, embedment, sepsis, and ectopic pregnancy if conception occurs require prompt recognition. A pregnancy with an IUD in place carries miscarriage and infection risk. New severe lower-abdominal pain, pregnancy symptoms, fever, purulent discharge, missing strings, or heavy bleeding needs assessment.

Class Pathway

  • Progestin contraception

  • Progesterone-receptor agonist

  • Formulation-specific ovulation, cervical-mucus, and endometrial effects

Description

Levonorgestrel is a potent synthetic progestin that binds progesterone receptors to delay ovulation in emergency dosing or, from an IUD, thicken cervical mucus, suppress endometrium, and impair sperm transport and fertilization. A 1.5-mg emergency-contraception tablet works before pregnancy, especially before the LH surge. It does not terminate an implanted pregnancy and is not an abortion drug. A levonorgestrel IUD releases tiny daily amounts inside the uterus and may not suppress every ovulation. MIRENA currently prevents pregnancy for up to eight years and treats heavy menstrual bleeding for up to five years. Other IUDs have different doses and durations. The same generic name therefore cannot be taught with one schedule, route, or counseling script.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, headache, dizziness, breast tenderness, abdominal pain, fatigue, irregular bleeding, spotting, or altered timing of the next menses

  • IUD perforation, expulsion, pelvic infection or sepsis, ectopic pregnancy if failure occurs, ovarian cyst, thrombosis in estrogen-containing combinations, liver injury, or delayed diagnosis of pregnancy
