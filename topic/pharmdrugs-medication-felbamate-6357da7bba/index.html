Class

Broad-spectrum NMDA-modulating antiseizure medication

Used To Treat

Severe refractory focal epilepsy in adults and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome in children when inadequate response to safer alternatives makes the risks acceptable.

Boxed Warning

Aplastic anemia and hepatic failure: both may appear abruptly and be fatal. Use only when epilepsy severity justifies the risk, obtain informed consent and baseline/serial CBC-reticulocytes and liver tests, and stop immediately for evidence of marrow suppression or liver injury.

Mechanism

Felbamate's full mechanism is uncertain; it inhibits NMDA-receptor activity, especially at the strychnine-insensitive glycine modulatory site, and may potentiate GABA-A responses while affecting sodium/calcium currents. The net effect reduces excitatory seizure propagation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach urgent reporting of fever, infection, sore throat, bruising/bleeding, pallor, profound fatigue, anorexia, vomiting, RUQ pain, dark urine, or jaundice.

  • Trend CBC with platelets/reticulocytes and liver tests, interactions with phenytoin/valproate/carbamazepine, seizure response, weight, sleep, and suicidality.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and frequent CBC/platelets/reticulocytes plus ALT/AST/bilirubin; antiseizure drug levels when interactions require.

Nclex Traps

  • Normal blood counts today do not eliminate felbamate risk; monitoring may detect injury but cannot reliably prevent aplastic anemia.

Description

Felbamate is a highly restricted broad-spectrum antiseizure drug that antagonizes the NMDA-receptor glycine site and may enhance GABA-A inhibition, reducing excitatory seizure propagation in severe refractory epilepsy. Its boxed warnings are fatal aplastic anemia and acute hepatic failure, neither of which can be reliably predicted by routine testing.
