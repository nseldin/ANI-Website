Definition

Apley distraction test is a prone knee maneuver. It is a clue to collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain. A positive finding means pain occurs with traction or is greater than during compression.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Apley distraction test means pain occurs with traction or is greater than during compression.

Why It Matters

A positive Apley distraction test response can signal collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain. Distraction unloads menisci while stressing collateral and capsular structures. Apley distraction test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain

Assessment Method

With the knee flexed, stabilize the thigh, pull upward on the lower leg, and rotate gently.

Positive Finding

Pain occurs with traction or is greater than during compression.

Mechanism

Distraction unloads menisci while stressing collateral and capsular structures.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Apley distraction test response raises concern for collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain because distraction unloads menisci while stressing collateral and capsular structures. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Apley distraction test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Apley distraction test to focus evaluation of collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain and the reviewed alternatives: hamstring strain, posterolateral injury, guarding. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the knee flexed, stabilize the thigh, pull upward on the lower leg, and rotate gently. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Apley distraction test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The distinction between compression and distraction pain is unreliable. Therefore, Apley distraction test cannot confirm or exclude collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hamstring strain

  • posterolateral injury

  • guarding

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hamstring strain, posterolateral injury, guarding, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Apley distraction test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited comparative adjunct. When performed as described, Apley distraction test can adjust concern for collateral ligament rather than meniscal pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Apley distraction test: The distinction between compression and distraction pain is unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Apley distraction test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the knee flexed, stabilize the thigh, pull upward on the lower leg, and rotate gently. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use gentle traction and stop for instability or severe pain.
