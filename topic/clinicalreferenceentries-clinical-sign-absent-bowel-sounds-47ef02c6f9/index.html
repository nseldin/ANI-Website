Definition

Absent bowel sounds means no intestinal sound is heard after correct technique and a sufficiently long continuous listening period have been completed.

Plain Language

The examiner cannot hear the intestines moving even after listening carefully for the required amount of time.

Why It Matters

Absent sounds can occur when intestinal movement has stopped or become severely reduced because of ileus, advanced obstruction, peritonitis, ischemia, medication effects, or serious illness. When combined with severe pain, vomiting, distention, rigidity, bleeding, or shock, urgent evaluation is required.

Associated Conditions

  • marked suppression of intestinal motility from ileus, late or severe mechanical obstruction, peritonitis, bowel ischemia, severe systemic illness, medication effect, or a postoperative state

Assessment Method

Auscultate before percussion or palpation; if no sound is initially heard, verify stethoscope function and a quiet environment, then listen continuously over the periumbilical region for the full prolonged interval required by the assessment method and local policy, with the cited Clinical Methods approach using three full minutes; document distention, pain, rigidity, nausea or vomiting, stool, flatus, surgery, medications, vital signs, perfusion, and mental status.

Positive Finding

No bowel sound is heard during an adequate continuous listening period after technique and equipment have been confirmed.

Mechanism

When intestinal smooth-muscle contraction and propulsion become profoundly reduced or stop, little or no gas and fluid movement occurs to generate audible sound.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Absent bowel sounds result raises concern for marked suppression of intestinal motility from ileus, late or severe mechanical obstruction, peritonitis, bowel ischemia, severe systemic illness, medication effect, or a postoperative state because when intestinal smooth-muscle contraction and propulsion become profoundly reduced or stop, little or no gas and fluid movement occurs to generate audible sound. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Absent bowel sounds does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Absent bowel sounds to focus evaluation of marked suppression of intestinal motility from ileus, late or severe mechanical obstruction, peritonitis, bowel ischemia, severe systemic illness, medication effect, or a postoperative state and the reviewed alternatives: hypoactive bowel sounds, normal prolonged quiet interval, stethoscope failure, poor acoustic transmission. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate before percussion or palpation; if no sound is initially heard, verify stethoscope function and a quiet environment, then listen continuously over the periumbilical region for the full prolonged interval required by the assessment method and local policy, with the cited Clinical Methods approach using three full minutes; document distention, pain, rigidity, nausea or vomiting, stool, flatus, surgery, medications, vital signs, perfusion, and mental status. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Absent bowel sounds as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal bowel activity includes quiet intervals, inadequate listening and environmental noise can create false absence, and absent sounds cannot distinguish ileus, obstruction, ischemia, medication effect, or peritonitis. Therefore, Absent bowel sounds cannot confirm or exclude marked suppression of intestinal motility from ileus, late or severe mechanical obstruction, peritonitis, bowel ischemia, severe systemic illness, medication effect, or a postoperative state by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hypoactive bowel sounds

  • normal prolonged quiet interval

  • stethoscope failure

  • poor acoustic transmission

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as hypoactive bowel sounds, normal prolonged quiet interval, stethoscope failure, poor acoustic transmission.

False Negative Situations

Absent bowel sounds may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.

Modern Status

Current red-flag contextual finding, not a standalone diagnosis; it can accompany ileus or advanced obstruction and requires immediate correlation with the complete abdominal and systemic assessment. Interpret Absent bowel sounds in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Normal bowel activity includes quiet intervals, inadequate listening and environmental noise can create false absence, and absent sounds cannot distinguish ileus, obstruction, ischemia, medication effect, or peritonitis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Absent bowel sounds, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate before percussion or palpation; if no sound is initially heard, verify stethoscope function and a quiet environment, then listen continuously over the periumbilical region for the full prolonged interval required by the assessment method and local policy, with the cited Clinical Methods approach using three full minutes; document distention, pain, rigidity, nausea or vomiting, stool, flatus, surgery, medications, vital signs, perfusion, and mental status. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Absent bowel sounds with severe or worsening pain, progressive distention, persistent or feculent vomiting, inability to pass stool or gas, rigidity, rebound tenderness, fever, bloody stool, shock, or altered mental status requires urgent medical and surgical assessment; do not delay imaging or stabilization while repeatedly listening.
