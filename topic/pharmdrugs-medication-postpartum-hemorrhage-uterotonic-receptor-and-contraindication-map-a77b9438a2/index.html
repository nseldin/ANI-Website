Used To Treat

Postpartum hemorrhage caused by uterine atony, while a hemorrhage protocol simultaneously evaluates trauma, retained tissue, and coagulopathy. Oxytocin is commonly first-line; methylergonovine, carboprost, and misoprostol provide additional receptor pathways when atony persists and their contraindications permit use.

Description

Uterotonics treat one major cause of postpartum hemorrhage: an atonic uterus that remains soft instead of contracting around the open placental-bed vessels. Oxytocin activates myometrial oxytocin receptors; methylergonovine produces sustained uterine and vascular smooth-muscle contraction; carboprost is a prostaglandin F2-alpha analogue; and misoprostol is a prostaglandin E1 analogue. The drugs are not interchangeable checkboxes. Severe hypertension makes methylergonovine dangerous because it can intensify vasoconstriction, while asthma makes carboprost dangerous because prostaglandin signaling can constrict bronchi. A firm uterus with continued bleeding points away from simple atony and toward laceration, retained tissue, inversion, rupture, or coagulopathy.

Mechanism

After placental separation, uterine muscle fibers normally shorten and compress spiral arteries like living ligatures. Oxytocin receptors couple through Gq to phospholipase C, inositol trisphosphate, intracellular calcium release, calmodulin, and myosin-light-chain kinase, creating rhythmic contraction. Carboprost activates FP prostanoid receptors and the same calcium-contraction machinery, often producing stronger systemic smooth-muscle effects. Methylergonovine engages serotonergic and alpha-adrenergic pathways to produce a more sustained tetanic contraction and vasoconstriction. Misoprostol is converted to misoprostol acid, which activates EP prostanoid receptors, raises myometrial calcium signaling, and promotes cervical and uterine effects. None repairs a laceration or replaces blood, fibrinogen, tranexamic acid, source control, or massive-transfusion support when those are needed.

Boxed Warning

There is no single class boxed warning, but each option has a different immediate stop rule. Oxytocin can cause tachysystole, fetal hypoxia during labor, hypotension with rapid bolus, and water intoxication with prolonged high-dose electrolyte-free infusion. Methylergonovine can precipitate severe hypertension, stroke, coronary vasospasm, or myocardial ischemia and should not be used in hypertension or preeclampsia. Carboprost can cause bronchospasm and pulmonary compromise, so asthma or significant pulmonary disease changes selection. Misoprostol can cause uterine tachysystole or rupture in pregnancy, with risk rising after prior uterine surgery and later gestation.

Adverse Effects

  • Cramping, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fever, shivering, headache, and blood-pressure changes vary by agent

  • Tachysystole or rupture, severe hypertension or coronary vasospasm, bronchospasm, pulmonary edema, hyponatremia, and delayed recognition of non-atony bleeding

Contraindications

  • Match the drug to the patient: avoid methylergonovine in hypertension or preeclampsia and avoid carboprost in asthma or clinically important pulmonary disease.

  • Do not keep escalating uterotonics when the uterus is firm or bleeding remains disproportionate; reassess the hemorrhage cause and activate procedural, surgical, and transfusion pathways.

Nursing Essentials

  • Quantify cumulative blood loss, palpate uterine tone, assess vital signs and perfusion, secure access, obtain hemorrhage labs, and document response after each intervention.

  • Say the contraindication aloud before administration and maintain closed-loop communication. A correct drug given while the team misses trauma or coagulopathy is still incomplete care.

Interactions

  • Other oxytocics can produce additive tachysystole; route and spacing matter, especially around dinoprostone or oxytocin.

  • Vasoconstrictors and strong CYP3A inhibitors can magnify methylergonovine ischemia, while bronchoactive drugs and pulmonary disease modify carboprost risk.

Key Labs

  • CBC, platelets, fibrinogen, PT/INR, aPTT, type and crossmatch, blood gas/lactate when shock is present, urine output, and serial temperature

  • Uterine tone, quantified blood loss, shock index, mental status, skin perfusion, respiratory status, blood pressure, and response after every uterotonic

Nclex Traps

  • A boggy uterus supports atony; a firm uterus with continued brisk bleeding should trigger a search for trauma, retained tissue, inversion, rupture, or coagulation failure.

  • Methylergonovine equals hypertension danger; carboprost equals bronchospasm danger. Those pairings reflect mechanisms, not arbitrary mnemonics.
