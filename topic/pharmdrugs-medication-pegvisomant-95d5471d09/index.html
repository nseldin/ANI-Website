Class

Growth hormone receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Acromegaly when surgery, radiation, or other medical therapy has not adequately controlled disease or is not appropriate, with dosing guided by IGF-1 rather than growth-hormone concentration.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for pegvisomant in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

A modified GH molecule binds the growth-hormone receptor but prevents the conformational change and receptor signaling needed for JAK2/STAT activation. Hepatic IGF-1 production falls even though measured GH may remain elevated or become uninterpretable.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use IGF-1, symptoms, and protocol titration rather than GH level to assess response; rotate injection sites and monitor for lipohypertrophy.

  • Trend liver tests, glucose/diabetes medication needs, visual/headache symptoms, and pituitary imaging because the underlying adenoma remains present.

Key Labs

  • IGF-1, baseline and periodic liver tests, glucose/A1c, and pituitary MRI/visual-field follow-up as ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Pegvisomant blocks peripheral GH action; it does not suppress the pituitary tumor, and GH levels should not guide dosing.

Description

Pegvisomant is a pegylated growth-hormone receptor antagonist that blocks GH signaling in the liver and lowers IGF-1, the main mediator of acromegaly's tissue overgrowth and metabolic effects. It controls hormone action but does not shrink the pituitary tumor, so liver injury, glucose improvement, injection-site lipohypertrophy, and tumor surveillance matter.
