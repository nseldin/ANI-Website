Definition

Holster sign is an observed rash-distribution sign. It is a clue to dermatomyositis. A positive finding means violaceous or erythematous plaques occupy the outer thigh like a holster.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Holster sign means violaceous or erythematous plaques occupy the outer thigh like a holster.

Why It Matters

The observed Holster sign finding can signal dermatomyositis. Autoimmune interface dermatitis follows a characteristic extensor and friction distribution. Holster sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dermatomyositis

Assessment Method

Inspect lateral thighs and hips.

Positive Finding

Violaceous or erythematous plaques occupy the outer thigh like a holster.

Mechanism

Autoimmune interface dermatitis follows a characteristic extensor and friction distribution.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Holster sign finding raises concern for dermatomyositis because autoimmune interface dermatitis follows a characteristic extensor and friction distribution. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Holster sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Holster sign to focus evaluation of dermatomyositis and the reviewed alternatives: eczema, psoriasis, cutaneous lupus. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect lateral thighs and hips. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Holster sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Clothing pressure and other rashes mimic the pattern. Therefore, Holster sign cannot confirm or exclude dermatomyositis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • eczema

  • psoriasis

  • cutaneous lupus

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as eczema, psoriasis, cutaneous lupus.

False Negative Situations

Holster sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of dermatomyositis.
