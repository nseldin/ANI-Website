Definition

Pemberton sign is a provocative vascular-airway sign. It is a clue to thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass. A positive finding means facial plethora, cyanosis, neck-vein distention, stridor, or distress develops.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pemberton sign means facial plethora, cyanosis, neck-vein distention, stridor, or distress develops.

Why It Matters

The observed Pemberton sign finding can signal thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass. Arm elevation narrows the thoracic inlet and a retrosternal mass compresses venous return or airway. Pemberton sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass

Assessment Method

Ask a stable patient to raise both arms until they touch the sides of the head while observing face and breathing.

Positive Finding

Facial plethora, cyanosis, neck-vein distention, stridor, or distress develops.

Mechanism

Arm elevation narrows the thoracic inlet and a retrosternal mass compresses venous return or airway.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pemberton sign finding raises concern for thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass because arm elevation narrows the thoracic inlet and a retrosternal mass compresses venous return or airway. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pemberton sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pemberton sign to focus evaluation of thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass and the reviewed alternatives: thoracic outlet obstruction, heart failure, anxiety. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask a stable patient to raise both arms until they touch the sides of the head while observing face and breathing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pemberton sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A negative sign does not exclude intrathoracic extension. Therefore, Pemberton sign cannot confirm or exclude thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • thoracic outlet obstruction

  • heart failure

  • anxiety

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as thoracic outlet obstruction, heart failure, anxiety.

False Negative Situations

Pemberton sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass.

Modern Status

Current red-flag adjunct. When performed as described, Pemberton sign can adjust concern for thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Pemberton sign: A negative sign does not exclude intrathoracic extension.

Safety Precautions

Observe Pemberton sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask a stable patient to raise both arms until they touch the sides of the head while observing face and breathing. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Stop immediately for dyspnea, stridor, syncope, or cyanosis.

Nursing Relevance

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask a stable patient to raise both arms until they touch the sides of the head while observing face and breathing. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, thoracic-inlet compression by substernal goiter or mass, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Common Pitfalls
