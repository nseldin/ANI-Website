Class

Single-dose oral influenza PA cap-dependent endonuclease inhibitor

Used To Treat

Single-dose treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza A or B within two days of symptom onset and post-exposure prophylaxis in eligible patients at least five years old. It is not routinely recommended for hospitalized, severe, progressive, pregnant, breastfeeding, or severely immunocompromised patients because supporting data are limited.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not coadminister with dairy products, calcium-fortified beverages, antacids, laxatives, or supplements containing calcium, iron, magnesium, selenium, or zinc because chelation can lower drug exposure and efficacy. Monotherapy is not routinely recommended for hospitalized, severe, progressive, pregnant, breastfeeding, or severely immunocompromised patients because clinical data are insufficient and resistance may emerge. Serious hypersensitivity has occurred.

Mechanism

After hydrolysis of the prodrug, baloxavir acid binds the PA subunit of the influenza RNA-dependent RNA polymerase complex. The PA endonuclease requires divalent metal ions to cut capped 10- to 13-nucleotide fragments from host pre-mRNAs. Baloxavir coordinates those ions and prevents cleavage, so the PB2 cap-binding and PB1 polymerase subunits lack primers for viral mRNA transcription. Viral proteins and progeny production fall. The long terminal half-life supports single-dose treatment, but it also creates a prolonged selective window. PA substitutions, especially at I38, can reduce susceptibility and emerge during monotherapy, more often in some children or immunocompromised hosts with high or prolonged replication.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain current weight because the dose changes at the product threshold; give the complete single dose and separate it from polyvalent cations.

  • Teach that rapid viral-load reduction does not prove the patient is no longer contagious or prevent bacterial complications; continue infection control and reassess deterioration.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity to baloxavir or a formulation component.

  • Do not select only for single-dose convenience when pregnancy, severe disease, hospitalization, immune compromise, or inability to separate cations makes evidence or exposure unreliable.

Interactions

  • Polyvalent cations and dairy reduce absorption through chelation.

  • Separate from live attenuated intranasal influenza vaccine for the current recommended interval because the antiviral can inhibit vaccine-virus replication.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level; document weight, symptom onset, pregnancy/lactation, immune status, severity and interacting cation intake

  • Clinical response, hypersensitivity, secondary infection, prolonged viral shedding, and resistance when symptoms or testing persist

Nclex Traps

  • Baloxavir blocks cap-dependent endonuclease and viral transcription; it is not a neuraminidase inhibitor.

  • A single dose can fail if taken with polyvalent cations or if resistant PA variants emerge.

Description

Baloxavir marboxil is an oral prodrug converted to baloxavir acid, the first-in-class inhibitor of influenza PA cap-dependent endonuclease. Influenza cannot synthesize a conventional mRNA cap, so its polymerase steals capped fragments from host pre-mRNA and uses them as primers. Baloxavir chelates metal ions in the endonuclease active site and blocks this cap-snatching step, sharply reducing viral mRNA and protein synthesis after one weight-based dose. This target is upstream of neuraminidase and explains activity against some neuraminidase-resistant virus. It also explains the major administration interaction: calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc, selenium, antacids, and dairy can chelate baloxavir in the gut and reduce absorption.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, bronchitis, nausea, sinusitis, and headache, generally at rates similar to placebo

  • Hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis or angioedema, severe skin reaction, and PA I38 treatment-emergent resistance
