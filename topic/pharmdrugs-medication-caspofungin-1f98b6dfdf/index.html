Used To Treat

Candidemia and other invasive Candida infections, esophageal candidiasis, empirical therapy in selected febrile neutropenia, and salvage therapy for invasive aspergillosis when other agents fail or are not tolerated. It is not primary monotherapy for Aspergillus and does not treat Cryptococcus or Mucorales.

Mechanism

Fks1/Fks2 catalytic subunits synthesize glucan at the plasma membrane and extrude it into the wall. Caspofungin noncompetitively inhibits this complex, reducing glucan and triggering osmotic stress plus compensatory chitin production. FKS hot-spot mutations reduce binding and can cause class resistance, especially after echinocandin exposure. High protein binding and large molecular size explain poor urine, vitreous, and CSF concentrations; therefore an active candidemia drug can still be a poor choice for isolated Candida cystitis, endophthalmitis, or meningitis.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to caspofungin or components.

  • Do not use as sole therapy for cryptococcosis, mucormycosis, or a urinary/CNS Candida syndrome where pharmacokinetic exposure is inadequate.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify loading dose, daily dose, hepatic status, weight/age protocol, infusion time, and interacting inducers. Do not mix with dextrose-containing diluents when the product label prohibits it because instability can reduce delivered drug.

  • Monitor during infusion for histamine symptoms and anaphylaxis. Trend liver tests, serial cultures, species/susceptibility, catheter/source plan, and deep-focus symptoms.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine can increase caspofungin exposure/liver-test abnormalities; caspofungin can reduce tacrolimus concentrations, requiring level monitoring.

  • Rifampin, efavirenz, nevirapine, dexamethasone, phenytoin, and carbamazepine may reduce exposure and can require product-specific dose adjustment.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT/bilirubin, potassium, CBC, serial blood cultures, Candida species and susceptibility when indicated, tacrolimus level when coadministered, and infection-specific imaging.

  • No routine serum caspofungin level guides dosing; microbiologic clearance and source control are the response measures.

Nclex Traps

  • Caspofungin attacks glucan in the cell wall, not ergosterol in the membrane.

  • Renal failure does not require routine dose reduction; liver and interaction context matter more.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious anaphylaxis and histamine-mediated infusion reactions can occur. Hepatic injury and FKS-mediated breakthrough infection require monitoring; verify organism, site, and source control.

Class Pathway

  • Fks glucan-synthase inhibition

  • Fungal cell-wall weakening

  • Fungicidal Candida activity and hyphal-growth inhibition in Aspergillus

Description

Caspofungin is an IV echinocandin that inhibits the Fks-containing beta-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase complex, preventing production of a load-bearing fungal cell-wall polymer that human cells do not have. Candida walls become osmotically unstable and cells die; actively growing Aspergillus hyphal tips are damaged, but clinical activity is incomplete enough that caspofungin is a salvage or combination drug rather than standard primary Aspergillus monotherapy. A loading dose rapidly fills its distribution compartment before daily maintenance. It is not renally cleared to a clinically important degree and needs no renal or dialysis adjustment, but moderate hepatic impairment and enzyme-inducing co-medications can change dosing.

Adverse Effects

  • Fever, headache, phlebitis, nausea, diarrhea, rash, flushing, pruritus, facial swelling, bronchospasm, and elevated liver tests.

  • Anaphylaxis, serious skin reactions, clinically significant hepatic injury, hypokalemia, and resistant breakthrough Candida or Aspergillus infection.
