Class

Long-acting ester-prodrug ACE inhibitor with stable-coronary-disease indication

Used To Treat

Essential hypertension and stable coronary artery disease to reduce cardiovascular mortality or nonfatal myocardial infarction according to current U.S. labeling.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Perindopril carries the ACE-inhibitor fetal-toxicity warning. Stable coronary disease does not justify continued exposure during pregnancy.

Mechanism

After ester hydrolysis, perindoprilat competitively inhibits ACE. Lower angiotensin II reduces AT1-mediated arteriolar constriction, aldosterone, sodium retention, oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, hypertrophy, and fibrosis. Higher bradykinin increases nitric-oxide and prostacyclin signaling while creating cough and angioedema risk. Over time, lower vascular load and maladaptive signaling can reduce coronary-event risk in selected stable CAD patients, but it does not dissolve plaque or stop acute ischemia. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers glomerular pressure and can raise creatinine, while reduced aldosterone raises potassium. Perindoprilat is renally eliminated and is dialyzable, so kidney function shapes exposure and current label dosing.

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain that stable-CAD benefit is preventive and long term; chest pain still requires the patient's acute ischemia plan and urgent assessment when appropriate.

  • Verify renal function before dosing because the active metabolite is renally cleared and the U.S. label has specific restrictions.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, cough, facial or tongue swelling, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that persistent dry cough should be evaluated, while lip, tongue, throat, voice, or swallowing change is an emergency because bradykinin swelling can obstruct the airway.

Contraindications

  • Previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, hereditary or idiopathic angioedema, pregnancy, and relevant hypersensitivity.

  • Current labeling does not recommend use at creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min; do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or aliskiren in diabetes.

  • Pregnancy, previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, and hereditary or idiopathic angioedema where specified by labeling.

  • Do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or administer within 36 hours; do not combine aliskiren with an ACE inhibitor in diabetes.

Interactions

  • Diuretics can expose first-dose hypotension; potassium, NSAID, lithium, gold, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class.

  • Perindopril is removed during dialysis, while the procedure itself can alter pressure and volume; coordinate timing through the renal plan.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and duplicate RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • ACE inhibitor plus sacubitril/valsartan is contraindicated because overlapping peptide-degradation blockade increases angioedema risk.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR or creatinine clearance, volume, and coronary-disease clinical trajectory.

  • Continue lipid, glucose, antiplatelet, smoking, activity, and ischemia-risk monitoring because perindopril is one part of risk reduction.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, blood pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Perindopril's stable-CAD indication reduces future events; it is not a PRN antianginal.

  • The active metabolite is perindoprilat, and renal impairment has a drug-specific labeling consequence.

  • ACE inhibition lowers angiotensin II and raises bradykinin; those are separate consequences of inhibiting the same enzyme.

  • A modest early creatinine rise can reflect lower intraglomerular pressure, but oliguria, severe hyperkalemia, symptoms, or a large progressive decline is not routine adaptation.

Description

Perindopril is an oral ACE-inhibitor prodrug converted to active perindoprilat. It lowers angiotensin II and aldosterone, preserves bradykinin, relaxes arteries, and reduces remodeling. Its distinguishing U.S. indication is stable coronary artery disease: in addition to hypertension, it is labeled to reduce cardiovascular mortality or nonfatal myocardial infarction in that population while conventional antiplatelet, lipid, and risk-factor therapy continues. This is not an acute antianginal effect and the drug does not relieve chest pain on demand. Benefit comes from long-term vascular and neurohormonal modification. Perindoprilat elimination falls with renal impairment; current labeling does not recommend use when creatinine clearance is below 30 mL/min and uses lower exposure in lesser impairment. That agent-specific renal rule should not be replaced by a generic ACE-inhibitor sentence.

Adverse Effects

  • Cough, dizziness, hypotension, back pain, fatigue, potassium or creatinine increase, and GI symptoms can occur.

  • Renal accumulation, angioedema, neutropenia, hepatic injury, and fetal toxicity are the major severe concerns.

  • Class effects include dry cough, dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, creatinine increase, and less commonly rash or taste change.

  • Airway angioedema, severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, neutropenia in susceptible patients, hepatic injury, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
