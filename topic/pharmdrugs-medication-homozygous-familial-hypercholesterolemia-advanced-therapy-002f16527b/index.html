Used To Treat

A map for diagnosed homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, in which lifelong extreme LDL exposure can cause childhood xanthomas, aortic-root disease, and premature coronary disease and therefore requires specialist multi-pathway therapy.

Description

Homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) results from two pathogenic alleles affecting LDL-receptor function or related proteins such as APOB, PCSK9, or LDLRAP1. LDL-C is often extreme from birth, so cumulative arterial exposure begins decades before ordinary risk calculators. Maximal statin and ezetimibe therapy is still used when residual receptor function exists, but response may be limited because both depend largely on increasing LDL-receptor clearance. PCSK9 blockade also needs functioning receptors and works better in receptor-defective than receptor-negative disease. Lomitapide blocks microsomal triglyceride transfer protein, reducing hepatic VLDL assembly and therefore downstream LDL without requiring the receptor, but it causes hepatic fat, liver injury, GI effects, and vitamin deficiency and is restricted by REMS. Evinacumab blocks ANGPTL3 and lowers LDL through pathways that remain effective despite minimal LDL-receptor function. LDL apheresis physically removes ApoB particles and may still be needed every one to two weeks because the liver continues producing them.

Mechanism

The normal LDL receptor clears ApoB-100 particles. With little or no receptor activity, plasma residence time becomes extreme and receptor-upregulating drugs meet a biological ceiling. Lomitapide inhibits MTP, which normally transfers triglyceride to nascent ApoB in enterocytes and hepatocytes; without lipidation, ApoB is degraded and fewer chylomicrons or VLDL particles are secreted. This lowers LDL but deposits triglyceride in liver and impairs fat-soluble nutrient absorption. Evinacumab neutralizes ANGPTL3, releasing lipoprotein and endothelial lipases and promoting remnant processing through incompletely defined LDLR-independent routes. Apheresis directly filters LDL or ApoB from plasma, creating an immediate fall followed by rebound production. The mechanisms explain why combination therapy is required and why a normal-looking short-term LDL after apheresis does not mean the inherited production-clearance defect is cured.

Boxed Warning

Lomitapide carries a BOXED WARNING for hepatotoxicity: aminotransferase elevation and hepatic steatosis can progress toward steatohepatitis or cirrhosis, and access is restricted through JUXTAPID REMS. Advanced HoFH therapy belongs under a lipid specialist with liver, pregnancy, nutrition, infusion, and apheresis systems in place.

Adverse Effects

  • Untreated HoFH causes tendon or cutaneous xanthomas, corneal arcus, supravalvular or aortic-root stenosis, coronary disease, myocardial infarction, and death at unusually young ages.

  • Therapy can cause hepatic injury, fat-soluble-vitamin deficiency, GI intolerance, infusion reactions, hypersensitivity, venous-access burden, and rapid post-apheresis LDL rebound.

Contraindications

  • Do not diagnose HoFH from one LDL value alone; exclude secondary causes and integrate family history, physical findings, genetics, and treatment response.

  • Lomitapide is contraindicated in pregnancy and moderate or severe hepatic impairment and has extensive CYP3A restrictions. Evinacumab has product-specific hypersensitivity and pregnancy concerns.

Nursing Essentials

  • Coordinate genetic counseling, family cascade screening, xanthoma and vascular assessment, adherence, low-fat nutrition, vitamins, pregnancy prevention, liver monitoring, infusion care, and apheresis access.

  • Track pre- and post-apheresis LDL and the time-averaged exposure, not just the dramatic immediate drop. Missed sessions permit rapid particle rebound.

Interactions

  • Lomitapide is a major CYP3A interaction drug and raises simvastatin or lovastatin exposure; fat-soluble oral drugs and warfarin require close review.

  • Multiple lipid agents are often intentional because each addresses a different bottleneck. Do not stop background therapy when apheresis or evinacumab begins unless the specialist plan directs it.

Key Labs

  • Serial LDL-C, non-HDL-C, ApoB, liver enzymes, bilirubin, INR, hepatic imaging when directed, fat-soluble vitamins, essential fatty acids, pregnancy testing, and infusion or apheresis safety labs.

  • Because disease begins at birth, vascular imaging and valve or aortic assessment may be needed much earlier than in ordinary hypercholesterolemia.

Nclex Traps

  • HoFH is not simply a higher LDL version of routine dyslipidemia. Receptor biology changes which drugs can work.

  • Lomitapide lowers LDL independently of LDL receptors by blocking particle assembly; its liver fat is a direct consequence of trapping triglyceride inside hepatocytes.
