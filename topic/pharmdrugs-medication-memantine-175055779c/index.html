Class

NMDA receptor antagonist; cognitive symptom medication

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease dementia symptoms. It may be used alone or with a cholinesterase inhibitor when prescribed.

Mechanism

Blocks excessive NMDA receptor-mediated glutamate activity. This can reduce excitotoxic signaling and help cognition or function symptoms, but it does not reverse neurodegeneration or cure dementia.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess cognition, function, behavior, dizziness, confusion, headache, constipation, blood pressure, fall risk, renal function, and caregiver understanding.

  • Teach that benefit is gradual and symptom-focused. Report marked confusion, hallucinations, severe dizziness, fainting, constipation that becomes severe, or new urinary symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious hypersensitivity to memantine.

  • Use caution with severe renal impairment because dose adjustment may be needed.

Interactions

  • Other NMDA antagonists such as amantadine, ketamine, or dextromethorphan may increase CNS adverse effects.

  • Urine alkalinizing drugs or conditions can reduce memantine clearance and increase toxicity risk.

Key Labs

  • Renal function: creatinine and eGFR for dose safety, especially in older adults or kidney disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Memantine is not a cholinesterase inhibitor. It is an NMDA receptor antagonist.

  • It does not cure dementia. NCLEX focus is safety, function, caregiver teaching, fall prevention, medication adherence, and monitoring for CNS adverse effects.

Description

Memantine is an NMDA receptor antagonist and cognitive symptom medication: it blocks excessive NMDA receptor-mediated glutamate activity. It is used for moderate-to-severe Alzheimer disease dementia symptoms. It may be used alone or with a cholinesterase inhibitor when prescribed.
