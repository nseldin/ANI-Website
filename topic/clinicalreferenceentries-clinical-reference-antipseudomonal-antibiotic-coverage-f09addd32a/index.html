Summary

Antipseudomonal coverage means antibiotics with activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa when the isolate/site is susceptible; common anchors include cefepime, ceftazidime, piperacillin-tazobactam, selected carbapenems, aztreonam, ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin, and aminoglycosides.

Quick Answer

Common **Pseudomonas** coverage anchors: **cefepime**, **ceftazidime**, **piperacillin-tazobactam**, **meropenem/imipenem**, **aztreonam**, **ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin**, and **tobramycin/amikacin/gentamicin** when susceptible. Match culture, site, renal function, allergy, severity, and stewardship.

Sections

    • Beta-lactam anchors

    • Piperacillin-tazobactam; cefepime or ceftazidime; meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin; aztreonam in selected gram-negative/allergy contexts.

    • Non-beta-lactam anchors

    • Ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin when susceptible; aminoglycosides such as tobramycin, amikacin, or gentamicin with renal/ototoxicity monitoring.

    • Resistant-organism options

    • Ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam, cefiderocol, or polymyxins may appear in resistant cases depending culture, site, renal function, and local guidance.

    • NCLEX trap

    • Pseudomonas coverage is not automatic just because the drug is an antibiotic. Ceftriaxone is a common trap: it is not an antipseudomonal cephalosporin.
