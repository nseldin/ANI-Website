Used To Treat

Known or suspected long-acting anticoagulant rodenticide exposure causing delayed INR elevation or bleeding, including contaminated synthetic-cannabinoid outbreaks and intentional rodenticide ingestion.

Description

Superwarfarins such as brodifacoum inhibit vitamin K recycling like warfarin but persist far longer in the body, so coagulopathy can be delayed, severe, and recurrent for weeks to months. A patient can look well soon after exposure because previously synthesized clotting factors still circulate; bleeding appears as factors II, VII, IX, and X lose activity. Hematuria, bruising, epistaxis, gingival bleeding, heavy menstrual bleeding, abdominal or back pain, hematemesis, melena, neurologic change, and unexplained anemia are warning signs. A high INR without prescribed warfarin, especially after synthetic-cannabinoid use or rodenticide access, should trigger poison-center and public-health consideration. Active major bleeding requires immediate factor replacement under hemorrhage protocol plus vitamin K1, while vitamin K1 alone rebuilds clotting capacity over hours. Because brodifacoum remains after the INR normalizes, stopping vitamin K solely on one normal result can cause dangerous relapse.

Mechanism

Vitamin K epoxide reductase normally recycles reduced vitamin K, which gamma-carboxylates clotting factors so they can bind calcium and assemble on phospholipid surfaces. Brodifacoum and related long-acting antagonists block this cycle, gradually producing under-carboxylated, ineffective factors II, VII, IX, and X and anticoagulant proteins C and S. Phytonadione supplies vitamin K1 that can restore hepatic factor synthesis when sufficient alternative reduction is available, but synthesis takes time. Four-factor prothrombin-complex concentrate or plasma supplies functional factors immediately for major bleeding, yet those factors are temporary because the poison remains. Very high lipid solubility, tissue storage, enterohepatic cycling, and long terminal elimination explain why high-dose oral vitamin K may be needed for months and why serial quantitative brodifacoum testing, when available, can help specialists decide when therapy can safely stop.

Administration Timing

  • Call 911 for active major bleeding, severe headache, weakness, fainting, hematemesis, melena, abdominal or back pain, or pregnancy bleeding. Obtain CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, type and screen, and targeted imaging without delaying hemorrhage control.

  • For life-threatening bleeding, use the institutional anticoagulant-emergency pathway with rapid factor replacement and IV vitamin K1 under toxicology, hematology, and transfusion guidance. For stable coagulopathy, use specialist-selected oral vitamin K1 and monitor the INR response; doses in outbreak cases can be much larger and longer than ordinary dietary supplementation.

  • Arrange guaranteed medication access and frequent INR follow-up through and after therapy. Stop vitamin K only under a poison-center or hematology plan that accounts for serial INR off therapy and, when available, falling or cleared long-acting anticoagulant concentration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask specifically about K2, Spice, other synthetic cannabinoids, rodenticide products, occupational exposure, self-harm, prescribed anticoagulants, antibiotics, supplements, liver disease, and whether household or community contacts are bleeding.

  • Use bleeding precautions and avoid unnecessary invasive procedures and intramuscular injections because deficient vitamin-K-dependent factors make even minor tissue trauma a potential hemorrhage source. Inspect urine, stool, gums, skin, neurologic status, abdominal and back symptoms, and menstrual or postpartum bleeding, and trend hemodynamics and hemoglobin because occult intracranial, retroperitoneal, uterine, or gastrointestinal bleeding may appear before external blood loss.

  • Before discharge, confirm the exact vitamin K formulation, tablet burden, pharmacy supply, insurance or public-health assistance, dosing schedule, laboratory appointments, and a plan for missed doses because access failure can directly cause recurrent hemorrhage.

Key Labs

  • Trend serial PT/INR because it reflects loss and recovery of vitamin-K-dependent clotting activity; obtain CBC, fibrinogen, aPTT, comprehensive metabolic panel, liver studies, urinalysis, type and screen, and bleeding-site imaging because these identify blood loss, competing coagulopathy, organ dysfunction, and transfusion needs. Mixing studies and factor assays can distinguish factor deficiency from an inhibitor when the diagnosis is unclear.

  • Reference-laboratory anticoagulant panels and quantitative brodifacoum concentrations can confirm and follow exposure, but turnaround and cost limit emergency usefulness. A normal early INR does not exclude a delayed long-acting-anticoagulant effect.

Adverse Effects

  • Poisoning can cause intracranial, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, retroperitoneal, uterine, soft-tissue, or postoperative hemorrhage, shock, anemia, compartment syndromes, and death.

  • IV phytonadione can rarely cause serious hypersensitivity-like reactions; PCC raises thrombosis risk; plasma adds volume; and prolonged oral vitamin K creates adherence and access burdens. Rescue therefore pairs immediate hemostasis with careful longitudinal monitoring.

Contraindications

  • Do not use one normal early INR to clear a credible exposure, and do not stop vitamin K after one normal treated INR because the result may only show that replacement is working while brodifacoum persists.

  • Do not give routine invasive injections, aspirin, NSAIDs, or interacting anticoagulants during active coagulopathy unless a specialist has balanced the competing indication. Cholestyramine can reduce oral vitamin K absorption if timed together.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Any major bleeding, falling hemoglobin, hemodynamic instability, severe headache or neurologic finding, pregnancy bleeding, INR rebound, inability to obtain vitamin K, or cluster of similar patients requires hospital, hematology, toxicology, transfusion, and public-health escalation.

Interactions

  • Antibiotics, malnutrition, liver disease, warfarin, and other anticoagulants can deepen coagulopathy. Antiplatelet drugs and NSAIDs increase bleeding even when they do not further raise the INR.

  • Cholestyramine has been considered to interrupt enterohepatic cycling, but it can bind medicines and reduce oral vitamin K absorption; use only under a timed specialist protocol rather than adding it casually.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    Exploratory rodenticide ingestion often differs from sustained adult exposures, but exact product and amount still require immediate poison-center risk assessment.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Maternal and fetal bleeding risk requires urgent obstetric, toxicology, and hematology coordination; do not delay maternal hemostatic rescue.

  • Population

    Limited medication access

    Note

    Months of high tablet burden can be financially impossible, so pharmacy, public-health, and social-work coordination is a clinical safety intervention.

Evidence Limitations

  • Optimal long-term dose and stopping criteria are not defined by randomized trials. CDC outbreaks show prolonged high-dose vitamin K needs and relapse risk, while quantitative brodifacoum-guided discontinuation remains specialized and not universally available.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal early INR does not rule out delayed superwarfarin coagulopathy.

  • PCC replaces factors immediately, while vitamin K rebuilds them more slowly and must often continue long term.

  • A normal INR during vitamin K therapy does not prove that brodifacoum has cleared.

Regulatory Status

Phytonadione and factor-replacement products have labeled uses, but months-long high-dose treatment of superwarfarin poisoning is specialist-directed. Brodifacoum assays and cholestyramine strategies are not universal standards.

Boxed Warning

SUPERWARFARIN WARNING: call 911 for major bleeding, severe headache, fainting, vomiting blood, black stool, or pregnancy bleeding. Brodifacoum can cause delayed and months-long coagulopathy; replace clotting factors for life-threatening hemorrhage, give specialist-directed vitamin K1, and never stop follow-up after one normal INR.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning identifies the emergency, explains the mechanism of harm, and separates immediate stabilization from specialist antidote or elimination decisions.
