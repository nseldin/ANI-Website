Name

Generic

ethylmorphine

Class

Opioid analgesic

Used To Treat

Moderate to severe pain; selected opioids are also used for anesthesia, severe cough, opioid use disorder, or palliative dyspnea depending on the agent.

Boxed Warning

Class boxed warnings commonly include addiction, abuse/misuse, life-threatening respiratory depression, accidental ingestion, neonatal opioid withdrawal, and major danger with benzodiazepines/CNS depressants.

Mechanism

Ethylmorphine works by stimulating opioid receptors to alter pain perception and CNS response. The priority nursing mechanism-to-risk link is respiratory depression.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess respiratory rate, sedation, oxygenation, pain, bowel function, fall risk.

  • Hold/clarify for respiratory depression or excessive sedation.

  • Antidote: naloxone for clinically significant opioid toxicity.

Contraindications

  • Significant respiratory depression

  • acute/severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings

  • known paralytic ileus or GI obstruction for many opioids

Interactions

  • Benzodiazepines, alcohol, sedatives, muscle relaxants, gabapentinoids increase respiratory depression.

  • MAOIs/serotonergic drugs with some opioids can increase serotonin syndrome risk.

Key Labs

  • oxygen saturation/ABG if unstable

  • renal/hepatic function

  • bowel pattern

Nclex Traps

  • Pain control matters, but airway and breathing win. Excess sedation plus low RR is not 'sleeping it off.'

Description

Ethylmorphine is an opioid analgesic: it works by stimulating opioid receptors to alter pain perception and CNS response. It is used for moderate to severe pain; selected agents also treat opioid use disorder, severe cough, anesthesia needs, or palliative dyspnea.
