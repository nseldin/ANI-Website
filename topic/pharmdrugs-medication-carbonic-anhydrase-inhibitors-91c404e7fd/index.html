Used To Treat

Glaucoma, prevention or treatment of acute mountain sickness, selected edema or decongestion strategies, selected seizure therapy, and correction of selected chloride-depletion metabolic alkalosis. Carbonic anhydrase inhibition also reduces cerebrospinal-fluid production, although indication and formulation matter.

Description

Carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors are weak natriuretics but powerful acid-base drugs. Acetazolamide blocks carbonic anhydrase on the proximal tubular brush border and inside cells, interrupting the hydrogen-bicarbonate cycle required to reclaim filtered bicarbonate. Bicarbonate, sodium, potassium, and water are lost in urine; urine becomes alkaline while blood develops a hyperchloremic, non-anion-gap metabolic acidosis. The drug also reduces aqueous-humor and cerebrospinal-fluid formation and alters ventilation. At altitude, the induced metabolic acidosis stimulates ventilation and improves oxygenation, which is why a diuretic can prevent acute mountain sickness. The effect self-limits as body bicarbonate falls, leaving less filtered bicarbonate to waste. This explains why acetazolamide is not a substitute for a loop in severe salt-water overload and why its acid-base context must be known before use.

Mechanism

Filtered bicarbonate combines with secreted hydrogen in the proximal lumen. Brush-border carbonic anhydrase converts carbonic acid to carbon dioxide and water; carbon dioxide enters the cell, where cytosolic carbonic anhydrase recreates bicarbonate and hydrogen. Bicarbonate exits to blood, while hydrogen recycles through NHE3. Inhibition collapses this system, reducing NHE3-linked sodium uptake and bicarbonate reclamation. Downstream sodium delivery promotes potassium loss, while systemic bicarbonate depletion produces non-anion-gap acidosis. In the eye, reduced bicarbonate-dependent sodium and fluid transport lowers aqueous-humor production and intraocular pressure. In the choroid plexus it reduces CSF secretion. At altitude, lower bicarbonate offsets respiratory alkalosis and sustains a greater ventilatory response during wakefulness and sleep. In hepatic dysfunction, alkaline urine reduces conversion of diffusible ammonia to trapped ammonium, which can impair ammonia excretion and worsen encephalopathy.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe sulfonamide reactions, electrolyte depletion, marked acidosis, kidney injury, and hepatic encephalopathy are the high-alert risks. The urine alkalinization that helps some indications can also promote calcium-phosphate stones and reduce ammonium trapping.

Adverse Effects

  • Paresthesia, fatigue, taste change, polyuria, nausea, hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, alkaline urine, and calcium-phosphate nephrolithiasis follow the mechanism.

  • Severe sulfonamide reactions, cytopenias, hepatic injury, crystalluria, kidney failure, and encephalopathy in cirrhosis are uncommon but serious.

Contraindications

  • Marked renal or hepatic disease, cirrhosis, depressed sodium or potassium, hyperchloremic acidosis, adrenal insufficiency, and sulfonamide hypersensitivity are major label contraindication patterns.

  • Do not use altitude prophylaxis to continue ascent through severe symptoms. Ataxia, confusion, dyspnea at rest, or declining oxygenation requires descent and emergency management.

Nursing Essentials

  • Establish the indication and baseline bicarbonate, sodium, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume status, liver disease, and stone history. The desired bicarbonate change differs between altitude prevention, glaucoma, alkalosis, and decongestion.

  • Teach paresthesia as common but review severe weakness, confusion, reduced urine, rash, fever, flank pain, or breathing change urgently. Maintain hydration according to the indication rather than giving a generic high-fluid instruction.

Interactions

  • High-dose aspirin can cause severe toxicity with acetazolamide, including acidosis and CNS effects. Other carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors add acidosis and stone risk.

  • Urine alkalinization changes renal elimination of weak acids and bases; sodium bicarbonate increases stone risk; topiramate adds carbonic-anhydrase inhibition and acidosis.

Key Labs

  • Bicarbonate, chloride, sodium, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, CBC when prolonged, urine pH, and clinical indication endpoints such as intraocular pressure, altitude symptoms, or alkalosis correction.

  • A falling bicarbonate is expected pharmacology but not infinitely acceptable; severity, symptoms, respiratory reserve, kidney function, and treatment goal determine whether it is therapeutic or harmful.

Nclex Traps

  • Acetazolamide makes urine alkaline while making blood acidotic. Mixing those compartments reverses the answer.

  • It can help metabolic alkalosis because it wastes bicarbonate, but it can worsen cirrhotic encephalopathy because alkaline urine traps less ammonium.
