Used To Treat

LDL-C and cardiovascular-risk reduction in label-defined adults and pediatric familial hypercholesterolemia. Doses of 40-80 mg are generally moderate intensity; lower doses are low intensity.

Mechanism

Pravastatin is administered in its active hydroxy-acid form and competitively inhibits HMG-CoA reductase after carrier-mediated hepatic uptake. Falling hepatocyte cholesterol activates SREBP2 and LDL-receptor transcription. Unlike simvastatin or lovastatin, it does not require lactone hydrolysis and is not substantially cleared by CYP3A4. Non-CYP isomerization, conjugation, biliary excretion, and renal elimination handle the drug. Cyclosporine can still produce a large exposure increase through transporter effects, while clarithromycin can increase concentration by non-CYP mechanisms. The shorter elimination profile supports evening use. In severe renal impairment, reduced clearance increases systemic exposure and muscle risk, explaining the conservative starting dose.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Pravastatin can still cause myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, IMNM, and serious liver injury. Review renal function and transporter interactions; 'low CYP' must never be translated as interaction free.

Contraindications

  • Active liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis and serious hypersensitivity are major class barriers; use in stable chronic liver disease is individualized rather than automatically prohibited.

  • Most pregnant patients discontinue because therapy is preventive and fetal cholesterol biology matters. Current labeling permits rare individualized high-risk decisions; breastfeeding is generally not recommended during treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline lipid panel, exact product and dose, expected intensity, liver and thyroid context, kidney function when relevant, pregnancy plans, muscle history, alcohol, and all interacting medicines or supplements.

  • Teach urgent review for severe weakness, dark urine, oliguria, jaundice, or systemic illness. Mild symptoms still deserve assessment, but one episode does not prove permanent intolerance to every statin.

  • Start at 10 mg in significant renal impairment under current labeling and titrate from measured response rather than assuming the usual adult start.

  • Use pravastatin's lower CYP interaction burden strategically, but continue medication reconciliation for cyclosporine, macrolides, fibrates, and renal decline.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine markedly raises exposure and requires a low maximum. Clarithromycin and other named drugs have product-specific dose limits.

  • Gemfibrozil is avoided; other fibrates, niacin at lipid doses, colchicine, and myotoxic drugs add muscle risk. Bile-acid sequestrants require administration spacing.

Key Labs

  • Repeat lipids after initiation or dose change to verify the expected percentage LDL reduction. Obtain baseline hepatic assessment; repeat liver tests for symptoms or clinical concern rather than routine asymptomatic monthly screening.

  • Use CK, creatinine, potassium, phosphate, urinalysis, and urine output for significant muscle symptoms or dark urine. Check TSH or other reversible contributors when the presentation suggests them.

Nclex Traps

  • Statins lower LDL mainly by increasing hepatic LDL-receptor clearance after intracellular cholesterol falls; they do not simply dissolve plaque or block intestinal fat.

  • Intensity is defined by expected percentage LDL reduction, not by comparing milligram numbers across drugs.

  • Pravastatin is not high intensity even at its maximum labeled dose. Add or switch therapy when a 50-percent LDL reduction is required.

  • Hydrophilic and low CYP are comparative descriptors, not immunity from myopathy.

  • Active hydroxy-acid statin

  • Carrier-mediated hepatic uptake with little CYP metabolism

  • Renal and biliary elimination

Description

Pravastatin, branded Pravachol, is an active, relatively hydrophilic HMG-CoA-reductase inhibitor that lowers hepatocyte cholesterol, increases LDL-receptor clearance, and produces low- to moderate-intensity LDL reduction while undergoing little cytochrome P450 metabolism. Its low CYP3A dependence makes it useful when CYP3A interactions limit simvastatin, lovastatin, or atorvastatin, but transporters, cyclosporine, clarithromycin, and kidney function still matter. Significant renal impairment requires a 10-mg starting dose under current labeling because renal clearance contributes to exposure. Pravastatin is commonly taken in the evening to align its shorter activity with nighttime synthesis, although the exact prescribed schedule should be followed. Lower interaction potential is a relative advantage, not proof that muscle injury is impossible.

Adverse Effects

  • Muscle symptoms can range from aches with normal CK to myopathy and rare rhabdomyolysis with hyperkalemia and myoglobin-mediated acute kidney injury. Risk rises with greater exposure, interacting drugs, CKD, hypothyroidism, frailty, and acute illness.

  • Mild aminotransferase and glucose or A1c increases can occur. Serious liver failure is rare, and the small diabetes signal usually does not outweigh prevention benefit when the indication is strong.
