Risk Factors

  • Motion, agitation, severe low flow, or poor contrast bolus can create misleading perfusion maps.

  • Implanted devices, metal, claustrophobia, or instability may limit MRI even when DWI would be informative.

  • Renal dysfunction and contrast allergy influence planning, but emergency benefit and risk are individualized because delaying vessel imaging can also cause harm.

Signs Symptoms

  • A focal disabling syndrome with normal NCCT still may be ischemic stroke because early tissue change can be invisible.

  • Cortical signs or severe posterior findings raise the need for vessel imaging because LVO may require thrombectomy.

  • Clinical-imaging mismatch should trigger reassessment for timing, mimic, distal occlusion, seizure, technical artifact, or an alternate lesion.

Diagnostics

  • NCCT: identify hemorrhage, mass effect, and early ischemic change before reperfusion decisions.

  • CTA: identify vessel occlusion, tandem disease, dissection, aneurysm, and collateral anatomy.

  • CTP or MR perfusion: estimate tissue flow; DWI and ADC: confirm acute restricted diffusion; DWI-FLAIR comparison: support selected unknown-onset pathways.

Labs

  • Bedside glucose accompanies imaging because mimic treatment should not wait.

  • Renal function and pregnancy context are considered for contrast and radiation, but suspected LVO CTA/CTP should not wait for a creatinine result when the current emergency pathway permits immediate imaging because treatment delay can cause irreversible brain injury.

  • Coagulation and platelet data affect treatment safety, not whether NCCT can identify hemorrhage.

Treatments

  • Choose the minimum imaging needed to answer the immediate treatment question because unnecessary sequencing delays reperfusion.

  • Proceed with standard-window IVT without waiting for advanced mismatch imaging when otherwise eligible because the guideline prioritizes speed.

  • Use advanced tissue and vascular imaging for extended-window or uncertain-onset selection because clock time alone cannot show remaining penumbra.

Nursing Priorities

  • Verify LKW, contrast history, renal context, pregnancy possibility when relevant, implants, IV access, and ability to lie safely without delaying the emergency pathway.

  • Monitor airway and neurologic status during transport and scanning because deterioration can occur away from the bedside.

  • Report the exact study completed and result - hemorrhage, occlusion site, core/mismatch - because saying 'CT negative' hides the question each scan answered.

Complications

  • Imaging delay can allow core expansion and loss of treatment benefit.

  • Contrast reaction, extravasation, renal injury, motion artifact, and incidental findings can complicate evaluation.

  • False reassurance from a normal NCCT can delay recognition of early ischemia, posterior infarct, or LVO.

Contraindications

  • Do not call NCCT a test that rules out ischemic stroke.

  • Do not confuse CTA with CTP or DWI with ADC.

  • Do not delay clear standard-window IVT eligibility for advanced imaging that the pathway does not require.

Red Flags

  • LVO syndrome with no CTA yet obtained

  • Clinical deterioration during imaging

  • New hemorrhage, mass effect, basilar occlusion, or large-core/edema finding

Patient Education

  • Explain that the first CT mainly checks for bleeding, while other scans look at arteries and threatened tissue.

  • Clarify that a 'normal CT' does not always mean no stroke.

  • Explain contrast and MRI screening briefly while emphasizing that rapid imaging protects treatment options.

Nclex Traps

  • NCCT answers bleeding first; CTA answers vessel; perfusion and diffusion estimate tissue injury.

  • DWI bright plus low ADC supports true restricted diffusion.

  • Advanced imaging is valuable, but avoidable delay can cost viable tissue.

Name

Acute stroke imaging: noncontrast CT, CTA, CTP, DWI, and ADC

Category

Acute stroke diagnostic imaging

Definition

Acute stroke imaging is a diagnostic sequence that separates hemorrhage from ischemia, identifies blocked or abnormal arteries, and estimates how much brain tissue is already infarcted versus potentially salvageable. Noncontrast CT rapidly looks for hemorrhage and major established injury. CT angiography maps arteries and identifies an occlusion, stenosis, dissection, or aneurysm. CT perfusion estimates delayed flow and core-penumbra mismatch. MRI diffusion-weighted imaging detects restricted water movement from acute ischemic injury, while the apparent diffusion coefficient map helps confirm true restriction rather than T2 shine-through. No single study answers hemorrhage, vessel, tissue, and cause questions equally well.

Etiology

Imaging visualizes the consequences and mechanisms of ischemia, hemorrhage, vascular obstruction, and collateral flow, while the pattern helps narrow rather than single-handedly prove the cause. Modality choice depends on stability, onset, local speed, implanted devices, motion, renal and contrast context, transfer plan, and whether standard- or extended-window selection is needed.

Pathology

Hemorrhage appears hyperdense on NCCT early, while acute ischemia may initially be subtle because cytotoxic edema takes time to change x-ray attenuation. CTA fills arteries with iodinated contrast, exposing a missing column at the occlusion. CTP models contrast passage through tissue. DWI becomes bright when water diffusion is restricted, and low ADC supports cytotoxic edema; FLAIR changes often develop later and can help estimate mismatch in unknown-onset stroke.

Pathophysiology

NCCT is fast and widely available, making it the first safety split before thrombolysis, but a normal scan does not exclude early ischemia or LVO. CTA answers 'where is the vessel blocked?' and should not be confused with CTP, which answers 'how is tissue being perfused?' DWI is highly sensitive for small and posterior infarcts, while ADC confirms restriction. CTP and DWI-perfusion thresholds are mathematical estimates vulnerable to motion, poor bolus, low cardiac output, prior infarct, seizure, and software differences. The 2026 guideline supports no advanced-imaging delay for otherwise eligible disabling stroke within 4.5 hours and uses mismatch imaging for selected unknown-onset, 4.5-to-9-hour, or extended EVT pathways.
