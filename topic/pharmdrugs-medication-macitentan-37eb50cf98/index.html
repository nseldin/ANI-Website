Class

Dual endothelin ETA/ETB receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (WHO group 1) to reduce disease progression and hospitalization, often as part of combination PAH therapy.

Boxed Warning

Embryo-fetal toxicity: macitentan is contraindicated during pregnancy. Exclude pregnancy before treatment, use effective contraception during therapy and for 1 month after stopping, and discontinue promptly if pregnancy occurs. Doses above 10 mg daily have not been studied in PAH and are not recommended; that is an evidence boundary, not a reason to escalate when response is inadequate.

Mechanism

Macitentan and its active metabolite prevent endothelin-1 from activating ETA and ETB receptors. On pulmonary smooth muscle, these receptors drive Gq-phospholipase-C-IP3-calcium contraction and proliferative programs involving hypertrophy, fibrosis, and inflammation. Blocking sustained receptor occupancy lowers pulmonary vascular resistance and right-ventricular afterload over time. Because endothelial ETB also clears endothelin and releases vasodilators, dual blockade is a balanced system effect rather than simple removal of one harmful receptor.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm pregnancy status and contraception, baseline hemoglobin, edema/weight, blood pressure, liver tests, oxygenation, left- and right-heart status, and every CYP3A4/CYP2C9 drug before the first 10-mg daily dose.

  • Repeat hemoglobin as clinically indicated because decline begins early and then often stabilizes. Obtain baseline liver tests and repeat for symptoms or clinical concern rather than incorrectly applying bosentan's automatic monthly schedule.

  • Evaluate new fluid retention for drug effect, left-heart dysfunction, or worsening PAH. Acute pulmonary edema requires PVOD consideration and possible discontinuation.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy and serious hypersensitivity to macitentan or a component.

  • Do not exceed 10 mg daily; evaluate significant liver injury, severe anemia, or clinically important fluid retention before continuing.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers because they can reduce exposure and efficacy, and avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors because they increase exposure.

  • Avoid moderate dual CYP3A4/CYP2C9 inhibitors such as fluconazole or amiodarone, and avoid combined moderate inhibition of both pathways, because modeling predicts a large exposure increase.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing before treatment; baseline and clinically indicated ALT/AST/bilirubin; hemoglobin/hematocrit; and PAH functional, oxygenation, BNP/NT-proBNP, edema, and right-heart trends.

Nclex Traps

  • Macitentan is dual ETA/ETB blockade with an active metabolite; it is not simply long-acting ambrisentan.

  • Bosentan requires monthly liver tests; macitentan uses baseline and clinically indicated liver testing under current labeling.

  • More than 10 mg is unstudied and not recommended, so inadequate response calls for PAH strategy reassessment rather than dose escalation.

Class Pathway

PAH therapy > Endothelin receptor antagonist > Tissue-penetrating dual ETA and ETB blockade

Description

Macitentan is a once-daily, tissue-penetrating dual ETA/ETB endothelin-receptor antagonist that blocks endothelin-1-driven pulmonary vasoconstriction and vascular remodeling; its active metabolite sustains receptor blockade in adult WHO Group 1 PAH. This lowers pulmonary vascular resistance over time and reduces disease progression and hospitalization, but it does not provide acute rescue vasodilation. Its major teaching points are boxed embryo-fetal toxicity, early hemoglobin decline, fluid retention that can resemble right-heart failure, liver injury surveillance, PVOD-associated pulmonary edema, and exposure changes from CYP3A4/CYP2C9 interactions.

Adverse Effects

  • Anemia or hemoglobin decrease, nasopharyngitis, bronchitis, headache, influenza, urinary infection, and edema or fluid retention can occur.

  • Serious concerns include hepatotoxicity, decompensated heart failure from fluid retention, hypersensitivity, symptomatic hypotension, and pulmonary edema suggesting PVOD.
