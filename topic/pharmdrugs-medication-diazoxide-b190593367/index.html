Class

Pancreatic beta-cell KATP channel opener and insulin-release inhibitor

Used To Treat

Persistent hypoglycemia caused by confirmed hyperinsulinism, including congenital hyperinsulinism and selected inoperable insulin-secreting tumors when definitive therapy is unavailable or incomplete.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for diazoxide in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Diazoxide opens beta-cell KATP channels, allowing potassium efflux and preventing membrane depolarization. Voltage-gated calcium channels remain closed, intracellular calcium stays low, and insulin granules are not released. Vascular KATP opening also explains vasodilation and reflex effects.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm true hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia; trend bedside glucose closely during initiation/titration and watch for hyperglycemia or ketoacidosis.

  • Monitor daily weight, edema, urine output, respiratory status, heart failure, uric acid/gout, CBC, and echocardiographic/pulmonary-hypertension cues in infants.

Key Labs

  • Frequent glucose, insulin/C-peptide diagnostic context, electrolytes, renal function, uric acid, CBC, and cardiopulmonary assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Diazoxide opens KATP channels and inhibits insulin; sulfonylureas close KATP channels and stimulate insulin.

Description

Oral diazoxide raises glucose by shutting down insulin release, which is the opposite of a sulfonylurea. It opens ATP-sensitive potassium channels in pancreatic beta cells, keeping the membrane too hyperpolarized to trigger calcium-dependent insulin exocytosis; fluid retention, heart failure, hyperuricemia, and pulmonary hypertension in infants are major safety limits.
