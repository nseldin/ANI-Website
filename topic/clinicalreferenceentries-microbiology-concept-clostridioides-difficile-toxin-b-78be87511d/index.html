    Clostridioides difficile toxin B, abbreviated TcdB, is a major glucosylating cytotoxin produced by toxigenic C. difficile strains and is a central mediator of epithelial damage in C. difficile infection.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    TcdB enters susceptible colonic cells and inactivates Rho-family regulatory proteins by glucosylation. Loss of normal cytoskeletal control causes cell rounding, barrier disruption, cell injury, and inflammatory signaling. That epithelial failure helps explain the transition from colonization to toxin-mediated diarrhea and colitis.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    TcdB is distinct from TcdA. Clinically important TcdA-negative and TcdB-positive strains demonstrate why the two toxins must not be collapsed into one identity card or assumed to be present together in every isolate.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    TcdB helps drive mucosal injury and severe CDI. Diagnostic assays may detect free toxin, toxin genes, or other organism markers; each has different implications, so the result must be integrated with symptoms and the testing algorithm.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    A positive molecular toxin-gene result can occur in colonization and does not by itself prove active toxin-mediated disease. Conversely, toxin assays can be less sensitive. This mechanism card explains the toxin but does not replace clinical diagnosis or the dedicated C. difficile toxin-test card.
