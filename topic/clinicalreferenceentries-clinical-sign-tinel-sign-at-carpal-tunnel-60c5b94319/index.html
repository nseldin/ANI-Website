Definition

Tinel sign at carpal tunnel is a bedside nerve-percussion sign. It is a clue to median nerve irritability or regeneration. A positive finding means tingling radiates into median-innervated digits.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tinel sign at carpal tunnel means tingling radiates into median-innervated digits.

Why It Matters

A positive Tinel sign at carpal tunnel response can signal median nerve irritability or regeneration. Mechanical stimulation depolarizes an irritated or regenerating nerve. Tinel sign at carpal tunnel cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • median nerve irritability or regeneration

Assessment Method

Tap gently over the volar carpal tunnel.

Positive Finding

Tingling radiates into median-innervated digits.

Mechanism

Mechanical stimulation depolarizes an irritated or regenerating nerve.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Tinel sign at carpal tunnel response raises concern for median nerve irritability or regeneration because mechanical stimulation depolarizes an irritated or regenerating nerve. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tinel sign at carpal tunnel does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

A negative result is common and nonspecific local tingling is not positive. Therefore, Tinel sign at carpal tunnel cannot confirm or exclude median nerve irritability or regeneration by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from neuroma, polyneuropathy, anxiety, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Tinel sign at carpal tunnel may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
