Definition

Schistocytes is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to mechanical red-cell fragmentation. A positive finding means triangular, helmet-shaped, or irregular red-cell fragments are increased.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Schistocytes means triangular, helmet-shaped, or irregular red-cell fragments are increased.

Why It Matters

The Schistocytes laboratory finding can signal mechanical red-cell fragmentation. Fibrin strands, damaged microvessels, or prosthetic surfaces shear erythrocytes. Schistocytes cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • mechanical red-cell fragmentation

Assessment Method

Review a properly prepared peripheral smear using standardized morphology criteria.

Positive Finding

Triangular, helmet-shaped, or irregular red-cell fragments are increased.

Mechanism

Fibrin strands, damaged microvessels, or prosthetic surfaces shear erythrocytes.

Clinical Interpretation

The Schistocytes laboratory finding raises concern for mechanical red-cell fragmentation because fibrin strands, damaged microvessels, or prosthetic surfaces shear erythrocytes. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Schistocytes does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Schistocytes to focus evaluation of mechanical red-cell fragmentation and the reviewed alternatives: severe burns, malignant hypertension, smear artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review a properly prepared peripheral smear using standardized morphology criteria. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Schistocytes as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Small numbers and smear artifact occur; quantify by laboratory standard rather than eyeballing. Therefore, Schistocytes cannot confirm or exclude mechanical red-cell fragmentation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • severe burns

  • malignant hypertension

  • smear artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with severe burns, malignant hypertension, smear artifact.

False Negative Situations

Schistocytes may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
