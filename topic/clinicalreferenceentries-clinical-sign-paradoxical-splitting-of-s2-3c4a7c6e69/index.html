Definition

Paradoxical splitting of S2 is an auscultatory cardiac sign. It is a clue to delayed aortic valve closure. A positive finding means the split appears during expiration and narrows or disappears with inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Paradoxical splitting of S2 means the split appears during expiration and narrows or disappears with inspiration.

Why It Matters

A positive Paradoxical splitting of S2 response can signal delayed aortic valve closure. Markedly delayed left-ventricular ejection causes A2 to follow P2; inspiratory P2 delay moves the sounds together. Paradoxical splitting of S2 cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • delayed aortic valve closure

Assessment Method

Listen to S2 through respiration.

Positive Finding

The split appears during expiration and narrows or disappears with inspiration.

Mechanism

Markedly delayed left-ventricular ejection causes A2 to follow P2; inspiratory P2 delay moves the sounds together.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Paradoxical splitting of S2 response raises concern for delayed aortic valve closure because markedly delayed left-ventricular ejection causes A2 to follow P2; inspiratory P2 delay moves the sounds together. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Paradoxical splitting of S2 does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Paradoxical splitting of S2 to focus evaluation of delayed aortic valve closure and the reviewed alternatives: single S2, wide split, paced rhythm. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Listen to S2 through respiration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Paradoxical splitting of S2 as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sound identification is difficult and multiple lesions can coexist. Therefore, Paradoxical splitting of S2 cannot confirm or exclude delayed aortic valve closure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • single S2

  • wide split

  • paced rhythm

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from single S2, wide split, paced rhythm, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Paradoxical splitting of S2 may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
