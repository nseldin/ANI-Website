Class

Sulfone dihydropteroate-synthase inhibitor with antimicrobial and neutrophil-modulating effects

Used To Treat

Leprosy as part of multidrug therapy, dermatitis herpetiformis, and guideline-supported alternative prophylaxis or treatment combinations for Pneumocystis and toxoplasmosis. Oral systemic dapsone and topical acne dapsone have different exposure and indications.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Potentially fatal agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, severe hemolysis, methemoglobinemia, hepatitis, DRESS, and severe cutaneous reactions can occur. Check G6PD before systemic use and obtain baseline/serial CBC and liver tests. G6PD-normal patients can still develop methemoglobinemia. Cyanosis, dyspnea, headache, fatigue, tachycardia, or a pulse-oximetry saturation near 85% that does not correct as expected should prompt co-oximetry.

Mechanism

Dihydropteroate synthase normally condenses para-aminobenzoic acid with a pteridine precursor during microbial folate synthesis. Dapsone competitively occupies the PABA site, reducing dihydrofolate and downstream nucleotide production. Combining it with trimethoprim or pyrimethamine blocks a second folate step and improves activity for selected opportunistic infections. In inflammatory disease, dapsone inhibits neutrophil myeloperoxidase, reduces hypochlorous-acid formation, and dampens neutrophil adhesion or chemotaxis. CYP-mediated N-hydroxylation produces reactive hydroxylamine metabolites. Red cells use NADPH and reduced glutathione to reverse oxidant injury; when capacity is exceeded, hemoglobin iron becomes ferric methemoglobin and denatured hemoglobin plus membrane damage cause hemolysis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain G6PD, CBC, reticulocytes, bilirubin, liver tests, and baseline oxygenation. Teach fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers, rash, jaundice, dark urine, pallor, cyanosis, or neuropathy as urgent signals.

  • Distinguish methemoglobinemia from ordinary hypoxemia: PaO2 may be normal because dissolved oxygen is present while hemoglobin cannot carry it effectively.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity; avoid or use an alternative in significant G6PD deficiency or severe anemia according to indication and guideline.

  • Do not use dapsone alone for toxoplasmosis protection and do not assume normal pulse oximetry excludes tissue hypoxia.

Interactions

  • Trimethoprim can raise dapsone and hydroxylamine exposure while adding folate blockade; other oxidants increase hemolysis/methemoglobin risk.

  • Rifampin lowers dapsone concentrations; probenecid may reduce elimination; antimalarials and marrow suppressants can add hematologic toxicity.

Key Labs

  • G6PD, CBC with differential, reticulocyte count, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin, liver and renal function, and methemoglobin by co-oximetry when symptomatic

  • Oxygenation and saturation gap, rash/systemic symptoms, neuropathy, infection prophylaxis efficacy, adherence to complete multidrug regimens, and disease response

Nclex Traps

  • Normal G6PD lowers but does not eliminate methemoglobinemia risk.

  • A low pulse-oximetry reading with normal PaO2 suggests dyshemoglobinemia because PaO2 measures dissolved oxygen, not hemoglobin function.

Description

Dapsone is a sulfone antimicrobial that mimics para-aminobenzoic acid and inhibits microbial dihydropteroate synthase, reducing folate synthesis in susceptible Mycobacterium leprae, Pneumocystis, Toxoplasma combination regimens, and other organisms. It also suppresses neutrophil myeloperoxidase and oxidant-mediated tissue injury, which contributes to benefit in dermatitis herpetiformis and neutrophilic dermatoses. Hepatic N-hydroxylation creates dapsone hydroxylamine, an oxidant metabolite that converts ferrous hemoglobin to methemoglobin and damages red-cell membranes. G6PD deficiency weakens glutathione protection and magnifies hemolysis, but methemoglobinemia can occur even with normal G6PD.

Adverse Effects

  • Dose-related hemolysis, methemoglobinemia, nausea, anorexia, headache, insomnia, and peripheral neuropathy

  • Agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, DRESS/dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome, hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, severe skin reaction, and renal injury
