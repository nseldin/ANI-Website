Definition

Shigella dysenteriae is a clinically important bacterium associated with shigellosis, bacillary dysentery, and hemolytic uremic syndrome. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Shigella dysenteriae is a Gram-negative bacillus with a facultative anaerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and facultative intracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Meticulous hand hygiene, sanitation, exclusion guidance for high-risk settings, and public-health follow-up limit fecal-oral spread. The reviewed route description is fecal oral transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 1 to 2 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    A very low infectious dose, colonic epithelial invasion, cell-to-cell spread, and Shiga toxin in toxigenic strains drive inflammatory dysentery and systemic complications. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Shigella dysenteriae is associated with shigellosis, bacillary dysentery, and hemolytic uremic syndrome. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
