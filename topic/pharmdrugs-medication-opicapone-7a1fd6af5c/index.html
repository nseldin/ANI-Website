Used To Treat

Adjunct to carbidopa/levodopa in Parkinson disease when end-of-dose wearing-off or 'off' episodes persist.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for opicapone in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Opicapone selectively and reversibly inhibits peripheral COMT, reducing conversion of levodopa to 3-O-methyldopa and extending levodopa's plasma availability and motor benefit. It does not replace carbidopa and has no meaningful direct dopamine-receptor agonism.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give at bedtime with the required food-free interval; track on/off time and expect that levodopa reduction may be needed for dyskinesia.

  • Monitor sleep attacks, orthostasis/syncope, hallucinations/psychosis, impulse-control behavior, hyperpyrexia/confusion with abrupt dopaminergic reduction, and interactions with catecholamine drugs.

Key Labs

  • Clinical motor diary, BP/orthostasis, liver function for dosing context, and CK when severe dyskinesia/rigidity suggests muscle injury.

Nclex Traps

  • COMT inhibition extends levodopa; worsening dyskinesia often means dopaminergic exposure is now too high, not that Parkinson disease suddenly progressed.

Description

Opicapone is a once-daily peripheral COMT inhibitor that prolongs levodopa exposure. Once carbidopa blocks peripheral decarboxylation, COMT becomes a major competing pathway; inhibiting it sends more levodopa toward the brain but can also amplify dyskinesia, hallucinations, orthostasis, sleep attacks, and impulse-control problems.
