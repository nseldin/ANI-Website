Plain Meaning

A protective vascular and immune response to injury, infection, irritants, ischemia, or immune activation.

Word Parts

  • Root

    inflammare

    Meaning

    Latin root meaning to set on fire

  • Root

    -ation

    Meaning

    process or action

Word Origin

The word comes from the old visible pattern of heat, redness, swelling, and pain. Modern pathology explains those signs through vascular leak, immune-cell traffic, and mediator release.

Pathology

Inflammation begins when injured cells, microbes, or immune triggers release signals that dilate vessels, increase vascular permeability, and recruit leukocytes. Acute inflammation is fast and often neutrophil-predominant. Chronic inflammation lasts longer, often involves macrophages and lymphocytes, and can cause tissue destruction, fibrosis, granulomas, or autoimmune injury.

Etiology

Triggers include infection, trauma, burns, ischemia-reperfusion injury, crystals, toxins, allergens, autoimmune disease, necrotic tissue, foreign bodies, and chronic metabolic injury.

Signs Symptoms

  • Local redness, heat, swelling, pain, and loss of function

  • Systemic fever, malaise, tachycardia, leukocytosis, elevated CRP or ESR when inflammatory burden is significant

  • Chronic inflammation may present as fatigue, anemia of inflammation, weight loss, fibrosis, or organ dysfunction

Diagnostics

  • Assessment separates infection from sterile inflammation because fever and high WBC can occur in both

  • CBC differential, CRP, ESR, cultures, imaging, urinalysis, or tissue sampling may be used depending on suspected source

  • Trend organ function because inflammation can injure tissue even when the original trigger is not bacterial

Treatments

  • Remove or treat the trigger: antimicrobial therapy for infection, source control, stopping irritants, treating autoimmune disease, or repairing injury

  • Anti-inflammatory therapy may include NSAIDs, corticosteroids, immunomodulators, biologics, or local supportive care depending on cause

  • Support perfusion, oxygenation, pain control, temperature, fluids, nutrition, and tissue protection

Nursing Priorities

  • Do not treat the word inflammation as automatically infectious

  • Assess for sepsis cues when inflammation is systemic: altered mental status, hypotension, tachypnea, rising lactate, oliguria, or mottling

  • Connect chronic inflammation with fibrosis, anemia, cachexia, and impaired healing

Complications

  • Abscess

  • Sepsis when infectious

  • Fibrosis

  • Chronic pain

  • Autoimmune tissue damage

  • Organ dysfunction

Patient Education

  • Report worsening fever, spreading redness, severe pain, shortness of breath, confusion, low urine output, or rapidly worsening swelling.

  • Use prescribed anti-inflammatory or antimicrobial medication exactly as directed.

  • Avoid stopping immunosuppressive therapy abruptly unless the prescriber instructs it.

Nclex Traps

  • Inflammation and infection overlap but are not identical.

  • Steroids can improve inflammation while hiding fever and raising glucose.

  • Chronic inflammation can quietly remodel tissue into fibrosis.
