Definition

Crossed straight-leg raise is a bedside neural-tension maneuver. It is a clue to large medial lumbar disc herniation. A positive finding means typical radicular pain appears in the affected leg.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Crossed straight-leg raise means typical radicular pain appears in the affected leg.

Why It Matters

A positive Crossed straight-leg raise response can signal large medial lumbar disc herniation. Tension transmitted across the dural sac pulls the contralateral compressed nerve root. Crossed straight-leg raise cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large medial lumbar disc herniation

Assessment Method

Raise the unaffected straight leg.

Positive Finding

Typical radicular pain appears in the affected leg.

Mechanism

Tension transmitted across the dural sac pulls the contralateral compressed nerve root.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Crossed straight-leg raise response raises concern for large medial lumbar disc herniation because tension transmitted across the dural sac pulls the contralateral compressed nerve root. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Crossed straight-leg raise does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Crossed straight-leg raise to focus evaluation of large medial lumbar disc herniation and the reviewed alternatives: hamstring pain, hip disease, cauda equina syndrome. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Raise the unaffected straight leg. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Crossed straight-leg raise as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Absence is common and bilateral symptoms complicate laterality. Therefore, Crossed straight-leg raise cannot confirm or exclude large medial lumbar disc herniation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hamstring pain

  • hip disease

  • cauda equina syndrome

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hamstring pain, hip disease, cauda equina syndrome, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Crossed straight-leg raise may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current relatively specific but insensitive adjunct. When performed as described, Crossed straight-leg raise can adjust concern for large medial lumbar disc herniation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Crossed straight-leg raise: Absence is common and bilateral symptoms complicate laterality.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Crossed straight-leg raise, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Raise the unaffected straight leg. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: New bladder, saddle, or major motor deficits require emergency imaging, not more provocation.
