Used To Treat

Hyperthyroidism, Graves disease, thyroid storm protocols, and pre-thyroidectomy preparation.

Mechanism

Methimazole works by blocking thyroid peroxidase-mediated iodination and coupling steps needed to build T3 and T4. Propylthiouracil also reduces peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion. Because stored thyroid hormone must clear, symptoms improve over time rather than instantly; agranulocytosis and liver injury cues are priority safety anchors.

Boxed Warning

Propylthiouracil carries severe liver injury warning; antithyroid drugs can cause agranulocytosis.

Contraindications

  • Serious prior reaction

  • severe hepatic injury requires clarification

  • Clarify pregnancy trimester, serious hepatic disease, prior severe reaction to antithyroid drugs, or infection symptoms suggesting agranulocytosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach to report fever/sore throat immediately.

  • Monitor CBC and liver symptoms/labs as ordered.

  • Used in hyperthyroidism/thyroid storm protocols.

  • Teach to report fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers, jaundice, dark urine, severe fatigue, or right upper quadrant pain immediately.

  • Monitor thyroid response, CBC if infection symptoms occur or ordered, and liver symptoms/labs when ordered.

Interactions

  • Warfarin sensitivity can change as thyroid state changes.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • CBC/ANC

  • LFTs

  • TSH/free T4/T3

  • TSH/free T4/T3 trend, CBC with differential/ANC when indicated, liver function tests if symptoms or ordered, temperature and heart rate.

Description

Methimazole is a thionamide antithyroid medication: it works by blocking thyroid peroxidase-mediated iodination and coupling steps needed to build T3 and T4. It is used for hyperthyroidism, Graves disease, thyroid storm protocols, and pre-thyroidectomy preparation.
