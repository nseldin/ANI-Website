Mechanism

Aluminum hydroxide works by neutralizing gastric acid and binding phosphate in the GI tract through aluminum-phosphate complex formation. Constipation, aluminum accumulation in kidney disease, and timing interactions with other oral drugs matter.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess kidney function, constipation, phosphate level if used as a binder, and other oral medications that may bind or have reduced absorption.

  • Separate from interacting medications according to facility guidance.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe kidney disease unless specifically ordered because aluminum accumulation can be unsafe.

Interactions

  • Can reduce absorption of many drugs, including tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, levothyroxine, iron, and some TB medications.

Key Labs

  • Phosphate if used as binder; renal function for safety.

Nclex Traps

  • Aluminum products constipate and can bind other meds. In kidney disease, accumulation is the safety concern.

Description

Aluminum hydroxide is a phosphate binder: it works by neutralizing gastric acid and binding phosphate in the GI tract through aluminum-phosphate complex formation. It is used for occasional heartburn/acid indigestion and selected hyperphosphatemia situations when ordered.
