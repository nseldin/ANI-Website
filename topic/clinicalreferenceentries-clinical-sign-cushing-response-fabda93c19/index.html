Definition

Cushing response is a vital-sign constellation. It is a clue to critically raised intracranial pressure with impending herniation. A positive finding means widening pulse pressure with bradycardia and irregular respirations develops.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cushing response means widening pulse pressure with bradycardia and irregular respirations develops.

Why It Matters

The Cushing response pattern can signal critically raised intracranial pressure with impending herniation. Raised intracranial pressure lowers cerebral perfusion, provoking sympathetic hypertension followed by baroreflex bradycardia and brainstem respiratory dysfunction. Cushing response cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • critically raised intracranial pressure with impending herniation

Assessment Method

Trend blood pressure, pulse, respiratory pattern, consciousness, and pupils.

Positive Finding

Widening pulse pressure with bradycardia and irregular respirations develops.

Mechanism

Raised intracranial pressure lowers cerebral perfusion, provoking sympathetic hypertension followed by baroreflex bradycardia and brainstem respiratory dysfunction.

Evidence Limitations

The complete triad is late and absence does not exclude dangerous intracranial pressure. False Negative Situations
