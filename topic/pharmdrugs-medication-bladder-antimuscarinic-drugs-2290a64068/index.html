Used To Treat

Overactive bladder with urgency, frequency, and urge incontinence; selected oxybutynin formulations also treat neurogenic detrusor overactivity in age-approved patients.

Description

Bladder antimuscarinics reduce involuntary detrusor squeezing during filling by preventing acetylcholine from activating muscarinic receptors, especially the M3 pathway that drives smooth-muscle contraction. The same receptors support salivation, bowel motility, sweating, accommodation, cognition, and bladder emptying, so therapeutic storage and anticholinergic toxicity are inseparable. Drug selection therefore depends on CNS penetration, renal clearance, CYP metabolism, QT risk, bowel function, glaucoma, outlet obstruction, and total anticholinergic burden rather than assuming every OAB pill is equivalent.

Mechanism

Parasympathetic pelvic nerves release acetylcholine onto detrusor M3 receptors. M3 couples to Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, intracellular calcium release, myosin activation, and contraction. Competitive antagonism flattens this calcium signal and increases storage capacity. Oxybutynin is a CNS-penetrant tertiary amine with substantial heat/cognitive burden; tolterodine and fesoterodine share active 5-hydroxymethyl tolterodine; solifenacin and darifenacin are relatively M3-preferring; trospium is a quaternary, predominantly renally eliminated compound with lower passive blood-brain-barrier penetration, not zero CNS risk.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: All members can precipitate urinary retention, gastric retention/ileus, blurred vision, narrow-angle glaucoma crisis, overheating from reduced sweating, delirium and falls. New inability to void, severe constipation/abdominal distention, eye pain/halos, confusion, or heat illness requires prompt assessment.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, dry eyes and reduced sweating

  • Urinary retention and increased post-void residual

  • Somnolence, confusion, hallucinations, memory impairment and falls

  • Tachycardia; selected products add QT, CYP or angioedema concerns

Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Use cautiously with bladder outlet obstruction, severe constipation, autonomic neuropathy and myasthenia gravis

  • Avoid unnecessary anticholinergic stacking in cognitively vulnerable older adults

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure symptom burden and post-void residual instead of treating urinary frequency caused by retention, hyperglycemia, diuretics, infection or excess intake as OAB.

  • Track cognition, falls, bowel pattern, oral health, vision and heat exposure in addition to bladder symptoms.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, sedatives and opioids amplify delirium, constipation and retention.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors raise several tertiary agents; CYP2D6 status changes tolterodine exposure.

  • Solid oral potassium can injure a GI tract whose motility is slowed; follow product-specific warnings.

Key Labs

  • Post-void residual and renal function

  • Hepatic function and ECG/QT risk for selected agents

  • Cognition, bowel function, temperature tolerance and symptom diary

Nclex Traps

  • Antimuscarinics improve storage but can worsen emptying.

  • Trospium may have less CNS penetration, yet its label still reports CNS effects and renal accumulation.

  • Dry mouth is a mechanistic clue, not the only toxicity to monitor.
