Class

Local imidazole antifungal; fungal CYP51 inhibitor with clinically important warfarin interaction potential

Used To Treat

Oropharyngeal candidiasis with once-daily adhesive buccal tablets in adults, vulvovaginal candidiasis with labeled products, and susceptible superficial skin fungal infections.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity can occur. Warfarin anticoagulation can be enhanced even with local miconazole; monitor INR and bleeding and coordinate an alternative when risk is high.

Mechanism

Miconazole binds fungal CYP51 heme and blocks demethylation of lanosterol. Abnormal sterols accumulate and membrane-dependent transport and growth fail. Buccal adhesion maintains salivary concentration, explaining once-daily local dosing and why placement matters. Human CYP2C9 inhibition slows S-warfarin clearance, and even buccal or vaginal absorption can be clinically meaningful in a sensitive anticoagulated patient. The same route-specific logic explains high local efficacy with low overall systemic exposure and inability to treat candidemia.

Nursing Essentials

  • For Oravig, place the rounded side against the upper gum above an incisor after brushing, alternate sides, hold briefly until adherent, and do not chew/crush/swallow. If it falls off early, follow the label replacement instructions.

  • Ask every patient using warfarin about vaginal, oral, or skin miconazole. Escalate bleeding, bruising, melena, hematuria, severe headache, or an unexpectedly high INR.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to miconazole or formulation components; Oravig also contains milk-protein concentrate and is contraindicated with relevant hypersensitivity.

  • Do not use a buccal tablet in a way that creates aspiration/swallowing risk or at an ulcerated placement site without clinician guidance.

Interactions

  • Warfarin is the high-yield interaction through CYP2C9 inhibition; miconazole can also affect selected CYP2C9/3A4 substrates.

  • Other vaginal products, tampons, condoms/diaphragms, and local oil bases may have product-specific compatibility considerations; follow the exact formulation label.

Key Labs

  • INR/prothrombin time and bleeding assessment with warfarin, no routine organ labs for uncomplicated local therapy, and KOH/culture for recurrent or refractory infection.

  • A rising INR after a 'local' antifungal is pharmacologically plausible and should not be dismissed.

Nclex Traps

  • Local miconazole can still cause a major warfarin interaction.

  • The buccal tablet adheres to the gum; swallowing it removes the intended sustained local delivery.

Class Pathway

  • Lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase inhibition

  • Ergosterol depletion

  • Buccal, topical, or vaginal local exposure

Description

Miconazole is a mainly local imidazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51, depletes ergosterol, and disrupts membrane function at buccal, vaginal, or skin sites. The Oravig buccal tablet adheres above the incisor and releases drug into saliva for many hours; it should not be chewed, crushed, or swallowed. Vaginal and topical products concentrate at their application site and do not treat invasive infection. Despite local use, miconazole can inhibit human CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 enough to markedly increase warfarin effect and bleeding, so 'topical' does not mean interaction free.

Adverse Effects

  • Local burning/irritation, altered taste, nausea, diarrhea, headache, abdominal pain, vomiting, and application-site discomfort.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe skin reaction, and interaction-mediated major bleeding with warfarin.
