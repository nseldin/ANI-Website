Definition

Oil-drop nail sign is an observed nail sign. It is a clue to nail-bed psoriasis. A positive finding means a translucent yellow-red patch is visible beneath the nail plate.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Oil-drop nail sign means a translucent yellow-red patch is visible beneath the nail plate.

Why It Matters

The observed Oil-drop nail sign finding can signal nail-bed psoriasis. Focal nail-bed inflammation and parakeratosis alter light transmission like a drop of oil. Oil-drop nail sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • nail-bed psoriasis

Assessment Method

Inspect nails under good light.

Positive Finding

A translucent yellow-red patch is visible beneath the nail plate.

Mechanism

Focal nail-bed inflammation and parakeratosis alter light transmission like a drop of oil.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Oil-drop nail sign finding raises concern for nail-bed psoriasis because focal nail-bed inflammation and parakeratosis alter light transmission like a drop of oil. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Oil-drop nail sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Oil-drop nail sign to focus evaluation of nail-bed psoriasis and the reviewed alternatives: yellow nail syndrome, trauma, fungal infection. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect nails under good light. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Oil-drop nail sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It may be absent and fungal disease can coexist. Therefore, Oil-drop nail sign cannot confirm or exclude nail-bed psoriasis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • yellow nail syndrome

  • trauma

  • fungal infection

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as yellow nail syndrome, trauma, fungal infection.

False Negative Situations

Oil-drop nail sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of nail-bed psoriasis.
