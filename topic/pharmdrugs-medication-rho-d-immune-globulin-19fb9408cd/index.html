High-alert warning focus: confirm Rh status, antibody screen/indirect Coombs sensitization status, infant/fetal Rh context, timing, dose, and transfusion history. Class

Passive anti-D immunoglobulin; Rh sensitization prophylaxis

Used To Treat

Prevents Rh(D) alloimmunization in eligible unsensitized Rh-negative pregnant or postpartum clients after exposure to Rh-positive fetal blood, sensitizing events, or incompatible transfusion contexts.

Description

Rho(D) immune globulin is passive anti-D IgG that binds fetal Rh-positive red cells in an unsensitized Rh-negative client, promoting clearance before the maternal immune system forms permanent anti-D memory. The first-read point is prevention: verify Rh status and antibody screen/indirect Coombs context, because it does not treat an already sensitized pregnancy.

Mechanism

Anti-D IgG binds Rh(D)-positive fetal red cells in maternal circulation, promoting immune clearance and masking the D antigen before maternal B cells become actively sensitized. That prevents future IgG anti-D production that could cross the placenta and hemolyze Rh-positive fetal red cells in later pregnancies.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the mother/client is Rh-negative and not already sensitized when prophylaxis is intended.

  • Know timing triggers: antenatal prophylaxis per protocol, postpartum after Rh-positive infant, miscarriage/abortion, ectopic pregnancy, abdominal trauma, amniocentesis/CVS, external cephalic version, antepartum bleeding, or other sensitizing events.

  • Do not confuse prevention with treatment; sensitized pregnancy needs fetal surveillance and specialist management.

Contraindications

  • Do not use routine Rh prophylaxis for clients who are Rh-positive or already alloimmunized to D. Clarify serious immune-globulin reactions, IgA deficiency with antibodies, hemolytic anemia context, and product-specific IV/IM restrictions.

Interactions

  • Live vaccines such as MMR or varicella may have reduced response after immune globulin; follow immunization timing guidance. Transfusion products require compatibility and product-specific review.

Key Labs

  • Maternal blood type/Rh.

  • Antibody screen/indirect Coombs.

  • Infant/fetal Rh status when available.

  • Kleihauer-Betke or fetal bleed screen when large fetomaternal hemorrhage is possible.

Nclex Traps

  • Rho(D) immune globulin prevents maternal anti-D formation; it does not remove antibodies once sensitization has occurred.

  • Give to the eligible unsensitized Rh-negative client, not automatically to every postpartum client.

  • Maternal IgG anti-D can cross the placenta and hemolyze fetal RBCs in later pregnancies.
