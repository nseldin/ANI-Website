Used To Treat

Herpes zoster, genital herpes treatment or suppression, herpes labialis, and selected HSV transmission-reduction or pediatric varicella indications. It improves oral acyclovir exposure but does not eliminate viral latency.

Mechanism

Valacyclovir is absorbed through peptide transport pathways and hydrolyzed by valacyclovir hydrolase to acyclovir plus L-valine. Viral thymidine kinase then converts acyclovir to the monophosphate, and host kinases generate acyclovir triphosphate. The triphosphate competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate at viral DNA polymerase, becomes incorporated, and lacks the 3-prime hydroxyl needed for continued chain elongation. This produces functional chain termination and traps polymerase. Viral thymidine-kinase mutation can cause cross-resistance to acyclovir and famciclovir. Kidney elimination of acyclovir means reduced filtration or secretion raises plasma and CNS exposure; adequate hydration lowers urinary concentration and crystal formation but does not replace dose adjustment.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Acute renal failure and central nervous system reactions, including agitation, hallucinations, confusion, delirium, seizures, and encephalopathy, are most likely with renal impairment or excessive dosing. Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura/hemolytic uremic syndrome has occurred in advanced HIV disease and transplant recipients receiving very high valacyclovir doses. Stop and evaluate a falling platelet count, hemolysis, neurologic change, or renal decline.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity to valacyclovir, acyclovir, or a formulation component.

  • Adjust dose for creatinine clearance and indication; do not assume the convenient oral schedule is safe during acute kidney injury or dehydration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach that suppression lowers recurrence and transmission risk but does not eliminate asymptomatic viral shedding; barrier and disclosure counseling remain relevant.

  • For shingles, begin as early as possible and assess eye, ear, motor, disseminated, or immunocompromised involvement because these features can require urgent specialty care or IV acyclovir.

Interactions

  • Nephrotoxins and drugs competing for active tubular secretion can increase renal or neurologic toxicity.

  • High-dose immunosuppressive regimens and mycophenolate require closer renal and hematologic review rather than routine outpatient assumptions.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR and hydration; CBC, platelets, hemolysis studies, and liver tests when high-dose, prolonged, or immunocompromised therapy warrants them

  • Lesion timing, healing and new-lesion formation, pain, ocular/neurologic signs, recurrence frequency, adherence, and transmission counseling

Nclex Traps

  • Valacyclovir becomes acyclovir; it has better oral delivery, not a different viral target.

  • Suppression reduces but does not abolish HSV transmission because asymptomatic shedding can continue.

Description

Valacyclovir is an oral L-valyl acyclovir prodrug that is rapidly hydrolyzed to acyclovir, increasing bioavailability and allowing less frequent dosing while preserving infected-cell activation and viral DNA-polymerase inhibition. The resulting acyclovir is selectively phosphorylated in HSV- or VZV-infected cells and arrests viral DNA synthesis. The prodrug therefore improves delivery rather than changing the antiviral target. Renal dosing and hydration still matter because the active drug is acyclovir; excessive exposure can cause acute kidney injury and neurotoxicity. Early treatment matters because the drug blocks new viral DNA production but cannot reverse tissue damage already produced or remove dormant ganglionic genomes.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, abdominal discomfort, dizziness, and rash

  • Acute kidney injury, neurotoxicity, severe hypersensitivity or skin reaction, and rare TTP/HUS in severely immunocompromised high-dose exposure
