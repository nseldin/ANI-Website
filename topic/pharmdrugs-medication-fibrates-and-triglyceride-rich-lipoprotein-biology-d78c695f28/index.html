Used To Treat

Severe hypertriglyceridemia after secondary causes and nutrition are addressed, plus selected mixed dyslipidemia. The immediate objective at very high triglycerides is often pancreatitis-risk reduction rather than LDL-event prevention.

Description

Fenofibrate and gemfibrozil are fibrates: drugs that activate the nuclear receptor PPAR-alpha and shift liver, muscle, and lipoprotein metabolism toward triglyceride clearance. They increase lipoprotein-lipase activity and fatty-acid oxidation, reduce ApoC-III inhibition of lipolysis, and decrease hepatic VLDL output, so triglycerides fall and HDL-C may rise modestly. LDL-C can fall, remain unchanged, or rise when severe hypertriglyceridemia improves because large VLDL particles are converted into LDL; that laboratory change must be interpreted with non-HDL-C and ApoB rather than called automatic treatment failure. Fibrates are not interchangeable with statins: their strongest effect is on triglyceride-rich particles, and routine addition to statins has not produced universal ASCVD benefit. Their role becomes clearest when triglycerides are high enough to create chylomicronemia and pancreatitis concern.

Mechanism

PPAR-alpha forms a heterodimer with retinoid X receptor and binds DNA response elements, increasing transcription of lipoprotein lipase, fatty-acid transport and beta-oxidation proteins while reducing ApoC-III. Less ApoC-III removes a brake on lipoprotein lipase and hepatic remnant uptake. More lipolysis clears chylomicron and VLDL triglyceride, and increased oxidation reduces fatty-acid substrate for hepatic VLDL assembly. Fibrates also increase ApoA-I and ApoA-II expression, contributing to HDL change. PPAR-alpha activity changes bile composition and can increase cholesterol saturation, explaining gallstones. Muscle toxicity rises when a fibrate increases statin exposure or when kidney impairment raises fibrate concentration. Fenofibrate is generally preferred if combination with a statin is necessary because gemfibrozil more strongly inhibits statin glucuronidation and OATP1B1 transport.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Fibrates can cause serious hepatotoxicity, myopathy or rhabdomyolysis, gallstones, pancreatitis from biliary disease, and kidney-function changes. A statin-fibrate combination requires a specific reason and interaction plan; gemfibrozil is the higher-risk partner.

Adverse Effects

  • Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, aminotransferase elevation, reversible creatinine rise, gallstones, cholecystitis, and rash can occur.

  • Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis risk rises with statins, CKD, hypothyroidism, older age, diabetes, and interacting drugs.

Contraindications

  • Severe renal dysfunction, active liver disease, primary biliary cirrhosis or significant gallbladder disease, and breastfeeding are major label-dependent barriers.

  • Do not use a fibrate as the only response to severe triglycerides without addressing alcohol, uncontrolled diabetes, diet, hypothyroidism, pregnancy, nephrotic syndrome, and triglyceride-raising medicines.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain fasting triglycerides when needed, non-HDL-C, kidney and liver function, gallbladder history, thyroid and diabetes context, alcohol and nutrition history, and a complete statin interaction review.

  • Teach right-upper-quadrant pain, jaundice, severe muscle symptoms, dark urine, and reduced urine as escalation signs. Recheck response after the drug and secondary causes have had time to change.

Interactions

  • Gemfibrozil with simvastatin is contraindicated and with many other statins is avoided or tightly restricted. Fenofibrate still adds muscle risk but has fewer pharmacokinetic effects.

  • Warfarin effect can increase as protein binding and metabolism change; monitor INR closely when starting, stopping, or changing a fibrate.

Key Labs

  • Triglycerides, total cholesterol, HDL-C, calculated or direct LDL-C when interpretable, non-HDL-C, ApoB selectively, ALT/AST/bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, and symptom-triggered CK and urinalysis.

  • A creatinine rise with fenofibrate can be reversible and hemodynamic or metabolic, but progressive kidney dysfunction, oliguria, or muscle injury requires action.

Nclex Traps

  • Fibrates primarily lower triglycerides; they are not simply weaker statins.

  • An LDL-C increase after clearing very large VLDL can reflect conversion to LDL. Assess ApoB and non-HDL burden instead of reading one number in isolation.
