Class

Phenothiazine dopamine D2 antagonist antiemetic

Used To Treat

Severe nausea/vomiting and selected migraine-associated nausea; it also has antipsychotic-class pharmacology at different dosing contexts.

Mechanism

Blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the chemoreceptor trigger zone to reduce dopamine-triggered emesis, while phenothiazine receptor spillover explains EPS, sedation, anticholinergic effects, hypotension, and QT concerns.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess EPS/dystonia, akathisia, sedation, orthostatic hypotension, anticholinergic burden, QT risk, neuroleptic malignant syndrome symptoms, and fall risk.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe CNS depression, coma, significant bone-marrow suppression, Parkinson disease, severe hypotension, or hypersensitivity to phenothiazines.

Interactions

  • Other dopamine blockers increase EPS/NMS risk. CNS depressants, anticholinergics, antihypertensives, and QT-prolonging drugs increase toxicity concerns.

Key Labs

  • ECG/electrolytes if QT risk; CBC if prolonged therapy or marrow-risk context.

Nclex Traps

  • Prochlorperazine can stop vomiting through D2 blockade but can also cause acute dystonia/EPS; treat new tongue, neck, eye, or jaw spasms as medication toxicity until proven otherwise.

Description

Prochlorperazine is a phenothiazine dopamine D2 antagonist antiemetic: it blocks dopamine D2 receptors in the chemoreceptor trigger zone to reduce dopamine-triggered emesis, while phenothiazine receptor spillover explains EPS, sedation, anticholinergic effects, hypotension, and QT concerns. It is used for severe nausea/vomiting and selected migraine-associated nausea. It also has antipsychotic-class pharmacology at different dosing contexts.
