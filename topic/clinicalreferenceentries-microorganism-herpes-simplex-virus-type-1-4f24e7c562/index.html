Definition

Herpes simplex virus type 1 is a clinically important virus associated with orolabial herpes, genital herpes, herpetic keratitis, and herpes encephalitis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    HSV-1 replicates at mucocutaneous sites, establishes latency in sensory ganglia, and can reactivate; rare spread to the eye or brain can be destructive. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Herpes simplex virus type 1 is associated with orolabial herpes, genital herpes, herpetic keratitis, and herpes encephalitis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
