Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, bulimia nervosa, panic disorder, and premenstrual dysphoric disorder; with olanzapine, selected bipolar depressive episodes and treatment-resistant depression.

Mechanism

Fluoxetine selectively inhibits presynaptic SERT, reducing serotonin reuptake and increasing extracellular 5-HT. Early serotonin feedback activates inhibitory autoreceptors, initially limiting neuronal firing; repeated exposure desensitizes parts of this feedback system and promotes downstream BDNF/neuroplastic and circuit-level adaptation, explaining why transporter blockade is immediate but therapeutic response is delayed. Hepatic CYP2D6 and other pathways form norfluoxetine, an active SERT inhibitor with a long half-life. Fluoxetine also strongly inhibits CYP2D6, raising exposure to susceptible antipsychotics, TCAs and antiarrhythmics and reducing activation of prodrugs such as tamoxifen.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Fluoxetine has a uniquely long washout: allow at least 5 weeks after stopping before an MAOI because residual parent drug and norfluoxetine can still provoke serotonin toxicity.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent MAOI, linezolid/methylene-blue serotonergic treatment unless emergency protocol is followed, or use within the required washout

  • Pimozide or thioridazine because CYP/QT interactions can become dangerous

  • Avoid antidepressant monotherapy in unrecognized bipolar mania/mixed states; screen before initiation

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess energy, agitation, sleep and suicidality early: activation can improve physical drive before hopelessness resolves, creating a vulnerable interval.

  • Explain that a missed dose is not corrected with extra medication and that a long half-life reduces abrupt withdrawal but does not make sudden self-discontinuation or drug switching interaction-free.

Interactions

  • Wait at least 14 days after an MAOI before fluoxetine and at least 5 weeks after fluoxetine before an MAOI.

  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibition can raise TCAs, several antipsychotics and class IC antiarrhythmics and can reduce tamoxifen activation.

  • Other serotonergic drugs, NSAIDs/anticoagulants, diuretics and QT-risk drugs add serotonin syndrome, bleeding, hyponatremia or dysrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Sodium when older, volume depleted, taking diuretics or symptomatic

  • Bleeding, weight, sleep, sexual function and mood/mania/suicide assessment

  • ECG/electrolytes and interaction-specific drug levels when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Serotonin changes immediately; mood response is delayed by neural adaptation, not delayed tablet absorption.

  • Fluoxetine causes less abrupt discontinuation syndrome but a much longer interaction tail.

  • Bulimia dosing/indication does not make fluoxetine a general weight-loss drug.

Description

Fluoxetine is an activating, long-duration SSRI that blocks the serotonin transporter and gradually changes mood, anxiety, obsession, and appetite-control circuits. Serotonin increases at synapses within hours, but symptom relief usually takes weeks because autoreceptors, receptor sensitivity, gene expression, and network plasticity must adapt. Fluoxetine and active norfluoxetine persist for days to weeks, so missed doses cause less abrupt withdrawal than short-half-life SSRIs, but interactions and adverse effects can also continue long after the last tablet.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea/diarrhea, insomnia, activation/anxiety, headache and sweating

  • Sexual dysfunction and delayed orgasm; appetite/weight effects vary over time

  • Serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia/SIADH, abnormal bleeding and manic activation

  • Seizures, QT-related dysrhythmia in susceptible patients and persistent interaction after discontinuation
