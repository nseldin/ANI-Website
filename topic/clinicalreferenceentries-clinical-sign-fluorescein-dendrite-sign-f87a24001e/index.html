Definition

Fluorescein dendrite sign is a fluorescein eye sign. It is a clue to herpes simplex epithelial keratitis. A positive finding means a branching epithelial ulcer with terminal bulbs fluoresces.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Fluorescein dendrite sign means a branching epithelial ulcer with terminal bulbs fluoresces.

Why It Matters

The observed Fluorescein dendrite sign finding can signal herpes simplex epithelial keratitis. HSV spreads from cell to cell along corneal epithelium, creating branching ulceration. Fluorescein dendrite sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • herpes simplex epithelial keratitis

Assessment Method

Stain the cornea and examine with slit lamp and cobalt-blue light.

Positive Finding

A branching epithelial ulcer with terminal bulbs fluoresces.

Mechanism

HSV spreads from cell to cell along corneal epithelium, creating branching ulceration.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Fluorescein dendrite sign finding raises concern for herpes simplex epithelial keratitis because hSV spreads from cell to cell along corneal epithelium, creating branching ulceration. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Fluorescein dendrite sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Fluorescein dendrite sign to focus evaluation of herpes simplex epithelial keratitis and the reviewed alternatives: contact-lens abrasion, toxic keratitis, recurrent erosion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Stain the cornea and examine with slit lamp and cobalt-blue light. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Fluorescein dendrite sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Zoster lesions lack classic terminal bulbs and healing epithelium can branch. Therefore, Fluorescein dendrite sign cannot confirm or exclude herpes simplex epithelial keratitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as contact-lens abrasion, toxic keratitis, recurrent erosion.

False Negative Situations

Fluorescein dendrite sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of herpes simplex epithelial keratitis.
