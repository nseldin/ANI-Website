Definition

Pappenheimer bodies is a microscopic and iron-stain sign. It is a clue to iron-containing red-cell inclusions. A positive finding means small peripheral basophilic granules contain iron.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pappenheimer bodies means small peripheral basophilic granules contain iron.

Why It Matters

The Pappenheimer bodies laboratory finding can signal iron-containing red-cell inclusions. Mitochondrial or siderosomal iron persists in mature erythrocytes when iron handling or splenic clearance is abnormal. Pappenheimer bodies cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • iron-containing red-cell inclusions

Assessment Method

Review smear and confirm with Prussian blue iron stain when needed.

Positive Finding

Small peripheral basophilic granules contain iron.

Mechanism

Mitochondrial or siderosomal iron persists in mature erythrocytes when iron handling or splenic clearance is abnormal.

Clinical Interpretation

The Pappenheimer bodies laboratory finding raises concern for iron-containing red-cell inclusions because mitochondrial or siderosomal iron persists in mature erythrocytes when iron handling or splenic clearance is abnormal. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pappenheimer bodies does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pappenheimer bodies to focus evaluation of iron-containing red-cell inclusions and the reviewed alternatives: basophilic stippling, parasites, stain precipitate. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review smear and confirm with Prussian blue iron stain when needed. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pappenheimer bodies as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

They can resemble Howell-Jolly bodies or stain artifact. Therefore, Pappenheimer bodies cannot confirm or exclude iron-containing red-cell inclusions by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • basophilic stippling

  • parasites

  • stain precipitate

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with basophilic stippling, parasites, stain precipitate.

False Negative Situations

Pappenheimer bodies may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
