Class

Neurokinin-1 receptor antagonist antiemetic

Used To Treat

Prevention of acute and delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting and selected postoperative nausea/vomiting regimens, usually as part of a combination plan.

Mechanism

Blocks neurokinin-1 receptors so substance P cannot activate vomiting-center pathways in the brainstem, which is why it is especially useful for delayed chemotherapy-related emesis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify timing before chemotherapy, companion antiemetics, liver/CYP3A4 interaction risk, pregnancy/contraception teaching, and ability to complete the regimen.

  • Teach that it prevents nausea/vomiting; it is not usually a rescue-only single-agent answer.

Contraindications

  • Clarify hypersensitivity and contraindicated CYP3A4 substrate combinations per labeling.

Interactions

  • CYP3A4 interactions are central. Aprepitant can reduce hormonal contraceptive effectiveness; backup contraception teaching is important.

Key Labs

  • Liver function when clinically indicated and medication-interaction review.

Nclex Traps

  • Aprepitant is the substance P/NK1 pathway drug, not a serotonin blocker. Think delayed chemotherapy vomiting and interaction screening.

Description

Aprepitant is a neurokinin-1 receptor antagonist antiemetic: it blocks neurokinin-1 receptors so substance P cannot activate vomiting-center pathways in the brainstem, which is why it is especially useful for delayed chemotherapy-related emesis. It is used for prevention of acute and delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting and selected postoperative nausea/vomiting regimens, usually as part of a combination plan.
