Used To Treat

Eradication of dormant P. vivax or P. ovale liver hypnozoites after appropriate blood-stage treatment, plus selected transmission-blocking or prophylaxis uses. Primaquine is usually a multi-day course; tafenoquine's long half-life permits single-dose radical cure in eligible patients but prolongs any oxidant exposure.

Description

Radical cure means removing the liver reservoir that can reactivate weeks or months after the bloodstream appears clear. Primaquine and tafenoquine are 8-aminoquinolines whose oxidative metabolites injure dormant hypnozoites and gametocytes. Red cells depend on the pentose-phosphate pathway enzyme G6PD to generate NADPH, which keeps glutathione reduced and protects hemoglobin and membrane sulfhydryl groups. When G6PD activity is low, oxidant stress denatures hemoglobin into Heinz bodies and damages the membrane, producing intravascular and splenic hemolysis. This is why quantitative testing is not administrative paperwork and why a longer-acting drug can be less forgiving.

Mechanism

Both drugs undergo hepatic metabolism to redox-active intermediates that cycle electrons, produce reactive oxygen species, and disturb parasite mitochondrial electron transport and membrane function. Hypnozoites are susceptible to this oxidative pressure, but erythrocytes cannot synthesize new enzyme or repair proteins because they lack nuclei. G6PD generates NADPH from the pentose-phosphate pathway; NADPH allows glutathione reductase to restore reduced glutathione, which detoxifies peroxides. In deficiency, glutathione protection fails, hemoglobin oxidizes and precipitates, and rigid damaged cells are destroyed. Tafenoquine has a terminal half-life measured in days to weeks, so once administered it cannot be withdrawn; primaquine's shorter exposure allows daily reassessment and specialized modified regimens in selected intermediate deficiency.

Boxed Warning

Both drugs can cause severe hemolytic anemia in G6PD deficiency. Obtain quantitative G6PD activity before treatment; a qualitative screen may miss intermediate activity, particularly in heterozygous females because circulating cells are a mosaic. Tafenoquine is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency or unknown status, pregnancy, breastfeeding when the infant is G6PD deficient or status is unknown, and history of psychotic disorder. Primaquine is also contraindicated in pregnancy because fetal G6PD status cannot be established. Stop and evaluate dark urine, jaundice, pallor, dyspnea, tachycardia, dizziness, or falling hemoglobin.

Adverse Effects

  • Abdominal pain, nausea, headache, dizziness, and methemoglobinemia

  • Dose-related hemolysis, acute kidney injury secondary to severe hemolysis, and tafenoquine-associated psychiatric or hypersensitivity concerns

Contraindications

  • Unknown or deficient quantitative G6PD activity, pregnancy, and incompatible breastfeeding status; additional tafenoquine age and psychiatric restrictions apply.

  • Do not confuse blood-stage clearance with radical cure eligibility. Finish an effective blood-stage regimen and verify species and exposure history.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document the numeric G6PD result, assay reference, timing relative to recent hemolysis or transfusion, and pregnancy/lactation status; reticulocytosis can falsely elevate measured activity after hemolysis.

  • Teach hemolysis symptoms and arrange follow-up. A normal baseline hemoglobin does not protect a G6PD-deficient patient from drug-triggered destruction.

Interactions

  • Other oxidant or marrow-toxic drugs can compound anemia or methemoglobinemia.

  • CYP and transporter interactions are product-specific; tafenoquine's long persistence makes delayed adverse effects and interaction review especially important.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative G6PD activity, baseline CBC/hemoglobin, pregnancy assessment, and species confirmation

  • Follow-up CBC, reticulocytes, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin, urinalysis, oxygenation and methemoglobin when symptoms suggest oxidant injury

Nclex Traps

  • A negative qualitative G6PD screen is not always enough for tafenoquine; quantitative activity is required.

  • Tafenoquine's single dose improves adherence but does not make it safer: long persistence means the exposure cannot be stopped after hemolysis begins.
