Definition

Burkholderia cepacia complex is a clinically important bacterium associated with chronic pulmonary infection in cystic fibrosis, cepacia syndrome, bloodstream infection, and device-associated infection. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Burkholderia cepacia complex is a Gram-negative bacillus with a obligate aerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, motile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

  • Content

    Strict hand and equipment hygiene and cystic-fibrosis infection-control guidance help prevent patient-to-patient transmission. The reviewed route description is contact transmission. Colonization may be chronic before clinical decline, and acute device infection has a different timeline. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.
