Definition

Central obesity means excess body fat is concentrated around the abdomen or trunk rather than distributed evenly. It describes body-fat location, not a person's behavior or worth.

Plain Language

More body fat is stored around the belly and trunk than around the hips and limbs.

Why It Matters

Abdominal fat can raise the risk of insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, abnormal lipids, fatty liver disease, heart disease, and stroke even when BMI does not tell the whole story. A new Cushingoid pattern with weakness, easy bruising, wide purple striae, or glucocorticoid exposure needs cause-directed evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • preferential abdominal or truncal fat accumulation from common obesity, insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, glucocorticoid excess or treatment, aging, menopause, reduced activity, sleep disturbance, or selected lipodystrophy patterns

Assessment Method

Measure weight, height, BMI, and waist circumference with a standardized landmark and technique when appropriate; review the longitudinal trend, medications including glucocorticoids, blood pressure, glucose and lipid risk, sleep, activity, and other endocrine features without judgment.

Positive Finding

Fat is disproportionately concentrated around the abdomen or trunk compared with the hips and limbs, often reflected by an increased waist measurement even when BMI does not fully represent risk.

Mechanism

Subcutaneous and visceral fat distribution reflects energy balance, genetics, sex hormones, cortisol and insulin signaling, age, medications, and activity; deeper abdominal fat is metabolically active and can increase insulin resistance and inflammation.

Evidence Limitations

Waist thresholds and risk vary with sex, age, ancestry, body build, and guideline; pregnancy, ascites, large masses, edema, and measurement technique can distort the result. Therefore, Central obesity cannot confirm or exclude preferential abdominal or truncal fat accumulation from common obesity, insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, glucocorticoid excess or treatment, aging, menopause, reduced activity, sleep disturbance, or selected lipodystrophy patterns by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as generalized obesity, ascites, pregnancy, abdominal mass.

False Negative Situations

Central obesity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of preferential abdominal or truncal fat accumulation from common obesity, insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, glucocorticoid excess or treatment, aging, menopause, reduced activity, sleep disturbance, or selected lipodystrophy patterns.
