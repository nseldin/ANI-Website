Definition

Microbiome is the broader ecological and functional system associated with a microbial community. Depending on context, it includes the microorganisms, their collective genetic material, metabolic products, interactions, and surrounding environment. It should not be used as an exact synonym for microbiota, which refers specifically to the organisms.

  • Content

    Microbial genes expand the biochemical activities occurring at a body site. Community metabolism can transform dietary compounds and medications, produce signaling molecules, and influence barrier and immune function. The host simultaneously shapes the community through anatomy, secretions, oxygen, diet, immunity, and behavior, making the relationship bidirectional rather than a simple list of species.

    Gut-microbiome studies may measure organisms by sequencing marker genes, estimate gene content with metagenomics, or measure metabolites. Each method answers a different question: who may be present, what genes are present, or what biochemical activity is occurring.

    Microbiome research helps explain colonization resistance, antibiotic-associated disruption, and why the same exposure can affect people differently. Its strongest current clinical uses remain condition- and method-specific. A consumer microbiome report should not be treated as a diagnosis or as a reason to start supplements, antimicrobials, or major diet changes without clinical context.
