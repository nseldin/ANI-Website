Class

ActRIIA-Fc activin/TGF-beta-superfamily ligand-trap pulmonary-vascular signaling modulator

Used To Treat

Adults with WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension to improve exercise capacity and WHO functional class and reduce clinical worsening, including PAH hospitalization, lung transplantation, and death. It is usually added to established background PAH therapy rather than substituted for an acute vasodilator.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current WINREVAIR labeling has no boxed warning. The defining hazards arise from the same hematopoietic pathway effects that can raise hemoglobin while lowering platelets. Severe erythrocytosis can promote hyperviscosity and thrombosis; severe thrombocytopenia and serious gastrointestinal or intracranial bleeding can occur, especially with prostacyclin infusion, antithrombotic therapy, or a low platelet count. Check hemoglobin and platelets before each of the first five doses, longer while unstable, and periodically thereafter. Do not initiate below 50,000 platelets/mm3 and do not administer during serious bleeding. Embryo-fetal toxicity and possible impaired fertility require specific counseling.

Mechanism

PAH involves an imbalance between pro-proliferative ActRIIA-Smad2/3 signaling and anti-proliferative BMPRII-Smad1/5/8 signaling. Sotatercept combines the extracellular ligand-binding domain of human ActRIIA with an IgG1 Fc domain. Acting as a circulating decoy receptor, it binds activin A and other compatible ligands, reduces receptor-driven Smad2/3 signaling, and restores a more favorable relationship with BMPRII signaling. Endothelial and smooth-muscle proliferation and inflammatory remodeling then decline, vessel walls become less obstructive, pulmonary vascular resistance falls, and the right ventricle pumps against less afterload. Hemodynamic improvement develops over weeks because structural and signaling changes are slower than direct vasodilation; the approximately 24-day effective half-life supports dosing every three weeks and produces accumulation toward steady state over about 15 weeks.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before the first dose, verify WHO Group 1 PAH, body weight, background therapy, WHO class, exercise capacity, oxygenation, hemoglobin, platelets, bleeding/thrombosis history, telangiectasias, prostacyclin or antithrombotic use, pregnancy status, and the patient's ability to prepare a weight-based injection safely.

  • Start at 0.3 mg/kg SC and move to the 0.7 mg/kg every-three-week target only after acceptable hemoglobin and platelet results. The interval is not arbitrary: a roughly 24-day effective half-life sustains exposure, while repeat dosing accumulates for about 15 weeks. If a dose is more than three days late, reset the calendar to preserve three-week spacing.

  • Escalate persistent epistaxis, melena, hematemesis, severe headache, focal neurologic change, symptoms of hyperviscosity or thrombosis, worsening oxygenation, or new right-heart-failure findings. Worsening hypoxemia despite improved hemodynamics can reflect intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting and deserves focused evaluation.

Contraindications

  • Current U.S. labeling lists no formal contraindications. That does not mean unrestricted use: do not initiate when platelets are below 50,000/mm3, do not administer during serious bleeding, and delay or modify doses for label-defined hemoglobin rises or platelet decline.

  • It is not established for patients younger than 18 years, and it is not a rescue drug for acute decompensated PAH. New pulmonary edema, severe hypoxemia, or hemodynamic collapse requires urgent diagnostic and supportive management rather than an extra dose.

Interactions

  • Serious bleeding is more likely with concomitant prostacyclin infusion and/or antithrombotic agents, particularly when platelet counts are low. This is a pharmacodynamic interaction: impaired platelet number and platelet inhibition can compound one another even without a metabolic interaction.

  • As a recombinant fusion protein cleared by catabolic pathways, sotatercept is not expected to behave like a CYP-metabolized small molecule. Still reconcile anticoagulants, antiplatelets, prostacyclin-pathway therapy, and other bleeding risks before every dose.

Key Labs

  • Hemoglobin and platelet count before dose 1 and before each of the first five doses, longer while values are unstable, then periodically; use the label's change-from-baseline and upper-limit thresholds rather than waiting for symptoms.

  • Pregnancy testing before treatment, blood pressure, pulse oximetry and oxygen trend, bleeding/thrombosis assessment, NT-proBNP, six-minute walk distance, WHO functional class, and right-heart/hemodynamic measures as clinically directed.

Nclex Traps

  • Sotatercept modifies vascular-remodeling signaling; it is not interchangeable with PDE5 inhibitors, endothelin antagonists, prostacyclins, riociguat, or inhaled nitric oxide and cannot rescue an acute pulmonary-hypertensive crisis.

  • A higher hemoglobin is not automatically beneficial in PAH. Excessive erythrocytosis raises viscosity and thrombosis risk, while a falling platelet count raises bleeding risk; both can require a dose delay.

  • No labeled contraindications does not mean no stop rules. Platelets below 50,000/mm3 prevent initiation, active serious bleeding prevents administration, and new unexplained hypoxemia can require discontinuation evaluation.

    Pregnancy, lactation, and fertility

    Note

    Animal data indicate fetal harm and impaired male or female fertility. Test for pregnancy before treatment, use effective contraception during therapy and for at least four months after the final dose, and avoid breastfeeding during treatment and for four months after the final dose.

  • Type

    Label

    Pediatric use

    Note

    Safety and effectiveness are not established below age 18; do not extrapolate the adult weight-based regimen to children.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adults

    Note

    Trial exposure included adults through age 81 without a clinically significant age-related pharmacokinetic difference. Individualize around bleeding risk, polypharmacy, oxygenation, frailty, and the ability to reconstitute and measure an exact dose.

  • Type

    renal-hepatic

    Label

    Kidney and liver context

    Note

    Renal impairment, including dialysis, did not meaningfully change pharmacokinetics and the drug is not dialyzable. Hepatic impairment has not been studied, so avoid inventing a liver-dose rule and use specialty judgment.

Class Pathway

PAH therapy > Activin/TGF-beta signaling > ActRIIA-Fc ligand trap > Vascular-remodeling modulation

Description

Sotatercept-csrk is a long-acting subcutaneous disease-modifying biologic that binds activin A and related TGF-beta-superfamily ligands as a soluble ActRIIA-Fc decoy, reducing pro-proliferative Smad2/3 signaling and pulmonary-vascular remodeling in adult WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension. By rebalancing that pathway toward protective BMPRII-Smad1/5/8 signaling, it lowers pulmonary vascular resistance and right-ventricular afterload over time rather than producing immediate rescue vasodilation. It is given every three weeks because its effective half-life is about 24 days; the same slow exposure means it is not an emergency treatment for acute hypoxemia, hypotension, or right-heart collapse.

Adverse Effects

  • Common reactions include headache, epistaxis, rash, telangiectasia, diarrhea, dizziness, erythema, gingival bleeding, increased hemoglobin, and infections; these are clinically connected to vascular and hematopoietic signaling rather than simple injection intolerance.

  • Serious effects include erythrocytosis with hyperviscosity or thrombosis, severe thrombocytopenia, gastrointestinal or intracranial bleeding, and worsening hypoxemia from intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting despite improved PAH hemodynamics. Pericardial effusion and injection-site reactions have also been reported after approval.
