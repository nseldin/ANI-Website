Class

Intravenous P-selectin-blocking monoclonal antibody for sickle-cell vaso-occlusive-crisis reduction

Used To Treat

Reduction of vaso-occlusive-crisis frequency in adults and eligible adolescents with sickle-cell disease under current U.S. labeling. It may be used with or without hydroxyurea according to the specialist plan. It is preventive and does not replace acute pain, acute chest syndrome, stroke, infection, or transfusion management.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious infusion-related reactions can occur during or within 24 hours of infusion and may include pain, fever, chills, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, dizziness, pruritus, urticaria, sweating, dyspnea, wheeze, or blood-pressure change. Severe reactions may require discontinuation. Corticosteroid use during a sickle infusion reaction requires careful assessment because steroids can increase sickle complications.

Mechanism

Crizanlizumab-tmca is a humanized IgG2 kappa monoclonal antibody that binds P-selectin and blocks interaction with P-selectin glycoprotein ligand-1 and other ligands. Activated endothelium and platelets use P-selectin to tether and roll leukocytes, platelets, and sickled erythrocyte-associated complexes under flow. Adhesion slows microvascular transit, increasing deoxygenation time, HbS polymerization, inflammation, and occlusion. P-selectin blockade reduces these cellular contacts and interrupts the adhesion-amplification loop. It does not raise HbF or inhibit HbS polymerization. In vitro, crizanlizumab can cause platelet clumping in EDTA tubes, producing a falsely low automated count; rapid analysis or citrate/heparin anticoagulant can improve accuracy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor before, during, and after infusion. Stop or slow and treat according to reaction severity; assess oxygenation, blood pressure, chest findings, and whether pain began with the infusion.

  • If the platelet count is unexpectedly low, notify the laboratory and repeat using promptly analyzed citrate or heparin anticoagulated blood according to protocol.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but severe hypersensitivity or severe infusion reaction can require permanent discontinuation

  • Do not use as sole treatment of an acute vaso-occlusive crisis or acute chest syndrome

  • Do not accept an unexpected automated platelet count without considering specimen interference

Interactions

  • Hydroxyurea may be continued under a specialist plan because the mechanisms differ.

  • Premedication and reaction treatment, especially corticosteroids, should follow sickle-specific risk assessment rather than routine infusion habits.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets with specimen-method awareness, hemoglobin/reticulocytes, renal/liver context, and crisis frequency

  • Infusion vital signs, oxygenation, pain timing, chest/neurologic symptoms, and hospitalizations or transfusions

Nclex Traps

  • Crizanlizumab blocks P-selectin adhesion; it does not change HbS polymerization or provide acute analgesia.

  • A low platelet count can be a laboratory artifact from EDTA clumping, so verify before treating thrombocytopenia.

Description

Crizanlizumab is an IV monoclonal antibody that blocks P-selectin, an adhesion molecule displayed by activated endothelial cells and platelets. P-selectin binds PSGL-1 and related ligands on leukocytes and other blood cells, slowing them along the vessel wall and helping create the sticky inflammatory traffic that contributes to sickle vaso-occlusion. Blocking this interaction reduces crisis frequency without changing the HbS molecule itself. Infusion reactions can cause severe pain, fever, dyspnea, or hemodynamic symptoms that resemble a vaso-occlusive episode, so the nurse must assess timing and physiology rather than label every infusion-associated pain as baseline disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion-related reaction with severe pain, fever, dyspnea, wheeze, nausea, hypotension, or hypertension

  • Nausea, arthralgia, back pain, fever, abdominal pain, or pruritus

  • Apparent thrombocytopenia from EDTA-dependent in-vitro platelet clumping
