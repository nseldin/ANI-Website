Contraindications

  • Hypercalcemia, vitamin-D toxicity and serious hypersensitivity

  • Avoid duplicate vitamin-D analogs or uncontrolled calcium/phosphate intake

  • Clarify the phosphorus-binder/diet plan and PTH targets appropriate to the CKD context before giving.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach the difference between active hormone microgram dosing and ordinary vitamin-D supplementation; dosing errors can be dangerous.

  • Trend calcium, phosphorus and PTH together because correcting one value can worsen another.

Interactions

  • Thiazides and calcium products raise hypercalcemia risk.

  • Hypercalcemia potentiates digoxin toxicity.

  • Magnesium antacids can cause hypermagnesemia in dialysis; enzyme inducers may alter vitamin-D effect.

Key Labs

  • Calcium and phosphorus frequently during titration

  • Magnesium, intact PTH and alkaline phosphatase

  • Renal function, urine calcium and calcium-phosphate trend

Nclex Traps

  • Calcitriol does not need renal activation.

  • It raises phosphate as well as calcium.

  • Suppressing PTH too far can produce low-turnover adynamic bone.

Class

Active vitamin D3 hormone; nuclear vitamin-D-receptor agonist

Used To Treat

Selected secondary hyperparathyroidism/metabolic bone disease in moderate-to-severe CKD, hypocalcemia in chronic dialysis, and hypocalcemia from postsurgical, idiopathic or pseudo-hypoparathyroidism.

Mechanism

Calcitriol enters cells, binds VDR, heterodimerizes with RXR and regulates vitamin-D-response elements in DNA. Enterocyte calcium channels, calbindin and transport proteins increase; phosphate absorption also rises. Parathyroid transcription and gland proliferation fall. Because the drug is already active, its therapeutic window is narrow and dose changes can produce days of altered mineral balance without a kidney activation checkpoint.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Excess calcitriol causes hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria and hyperphosphatemia. During titration, calcium requires frequent measurement; stop promptly for hypercalcemia. Chronic calcium-phosphate excess can calcify vessels, kidneys and other soft tissues. Hypercalcemia increases digitalis arrhythmia risk.

Description

Calcitriol is 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3, the active hormone normally produced by renal 1-alpha-hydroxylation. Giving the active form bypasses a failing kidney or absent PTH activation signal. Nuclear VDR activation increases intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption and suppresses PTH, correcting hypocalcemia but also risking hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia and soft-tissue or vascular calcification.

Adverse Effects

  • Weakness, headache, nausea, constipation, abdominal pain and anorexia

  • Polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration, confusion and arrhythmia from hypercalcemia

  • Hyperphosphatemia, nephrocalcinosis and soft-tissue/vascular calcification

  • Adynamic bone if PTH is oversuppressed in CKD
