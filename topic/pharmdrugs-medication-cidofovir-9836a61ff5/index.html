Class

Acyclic nucleotide phosphonate and host-kinase-activated viral DNA-polymerase inhibitor

Used To Treat

CMV retinitis in adults with AIDS under current labeling, with specialist off-label use for selected severe DNA-virus infections. It is generally a salvage option because nephrotoxicity is substantial and every dose requires probenecid plus intravenous saline.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: dose-dependent nephrotoxicity can produce acute renal failure and dialysis; cidofovir is contraindicated above label renal thresholds, with significant proteinuria, or with concurrent nephrotoxins. Administer every dose with probenecid and IV saline exactly as labeled. Neutropenia requires CBC monitoring. Animal data show carcinogenicity, teratogenicity, and hypospermia; use contraception and counsel about fertility. Intravitreal administration is contraindicated because severe uveitis and ocular hypotony have occurred.

Mechanism

Cellular kinases convert cidofovir to cidofovir diphosphate and a long-lived choline adduct. Cidofovir diphosphate competes with deoxycytidine triphosphate at viral DNA polymerase, becomes incorporated into DNA, and slows or terminates chain elongation after consecutive incorporation events. Because the first phosphate is built into the phosphonate structure, viral kinase loss does not block activation. CMV UL54 polymerase mutation can reduce susceptibility and can overlap with ganciclovir resistance. In the kidney, basolateral OAT1 transports cidofovir from blood into proximal tubular cells more efficiently than it exits into urine. Probenecid blocks OAT1 and saline reduces concentration, decreasing but not eliminating tubular necrosis, proteinuria, glycosuria, bicarbonate loss, and falling filtration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before every dose, verify creatinine, calculated clearance, urine protein, CBC, probenecid timing, saline orders, medication holds, weight, and pregnancy precautions.

  • A small creatinine rise or new proteinuria can be the first sign of proximal tubular injury; do not wait for oliguria because Fanconi-type losses may precede major filtration failure.

Contraindications

  • Baseline renal dysfunction or proteinuria beyond label thresholds, inability to receive probenecid or saline, concurrent nephrotoxic medication, and direct intraocular administration.

  • Sulfonamide hypersensitivity complicates required probenecid; specialist risk assessment is necessary rather than omitting renal protection.

Interactions

  • Nephrotoxins must be stopped for the label-specified interval and avoided; tenofovir, aminoglycosides, amphotericin, NSAIDs, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine deserve particular scrutiny.

  • Probenecid changes renal secretion of many drugs, including penicillins, methotrexate, and others; review the entire medication list around each infusion.

Key Labs

  • Serum creatinine and calculated clearance, urine protein, urinalysis for glucose/protein, bicarbonate, phosphate, potassium, CBC with differential, and liver tests

  • Visual symptoms and ophthalmologic findings, hydration, urine output, infusion reactions, probenecid tolerance, pregnancy prevention, and CMV response/resistance

Nclex Traps

  • Probenecid and saline are required nephroprotection with cidofovir; giving the antiviral alone is unsafe.

  • Cidofovir bypasses UL97 but still depends on viral polymerase susceptibility, so UL54 resistance can matter.

Description

Cidofovir is an acyclic cytosine nucleotide-phosphonate antiviral that already contains a phosphate-like group, bypasses viral thymidine kinase and CMV UL97, and is converted by host kinases into a diphosphate that inhibits viral DNA polymerase. That activation independence can preserve activity against UL97-mutant CMV or thymidine-kinase-deficient herpesvirus. Its defining problem is active uptake by organic-anion transporter 1 into proximal tubular cells, where high intracellular concentrations cause dose-dependent nephrotoxicity. Probenecid competes for transporter uptake and IV saline lowers tubular concentration. These protective measures are mandatory components of administration, not optional add-ons.

Adverse Effects

  • Proteinuria, creatinine elevation, nausea, vomiting, fever, rash, headache, and probenecid-related hypersensitivity or GI effects

  • Proximal tubular injury/Fanconi syndrome, acute kidney failure, neutropenia, uveitis, ocular hypotony, metabolic acidosis, fertility impairment, and fetal toxicity
