Definition

Hoover respiratory sign is an observed breathing sign. It is a clue to severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease. A positive finding means the lower ribs move inward rather than outward during inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hoover respiratory sign means the lower ribs move inward rather than outward during inspiration.

Why It Matters

The observed Hoover respiratory sign finding can signal severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease. A flattened diaphragm pulls horizontally on the lower ribs instead of lifting them outward. Hoover respiratory sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease

Assessment Method

Observe or palpate the lower lateral rib margins during inspiration.

Positive Finding

The lower ribs move inward rather than outward during inspiration.

Mechanism

A flattened diaphragm pulls horizontally on the lower ribs instead of lifting them outward.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hoover respiratory sign finding raises concern for severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease because a flattened diaphragm pulls horizontally on the lower ribs instead of lifting them outward. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hoover respiratory sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hoover respiratory sign to focus evaluation of severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease and the reviewed alternatives: pectus deformity, paradoxical breathing, flail chest. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe or palpate the lower lateral rib margins during inspiration. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hoover respiratory sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity and chest-wall deformity reduce visibility; it does not grade obstruction alone. Therefore, Hoover respiratory sign cannot confirm or exclude severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pectus deformity

  • paradoxical breathing

  • flail chest

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pectus deformity, paradoxical breathing, flail chest.

False Negative Situations

Hoover respiratory sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease.

Modern Status

Current adjunct for severe hyperinflation. When performed as described, Hoover respiratory sign can adjust concern for severe hyperinflation in obstructive lung disease, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Hoover respiratory sign: Obesity and chest-wall deformity reduce visibility; it does not grade obstruction alone.

Observe Hoover respiratory sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe or palpate the lower lateral rib margins during inspiration. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not confuse this respiratory Hoover sign with the functional leg-weakness test.
