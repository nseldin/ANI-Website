Definition

Ortolani sign is an infant hip-reduction maneuver. It is a clue to reducible developmental hip dislocation. A positive finding means a palpable clunk indicates the femoral head reducing into the acetabulum.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ortolani sign means a palpable clunk indicates the femoral head reducing into the acetabulum.

Why It Matters

A positive Ortolani sign response can signal reducible developmental hip dislocation. A dislocated femoral head crosses the posterior acetabular rim into the socket. Ortolani sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • reducible developmental hip dislocation

Assessment Method

With the infant supine, flex hips and knees, gently abduct while lifting the greater trochanter anteriorly.

Positive Finding

A palpable clunk indicates the femoral head reducing into the acetabulum.

Mechanism

A dislocated femoral head crosses the posterior acetabular rim into the socket.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ortolani sign response raises concern for reducible developmental hip dislocation because a dislocated femoral head crosses the posterior acetabular rim into the socket. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ortolani sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ortolani sign to focus evaluation of reducible developmental hip dislocation and the reviewed alternatives: tendon snap, normal ligament click, fracture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the infant supine, flex hips and knees, gently abduct while lifting the greater trochanter anteriorly. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ortolani sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A soft click is not a clunk and older fixed dislocations may be negative. Therefore, Ortolani sign cannot confirm or exclude reducible developmental hip dislocation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tendon snap

  • normal ligament click

  • fracture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from tendon snap, normal ligament click, fracture, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ortolani sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ortolani sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the infant supine, flex hips and knees, gently abduct while lifting the greater trochanter anteriorly. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use minimal force and limit repeated examinations.
