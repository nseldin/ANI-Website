Class

Cardiac myosin inhibitor

Used To Treat

Adults with symptomatic NYHA class II-III obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy to improve functional capacity and symptoms by reducing dynamic left-ventricular outflow obstruction.

Boxed Warning

Risk of heart failure from systolic dysfunction: assess LVEF before and during therapy, do not initiate when LVEF is below the labeled threshold, and interrupt for LVEF below 50% or worsening heart-failure status. CYP2C19/CYP3A interactions can abruptly raise exposure; restricted safety-program requirements apply under current labeling.

Mechanism

Mavacamten allosterically and reversibly inhibits cardiac myosin, shifting more myosin heads into an energy-sparing super-relaxed state and reducing force-producing systolic and residual diastolic cross bridges. This lowers hypercontractility and LVOT gradient but can excessively depress systolic function.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify scheduled echocardiograms/LVEF and assess dyspnea, edema, chest pain, fatigue, palpitations, infection, or new atrial fibrillation before each treatment decision.

  • Screen every medication change for CYP2C19/CYP3A effects and avoid unplanned stacking with other negative inotropes.

Key Labs

  • Echocardiographic LVEF and LVOT gradient, rhythm/ECG context, pregnancy testing when applicable, and medication-interaction review.

Nclex Traps

  • Mavacamten treats hypercontractility by reducing myosin force; its signature danger is converting obstructive HCM into systolic dysfunction.

Description

Mavacamten is a cardiac myosin inhibitor that directly turns down excessive actin-myosin cross-bridge formation in obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. It can relieve hypercontractile LV outflow obstruction, but too much myosin inhibition lowers ejection fraction and causes systolic heart failure, making scheduled echocardiography and interaction control part of the drug's identity.
