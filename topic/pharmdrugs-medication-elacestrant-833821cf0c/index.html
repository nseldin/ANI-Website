Class

Oral selective estrogen-receptor degrader and antagonist

Description

Elacestrant is an oral endocrine therapy that binds estrogen receptor alpha, blocks estrogen-driven transcription, and promotes receptor degradation. It is selected for tumors with an ESR1 mutation because those mutations can keep the receptor active despite aromatase inhibition. The mutation does not make every endocrine drug useless, but it changes which pathway is most rational to target and must be confirmed with an FDA-authorized test.

Mechanism

Elacestrant binds estrogen receptor alpha and acts as an antagonist while destabilizing the receptor and promoting degradation. Less receptor protein means less estrogen-response gene transcription and slower proliferation in ER-positive tumor cells. ESR1 ligand-binding-domain mutations can produce estrogen-independent receptor activity and resistance to prior endocrine therapy; direct receptor degradation can retain activity in this setting.

Used To Treat

Postmenopausal women or adult men with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer that progressed after at least one line of endocrine therapy.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. Major risks include dyslipidemia, embryo-fetal harm, hepatically increased exposure, and clinically important CYP3A interactions.

Contraindications

  • The current label lists no formal contraindication; avoid use in severe hepatic impairment and reduce the dose in moderate hepatic impairment according to the label.

  • Do not use without the required ESR1-mutation context because the indication is biomarker-selected, not a general substitute for every endocrine regimen.

Adverse Effects

  • Musculoskeletal pain, nausea, fatigue, vomiting, decreased appetite, diarrhea, headache, constipation, abdominal pain, hot flush, and dyspepsia.

  • Increased cholesterol and triglycerides, increased liver tests, embryo-fetal harm, and reduced treatment effectiveness from interacting CYP3A inducers.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors because they increase elacestrant exposure and toxicity.

  • Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A inducers because they reduce exposure and can cause treatment failure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify ER-positive, HER2-negative disease, documented ESR1 mutation, prior endocrine therapy, menopausal or male status, liver function, pregnancy status when applicable, and the complete interaction list.

  • Measure lipids before treatment and periodically because the drug can raise cholesterol and triglycerides.

  • Trend pain, nausea, nutrition, adherence, hepatic symptoms, and objective tumor response rather than assuming symptom improvement proves cancer control.

Key Labs

  • Fasting lipid panel before and during therapy

  • Liver tests and hepatic-impairment assessment

  • Pregnancy test when applicable

  • Disease-directed imaging and tumor assessment

Administration

  • Take 345 mg once daily with food at approximately the same time; food reduces nausea and vomiting and supports consistent exposure.

  • Swallow tablets whole. Use the label's missed-dose and dose-reduction rules rather than doubling.

Patient Education

  • Report severe persistent nausea, inability to eat, jaundice, dark urine, or new severe pain. Keep lipid and oncology follow-up.

  • Use effective contraception and do not breastfeed during treatment and for the label-defined period afterward.

Nclex Traps

  • Elacestrant is used in a biomarker-selected metastatic breast-cancer pathway; do not treat it as general estrogen replacement or routine endocrine therapy for every breast cancer.

  • Food administration, lipid monitoring, and CYP3A interaction review are part of treatment safety, not optional counseling details.

Routes

  • Oral tablet taken once daily with food.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Clinically important dyslipidemia or severe gastrointestinal intolerance that causes dehydration or inability to maintain intake.

  • Embryo-fetal toxicity.

Toxicity Management

  • No specific antidote exists. Hold treatment and provide antiemetic, hydration, metabolic, or hepatic support according to symptoms; contact oncology or poison control after a substantial overdose.

Special Populations

  • Indicated for postmenopausal women or adult men in the labeled breast-cancer setting; it can cause fetal harm, and breastfeeding is avoided during and after therapy for the label-defined interval.

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established. Older adults require attention to intake, falls, lipids, and interacting drugs.

  • Avoid severe hepatic impairment and use the label-directed modification in moderate impairment; verify current guidance when renal function is severely reduced.
