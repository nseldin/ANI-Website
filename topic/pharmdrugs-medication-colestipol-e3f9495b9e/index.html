Contraindications

  • Complete biliary obstruction or product hypersensitivity

  • Clarify severe constipation, bowel obstruction risk, dysphagia, gastroparesis and major GI surgery

  • Use caution when triglycerides are high or pancreatitis risk exists

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain a complete medication/supplement timing schedule and teach one-tablet-at-a-time swallowing with liquid.

  • Increase fiber/fluid only when clinically safe and monitor for absent stool, severe pain, distention or vomiting.

Interactions

  • Take other drugs at least 1 hour before or 4 hours after colestipol.

  • Can reduce absorption of thyroid hormone, propranolol, digoxin and many other oral agents; use product-specific monitoring.

  • Can reduce vitamins A, D, E and K and affect warfarin response indirectly.

Key Labs

  • Fasting lipid panel including triglycerides

  • TSH/free T4, INR or drug-specific monitoring when interacting therapies are used

  • Fat-soluble vitamin/coagulation status when long-term deficiency risk is present

Nclex Traps

  • Nonabsorbed does not mean interaction-free.

  • Colestipol lowers LDL but may raise triglycerides.

  • A binder used for bile-acid diarrhea is an off-label symptom-pathway use, not proof that every chronic diarrhea is bile-acid mediated.

Used To Treat

Adjunctive LDL-C reduction in primary hypercholesterolemia; it is also commonly used off-label to bind excess bile acids in bile-acid diarrhea after appropriate diagnostic evaluation.

Mechanism

Protonated amine groups exchange chloride for higher-affinity bile-acid anions. Interruption of enterohepatic recycling increases cholesterol 7-alpha-hydroxylase activity and hepatic cholesterol demand, which upregulates LDL-receptor-mediated clearance. Serum triglycerides can rise or remain unchanged, so the lipid effect is not uniformly beneficial.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Screen for severe constipation, obstruction, dysphagia and elevated triglycerides. Tablets must be swallowed one at a time with adequate liquid because esophageal obstruction has occurred. Separate other oral drugs by at least 1 hour before or 4 hours after unless a product-specific instruction is stricter.

Description

Colestipol is a large, water-insoluble, nonabsorbed anion-exchange polymer that binds intestinal bile acids and carries them out in stool. The liver responds by converting more cholesterol into bile acids and increasing LDL receptors, lowering serum LDL. Because the resin binds rather than recognizes molecules, it can also trap medications and vitamins and can worsen constipation or obstruction.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, bloating, abdominal pain, nausea and fecal impaction

  • Dysphagia or esophageal/GI obstruction

  • Increased triglycerides and reduced absorption of fat-soluble vitamins or other drugs

  • Rare hyperchloremic acidosis during prolonged use
