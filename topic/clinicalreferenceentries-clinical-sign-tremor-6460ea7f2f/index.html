Definition

Tremor is an involuntary rhythmic back-and-forth movement around a fixed position. It may occur at rest, while holding a posture, during movement, or as a target is approached.

Plain Language

Tremor means rhythmic shaking that the person is not intentionally producing.

Why It Matters

The timing and pattern help separate physiologic, essential, parkinsonian, cerebellar, medication-related, metabolic, and withdrawal causes. A new tremor with neurologic deficits, low glucose, fever, poisoning, or autonomic instability can require urgent treatment.

Assessment Method

Observe supported limbs at rest, sustained posture, and goal-directed movement; document body distribution, symmetry, rhythm, amplitude, frequency estimate, rest versus action relationship, onset, functional effect, handwriting or utensil use, medications, caffeine, alcohol and withdrawal history, glucose and metabolic context, and other neurologic findings.

Positive Finding

A body part moves back and forth rhythmically around a fixed position at rest, while holding posture, during movement, or near a target.

Mechanism

Alternating or synchronous activation within motor-control circuits produces rhythmic oscillation; the involved circuit and trigger differ among tremor syndromes.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Tremor response raises concern for oscillatory motor activity that may be physiologic, essential, parkinsonian, cerebellar, dystonic, metabolic, medication-induced, toxic, or withdrawal-related because alternating or synchronous activation within motor-control circuits produces rhythmic oscillation; the involved circuit and trigger differ among tremor syndromes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tremor as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal physiologic tremor can increase with anxiety, fatigue, fever, caffeine, medication, or withdrawal, and visual inspection alone cannot reliably determine exact frequency or cause. Therefore, Tremor cannot confirm or exclude oscillatory motor activity that may be physiologic, essential, parkinsonian, cerebellar, dystonic, metabolic, medication-induced, toxic, or withdrawal-related by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from clonus, myoclonus, chorea, fasciculations, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Tremor may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
