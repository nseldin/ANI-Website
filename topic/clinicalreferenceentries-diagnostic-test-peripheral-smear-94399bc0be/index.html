Summary

Peripheral smear is a high-yield Hematology diagnostic reference. Microscopic blood-cell review for morphology, hemolysis, blasts, parasites, and platelet clues.

Quick Answer

Peripheral smear: Microscopic blood-cell review for morphology, hemolysis, blasts, parasites, and platelet clues. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Normal morphology

    Meaning

    No major abnormal cell-shape, blast, parasite, or platelet morphology is seen.

  • Label

    Abnormal morphology

    Meaning

    Schistocytes, blasts, sickled cells, spherocytes, target cells, parasites, or platelet clumping can redirect diagnosis urgently.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Smear findings can explain why automated CBC numbers look abnormal and may reveal hemolysis, leukemia, or malaria-type emergencies.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Microscopic blood-cell review for morphology, hemolysis, blasts, parasites, and platelet clues.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
