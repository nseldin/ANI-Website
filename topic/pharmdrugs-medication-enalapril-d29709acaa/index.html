Used To Treat

Hypertension, symptomatic heart failure, and clinically stable asymptomatic left-ventricular dysfunction with reduced ejection fraction according to current product labeling. Enalapril's outcome role in ventricular dysfunction is more specific than simply calling it a blood-pressure drug.

Mechanism

After absorption, hepatic esterases convert enalapril to enalaprilat, which competitively inhibits ACE. Lower angiotensin II reduces AT1-mediated arteriolar constriction, aldosterone release, sodium retention, sympathetic drive, and myocardial fibrosis or hypertrophy. Higher bradykinin adds endothelial nitric-oxide and prostacyclin vasodilation while causing cough or angioedema in susceptible patients. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers intraglomerular pressure, explaining both albuminuria reduction and a possible early GFR fall. Enalaprilat binds ACE more persistently than the parent drug and is cleared mainly through the kidney, so renal dysfunction extends effect. In heart failure, lowering afterload and neurohormonal remodeling can improve forward flow and outcomes, but excessive pressure reduction in a hyponatremic, overdiuresed, or low-output patient can reduce kidney and cerebral perfusion.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Enalapril and its active metabolite cross into the mechanism that supports fetal renal function. Stop promptly when pregnancy is detected.

Contraindications

  • Previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, hereditary or idiopathic angioedema, pregnancy, and hypersensitivity.

  • Do not pair with sacubitril/valsartan or within 36 hours, and do not combine aliskiren in diabetes.

  • Pregnancy, previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, and hereditary or idiopathic angioedema where specified by labeling.

  • Do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or administer within 36 hours; do not combine aliskiren with an ACE inhibitor in diabetes.

Nursing Essentials

  • In heart failure, check sodium, creatinine, pressure, diuretic intensity, and volume before the first dose because low effective volume predicts symptomatic hypotension.

  • Do not substitute IV enalaprilat milligram-for-milligram for oral enalapril without product-specific guidance; prodrug conversion and route change the exposure profile.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, cough, facial or tongue swelling, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that persistent dry cough should be evaluated, while lip, tongue, throat, voice, or swallowing change is an emergency because bradykinin swelling can obstruct the airway.

Interactions

  • Diuretics can expose first-dose hypotension when intravascular volume is low; correcting volume or adjusting the diuretic may be safer than abandoning long-term benefit.

  • Lithium, NSAIDs, potassium products, and duplicate RAAS blockade require active monitoring.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and duplicate RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • ACE inhibitor plus sacubitril/valsartan is contraindicated because overlapping peptide-degradation blockade increases angioedema risk.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, sodium in heart-failure risk states, and urine output.

  • Trend heart-failure symptoms, hospitalization risk markers, congestion, and ventricular-function context rather than expecting an immediate inotropic effect.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, blood pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Enalapril is the oral prodrug; enalaprilat is the active molecule and IV product. Similar names do not make routes interchangeable.

  • Asymptomatic LV dysfunction is still a disease-modifying indication in selected patients because treatment can delay overt heart failure.

  • ACE inhibition lowers angiotensin II and raises bradykinin; those are separate consequences of inhibiting the same enzyme.

  • A modest early creatinine rise can reflect lower intraglomerular pressure, but oliguria, severe hyperkalemia, symptoms, or a large progressive decline is not routine adaptation.

Description

Enalapril is an oral ACE-inhibitor prodrug that is hydrolyzed to the active diacid enalaprilat. It reduces angiotensin II and aldosterone, increases bradykinin, lowers afterload and filling pressure, and reduces maladaptive ventricular remodeling. Its important distinction is evidence and formulation: oral enalapril treats hypertension and has labeled survival or hospitalization benefits in symptomatic heart failure and progression benefits in selected asymptomatic left-ventricular dysfunction. Enalaprilat is also available intravenously as a separate card, but IV access does not make the class rapidly titratable like nicardipine. The active metabolite is predominantly renally eliminated and can accumulate when kidney function falls. Volume-depleted heart-failure patients may become markedly hypotensive after the first dose because their pressure and filtration are strongly supported by RAAS tone.

Adverse Effects

  • First-dose hypotension is especially important in heart failure, hyponatremia, high-dose diuretic use, or volume depletion.

  • Renal accumulation of enalaprilat can prolong hypotension, potassium elevation, and kidney-function effects.

  • Class effects include dry cough, dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, creatinine increase, and less commonly rash or taste change.

  • Airway angioedema, severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, neutropenia in susceptible patients, hepatic injury, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
