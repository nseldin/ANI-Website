Definition

Finkelstein test is a bedside tendon-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to de Quervain tenosynovitis. A positive finding means pain occurs over the first dorsal extensor compartment.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Finkelstein test means pain occurs over the first dorsal extensor compartment.

Why It Matters

A positive Finkelstein test response can signal de Quervain tenosynovitis. The maneuver tensions abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis within an inflamed sheath. Finkelstein test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • de Quervain tenosynovitis

Assessment Method

Grasp the thumb and gently ulnar-deviate the wrist.

Positive Finding

Pain occurs over the first dorsal extensor compartment.

Mechanism

The maneuver tensions abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis within an inflamed sheath.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Finkelstein test response raises concern for de Quervain tenosynovitis because the maneuver tensions abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis within an inflamed sheath. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Finkelstein test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Finkelstein test to focus evaluation of de Quervain tenosynovitis and the reviewed alternatives: scaphoid injury, radial neuritis, CMC osteoarthritis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Grasp the thumb and gently ulnar-deviate the wrist. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Finkelstein test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Aggressive deviation hurts normal wrists and localization matters. Therefore, Finkelstein test cannot confirm or exclude de Quervain tenosynovitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • scaphoid injury

  • radial neuritis

  • CMC osteoarthritis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from scaphoid injury, radial neuritis, CMC osteoarthritis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Finkelstein test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; distinguish from Eichhoff self-test. When performed as described, Finkelstein test can adjust concern for de Quervain tenosynovitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Finkelstein test: Aggressive deviation hurts normal wrists and localization matters.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Finkelstein test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Grasp the thumb and gently ulnar-deviate the wrist. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use the true examiner-controlled technique gently; do not force a fist around the thumb.
