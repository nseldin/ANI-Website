Class Example Names

  • Mirabegron

Used To Treat

Overactive bladder with urgency, frequency, and urge urinary incontinence. Vibegron also has a current indication for OAB in adult men receiving pharmacologic treatment for benign prostatic hyperplasia.

Description

Mirabegron and vibegron improve the bladder's storage phase without blocking muscarinic receptors. Beta-3 receptor activation raises cyclic AMP inside detrusor smooth muscle, relaxing the bladder wall while it fills so capacity increases and urgency contractions become less likely. They avoid much of the dry mouth, constipation, heat intolerance, and cognitive burden of antimuscarinics, but they can still worsen urinary retention when an obstructed outlet must push against a relaxed, increasingly full bladder.

Mechanism

Beta-3 receptors couple primarily to Gs proteins. Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, cyclic AMP rises, protein kinase A changes calcium-handling and contractile proteins, and detrusor tone falls during filling. This does not directly open the urethral outlet or force an atonic bladder to empty. Mirabegron can increase blood pressure and is a moderate CYP2D6 inhibitor; vibegron has not shown clinically meaningful CYP2D6 inhibition and does not carry mirabegron's severe-uncontrolled-hypertension warning, although both require retention surveillance and vibegron can increase digoxin exposure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Assess bladder outlet obstruction and post-void residual when symptoms suggest retention. Mirabegron is not recommended in severe uncontrolled hypertension and can raise exposure to CYP2D6 substrates. Vibegron has postmarketing angioedema and a digoxin interaction. Neither drug is an emergency treatment for acute urinary retention.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nasopharyngitis, urinary tract infection and GI symptoms

  • Urinary retention, especially with outlet obstruction or combined antimuscarinic therapy

  • Mirabegron: hypertension, tachycardia and CYP2D6-mediated interactions

  • Vibegron: nausea, diarrhea, headache and rare angioedema

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity; vibegron labeling specifically contraindicates prior hypersensitivity

  • Do not mistake overflow retention or obstruction for uncomplicated OAB

  • Mirabegron: avoid severe uncontrolled hypertension and apply renal/hepatic dose limits

Nursing Essentials

  • Establish urgency/frequency pattern, urinalysis when indicated, post-void residual, blood pressure and evidence of BPH, prolapse, constipation or neurologic bladder before treatment.

  • Reassess after several weeks because bladder remodeling and symptom diaries, not one dose, establish benefit.

Interactions

  • Mirabegron inhibits CYP2D6 and can raise metoprolol, flecainide, propafenone, tricyclic and other narrow-index substrate exposure.

  • Mirabegron and vibegron can increase digoxin exposure; follow product-specific initiation and level guidance.

  • Combining either beta-3 agonist with an antimuscarinic increases retention risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure and pulse, especially with mirabegron

  • Renal and hepatic function for product-specific dosing

  • Post-void residual, symptom/voiding diary and digoxin concentration when relevant

Nclex Traps

  • Beta-3 agonists relax detrusor during filling; bethanechol contracts detrusor for nonobstructive emptying.

  • Vibegron is not simply mirabegron without a brand name: interaction and hypertension labeling differ.

  • Less anticholinergic burden does not mean no urinary-retention risk.
