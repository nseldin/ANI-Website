Used To Treat

Opioid-induced constipation in adults with chronic non-cancer pain, including stable pain related to prior cancer treatment.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for naloxegol in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Peripheral mu-receptor blockade reverses opioid suppression of enteric acetylcholine release, secretion, and peristalsis. PEGylation lowers passive CNS penetration and P-glycoprotein pumps the drug outward, limiting reversal of central analgesia at usual exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Stop maintenance laxatives before starting as directed, give on an empty stomach, and screen obstruction, bowel-wall lesions, renal function, strong/moderate CYP3A drugs, grapefruit, and P-gp interactions.

  • Monitor severe/persistent abdominal pain, diarrhea, dehydration, GI perforation, sweating/chills/yawning/rhinorrhea or loss of analgesia suggesting withdrawal.

Key Labs

  • Renal function for dose selection, hydration/electrolytes if diarrhea occurs, and bowel/withdrawal assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated because excess naloxegol can stop behaving as a quietly peripheral antagonist.

Description

Naloxegol is naloxone modified with polyethylene glycol so it crosses the blood-brain barrier poorly and antagonizes mu receptors mainly in the gut. It restores motility while usually preserving analgesia, but strong CYP3A inhibition can raise exposure enough to cause severe abdominal pain, diarrhea, and opioid withdrawal; obstruction and perforation risk must be excluded.
