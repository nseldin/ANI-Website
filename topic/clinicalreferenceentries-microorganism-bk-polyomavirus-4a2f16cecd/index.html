Definition

BK polyomavirus is a clinically important virus associated with BK polyomavirus-associated nephropathy and hemorrhagic cystitis after transplantation. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    BK polyomavirus persists in the urinary tract after common silent infection and can reactivate during immunosuppression, injuring renal tubular or urothelial cells. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    BK polyomavirus is associated with BK polyomavirus-associated nephropathy and hemorrhagic cystitis after transplantation. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
