Plain Meaning

How easily water, proteins, and solutes can move through the capillary wall into surrounding tissue.

Pathology

Capillary permeability is the leakiness of the small-vessel barrier. When endothelial cells and their junctions become inflamed or injured, fluid and proteins can leave the vascular space, causing edema, third spacing, low effective circulating volume, and organ swelling even when total body fluid is high.

Pathophysiology

Histamine, cytokines, burns, sepsis, anaphylaxis, ARDS, trauma, pancreatitis, and ischemia-reperfusion can open the endothelial barrier. Protein-rich fluid leaving capillaries lowers intravascular oncotic pull and worsens edema; lymphatic drainage may be overwhelmed.

Etiology

Sepsis, burns, anaphylaxis, pancreatitis, ARDS, major trauma, reperfusion injury, systemic inflammation, toxins, and severe allergic reactions.

Signs Symptoms

  • Rapid swelling, weeping edema, hypotension with warm skin in distributive shock, pulmonary edema in ARDS, ascites/effusions, or falling urine output from low effective circulating volume

  • Edema can coexist with intravascular depletion because the fluid has moved out of the usable vascular space

Diagnostics

  • Trend blood pressure/MAP, urine output, mental status, lactate, daily weight, edema, oxygenation, albumin, inflammatory/infectious clues, and chest imaging when lungs are involved

  • Interpret with Starling forces: permeability is different from high hydrostatic pressure or low oncotic pressure, though they can coexist.

  • In lung injury, connect capillary leak with noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, low compliance, severe shunt physiology, and refractory hypoxemia.

Treatments

  • Treat the trigger: source control/antibiotics for sepsis, epinephrine for anaphylaxis, burn/trauma care, pancreatitis care, or ARDS lung-protective support as ordered.

  • Support perfusion and oxygenation while avoiding reflexively flooding a leaky vascular bed.

Nursing Priorities

  • Recognize that edema can coexist with low effective circulating volume.

  • Trend MAP, urine output, mentation, lactate, oxygenation, daily weight, edema, albumin, and response to fluids/vasopressors/diuresis.

  • Escalate worsening respiratory distress, hypotension, falling urine output, mottling, confusion, or rapidly spreading edema.

Complications

  • Third spacing

  • Distributive shock

  • ARDS/noncardiogenic pulmonary edema

  • Hypotension

  • Low effective circulating volume

  • Organ edema and dysfunction

Patient Education

  • In acute illness, explain that swelling can happen because tiny vessels are leaky, not simply because too much fluid was taken in.

Nclex Traps

  • Capillary leak can make the client look fluid overloaded while organs are underperfused.

  • Edema from permeability is not the same mechanism as edema from high hydrostatic pressure.

  • ARDS pulmonary edema is permeability-driven, not classic left-sided heart failure fluid backup.
