    Definition

  • Content

    Bacteria and fungi carry out cellular metabolism, whereas viruses require living host cells for replication. Parasites have life-cycle stages adapted to particular hosts or tissues, and prions propagate by inducing abnormal protein folding rather than by using nucleic acid.

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Clinically important distinctions include aerobic versus anaerobic growth, intracellular versus extracellular replication, latent viral infection, and life-cycle transitions in parasites.

    Replication biology explains why specimen timing, tissue site, host immunity, and antimicrobial target selection matter. It also helps explain latency, relapse, persistent carriage, and why some drugs affect only actively replicating organisms.
