Used To Treat

Respiratory infections, atypical pneumonia, pertussis, selected STIs, MAC prophylaxis/treatment, and some GI motility uses depending on the agent.

Mechanism

Clarithromycin works by binding 23S rRNA on the 50S ribosomal subunit and blocking translocation during bacterial protein synthesis. It is often used for atypical respiratory pathogens; QT prolongation, CYP interactions with erythro/clarithro, GI effects, and local resistance matter.

Boxed Warning

Warnings vary; major concerns include QT prolongation, hepatotoxicity, and CYP interactions for selected agents.

Contraindications

  • Significant QT prolongation or interacting QT drugs may require clarification

  • serious prior macrolide reaction

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess QT risk, liver disease, interactions.

  • Teach to report palpitations/syncope or severe diarrhea.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Clarithromycin is categorized here as Macrolide 50S ribosomal inhibitor antibiotic. Pathway: Anti-infective medication > Protein synthesis inhibitor > Macrolide 50S inhibitor. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Warfarin, statins, QT-prolonging drugs; erythromycin/clarithromycin have more CYP interactions.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • LFTs if indicated

  • ECG/QT risk

  • INR if on warfarin

  • Allergies, cultures before first dose when ordered, renal function for renally cleared drugs, liver function for hepatotoxic drugs, CBC, diarrhea/C. difficile cues, and therapeutic levels for aminoglycosides/vancomycin when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Macrolide + other QT drugs can create torsades risk.

  • Do not delay urgent antibiotics for sepsis, but obtain cultures first if ordered and it does not delay care.
