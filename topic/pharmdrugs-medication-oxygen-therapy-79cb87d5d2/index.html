Used To Treat

Toxicity, overdose, reversal, or rescue situations for a specific medication or poison exposure.

Mechanism

Oxygen therapy works by raising the inspired oxygen gradient so alveolar oxygen and arterial oxygen content improve when ventilation/perfusion and hemoglobin allow. It treats hypoxemia but does not fix ventilation failure by itself, so work of breathing, CO2 retention risk, and pulse-ox/ABG response matter.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • Airway, breathing, circulation, mental status, ECG, glucose, electrolytes, renal/liver function, coagulation studies, drug levels when available, poison-control guidance, and recurrence after short-acting antidotes.

Nclex Traps

  • Antidote does not replace ABCs. Stabilize airway/breathing/circulation and monitor for recurrent toxicity.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for Oxygen therapy in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

Oxygen therapy is an antidote or reversal agent: it works by raising the inspired oxygen gradient so alveolar oxygen and arterial oxygen content improve when ventilation/perfusion and hemoglobin allow. It is used for toxicity, overdose, reversal, or rescue situations for a specific medication or poison exposure.
