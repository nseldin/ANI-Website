Definition

Gynecomastia means glandular breast tissue has enlarged in a male patient, usually as a mobile rubbery or firm disk centered beneath the nipple. Fat without gland enlargement is called pseudogynecomastia.

Plain Language

True gynecomastia is growth of breast gland tissue, not simply extra chest fat.

Why It Matters

Gynecomastia is often a benign physiologic or medication-related change, but it can also reveal hypogonadism, thyroid, liver, kidney, or testicular disease. A hard fixed or off-center mass, skin or nipple change, nipple discharge, abnormal nodes, testicular mass, or rapid one-sided growth requires prompt evaluation for cancer or another serious cause.

Associated Conditions

  • physiologic hormonal change in infancy, puberty, or aging, medication or substance effects, hypogonadism, liver or kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, malnutrition recovery, or hormone-producing testicular, adrenal, or other tumors

Assessment Method

Use consent, privacy, and a chaperone according to policy; inspect both breasts and palpate for a concentric subareolar glandular disk, tenderness, symmetry, skin or nipple change, discharge, axillary nodes, and an eccentric mass, then review onset, medications, substances, liver, kidney, thyroid, gonadal, and testicular findings.

Positive Finding

A rubbery or firm mobile disk of glandular tissue is centered beneath the nipple-areolar complex on one or both sides; recent tissue may be tender.

Mechanism

Estrogen action that is increased relative to androgen action stimulates breast ducts and stroma; the imbalance may be physiologic or caused by medication, organ disease, endocrine dysfunction, or tumor.

Evidence Limitations

Palpation is subjective, gland and fat often coexist, and tenderness or bilaterality cannot by itself exclude malignancy or establish a hormonal cause. Therefore, Gynecomastia cannot confirm or exclude physiologic hormonal change in infancy, puberty, or aging, medication or substance effects, hypogonadism, liver or kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, malnutrition recovery, or hormone-producing testicular, adrenal, or other tumors by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pseudogynecomastia, male breast cancer, lipoma, breast abscess.

False Negative Situations

Gynecomastia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of physiologic hormonal change in infancy, puberty, or aging, medication or substance effects, hypogonadism, liver or kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, malnutrition recovery, or hormone-producing testicular, adrenal, or other tumors.
