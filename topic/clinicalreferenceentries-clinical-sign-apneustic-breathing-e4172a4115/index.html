Definition

Apneustic breathing is an observed respiratory pattern. It is a clue to pontine dysfunction. A positive finding means prolonged inspiratory pauses occur before inadequate expiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Apneustic breathing means prolonged inspiratory pauses occur before inadequate expiration.

Why It Matters

The Apneustic breathing pattern can signal pontine dysfunction. Pontine injury disrupts the inspiratory off-switch that normally terminates inhalation. Apneustic breathing cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pontine dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe inspiratory and expiratory timing in a neurologically ill patient.

Positive Finding

Prolonged inspiratory pauses occur before inadequate expiration.

Mechanism

Pontine injury disrupts the inspiratory off-switch that normally terminates inhalation.

Clinical Interpretation

The Apneustic breathing pattern raises concern for pontine dysfunction because pontine injury disrupts the inspiratory off-switch that normally terminates inhalation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Apneustic breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Apneustic breathing to focus evaluation of pontine dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: agonal breathing, breath holding, ventilator dyssynchrony. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe inspiratory and expiratory timing in a neurologically ill patient. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Apneustic breathing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Ventilator settings and airway obstruction can mimic prolonged inspiration. Therefore, Apneustic breathing cannot confirm or exclude pontine dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • agonal breathing

  • breath holding

  • ventilator dyssynchrony

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by agonal breathing, breath holding, ventilator dyssynchrony without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Apneustic breathing may be absent even when pontine dysfunction is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.
