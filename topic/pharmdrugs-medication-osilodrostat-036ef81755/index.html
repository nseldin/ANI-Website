Adrenal cortisol-synthesis inhibitor (CYP11B1 inhibitor)

Used To Treat

Endogenous hypercortisolemia in adults with Cushing syndrome when pituitary/adrenal surgery is not an option or has not been curative.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for osilodrostat in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Osilodrostat inhibits CYP11B1, preventing 11-deoxycortisol from becoming cortisol. Cortisol falls, ACTH and upstream steroid precursors rise, and mineralocorticoid/androgen pathways may receive more substrate.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend urine-free or serum cortisol with symptoms, not the number alone; hold/adjust and give glucocorticoid rescue when protocol criteria for adrenal insufficiency are met.

  • Obtain ECG and correct potassium/magnesium; monitor BP, edema, glucose, infection/stress, acne/hirsutism, nausea, abdominal pain, weakness, and prolonged suppression after stopping.

Key Labs

  • Urine-free/serum cortisol, ACTH when ordered, potassium, magnesium, sodium, glucose, ECG/QTc, BP, and adrenal precursor/androgen studies when indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • A client can have low tissue cortisol despite an immunoassay result distorted by accumulated 11-deoxycortisol; symptoms and the ordered assay matter.

Description

Osilodrostat lowers cortisol by inhibiting adrenal 11-beta-hydroxylase, the final enzymatic step in cortisol synthesis. It can rapidly convert Cushing physiology into hypocortisolism or adrenal crisis, while accumulated precursors can drive hypertension, hypokalemia, edema, acne, and hirsutism; QT prolongation adds a separate rhythm risk.
