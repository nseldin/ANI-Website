Class

Potent moderately blood-soluble volatile halogenated general anesthetic

Used To Treat

Induction and especially maintenance of general anesthesia under trained anesthesia care; slower wash-in/wash-out than sevoflurane or desflurane makes emergence less rapid.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in known or suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility. Monitor for perioperative hyperkalemia in latent neuromuscular disease, QT prolongation, hepatic reaction and severe hypotension/respiratory depression. Desiccated CO2 absorbent can generate carbon monoxide; replace suspect absorbent before administration.

Mechanism

Isoflurane acts across GABA-A, glycine, potassium, nicotinic and other neural targets to suppress cortical integration and spinal motor responses. More anesthetic dissolves in blood before partial pressure equilibrates than with desflurane, producing slower wash-in/out. Systemic vascular resistance falls as concentration rises; ventilation becomes shallower and CO2 responsiveness decreases. Metabolism is minimal, so exhalation clears most drug.

Nursing Essentials

  • Continuously monitor inspired/end-tidal concentration, end-tidal CO2, oxygenation, ventilation, ECG, blood pressure and temperature; use a calibrated isoflurane vaporizer and functioning scavenging.

  • Expect reduced nondepolarizing paralytic requirement and slower recovery than with less soluble agents. Replace desiccated absorbent and consider carboxyhemoglobin when unexplained oxygen-delivery signs occur.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility, hypersensitivity to halogenated agents, or prior unexplained significant hepatic injury after exposure. Clarify severe hypovolemia/hypotension, long-QT risk and neuromuscular disease.

Interactions

  • Succinylcholine increases malignant-hyperthermia risk. Opioids, nitrous oxide and sedatives lower MAC. Isoflurane potentiates nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers. Other QT-prolonging or hypotensive drugs add risk.

Key Labs

  • Continuous end-tidal CO2 and anesthetic concentration, ECG, blood pressure, oxygenation and temperature

  • Carboxyhemoglobin after suspected absorber-related carbon monoxide exposure

  • Potassium, acid-base, lactate, creatine kinase, urine myoglobin and renal/coagulation studies for malignant hyperthermia

Nclex Traps

  • Greater blood solubility means slower, not faster, anesthetic concentration change.

  • Volatile anesthetics reduce the amount of nondepolarizing paralytic needed.

  • Normal pulse oximetry can be misleading in carbon monoxide exposure; co-oximetry is needed.

Description

Isoflurane is a potent volatile anesthetic that produces unconsciousness and immobility with dose-dependent vasodilation, hypotension and respiratory depression. Its greater blood solubility than sevoflurane or desflurane slows changes in depth and awakening. It is pungent enough to irritate the airway, strongly potentiates nondepolarizing paralytics, can trigger malignant hyperthermia and can form carbon monoxide when exposed to desiccated CO2 absorbents.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, respiratory depression, apnea, cough/breath-holding/laryngospasm, nausea/vomiting, shivering, delirium/agitation, arrhythmia/QT prolongation, malignant hyperthermia, perioperative hyperkalemia and rare hepatic injury.
