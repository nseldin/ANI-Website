Definition

Noble compression test is a bedside lateral-knee maneuver. It is a clue to iliotibial band friction syndrome. A positive finding means familiar lateral pain occurs near 30 degrees of flexion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Noble compression test means familiar lateral pain occurs near 30 degrees of flexion.

Why It Matters

A positive Noble compression test response can signal iliotibial band friction syndrome. The iliotibial band moves across compressed lateral epicondylar tissue at the symptomatic angle. Noble compression test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • iliotibial band friction syndrome

Assessment Method

Press over the lateral femoral epicondyle while extending the flexed knee.

Positive Finding

Familiar lateral pain occurs near 30 degrees of flexion.

Mechanism

The iliotibial band moves across compressed lateral epicondylar tissue at the symptomatic angle.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Noble compression test response raises concern for iliotibial band friction syndrome because the iliotibial band moves across compressed lateral epicondylar tissue at the symptomatic angle. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Noble compression test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Noble compression test to focus evaluation of iliotibial band friction syndrome and the reviewed alternatives: biceps femoris tendinopathy, popliteus injury, arthritis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Press over the lateral femoral epicondyle while extending the flexed knee. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Noble compression test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Local tenderness and other lateral-knee lesions reduce specificity. Therefore, Noble compression test cannot confirm or exclude iliotibial band friction syndrome by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • biceps femoris tendinopathy

  • popliteus injury

  • arthritis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from biceps femoris tendinopathy, popliteus injury, arthritis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Noble compression test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct. When performed as described, Noble compression test can adjust concern for iliotibial band friction syndrome, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Noble compression test: Local tenderness and other lateral-knee lesions reduce specificity.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Noble compression test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Press over the lateral femoral epicondyle while extending the flexed knee. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use moderate pressure; do not diagnose from generalized lateral tenderness.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Noble compression test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Familiar lateral pain occurs near 30 degrees of flexion. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Press over the lateral femoral epicondyle while extending the flexed knee. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, iliotibial band friction syndrome, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
