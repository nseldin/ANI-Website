Definition

Whispered pectoriloquy is an auscultatory voice sign. It is a clue to lung consolidation. A positive finding means whispered words become unusually clear and distinct.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Whispered pectoriloquy means whispered words become unusually clear and distinct.

Why It Matters

A positive Whispered pectoriloquy response can signal lung consolidation. Consolidated lung transmits high-frequency voice vibrations better than normally aerated lung. Whispered pectoriloquy cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lung consolidation

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to whisper a phrase while auscultating symmetric lung fields.

Positive Finding

Whispered words become unusually clear and distinct.

Mechanism

Consolidated lung transmits high-frequency voice vibrations better than normally aerated lung.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Whispered pectoriloquy response raises concern for lung consolidation because consolidated lung transmits high-frequency voice vibrations better than normally aerated lung. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Whispered pectoriloquy does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Whispered pectoriloquy to focus evaluation of lung consolidation and the reviewed alternatives: atelectasis, fibrosis, thin chest wall. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to whisper a phrase while auscultating symmetric lung fields. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Whispered pectoriloquy as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Quiet environment, voice strength, and chest thickness affect detection. Therefore, Whispered pectoriloquy cannot confirm or exclude lung consolidation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • atelectasis

  • fibrosis

  • thin chest wall

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from atelectasis, fibrosis, thin chest wall, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Whispered pectoriloquy may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; imaging confirms cause. When performed as described, Whispered pectoriloquy can adjust concern for lung consolidation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Whispered pectoriloquy: Quiet environment, voice strength, and chest thickness affect detection.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Whispered pectoriloquy, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to whisper a phrase while auscultating symmetric lung fields. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Compare symmetric sites and do not diagnose bacterial pneumonia from voice transmission alone.
