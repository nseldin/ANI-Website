Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric ages, adjunctive treatment of systolic heart failure, and reduction of mortality in hemodynamically stable patients treated within 24 hours after acute myocardial infarction. Kidney or albuminuria use should be tied to the patient's diagnosis and current guideline rather than assumed from the class name alone.

Mechanism

Lisinopril competitively inhibits ACE, also called kininase II. Less conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II reduces AT1-receptor activation, vascular smooth-muscle calcium signaling, aldosterone secretion, sympathetic facilitation, sodium retention, and cardiac or vascular remodeling. Less bradykinin degradation increases nitric-oxide and prostacyclin signaling, adding vasodilation but also sensitizing airway nerves and increasing vascular permeability. At the glomerulus, reduced angiotensin-II-mediated efferent constriction lowers capillary pressure. That can reduce albumin leakage and long-term nephron stress, yet it can sharply lower GFR when renal inflow is limited by dehydration, NSAIDs, severe heart failure, or bilateral renal-artery stenosis. Reduced aldosterone decreases collecting-duct potassium secretion, so hyperkalemia and creatinine must be interpreted together.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Lisinopril should be stopped as soon as pregnancy is detected. Its fetal danger follows direct suppression of the developing kidney's RAAS, not maternal blood-pressure lowering alone.

Contraindications

  • History of lisinopril- or ACE-inhibitor-related angioedema, hereditary or idiopathic angioedema, and pregnancy.

  • Do not use with aliskiren in diabetes or with sacubitril/valsartan; preserve the required 36-hour ACE inhibitor to ARNI separation.

  • Pregnancy, previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, and hereditary or idiopathic angioedema where specified by labeling.

  • Do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or administer within 36 hours; do not combine aliskiren with an ACE inhibitor in diabetes.

Nursing Essentials

  • For heart failure or post-MI use, trend pressure, perfusion, congestion, urine output, creatinine, potassium, symptoms, and evidence of remodeling benefit rather than judging success from one pressure value.

  • Because lisinopril is renally eliminated unchanged, acute kidney decline can prolong exposure and deepen hypotension or hyperkalemia even without a CYP interaction.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, cough, facial or tongue swelling, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that persistent dry cough should be evaluated, while lip, tongue, throat, voice, or swallowing change is an emergency because bradykinin swelling can obstruct the airway.

Interactions

  • NSAID plus diuretic plus lisinopril can collapse glomerular pressure through afferent constriction, low circulating volume, and efferent dilation.

  • Injectable gold has produced nitritoid reactions with ACE inhibitors; potassium and lithium interactions remain clinically important.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and duplicate RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • ACE inhibitor plus sacubitril/valsartan is contraindicated because overlapping peptide-degradation blockade increases angioedema risk.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR and potassium before and after initiation or titration, with earlier reassessment during acute illness, poor intake, diuresis, or hypotension.

  • For acute MI and heart failure, also trend pressure, urine output, congestion, perfusion, and clinically indicated sodium or bicarbonate.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, blood pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Lisinopril is active as swallowed; enalapril and several other ACE inhibitors are prodrugs. Renal impairment therefore changes clearance, not activation.

  • A cough can be bothersome; lip or tongue swelling is an airway emergency. Do not collapse those two bradykinin effects into one severity category.

  • ACE inhibition lowers angiotensin II and raises bradykinin; those are separate consequences of inhibiting the same enzyme.

  • A modest early creatinine rise can reflect lower intraglomerular pressure, but oliguria, severe hyperkalemia, symptoms, or a large progressive decline is not routine adaptation.

Description

Lisinopril is an active, oral ACE inhibitor that lowers angiotensin II and aldosterone while slowing bradykinin breakdown. It is not a prodrug and is not appreciably metabolized; absorbed drug is excreted unchanged in urine, so kidney function strongly affects exposure. The immediate teaching chain is: less angiotensin II relaxes arteries and reduces maladaptive cardiac remodeling, less aldosterone reduces sodium retention but raises potassium, efferent-arteriole dilation lowers intraglomerular pressure and can raise creatinine, and more bradykinin explains dry cough and angioedema. Lisinopril has labeled roles in hypertension, systolic heart failure, and early treatment of hemodynamically stable acute MI. Its once-daily convenience does not make it a rapid rescue drug, and a blood-pressure number should never be treated without checking perfusion, volume, kidney function, potassium, and pregnancy.

Adverse Effects

  • A persistent dry cough, dizziness, headache, fatigue, hypotension, and potassium or creatinine increase follow its class effects.

  • Acute MI dosing can produce hypotension in an unstable or volume-depleted patient, so the labeled mortality indication applies only when hemodynamics permit.

  • Class effects include dry cough, dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, creatinine increase, and less commonly rash or taste change.

  • Airway angioedema, severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, neutropenia in susceptible patients, hepatic injury, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
