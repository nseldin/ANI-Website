Definition

Pursed-lip breathing sign is an observed breathing behavior. It is a clue to expiratory flow limitation. A positive finding means the patient exhales slowly through narrowed lips.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pursed-lip breathing sign means the patient exhales slowly through narrowed lips.

Why It Matters

The observed Pursed-lip breathing sign finding can signal expiratory flow limitation. Mild expiratory back-pressure splints small airways open and slows dynamic collapse. Pursed-lip breathing sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • expiratory flow limitation

Assessment Method

Observe exhalation during rest or exertion.

Positive Finding

The patient exhales slowly through narrowed lips.

Mechanism

Mild expiratory back-pressure splints small airways open and slows dynamic collapse.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pursed-lip breathing sign finding raises concern for expiratory flow limitation because mild expiratory back-pressure splints small airways open and slows dynamic collapse. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pursed-lip breathing sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pursed-lip breathing sign to focus evaluation of expiratory flow limitation and the reviewed alternatives: anxiety breathing, learned breathing exercise, heart failure. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe exhalation during rest or exertion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pursed-lip breathing sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It can be taught voluntarily and is not diagnostic of COPD. Therefore, Pursed-lip breathing sign cannot confirm or exclude expiratory flow limitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • anxiety breathing

  • learned breathing exercise

  • heart failure

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as anxiety breathing, learned breathing exercise, heart failure.

False Negative Situations

Pursed-lip breathing sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of expiratory flow limitation.
