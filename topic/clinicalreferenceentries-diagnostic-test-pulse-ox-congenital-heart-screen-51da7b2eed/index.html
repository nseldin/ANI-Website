Summary

Pulse ox congenital heart screen is a high-yield Pediatrics diagnostic reference. Newborn pulse oximetry screen for critical congenital heart disease.

Quick Answer

Pulse ox congenital heart screen: Newborn pulse oximetry screen for critical congenital heart disease. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Pass

    Meaning

    Oxygen saturation pattern does not meet failed-screen criteria for critical congenital heart disease at that screen.

  • Label

    Fail / positive screen

    Meaning

    Low or discrepant preductal/postductal saturations suggest possible critical congenital heart disease and require prompt evaluation.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    A failed screen is not routine discharge; assess color, feeding, respiratory effort, pulses, murmur, and provider/newborn cardiac evaluation.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Newborn pulse oximetry screen for critical congenital heart disease.

    • Before / preparation

    • Confirm the purpose of screening, age/risk eligibility, prep requirements, and whether abnormal results require diagnostic confirmation.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Teach that screening estimates risk; it does not always diagnose. Promote follow-up and reduce fear with clear next steps.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Positive screening with symptoms, high-risk history, severe abnormality, or failure to follow up on a positive result.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • A failed screen requires prompt evaluation, not routine discharge reassurance.
