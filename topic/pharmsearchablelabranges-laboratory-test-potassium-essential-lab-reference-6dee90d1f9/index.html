Name

Potassium

Range

A common adult serum reference interval is about 3.5-5.0 mEq/L (the same numeric values as mmol/L); reference and critical limits vary by laboratory, specimen, method, population, and protocol.

Why

An abnormal potassium result signals altered cardiac and neuromuscular electrical excitability, so either direction can cause weakness or dangerous dysrhythmia. Hyperkalemia raises concern for impaired renal excretion, potassium-raising medicines, acid-base or cellular shifts, or tissue breakdown; hypokalemia points toward gastrointestinal or renal loss, diuretics, or intracellular shift, and serum potassium may be normal or high in DKA despite total-body depletion. Promptly assess rhythm and ECG, weakness, medicines, kidney function, urine output, glucose and acid-base status, and magnesium; cause-directed evaluation includes repeat laboratory testing and an ECG, especially when the result is unexpected or severe. When a specimen is hemolyzed or the result is discordant and the patient is stable, recollect per protocol to evaluate pseudohyperkalemia, but do not delay emergency escalation for ECG changes, paralysis, chest pain, hemodynamic instability, or rapid deterioration. A single potassium value does not diagnose the cause or total-body stores, and severity cannot be assigned from one universal cutoff; use the laboratory's critical value, symptoms, ECG, trend, and protocol.

Aliases

High Causes

  • Kidney failure

  • ACE inhibitors or ARBs

  • Potassium-sparing diuretics

  • Cell breakdown

  • Acidosis

  • Hemolyzed specimen

Low Causes

  • Loop/thiazide diuretics

  • Vomiting or nasogastric suction

  • Diarrhea

  • Insulin therapy

  • Alkalosis

Critical Concerns

  • ECG changes, weakness or paralysis, palpitations, chest pain, hemodynamic instability, and rapid deterioration require emergency evaluation regardless of a single numeric cutoff.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess ECG and rhythm, neuromuscular status, kidney function and urine output, glucose and acid-base status, magnesium, medicines, supplements, and salt substitutes.

  • Recollect an unexpected hemolyzed or discordant sample according to protocol when the patient is stable; do not delay emergency response when symptoms or ECG changes suggest genuine danger.

  • Verify renal function and urine output before ordered replacement and never give IV potassium by direct push.

Nclex Traps

  • In DKA, serum potassium can look normal or high while total-body potassium is depleted.

  • Hemolysis can falsely elevate potassium, but a hemolysis flag does not prove that the patient's potassium is safe.
