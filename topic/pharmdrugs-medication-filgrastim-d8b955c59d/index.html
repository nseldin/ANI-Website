Contraindications

  • Serious allergic reaction to filgrastim, pegfilgrastim, or E. coli-derived proteins is a contraindication under the product label.

  • Do not use a rising ANC as permission to stop antimicrobial evaluation or to normalize the count beyond the indication-specific goal; excess leukocytosis can be harmful.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact filgrastim product, indication, weight, concentration, syringe volume, and schedule because biosimilar labels and H-ARS indications must not be assumed identical.

  • Trend ANC and total WBC with infection findings because a rising count shows pharmacologic response but does not prove that infection is absent or controlled.

  • Ask about left-upper-quadrant or shoulder pain and monitor lungs, oxygenation, edema, blood pressure, urine, and skin because splenic rupture, ARDS, capillary leak, glomerulonephritis, and aortitis require urgent action.

  • Rotate subcutaneous sites and teach safe storage and handling when self-administration applies, while emergency H-ARS dosing remains incident-directed.

Interactions

  • Cytotoxic chemotherapy given too close to filgrastim can injure the proliferating progenitors the drug is stimulating; outside the emergency radiation indication, do not give filgrastim within 24 hours before or 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy because separating the exposures protects actively dividing marrow precursors.

  • Lithium can promote neutrophil release and may amplify leukocytosis, while other myeloid growth factors can duplicate stimulation; combine only with specialist monitoring.

Key Labs

  • Serial CBC with differential, ANC, WBC, platelets, hemoglobin, and reticulocytes; in H-ARS plot lymphocyte and lineage trends through nadir and recovery.

  • Creatinine, urinalysis and protein, liver tests, inflammatory markers and imaging when aortitis is suspected, plus cultures and sepsis studies whenever infection is possible.

Nclex Traps

  • Filgrastim supports neutrophil production; it does not kill bacteria or replace sepsis treatment.

  • Bone pain can be expected, but left-shoulder pain or hypoxemia is a serious warning.

  • Initially normal counts after radiation do not rule out a later nadir.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Patients with sickle-cell disease

    Note

    Rapid granulocyte stimulation can precipitate severe or fatal sickle crisis, so pain, hypoxemia, and hemolysis require immediate evaluation.

  • Population

    Patients with myeloid malignancy or predisposition to MDS/AML

    Note

    G-CSF can stimulate myeloid cells and long-term risk interpretation is complex; H-ARS survival benefit requires hematology-led risk assessment rather than automatic exclusion.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, children, and older adults

    Note

    Emergency benefit may outweigh limited subgroup evidence, but weight, marrow reserve, comorbidity, and label-specific product data must guide dosing and surveillance.

Used To Treat

Product-specific prevention or shortening of severe neutropenia in oncology, transplant, mobilization, and chronic-neutropenia settings, plus increased survival after acute exposure to myelosuppressive radiation. In H-ARS it stimulates surviving neutrophil precursors; it is not an antibiotic, platelet treatment, radiation shield, or decorporation agent.

Mechanism

Filgrastim binds and dimerizes the G-CSF receptor on committed myeloid progenitors and selected mature neutrophils, activating JAK/STAT, Ras/MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling. These pathways increase precursor survival, proliferation, neutrophil differentiation, marrow release, and selected mature-cell functions. The drug amplifies cells that survived radiation; it cannot restore an eradicated stem-cell compartment. Renal and neutrophil receptor-mediated clearance change as counts recover, which helps explain variable exposure and the need for serial CBC-guided duration.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): filgrastim can cause fatal splenic rupture, ARDS, capillary leak, severe hypersensitivity, sickle crisis, glomerulonephritis, and aortitis. Treat left-upper-quadrant pain, hypoxemia, edema, or vascular pain as urgent.

Description

Filgrastim is a short-acting recombinant G-CSF that activates G-CSF receptors on surviving myeloid progenitors, accelerating neutrophil proliferation, maturation, and release. It is given daily in many indications; in H-ARS this action can shorten the interval of dangerous neutropenia when enough marrow reserve remains. Because circulating counts can initially look normal after radiation, treatment and monitoring follow credible dose exposure and serial trends. Bone pain is common, but left-upper-quadrant pain, hypoxemia, edema, hematuria, or vascular inflammation can signal a serious complication.

Adverse Effects

  • Bone, back, limb, or musculoskeletal pain, headache, fever, injection-site reaction, leukocytosis, and thrombocytopenia can occur.

  • Splenic enlargement or rupture, ARDS, severe hypersensitivity, capillary leak, sickle-cell crisis, glomerulonephritis, aortitis, cutaneous vasculitis, and myelodysplastic or leukemic concerns in susceptible populations are serious risks.

Administration Timing

  • For credible H-ARS, use the current label's weight-based subcutaneous regimen as soon as possible after suspected or confirmed myelosuppressive radiation exposure; do not wait for the ANC nadir to prove injury.

  • Obtain baseline and serial CBC with differential and platelets, then continue label-directed dosing through the recovery endpoint established by the incident protocol.

  • For oncology indications, preserve the product's separation from cytotoxic chemotherapy; do not copy an H-ARS emergency schedule into routine chemotherapy or vice versa.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Respiratory distress, new infiltrates, splenic pain, hypotension with edema, anaphylaxis, sickle crisis, hematuria/proteinuria, or fever with vascular pain requires drug interruption and urgent evaluation.

  • Failure to recover counts, recurrent cytopenia, or worsening pancytopenia requires reassessment of radiation dose, marrow reserve, sepsis, consumption, and need for broader H-ARS or stem-cell support.

Evidence Limitations

  • The H-ARS survival indication was established under the FDA Animal Rule; human use relies on extrapolated exposure, safety, and pharmacology data.

  • Reference-product and biosimilar indications can differ over time, so do not route every filgrastim-like product to an H-ARS claim without checking its current label.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled for multiple product-specific neutropenia indications and to increase survival after acute myelosuppressive radiation exposure; H-ARS efficacy was established under the Animal Rule.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
