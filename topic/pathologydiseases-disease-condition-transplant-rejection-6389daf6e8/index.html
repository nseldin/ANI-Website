Definition

Transplant rejection is immune injury in which recipient antibody or cellular responses recognize donor graft antigens and damage graft vessels, parenchyma, or ducts; the pattern may be hyperacute, acute, or chronic. The characteristic pattern is new organ-specific dysfunction such as rising creatinine, cholestasis, dyspnea, arrhythmia, reduced output, pain, fever, or silent laboratory decline. Confirm or stage it with serial graft function and immunosuppressant exposure or adherence review; distinguish it from infection, medication toxicity, obstruction, vascular thrombosis, recurrent disease, and ischemia by timing, cultures, drug levels, imaging, and tissue. The priority is to contact the transplant team early and maintain prescribed immunosuppression unless directed otherwise because graft failure can complicate Transplant rejection. can follow if the process advances.

Mechanistically, recipient immunity recognizes graft antigens and injures graft vessels, parenchyma, or ducts in antibody-mediated, cellular, acute, or chronic patterns.

Signs Symptoms

  • Fever or hypothermia, chills, source pain/redness/drainage, cough, dysuria, diarrhea, rash, mucosal lesions, lymph nodes, mental-status change, hypotension, tachypnea, or immunosuppression cues reveal infection risk.

  • Escalate sepsis signs, neutropenic fever, rapidly spreading infection, airway swelling, meningitis signs, or shock.

Diagnostics

  • serial graft function and immunosuppressant exposure or adherence review

  • organ-specific ultrasound, vascular, cardiac, pulmonary, or biliary imaging

  • biopsy and donor-specific antibody or immune studies when they classify rejection

  • Distinguish Transplant rejection from infection, medication toxicity, obstruction, vascular thrombosis, recurrent disease, and ischemia by timing, cultures, drug levels, imaging, and tissue.

Labs

  • WBC may rise or fall

  • lactate may rise with sepsis

  • CRP/ESR may rise with inflammation

  • organ labs can worsen in severe infection

Treatments

  • contact the transplant team early and maintain prescribed immunosuppression unless directed otherwise

  • treat cellular or antibody-mediated rejection according to organ and severity

  • support failing graft function and prevent infection during intensified therapy

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for rapidly declining graft function, severe dyspnea, arrhythmia, oliguria, jaundice, fever with instability, or new neurologic change because urgent transplant-team treatment is required.

  • Assess medication adherence, recent dose changes, vomiting, interactions, infection, and graft-specific symptoms because low immunosuppressant exposure and immune activation can precipitate rejection.

  • Obtain ordered drug levels, organ-function tests, imaging, and biopsy specimens because rejection cannot be reliably distinguished from infection or toxicity by symptoms alone.

  • Administer prescribed immunosuppressive therapy on schedule while using infection precautions because missed doses worsen rejection and intensified therapy increases infection risk.

  • Monitor temperature, blood pressure, graft function, fluid balance, glucose, blood counts, kidney and liver function, and treatment toxicity to detect response and complications.

Complications

  • graft failure can complicate Transplant rejection.

  • thrombosis can complicate Transplant rejection.

  • severe infection can complicate Transplant rejection.

  • malignancy can complicate Transplant rejection.

  • treatment toxicity can complicate Transplant rejection.

  • return to organ-replacement support

Red Flags

  • Rapidly declining graft-specific function

  • Severe dyspnea, arrhythmia, or hemodynamic instability

  • Oliguria, jaundice, or rapidly worsening laboratory findings

  • Fever with hypotension during intensified immunosuppression

Patient Education

  • Teach exact immunosuppressant timing, interaction checks, laboratory follow-up, and why doses should never be stopped or doubled without transplant-team direction.

  • Review the graft-specific warning symptoms and the transplant contact pathway for same-day assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal physical examination does not exclude laboratory-only rejection

  • Never assume dysfunction is rejection before checking infection, obstruction, perfusion, toxicity, and adherence
