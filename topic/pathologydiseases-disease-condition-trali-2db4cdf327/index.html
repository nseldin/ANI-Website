Definition

TRALI develops when donor or recipient factors activate pulmonary neutrophils and endothelial permeability, causing noncardiogenic edema during or shortly after transfusion. The characteristic pattern is acute hypoxemia, bilateral pulmonary opacities, dyspnea, and often hypotension or fever without clear circulatory overload. Confirm or stage it with stop transfusion and assess oxygenation, hemodynamics, fluid status, and timing; distinguish it from TACO, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, sepsis, aspiration, and cardiogenic edema by volume signs, blood-bank testing, echo, and clinical context. The priority is to stop transfusion and provide lung-protective oxygen and ventilatory support because severe ARDS can complicate TRALI. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, fever, bone pain, lymphadenopathy, weight loss, neurologic change, or transfusion symptoms guide hematology/oncology priority.

  • Escalate neutropenic fever, active bleeding, severe anemia symptoms, tumor lysis signs, hypercalcemia crisis, or transfusion reaction.

Diagnostics

  • stop transfusion and assess oxygenation, hemodynamics, fluid status, and timing

  • chest imaging with evaluation for hydrostatic pressure and alternative ARDS risks

  • immediate blood-bank reaction investigation

  • Distinguish TRALI from TACO, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, sepsis, aspiration, and cardiogenic edema by volume signs, blood-bank testing, echo, and clinical context.

Labs

  • hemoglobin/hematocrit may fall with anemia or bleeding

  • platelets may fall with bleeding risk

  • ANC below 500 means severe neutropenia risk

  • calcium, potassium, phosphate, and uric acid matter in tumor lysis

Treatments

  • stop transfusion and provide lung-protective oxygen and ventilatory support

  • avoid reflexive diuresis when perfusion is low and overload is absent

  • report the donor and reaction for blood-safety investigation

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for rapidly increasing oxygen needs, bilateral pulmonary edema, hypotension, cyanosis, or respiratory fatigue within the reaction window because critical-care airway support may be required.

  • Stop the transfusion and notify the transfusion service immediately because transfusion-related acute lung injury can progress quickly after exposure to the implicated component.

  • Apply oxygen and prepare ventilatory support as ordered because inflammatory pulmonary capillary leak causes severe hypoxemia without simple circulatory overload.

  • Monitor oxygen saturation, respiratory effort, blood pressure, lung sounds, fluid balance, and chest imaging findings to distinguish TRALI from TACO and detect shock.

Complications

  • severe ARDS can complicate TRALI.

  • shock can complicate TRALI.

  • prolonged ventilation can complicate TRALI.

  • death can complicate TRALI.

Red Flags

  • Acute hypoxemia with bilateral pulmonary infiltrates during or soon after transfusion

  • Rapidly increasing oxygen requirement or respiratory fatigue

  • Hypotension, fever, or cyanosis

  • Pulmonary edema without a clear circulatory-overload pattern

Patient Education

  • Explain that TRALI is an acute lung reaction rather than a simple allergy and that the transfusion service must document and investigate it.

  • Teach the patient to report any recurrent breathing difficulty promptly and to share the reaction history before future transfusions.

Nclex Traps

  • TRALI is permeability edema and may worsen with unnecessary aggressive diuresis

  • A normal pretransfusion chest study does not distinguish TRALI from TACO after symptoms begin
