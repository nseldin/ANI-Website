Class

M3-selective bladder antimuscarinic; CYP2D6/CYP3A4 substrate and CYP2D6 inhibitor

Used To Treat

Adult overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge urinary incontinence.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with urinary retention, gastric retention or uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma. Severe constipation, fecal impaction and retention can occur. Do not use in severe hepatic impairment; cap at 7.5 mg with moderate hepatic impairment or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

Mechanism

Competitive M3 antagonism suppresses Gq-phospholipase-C-IP3-calcium signaling in detrusor smooth muscle. The same receptor drives GI propulsion, gland secretion, accommodation and sweating. CYP2D6 poor metabolizers and CYP3A4 inhibition increase exposure. Darifenacin also inhibits CYP2D6, providing a mechanistic reason to review metoprolol, tricyclics and other substrates rather than treating interactions as a generic warning.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess bowel pattern before and during therapy; constipation is a predictable M3 effect, not an unrelated complaint.

  • Swallow ER tablets whole and review hepatic status plus CYP3A4/CYP2D6 interactions before titration.

Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Severe hepatic impairment or serious hypersensitivity

  • Use cautiously with severe constipation, GI obstruction risk, myasthenia gravis and outlet obstruction

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors require a 7.5 mg maximum; inducers may reduce effect.

  • Darifenacin inhibits CYP2D6 and can raise selected substrate concentrations.

  • Other anticholinergics compound retention and GI/CNS toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Hepatic function

  • Post-void residual and bowel assessment

  • Medication-specific CYP2D6 monitoring such as pulse/BP for metoprolol when relevant

Nclex Traps

  • M3 selectivity does not mean bladder-only action.

  • Severe constipation can become fecal impaction or obstruction.

  • Darifenacin is both a CYP substrate and a CYP2D6 inhibitor.

Description

Darifenacin is an extended-release antimuscarinic with greater affinity for M3 than several other muscarinic subtypes. Blocking M3 reduces detrusor contraction but also strongly affects bowel and salivary function, so selectivity does not prevent severe constipation or retention. CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 clear the drug; strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and moderate hepatic impairment cap the dose, and darifenacin itself can raise CYP2D6-substrate exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth and constipation, sometimes severe

  • Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, nausea and blurred vision

  • Urinary retention

  • Dizziness, somnolence, confusion or hallucinations
