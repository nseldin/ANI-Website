Definition

Joffroy sign is an observed ocular-frontal sign. It is a clue to Graves disease. A positive finding means the forehead fails to wrinkle during upgaze.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Joffroy sign means the forehead fails to wrinkle during upgaze.

Why It Matters

The observed Joffroy sign finding can signal Graves disease. Persistent lid retraction reduces compensatory frontalis recruitment during attempted upgaze. Joffroy sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • Graves disease

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to look upward while keeping the head still.

Positive Finding

The forehead fails to wrinkle during upgaze.

Mechanism

Persistent lid retraction reduces compensatory frontalis recruitment during attempted upgaze.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Joffroy sign finding raises concern for Graves disease because persistent lid retraction reduces compensatory frontalis recruitment during attempted upgaze. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Joffroy sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Joffroy sign to focus evaluation of Graves disease and the reviewed alternatives: facial palsy, cosmetic botulinum toxin, normal variation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to look upward while keeping the head still. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Joffroy sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor standardization and voluntary eyebrow control make it nonspecific. Therefore, Joffroy sign cannot confirm or exclude Graves disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • facial palsy

  • cosmetic botulinum toxin

  • normal variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as facial palsy, cosmetic botulinum toxin, normal variation.

False Negative Situations

Joffroy sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of Graves disease.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable. Joffroy sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude Graves disease. Apply the reviewed limitation for Joffroy sign: Poor standardization and voluntary eyebrow control make it nonspecific.

Safety Precautions

Observe Joffroy sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to look upward while keeping the head still. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not use this historical sign as evidence of thyroid status.
