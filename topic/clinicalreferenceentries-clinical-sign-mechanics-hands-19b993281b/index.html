Definition

Mechanic's hands is an observed and palpated hand sign. It is a clue to antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy. A positive finding means hyperkeratotic fissured rough skin resembles manual labor changes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mechanic's hands means hyperkeratotic fissured rough skin resembles manual labor changes.

Why It Matters

The observed Mechanic's hands finding can signal antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy. Autoimmune interface inflammation and hyperkeratosis target friction surfaces of the hands. Mechanic's hands cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy

Assessment Method

Inspect radial fingers and palms.

Positive Finding

Hyperkeratotic fissured rough skin resembles manual labor changes.

Mechanism

Autoimmune interface inflammation and hyperkeratosis target friction surfaces of the hands.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mechanic's hands finding raises concern for antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy because autoimmune interface inflammation and hyperkeratosis target friction surfaces of the hands. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mechanic's hands does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mechanic's hands to focus evaluation of antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy and the reviewed alternatives: irritant dermatitis, psoriasis, tinea manuum. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect radial fingers and palms. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Mechanic's hands as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Occupation and eczema are common mimics. Therefore, Mechanic's hands cannot confirm or exclude antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as irritant dermatitis, psoriasis, tinea manuum.

False Negative Situations

Mechanic's hands may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of antisynthetase syndrome or inflammatory myopathy.
