Used To Treat

Adult hypertension and salt-water retention associated with heart failure under current U.S. labeling. It is selected primarily as a long-acting antihypertensive rather than as rescue treatment for acute pulmonary edema.

Mechanism

Indapamide reaches the distal tubular lumen and inhibits NCC, decreasing sodium-chloride uptake in the distal convoluted tubule. More sodium reaches ENaC downstream, increasing potassium and hydrogen secretion; volume contraction activates RAAS and aldosterone, reinforcing hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Lower intracellular sodium increases sodium-calcium exchange and reduces urinary calcium. Blocking the diluting segment impairs free-water clearance, so water intake and vasopressin can drive hyponatremia. Chronic antihypertensive action also involves reduced calcium influx and reactivity in vascular smooth muscle and improved arterial compliance. Because the pressure response plateaus while natriuretic and electrolyte effects continue to rise, increasing the dose can worsen sodium, potassium, uric acid, glucose, and kidney function without proportionate pressure benefit.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and hypersensitivity are core contraindication patterns. Symptomatic hyponatremia, major potassium or magnesium loss, hypotension, or evolving kidney injury requires reassessment.

  • Do not assume every thiazide is ineffective below one eGFR threshold; agent, dose, route, indication, and actual response matter.

  • The current label contraindicates anuria and hypersensitivity to indapamide or other sulfonamide-derived drugs. Severe hepatic disease increases encephalopathy risk from electrolyte disturbance.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check pressure, orthostasis, weight, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, and uric acid when relevant before and after initiation or escalation.

  • Teach earlier-day dosing, slow position changes, photosensitivity precautions, and urgent reporting of confusion, seizure, severe weakness, palpitations, eye pain, or sudden visual change.

  • Do not infer safety from a modest urine response. Trend sodium and potassium after initiation and titration because vascular pressure benefit and electrolyte toxicity can diverge.

Interactions

  • Lithium exposure can rise as sodium depletion increases proximal lithium reabsorption; NSAIDs can blunt effect and worsen kidney function.

  • SSRIs, carbamazepine, desmopressin, and high water intake magnify hyponatremia; low potassium increases digoxin-related arrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid, weight, and urine calcium when that is the indication.

  • Check sooner in older adults, low body mass, high water intake, CKD, or interacting medication use because severe sodium decline can appear quickly.

Nclex Traps

  • Thiazides lower urinary calcium while raising uric acid; those effects come from different transport responses.

  • They can cause profound hyponatremia because the diluting segment is blocked while concentrating ability and vasopressin action remain.

  • Indapamide's vascular effects help blood pressure, but it remains an NCC blocker capable of severe sodium and potassium disturbance.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe hyponatremia and hypokalemia can occur, particularly in older adults, lower body mass, high water intake, hepatic disease, or interacting therapy. Higher doses increase diuresis and metabolic toxicity without reliably adding antihypertensive benefit, so use the lowest effective dose and reassess chemistry.

Class Pathway

  • Distal convoluted tubule

  • NCC inhibition

  • Sustained peripheral vascular-resistance reduction

Description

Indapamide, historically branded Lozol, is a thiazide-like sulfonamide that inhibits distal NCC and also produces a durable reduction in vascular resistance. It is chemically distinct from benzothiadiazines but shares their sodium, potassium, magnesium, uric-acid, glucose, and calcium pattern. At standard antihypertensive doses, much of the chronic blood-pressure benefit persists after plasma volume partially recovers, reflecting reduced arteriolar responsiveness and altered calcium handling rather than continuous large urine output. The drug is highly lipophilic and extensively metabolized, with a long enough effect for once-daily dosing. Higher doses add biochemical toxicity more readily than blood-pressure benefit, which explains why dose escalation is not a simple way to turn indapamide into a loop diuretic. It can produce profound hyponatremia or hypokalemia despite its reputation as metabolically gentle.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, volume depletion, hyperuricemia or gout, photosensitivity, and glucose intolerance follow NCC blockade and compensatory distal transport.

  • Severe neurologic hyponatremia, arrhythmia, pancreatitis, severe skin reactions, and acute angle-closure glaucoma or myopia require urgent action.
