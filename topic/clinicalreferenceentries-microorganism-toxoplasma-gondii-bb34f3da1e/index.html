Definition

Toxoplasma gondii is an obligate intracellular protozoan parasite that can establish lifelong tissue cysts after acquired or congenital infection. Most immunocompetent people have no symptoms or a mild self-limited illness, but primary infection during pregnancy can injure the fetus, and reactivation during severe cellular immune suppression can cause encephalitis, eye disease, or disseminated infection.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    T. gondii is the organism; toxoplasmosis is the disease. Cats and other felids are the definitive hosts that shed environmentally resistant oocysts, while humans and many animals serve as intermediate hosts with tissue cysts. A positive antibody can indicate prior exposure rather than current active disease, so the parasite's identity must remain separate from a clinical diagnosis.

    Id

    identity-classification

    Title

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    The parasite is a motile apicomplexan with an apical complex that supports attachment and invasion of nucleated host cells. Rapidly multiplying tachyzoites drive acute tissue injury, while slowly dividing bradyzoites persist within tissue cysts, especially in muscle, retina, and brain. Current drugs act mainly against replicating stages and do not reliably eradicate dormant cysts, explaining relapse risk during persistent immune suppression.

    Id

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    People acquire infection by ingesting tissue cysts in undercooked meat, ingesting oocysts from contaminated soil, water, food, or cat feces, receiving an infected transplant or blood product rarely, or through transplacental spread after maternal infection. Ordinary contact with a person who has toxoplasmosis does not transmit the parasite. Latent cysts can persist for life and reactivate when T-cell control declines.

    Id

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Tachyzoites invade cells, multiply, rupture them, and disseminate before cellular immunity forces conversion to bradyzoite cysts. In the fetus, rapidly developing neural and ocular tissues are especially vulnerable. In advanced immunosuppression, failed control permits cyst reactivation, producing necrotizing brain lesions or retinochoroiditis. This stage biology explains why serology, PCR, imaging, and immune status must be interpreted together.

    Id

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Immunocompetent infection may resemble mononucleosis with lymphadenopathy and fatigue. Congenital infection can cause chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications, or later visual and neurologic impairment. Reactivation encephalitis can cause headache, confusion, fever, seizures, or focal deficits. New neurologic findings, visual loss, pregnancy with suspected acute infection, or severe disease in an immunocompromised patient needs urgent specialist evaluation.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Serologic IgG and IgM testing supports exposure and possible recent infection, but timing and false-positive IgM can complicate interpretation; confirmatory testing and avidity may be needed. PCR on amniotic fluid, cerebrospinal fluid, blood, or tissue can support selected diagnoses, yet a negative result does not always exclude active disease. Ocular disease relies heavily on examination, and cerebral disease integrates immune status, imaging, serology, and treatment response.

    Id

    diagnostic-identification

    Title

    Diagnostic identification

  • Content

    Preventive measures include cooking meat safely, washing produce and hands, avoiding untreated water, using gloves for soil, and having someone else manage cat litter during pregnancy when possible. Daily litter removal limits oocyst maturation, but cats are not the only source. Treatment depends on disease site, pregnancy timing, immune status, blood counts, and drug toxicity; pyrimethamine must be paired with other therapy and folinic-acid rescue rather than used alone.
