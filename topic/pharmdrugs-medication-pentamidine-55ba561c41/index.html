Class

Aromatic diamidine antiprotozoal with systemic or aerosolized Pneumocystis use

Used To Treat

IV alternative treatment of moderate-to-severe Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, aerosolized prophylaxis for selected patients, and selected leishmaniasis or trypanosomiasis indications. Route changes both effectiveness and toxicity; inhaled prophylaxis is not systemic treatment.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe hypotension, hypoglycemia followed by hyperglycemia/diabetes, pancreatitis, acute kidney injury, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, QT prolongation, ventricular dysrhythmia, and death have occurred with systemic therapy. Give with the patient recumbent, infuse slowly, monitor glucose and blood pressure during and after dosing, and continue glucose surveillance after therapy because late diabetes can follow early hypoglycemia.

Mechanism

The positively charged diamidine groups bind the minor groove of AT-rich DNA and interfere with topoisomerase and nucleic-acid-dependent processes. Pentamidine also impairs RNA, protein, phospholipid, and mitochondrial metabolism in susceptible organisms; the dominant Pneumocystis target remains incompletely defined. Host toxicity reflects tissue accumulation. In pancreatic beta cells, early membrane or mitochondrial injury can release stored insulin, causing hypoglycemia, while later cell loss produces impaired insulin secretion and hyperglycemia or permanent diabetes. Renal tubular injury reduces clearance and magnifies exposure. Effects on cardiac ion channels and loss of potassium, magnesium, or calcium prolong QT and permit torsades.

Nursing Essentials

  • For IV dosing, keep the patient recumbent, use a controlled slow infusion, and monitor blood pressure, ECG, glucose, kidney function, and electrolytes before, during, and after treatment.

  • For aerosolized prophylaxis, use the specified nebulizer in appropriate negative-pressure infection-control conditions; pretreat bronchospasm-prone patients according to protocol.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity. Relative risks include severe renal, pancreatic, marrow, electrolyte, or conduction disease.

  • Aerosolized pentamidine is not adequate treatment for active PCP and does not protect against toxoplasmosis or reliable extrapulmonary Pneumocystis disease.

Interactions

  • Other nephrotoxins, pancreatotoxic drugs, marrow suppressants, QT-prolonging agents, and electrolyte-wasting drugs can make toxicity multiplicative.

  • Foscarnet adds renal, calcium, magnesium, and QT risk; insulin or glucose-lowering drugs complicate dysglycemia recognition.

Key Labs

  • Frequent glucose, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, magnesium, calcium, liver tests, CBC/platelets, amylase/lipase when symptomatic, and ECG

  • Blood pressure during infusion, urine output, abdominal pain, mental status, cough/bronchospasm, oxygenation, late hyperglycemia, and PCP response

Nclex Traps

  • Pentamidine can cause early hypoglycemia and later diabetes; one normal glucose does not end monitoring.

  • Aerosolized prophylaxis does not equal IV treatment and does not cover toxoplasmosis.

Description

Pentamidine is an aromatic diamidine that accumulates in susceptible protozoa and Pneumocystis and disrupts DNA binding, RNA processing, protein synthesis, phospholipid metabolism, and mitochondrial function. No single target fully accounts for activity, which is why its card should not pretend to know one receptor. Systemic pentamidine is reserved for serious disease because it can injure pancreatic islet cells, kidney tubules, marrow, liver, and cardiac conduction. Early beta-cell injury can release insulin and cause profound hypoglycemia; later destruction can reduce insulin production and produce diabetes. Aerosolized drug reduces systemic exposure but can cause cough or bronchospasm and provides uneven extrapulmonary protection.

Adverse Effects

  • Metallic taste, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, headache, injection-site pain, cough or bronchospasm with aerosolized use

  • Profound hypotension, dysglycemia, pancreatitis, nephrotoxicity, cytopenias, liver injury, electrolyte depletion, QT prolongation, torsades, and sterile abscess
