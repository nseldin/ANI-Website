Definition

Pivot-shift test is a dynamic knee-instability maneuver. It is a clue to rotational instability from ACL deficiency. A positive finding means the lateral tibia reduces with a palpable clunk.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pivot-shift test means the lateral tibia reduces with a palpable clunk.

Why It Matters

A positive Pivot-shift test response can signal rotational instability from ACL deficiency. ACL deficiency permits anterior subluxation near extension that reduces as the iliotibial band changes leverage. Pivot-shift test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • rotational instability from ACL deficiency

Assessment Method

A trained examiner applies valgus and internal rotation while flexing the relaxed knee.

Positive Finding

The lateral tibia reduces with a palpable clunk.

Mechanism

ACL deficiency permits anterior subluxation near extension that reduces as the iliotibial band changes leverage.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Pivot-shift test response raises concern for rotational instability from ACL deficiency because aCL deficiency permits anterior subluxation near extension that reduces as the iliotibial band changes leverage. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pivot-shift test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pivot-shift test to focus evaluation of rotational instability from ACL deficiency and the reviewed alternatives: patellar subluxation, meniscal clunk, IT band snapping. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: A trained examiner applies valgus and internal rotation while flexing the relaxed knee. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pivot-shift test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Anesthesia and examiner experience greatly affect sensitivity. Therefore, Pivot-shift test cannot confirm or exclude rotational instability from ACL deficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • patellar subluxation

  • meniscal clunk

  • IT band snapping

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from patellar subluxation, meniscal clunk, IT band snapping, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Pivot-shift test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Specialist test; often limited by guarding. When performed as described, Pivot-shift test can adjust concern for rotational instability from ACL deficiency, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Pivot-shift test: Anesthesia and examiner experience greatly affect sensitivity.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Pivot-shift test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: A trained examiner applies valgus and internal rotation while flexing the relaxed knee. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not perform aggressively in an awake acutely injured patient.
