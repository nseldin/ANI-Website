Definition

Weiss ring is an observed vitreous sign. It is a clue to posterior vitreous detachment. A positive finding means a mobile ring-shaped opacity is seen near the optic axis.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Weiss ring means a mobile ring-shaped opacity is seen near the optic axis.

Why It Matters

The observed Weiss ring finding can signal posterior vitreous detachment. Vitreous separation detaches the glial annulus from around the optic disc. Weiss ring cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • posterior vitreous detachment

Assessment Method

Examine the vitreous after dilation.

Positive Finding

A mobile ring-shaped opacity is seen near the optic axis.

Mechanism

Vitreous separation detaches the glial annulus from around the optic disc.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Weiss ring finding raises concern for posterior vitreous detachment because vitreous separation detaches the glial annulus from around the optic disc. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Weiss ring does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Weiss ring to focus evaluation of posterior vitreous detachment and the reviewed alternatives: vitreous syneresis, inflammatory debris, lens opacity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine the vitreous after dilation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Weiss ring as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The ring may be fragmented or absent and does not rule out a tear. Therefore, Weiss ring cannot confirm or exclude posterior vitreous detachment by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • vitreous syneresis

  • inflammatory debris

  • lens opacity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as vitreous syneresis, inflammatory debris, lens opacity.

False Negative Situations

Weiss ring may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of posterior vitreous detachment.
