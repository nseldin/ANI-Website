Used To Treat

Schizophrenia; acute/maintenance bipolar I treatment; adjunctive major depression; irritability in autistic disorder; Tourette disorder, with age/formulation-specific indications. Long-acting products have distinct oral-overlap/loading rules.

Mechanism

Aripiprazole partially activates D2/D3 and 5-HT1A receptors and antagonizes 5-HT2A. When endogenous dopamine is high, receptor occupancy displaces dopamine and reduces net signaling; when dopamine is low, intrinsic activity prevents complete receptor silence. Preserved tuberoinfundibular signaling can lower prolactin, while partial nigrostriatal stimulation reduces parkinsonism for many patients but can produce severe akathisia. Reward-circuit modulation explains reports of pathologic gambling, shopping, eating and sexual urges. CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 determine exposure, so genotype and inhibitors/inducers materially change dose needs.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Aripiprazole is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. When used as an antidepressant adjunct it also carries suicidal-thoughts/behaviors warning context. Monitor akathisia and compulsive behaviors; these can cause profound distress despite relatively normal metabolic tests.

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity

  • Assess suicidality, impulse-control history, severe akathisia, seizure and cardiovascular risk

  • Use formulation-specific oral overlap/loading; do not interchange LAIs by name alone

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask what restlessness feels like and whether the patient can sit still; akathisia is often more distressing than observers realize.

  • Ask patient and family directly about new compulsive urges and reduce/stop under prescriber guidance when causally linked.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors such as fluoxetine/paroxetine and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors raise exposure.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inducers lower exposure and may make some formulations unsuitable.

  • Other dopamine blockers add EPS; antihypertensives and sedatives add orthostasis/impairment.

Key Labs

  • AIMS/EPS and akathisia scale/assessment

  • Weight, glucose/A1c, lipids and blood pressure

  • Impulse-control, suicidality and interaction review

Nclex Traps

  • Akathisia can look like worsening anxiety, mania or psychosis.

  • Partial agonism can lower prolactin but does not eliminate tardive dyskinesia or NMS.

  • Every long-acting aripiprazole product has its own initiation rules.

Description

Aripiprazole is a dopamine partial-agonist antipsychotic rather than a simple D2 blocker. It competes with dopamine while activating D2/D3 receptors less strongly, lowering signaling in dopamine-rich mesolimbic pathways while preserving some signaling in motor and prolactin pathways. This often produces less prolactin elevation, sedation and metabolic burden than several antagonist SGAs, but akathisia, insomnia, nausea and impulse-control disorders can be prominent. Partial agonism is a context-dependent stabilizer, not half-strength antagonism.

Adverse Effects

  • Akathisia, restlessness, insomnia, anxiety and nausea

  • Impulse-control disorders including gambling, shopping, binge eating and hypersexuality

  • EPS, tardive dyskinesia and NMS remain possible

  • Metabolic effects, orthostasis and somnolence, generally lower on average than several SGAs
