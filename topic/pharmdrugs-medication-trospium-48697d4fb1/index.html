Class

Quaternary-ammonium peripheral antimuscarinic; predominantly renal elimination

Used To Treat

Adult overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge urinary incontinence.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with urinary retention, gastric retention or uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma. CNS effects including dizziness, somnolence, confusion and hallucinations still occur. Severe renal impairment requires dose reduction; moderate impairment can also increase anticholinergic adverse effects.

Mechanism

Competitive muscarinic blockade suppresses M3 Gq-IP3-calcium signaling in detrusor muscle. Permanent positive charge limits passive CNS diffusion. Renal tubular secretion and filtration clear much of the drug, so impaired kidney function raises systemic anticholinergic exposure. Food, especially a high-fat meal, markedly lowers absorption; immediate-release tablets are therefore taken on an empty stomach or at least one hour before meals.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give immediate-release trospium on an empty stomach and do not infer treatment failure from a dose repeatedly taken with high-fat meals.

  • Monitor renal function, residual urine, cognition and bowel pattern despite lower expected CNS penetration.

Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Serious hypersensitivity

  • Dose-adjust severe renal impairment and use caution with moderate renal impairment or severe GI dysmotility

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics add retention, constipation and CNS toxicity.

  • Drugs eliminated by active renal tubular secretion may compete and raise exposure.

  • Alcohol/sedatives compound drowsiness even though CYP metabolism is limited.

Key Labs

  • Renal function

  • Post-void residual

  • Cognition, bowel function and symptom diary

Nclex Traps

  • Quaternary structure means less passive BBB entry, not zero delirium risk.

  • Trospium is a renal-clearance problem more than a CYP problem.

  • Food timing can meaningfully reduce absorption.

Description

Trospium is a positively charged quaternary antimuscarinic that reduces detrusor contraction but crosses lipid membranes and the blood-brain barrier less readily than tertiary agents. That can reduce, not abolish, CNS exposure. It undergoes little CYP metabolism and is substantially excreted by the kidney, making renal function and empty-stomach administration more important than CYP interaction management.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, dyspepsia and abdominal pain

  • Urinary retention and urinary tract infection

  • Dizziness, somnolence, confusion and hallucinations

  • Heat intolerance, blurred vision and tachycardia
