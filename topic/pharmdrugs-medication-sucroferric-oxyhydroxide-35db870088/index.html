Class

Minimally absorbed iron-based chewable phosphate binder

Used To Treat

Control of serum phosphorus in adults and pediatric patients age 9 years and older with CKD on dialysis under current labeling.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Monitor patients with hemochromatosis/iron accumulation, significant GI or hepatic disease, recent major GI surgery or peritoneal-dialysis peritonitis because these populations were not well represented. Tablets must be chewed, not swallowed whole. Dark stool is expected and can mask melena.

Mechanism

Ligand exchange binds phosphate across physiologic GI pH. The active iron-oxyhydroxide lattice remains largely insoluble, while sucrose/starch components are digested and a small amount of mononuclear iron can be absorbed. Binding lowers phosphorus without adding calcium. Chewing increases usable surface area and meal timing places binder beside the phosphate target.

Nursing Essentials

  • Chew or crush each tablet completely and divide the daily dose across meals according to phosphate load.

  • Trend phosphorus and stool/GI tolerance; distinguish expected dark stool from symptomatic bleeding.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication beyond practical hypersensitivity assessment

  • Use caution with iron-overload disorders and major GI/hepatic disease

  • Not useful away from meals

Interactions

  • Give levothyroxine at least 4 hours before.

  • Give doxycycline, cephalexin and aspirin at least 1 hour before under current labeling; review paricalcitol and other bindable drugs.

  • Monitor response/levels for oral drugs where reduced exposure would be important.

Key Labs

  • Serum phosphorus, calcium and PTH

  • Ferritin and transferrin saturation in iron-accumulation risk

  • Hemoglobin and GI assessment when dark stool could represent bleeding

Nclex Traps

  • Velphoro must be chewed and taken with food.

  • It contains iron but is not equivalent to ferric citrate as an anemia treatment.

  • Black stool can be benign discoloration or bleeding; context decides.

Description

Sucroferric oxyhydroxide is a largely insoluble polynuclear iron(III)-oxyhydroxide phosphate binder. In the GI tract, dietary phosphate replaces hydroxyl/water ligands on the iron surface and the bound phosphate leaves in stool. Very little iron is absorbed compared with ferric citrate, but iron stores and GI disease still matter. Tablets must be chewed or crushed and taken with meals.

Adverse Effects

  • Discolored feces and diarrhea

  • Nausea, constipation, vomiting, abdominal pain and tooth discoloration

  • Iron accumulation is uncommon but possible in susceptible patients

  • Binding interactions with selected oral drugs
