Definition

Dry skin, also called xerosis, means the outer skin barrier lacks enough moisture and surface lipids, so the skin becomes rough, tight, flaky, scaly, or cracked.

Plain Language

The skin has lost moisture and protective oils, making it rough, flaky, itchy, or cracked.

Why It Matters

Dryness is often environmental or age related, but persistent generalized dryness can accompany dermatitis, hypothyroidism, kidney disease, medication effects, or another systemic problem. Deep fissures can bleed and let microbes enter, so spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, yellow crust, fever, or rapidly worsening pain can indicate infection.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

With consent, inspect and gently palpate representative skin for roughness, flaking, fine scale, tightness, fissures, excoriations, erythema, pain, bleeding, drainage, and distribution; ask about itch, bathing, products, environment, work exposures, medications, systemic symptoms, and skin-care response.

Positive Finding

Skin feels rough or less supple and shows fine flaking, scaling, cracking, or an ashy or dull change from baseline; severe dryness may itch, sting, fissure, or bleed.

Mechanism

Reduced surface lipids and water-binding capacity weaken the outer skin barrier and increase water loss, producing rough scale and cracks that permit irritants and microbes to enter.

Evidence Limitations

Appearance and visible scale vary across skin tones and sites; recent moisturizer, bathing, edema, inflammation, and lighting alter the examination, and dry skin alone does not diagnose dehydration or hypothyroidism. Therefore, Dry skin cannot confirm or exclude low humidity, cold weather, aging, frequent hot bathing or harsh cleansers, occupational wet work, atopic dermatitis, hypothyroidism, kidney disease, medication effects, or inherited cornification disorders by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as ichthyosis, eczema, psoriasis, contact dermatitis.

False Negative Situations

Dry skin may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of low humidity, cold weather, aging, frequent hot bathing or harsh cleansers, occupational wet work, atopic dermatitis, hypothyroidism, kidney disease, medication effects, or inherited cornification disorders.
