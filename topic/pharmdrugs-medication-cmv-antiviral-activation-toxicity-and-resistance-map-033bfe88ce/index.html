Used To Treat

CMV retinitis, tissue-invasive CMV disease, and transplant-related CMV prevention or treatment according to population and protocol. Ganciclovir or oral valganciclovir is usually preferred when susceptible virus and marrow reserve permit; foscarnet or cidofovir is reserved for resistance, intolerance, or selected specialist situations because nephrotoxicity and metabolic toxicity are substantial.

Description

CMV therapy is a balance between the same viral endpoint and very different activation and toxicity pathways. Ganciclovir requires the CMV UL97 kinase and then inhibits UL54 DNA polymerase; valganciclovir is its well-absorbed oral prodrug. Foscarnet is a pyrophosphate mimic that directly binds the polymerase pyrophosphate site and needs no viral kinase, so UL97-resistant CMV may remain susceptible. Cidofovir is already a nucleotide monophosphate analogue and needs only host-cell phosphorylation, also bypassing UL97. The price of bypassing activation is not safety: foscarnet can cause renal injury, calcium/magnesium/potassium disturbances, and seizures, while cidofovir concentrates in proximal tubular cells and requires probenecid plus saline.

Mechanism

CMV UL97 phosphorylates ganciclovir to its monophosphate; host kinases create ganciclovir triphosphate, which competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate at UL54 viral DNA polymerase and slows or terminates DNA elongation. UL97 mutation commonly creates low-level ganciclovir resistance by reducing activation, while UL54 mutation can create broader resistance involving ganciclovir, cidofovir, or foscarnet. Foscarnet directly and reversibly occupies the pyrophosphate-binding site on viral DNA polymerase, blocking cleavage of pyrophosphate from incoming nucleotides without intracellular activation. Cidofovir enters as a phosphonate nucleotide analogue, is converted by cellular kinases to cidofovir diphosphate, and competes at viral polymerase. Organic-anion transporter uptake into proximal tubules explains cidofovir nephrotoxicity; probenecid blocks that uptake and saline reduces tubular concentration.

Boxed Warning

Ganciclovir and valganciclovir carry boxed warnings for severe hematologic toxicity, impaired fertility, fetal toxicity, mutagenicity, and carcinogenic potential. Foscarnet labeling emphasizes dose-limiting renal impairment and seizures related to mineral and electrolyte disturbances. Cidofovir carries boxed warnings for dose-dependent nephrotoxicity, neutropenia, carcinogenicity, teratogenicity, and hypospermia and must be given with probenecid and intravenous saline. These warnings are mechanism-linked: rapidly dividing marrow cells are vulnerable to ganciclovir, while renal tubular handling drives foscarnet and cidofovir injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Ganciclovir/valganciclovir: neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, infection, GI effects, renal accumulation, and reproductive or fetal toxicity

  • Foscarnet/cidofovir: acute kidney injury, electrolyte depletion, seizures, arrhythmia, genital ulceration, uveitis or ocular hypotony, neutropenia, and infusion toxicity

Contraindications

  • Do not substitute oral valganciclovir tablets milligram-for-milligram for ganciclovir capsules or IV ganciclovir; the prodrug changes bioavailability.

  • Cidofovir requires adequate baseline renal function, urine-protein screening, probenecid tolerance, and hydration. Foscarnet requires correct renal dosing and correctable electrolyte status.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend CBC and kidney function frequently enough for the induction intensity and patient reserve. A falling neutrophil count is not an incidental lab because infection risk rises while CMV disease may still require suppression.

  • For retinitis, symptom improvement does not replace serial dilated retinal examinations; lesion location and activity determine vision risk and induction duration.

Interactions

  • Other marrow suppressants intensify ganciclovir/valganciclovir cytopenia; nephrotoxins compound every agent's renal risk.

  • Foscarnet plus other QT- or electrolyte-altering drugs raises dysrhythmia risk. Cidofovir plus nephrotoxins is avoided, and probenecid itself has sulfonamide and transporter interactions.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, platelets, creatinine/eGFR, urine protein before cidofovir, calcium, magnesium, potassium, phosphate, and liver tests as indicated

  • CMV disease-site examination, ophthalmologic findings, viral load in the appropriate context, immune recovery, hydration, neurologic status, and resistance testing after true virologic failure

Nclex Traps

  • Valganciclovir is an oral prodrug of ganciclovir, not a marrow-sparing alternative.

  • UL97 resistance can spare foscarnet and cidofovir because they do not need UL97 activation; an UL54 polymerase mutation can create cross-resistance.
