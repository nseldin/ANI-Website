Class Example Names

  • Resmetirom

Used To Treat

Adults with noncirrhotic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, historically called NASH, and moderate-to-advanced F2-F3 fibrosis in conjunction with diet and exercise; current U.S. approval is accelerated and product-specific.

Description

MASH pharmacotherapy sits on top of metabolic risk control rather than replacing it. Resmetirom is the first liver-directed THR-beta agonist in this pathway: it increases hepatic thyroid-hormone signaling to reduce intrahepatic triglyceride burden while limiting THR-alpha-mediated cardiac and bone effects. The treatment population is defined by steatohepatitis plus F2-F3 fibrosis without cirrhosis, not by an incidental fatty-liver image alone.

Mechanism

Liver-predominant THR-beta activation changes transcription of genes governing fatty-acid oxidation, mitochondrial activity, lipoprotein handling and cholesterol metabolism. Less hepatic lipid stress can reduce inflammatory injury and fibrosis-driving signaling. Because treatment response and long-term clinical benefit are not identical, accelerated approval remains contingent on confirmatory evidence.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Resmetirom can cause hepatotoxicity and gallbladder events and can raise exposure to selected statins. Avoid moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment and decompensated cirrhosis. New jaundice, right-upper-quadrant pain, fever/rash/eosinophilia, myalgia/weakness or dark urine requires immediate evaluation rather than attribution to background MASH.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, pruritus, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain and dizziness

  • Drug-induced liver injury

  • Cholelithiasis, cholecystitis and gallstone pancreatitis

  • Statin-associated liver-test elevation, myopathy or rhabdomyolysis from increased exposure

Contraindications

  • No labeled absolute contraindication, but avoid Child-Pugh B/C hepatic impairment and decompensated cirrhosis

  • Clarify acute liver injury, obstructive gallbladder symptoms and non-MASH causes of fibrosis

  • Use only for the approved noncirrhotic F2-F3 population under specialist management

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm that the patient has the indicated fibrosis stage and no decompensated cirrhosis; document diet/exercise and cardiometabolic risk management.

  • Reconcile statin name/dose and CYP2C8/OATP interacting drugs before treatment and at every transition of care.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP2C8 inhibitors such as gemfibrozil; moderate inhibitors such as clopidogrel require dose modification.

  • Limit atorvastatin, pravastatin, rosuvastatin and simvastatin according to the label and monitor toxicity.

  • OATP1B1/1B3 inhibitors can increase resmetirom exposure; resmetirom can increase exposure of CYP2C8 substrates.

Key Labs

  • AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin at baseline and during therapy

  • Fasting lipid panel, thyroid function in appropriate patients, glucose/A1c and weight/metabolic risk

  • Creatine kinase and renal/urine assessment when myopathy or rhabdomyolysis is suspected; gallbladder imaging for compatible symptoms

Nclex Traps

  • Fatty liver alone is not the labeled population; F2-F3 fibrosis and noncirrhotic MASH matter.

  • Improved transaminases do not prove fibrosis resolution or eliminate hepatotoxicity surveillance.

  • Resmetirom complements, rather than replaces, weight, diabetes, lipid, blood-pressure and alcohol-risk management.
