Definition

Plasmodium malariae is a clinically important protozoan parasite associated with quartan malaria, chronic low-grade parasitemia, and malaria-associated nephrotic syndrome. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Use mosquito avoidance and recommended prophylaxis, and include remote travel history when evaluating unexplained periodic fever or hemolysis. The reviewed route description is vector borne transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 18 to 40 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Persistent low-density red-cell infection produces periodic fever and hemolysis and can remain detectable long after travel without a dormant liver stage. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Plasmodium malariae is associated with quartan malaria, chronic low-grade parasitemia, and malaria-associated nephrotic syndrome. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
