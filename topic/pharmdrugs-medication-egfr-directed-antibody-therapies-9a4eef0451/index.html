Used To Treat

Biomarker- and histology-selected solid tumors. Cetuximab and panitumumab are principally used in defined colorectal or head-and-neck settings, amivantamab targets EGFR- or MET-driven non-small-cell lung cancer, and necitumumab is retained as a historical squamous-NSCLC reference after U.S. marketing ended in 2026.

Description

EGFR-directed antibodies are IV or subcutaneous biologic cancer therapies that bind the extracellular epidermal growth factor receptor instead of entering the cell to block its kinase. Cetuximab, panitumumab, and necitumumab prevent ligand-driven EGFR activation; amivantamab is a bispecific antibody that binds both EGFR and MET. This distinction matters immediately: an activating RAS mutation can keep the downstream growth switch on despite upstream EGFR blockade, while normal EGFR signaling in skin and renal magnesium handling explains the characteristic acneiform eruption and delayed hypomagnesemia.

Mechanism

EGFR, also called HER1 or ErbB1, is a receptor tyrosine kinase. EGF-family ligand binding promotes receptor dimerization and intracellular autophosphorylation, activating RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK proliferation, PI3K-AKT-mTOR survival, and PLC-gamma signaling. Extracellular antibodies interrupt ligand binding and receptor activation and may promote receptor internalization or immune-effector killing. If RAS is constitutively activated downstream, however, the signal no longer depends on EGFR, which is why RAS-mutant colorectal cancer generally resists cetuximab or panitumumab. EGFR also supports keratinocyte and hair-follicle integrity and stimulates TRPM6-mediated magnesium reabsorption in the distal nephron; blocking those normal pathways produces follicular inflammation, xerosis, paronychia, and renal magnesium wasting that can secondarily lower potassium and calcium.

Boxed Warning

There is no single class-wide boxed warning. Cetuximab carries boxed warnings for serious or fatal infusion reactions and cardiopulmonary arrest; panitumumab carries a boxed warning for severe dermatologic toxicity; necitumumab carried boxed warnings for cardiopulmonary arrest and hypomagnesemia. Amivantamab has no boxed warning but can cause major infusion reactions, venous thromboembolism with lazertinib, ILD/pneumonitis, and severe skin or ocular toxicity. Product identity therefore determines the emergency plan.

Adverse Effects

  • Acneiform folliculitis, xerosis, fissures, pruritus, paronychia, and secondary skin infection from normal epithelial EGFR blockade

  • Hypomagnesemia with possible secondary hypokalemia or hypocalcemia; severe depletion can cause weakness, seizure, QT instability, or dysrhythmia

  • Infusion-related reactions, especially early with IV cetuximab or amivantamab

  • Diarrhea, mucositis, fatigue, ocular surface inflammation, and photosensitivity

  • Agent-specific ILD/pneumonitis, thromboembolism, cardiopulmonary arrest, and embryo-fetal harm

Contraindications

  • Do not treat RAS-mutant or unknown-RAS metastatic colorectal cancer with cetuximab or panitumumab as if upstream EGFR blockade can switch off constitutively active RAS; follow the exact label and regimen-specific exception, such as panitumumab plus sotorasib for selected KRAS G12C disease.

  • A prior severe hypersensitivity reaction, uncontrolled electrolyte depletion, active unexplained pneumonitis, or inability to provide agent-specific monitoring requires prescriber reassessment rather than automatic class substitution.

  • Necitumumab should not be presented as a currently marketed U.S. option; its card is retained for historical treatment, medication reconciliation, and toxicity recognition.

Interactions

  • Combining EGFR antibodies with specific chemotherapy or targeted drugs changes both benefit and toxicity; examples include cetuximab plus encorafenib, panitumumab plus sotorasib, and amivantamab plus lazertinib.

  • Panitumumab combined with bevacizumab-containing chemotherapy increased toxicity and mortality and is not a generic intensification strategy.

  • Anticoagulants, QT-prolonging drugs, magnesium-wasting drugs, nephrotoxins, and other pneumonitis-producing therapies can amplify agent-specific risks even when they do not change antibody concentrations.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm tumor site, histology, molecular result, exact antibody, formulation, partner drugs, and line of therapy before treatment; the shared EGFR target does not make these agents interchangeable.

  • Inspect skin, nails, eyes, mouth, respiratory status, hydration, and infection signs; trend magnesium, potassium, and calcium through the agent-specific post-treatment window because antibody persistence and renal wasting continue after the last infusion.

  • Know the first-dose emergency pattern: cetuximab requires alpha-gal/tick/red-meat risk assessment and H1 premedication, while IV amivantamab uses a split first dose and extensive premedication because most infusion reactions occur with initial exposure.

Key Labs

  • Tumor-specific RAS, BRAF, EGFR, MET, or companion-diagnostic result

  • Magnesium, potassium, calcium, creatinine, and hydration status before and during treatment and for the labeled period after therapy

  • CBC and liver tests according to combination regimen

  • Oxygenation and chest imaging for new cough or dyspnea

  • ECG when severe electrolyte depletion, syncope, palpitations, or other QT risk is present

Population Risks

  • Pregnancy: EGFR-directed therapy can harm a fetus; follow product-specific contraception and post-dose intervals.

  • Older adults and patients with cardiac disease, baseline electrolyte depletion, poor skin integrity, pulmonary disease, or multiple interacting anticancer drugs may have less reserve when toxicity develops.

  • A history of tick bites, alpha-gal syndrome, red-meat allergy, or severe mammalian-product allergy is particularly important before cetuximab.

Nclex Traps

  • An acneiform EGFR rash is follicular inflammation, not ordinary acne; severity, infection, pain, and functional impact determine management.

  • A normal magnesium level on the last infusion day does not end risk. Renal wasting and the long antibody half-life can produce delayed depletion, which is why several labels continue monitoring for at least 8 weeks.

  • Cetuximab and panitumumab both block EGFR, but cetuximab is chimeric IgG1 with alpha-gal and stronger infusion/immune-effector implications, whereas panitumumab is fully human IgG2 with a boxed dermatologic warning.
