Definition

Möbius sign is a bedside ocular-motor sign. It is a clue to thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness. A positive finding means one or both eyes cannot sustain convergence.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Möbius sign means one or both eyes cannot sustain convergence.

Why It Matters

The observed Möbius sign finding can signal thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness. Extraocular muscle dysfunction or fatigability in Graves orbitopathy impairs coordinated adduction. Möbius sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to follow a target slowly toward the nose.

Positive Finding

One or both eyes cannot sustain convergence.

Mechanism

Extraocular muscle dysfunction or fatigability in Graves orbitopathy impairs coordinated adduction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Möbius sign finding raises concern for thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness because extraocular muscle dysfunction or fatigability in Graves orbitopathy impairs coordinated adduction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Möbius sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Möbius sign to focus evaluation of thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness and the reviewed alternatives: convergence insufficiency, third nerve palsy, myasthenia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to follow a target slowly toward the nose. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Möbius sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, refractive error, attention, and baseline strabismus affect convergence. Therefore, Möbius sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • convergence insufficiency

  • third nerve palsy

  • myasthenia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as convergence insufficiency, third nerve palsy, myasthenia.

False Negative Situations

Möbius sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct. Möbius sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness. Apply the reviewed limitation for Möbius sign: Age, refractive error, attention, and baseline strabismus affect convergence.

Safety Precautions

Observe Möbius sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to follow a target slowly toward the nose. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Stop if diplopia or nausea worsens and do not equate convergence failure with thyrotoxicosis.

Nursing Relevance

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask the patient to follow a target slowly toward the nose. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, thyroid eye disease with convergence weakness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
