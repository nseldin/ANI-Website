Name

Serum osmolality

Range

Measured serum osmolality is reported in mOsm/kg of water, commonly by freezing-point depression. Mayo Clinic Laboratories lists 275-295 mOsm/kg as one example interval, but method, laboratory, age, and clinical context matter; use the printed interval. Measured osmolality is not interchangeable with a calculated estimate, and their difference is the osmolar or osmolal gap.

Why

An abnormal serum-osmolality result signals an imbalance between body water and dissolved particles. High measured osmolality raises concern for water deficit or hypernatremia, hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state, or arginine vasopressin deficiency or resistance (diabetes insipidus) when polyuria and dilute urine fit; a measured-calculated gap can raise concern for an unmeasured solute such as a toxic alcohol. Low measured osmolality usually supports hypotonic hyponatremia from excess water relative to solute; SIADH is one important cause, but adrenal, thyroid, kidney, heart, liver, medication, and fluid causes require evaluation. Promptly assess mental status, headache, nausea or vomiting, seizure activity, thirst and water access, vital signs, volume status, weight, intake and output, urine volume, glucose, medicines, and administered fluids. Cause-directed evaluation can include repeat serum sodium, glucose, BUN and measured osmolality with same-time urine osmolality and sodium, kidney function, endocrine testing, or an osmolar gap and specific toxin concentrations as ordered. Escalate emergently for seizure, reduced consciousness, cardiorespiratory instability, severe dehydration or hypernatremia with uncontrolled urine loss, or rapid deterioration; symptom severity and rate of change matter more than one osmolality number. One serum-osmolality result does not diagnose SIADH, diabetes insipidus, HHS, dehydration, or poisoning, and correction of sodium or water balance must follow a clinician-directed, closely monitored pathway because overly rapid change can cause neurologic harm.

High Causes

  • Water deficit or dehydration

  • Hypernatremia

  • Hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state

  • Arginine vasopressin deficiency or resistance with polyuria and dilute urine

  • An unmeasured solute or toxic alcohol when the osmolar gap and exposure history fit

Low Causes

  • Hypotonic hyponatremia from excess water relative to solute

  • SIADH after the required exclusion and urine studies

  • Heart, liver, kidney, adrenal, thyroid, medication, or administered-fluid causes depending context

Critical Concerns

  • Seizure, reduced consciousness, cardiorespiratory instability, or rapid deterioration with a sodium or water-balance disorder requires emergency escalation.

  • Known AVP deficiency with uncontrolled urine loss, severe dehydration, hypernatremia, impaired thirst, or inability to access water is a medical emergency.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess neurologic status, thirst and water access, vital signs, volume status, weight, intake and output, urine volume, glucose, medicines, and administered fluids.

  • Trend serum sodium and osmolality with same-time urine osmolality and sodium when ordered; use the same laboratory method during active correction when possible.

  • Follow the clinician-directed correction and monitoring plan; do not independently accelerate sodium or free-water correction.

Nclex Traps

  • Measured serum osmolality, calculated osmolarity, and the osmolar gap are related but different results.

  • Low serum osmolality does not by itself diagnose SIADH, and high serum osmolality with dilute polyuria does not by itself diagnose diabetes insipidus.

  • Treat symptoms and the underlying water-sodium disorder through the ordered pathway, not an isolated osmolality number.
