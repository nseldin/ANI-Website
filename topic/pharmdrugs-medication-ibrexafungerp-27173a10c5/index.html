Class

Oral triterpenoid fungal beta-1,3-D-glucan synthase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Vulvovaginal candidiasis and reduction of recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis in adult and postmenarchal pediatric females using distinct one-day or monthly-for-six-month regimens.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - embryo-fetal toxicity. BREXAFEMME is contraindicated in pregnancy based on fetal harm in animal studies. Verify nonpregnant status before treatment; when used monthly for recurrent-VVC reduction, reassess before each dose. Use effective contraception throughout treatment and for 4 days after the last dose. Strong CYP3A inhibitors require dose adjustment and strong/moderate inducers can reduce efficacy.

Mechanism

Ibrexafungerp inhibits the Fks glucan-synthase complex and decreases beta-1,3-D-glucan polymerization. Loss of this cell-wall scaffold produces osmotic instability and fungal death. Its partially distinct binding region can retain activity against some isolates with reduced echinocandin susceptibility, but FKS mutations can still cause cross-resistance and culture/susceptibility matters when infection persists.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm candidiasis rather than bacterial vaginosis, STI, dermatitis or noninfectious vulvovaginitis; obtain culture/speciation for recurrent, severe or treatment-refractory disease.

  • Distinguish the one-day VVC regimen from the one-day-each-month for six months recurrent-VVC regimen. Document pregnancy test timing, contraception and interacting CYP3A drugs.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing before treatment and before monthly doses, fungal culture/speciation when indicated, liver/renal assessment for complex patients, symptom resolution and recurrence pattern.

Nclex Traps

  • Ibrexafungerp is not an azole and does not inhibit ergosterol synthesis.

  • Symptom improvement does not remove the monthly pregnancy-verification requirement during recurrent-VVC prevention.

Description

Ibrexafungerp is the first oral triterpenoid inhibitor of fungal beta-1,3-D-glucan synthase. It weakens Candida cell walls through the same enzyme complex targeted by echinocandins but binds differently and is orally bioavailable. Its major safety distinction is a boxed embryo-fetal-toxicity warning, making pregnancy verification central to every treatment plan.
