Definition

von Graefe sign is an observed ocular movement sign. It is a clue to thyroid eye disease. A positive finding means the upper lid lags behind the downward-moving globe, exposing superior sclera.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive von Graefe sign means the upper lid lags behind the downward-moving globe, exposing superior sclera.

Why It Matters

A positive von Graefe sign response can signal thyroid eye disease. Fibrotic or overactive upper-lid retractors fail to relax smoothly during downgaze. von Graefe sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid eye disease

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to follow a slowly descending target.

Positive Finding

The upper lid lags behind the downward-moving globe, exposing superior sclera.

Mechanism

Fibrotic or overactive upper-lid retractors fail to relax smoothly during downgaze.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive von Graefe sign response raises concern for thyroid eye disease because fibrotic or overactive upper-lid retractors fail to relax smoothly during downgaze. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; von Graefe sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use von Graefe sign to focus evaluation of thyroid eye disease and the reviewed alternatives: Collier sign, mechanical eyelid disease, normal variant. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to follow a slowly descending target. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating von Graefe sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fatigue, attention, and baseline retraction affect appearance. Therefore, von Graefe sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Collier sign

  • mechanical eyelid disease

  • normal variant

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from Collier sign, mechanical eyelid disease, normal variant, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

von Graefe sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; not specific for hyperthyroidism. When performed as described, von Graefe sign can adjust concern for thyroid eye disease, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for von Graefe sign: Fatigue, attention, and baseline retraction affect appearance.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing von Graefe sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to follow a slowly descending target. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Move the target slowly and distinguish lid lag from fixed lid retraction.
