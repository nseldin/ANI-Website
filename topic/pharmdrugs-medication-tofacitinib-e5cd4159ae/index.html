Class

Oral targeted synthetic DMARD that inhibits Janus kinases, functionally strongest at JAK1 and JAK3

Used To Treat

Label-defined patients with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, ulcerative colitis, and polyarticular-course juvenile idiopathic arthritis after inadequate response or intolerance to specified prior therapy. Immediate-release and extended-release products are not milligram-for-milligram interchangeable, and ulcerative-colitis induction uses a distinct time-limited higher dose.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Serious infections including tuberculosis and invasive fungal, bacterial, viral, and opportunistic infections; higher all-cause mortality; malignancies including lymphoma and lung cancer; major adverse cardiovascular events; and thrombosis, including pulmonary embolism, deep-vein thrombosis, and arterial thrombosis, have occurred. Risks were demonstrated in a large rheumatoid-arthritis safety trial in patients age 50 or older with cardiovascular risk factors compared with TNF blockers. Use only after inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF blockers for relevant indications, assess individual cardiovascular, smoking, cancer, infection, and clot risk, and stop for serious infection, MACE, or thrombosis.

Mechanism

Cytokine receptors lack their own catalytic kinase. Ligand binding brings receptor-associated JAK pairs together; the JAKs phosphorylate one another and the receptor, recruit STAT proteins, then phosphorylate STATs so they dimerize and enter the nucleus. Tofacitinib occupies the ATP-binding region of JAK catalytic domains and reduces phosphorylation of JAK1/JAK3-dependent pathways, with additional JAK2/TYK2 effects at higher exposure. Reduced common-gamma-chain signaling alters T-, B-, and natural-killer-cell function; reduced IL-6 and interferon signaling lowers inflammatory transcription. This is functional pathway preference rather than absolute isoform selectivity. CYP3A4, with a smaller CYP2C19 contribution, clears the drug, so inhibitors can raise exposure and strong inducers can cause treatment failure. Renal and hepatic impairment also alter dosing.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain TB and viral-hepatitis assessment, CBC, liver tests, renal function, lipids, pregnancy context, and vaccination review before treatment; repeat CBC/liver testing and check lipids about 4 to 8 weeks after initiation.

  • Teach urgent evaluation for fever, shingles, chest pain, focal neurologic deficit, unilateral swelling, sudden dyspnea, severe abdominal pain, or unexplained weight loss; do not let a normal CRP overrule symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Current U.S. labeling lists no formal contraindication, but active serious infection requires avoidance or interruption and severe hepatic impairment is not recommended.

  • Clarify age, current or past smoking, ASCVD, VTE, cancer, TB/hepatitis exposure, recurrent zoster, diverticulitis or GI-perforation risk, cytopenias, renal/hepatic function, pregnancy, and vaccines.

Interactions

  • Avoid biologic DMARDs, other JAK inhibitors, or potent immunosuppressants such as azathioprine or cyclosporine; avoid live vaccines.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or combined moderate CYP3A4 plus strong CYP2C19 inhibition can require dose reduction; strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampin reduce exposure and should be avoided.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and hemoglobin, AST/ALT, creatinine/eGFR, fasting lipids, TB and hepatitis assessment, and pregnancy testing when relevant

  • Infection and zoster surveillance, skin/cancer screening, cardiovascular and VTE assessment, abdominal symptoms, and disease activity

Nclex Traps

  • An oral tablet is not automatically safer than an injection; broad intracellular cytokine blockade carries boxed infection, cancer, MACE, mortality, and thrombosis risks.

  • Xeljanz immediate-release and XR doses are formulation-specific, and ulcerative-colitis induction dosing should not be continued indefinitely without reassessment.

Description

Tofacitinib is an oral targeted synthetic DMARD and Janus kinase inhibitor that enters cells, inhibits JAK1/JAK3-predominant signaling, and prevents multiple cytokine receptors from activating STAT transcription programs. It is a small-molecule intracellular immunomodulator, not a biologic antibody. Because gamma-chain cytokines such as IL-2, IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15, and IL-21 use JAK3, and interferon and IL-6-family pathways use JAK1, one drug can reduce lymphocyte activation, inflammatory-cell survival, and acute inflammatory programs. The same breadth explains why it can treat several immune diseases and why infection, zoster, cytopenias, lipids, malignancy, major cardiovascular events, and thrombosis require more vigilance than its convenient oral route suggests.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, nasopharyngitis, headache, diarrhea, hypertension, acne, herpes zoster, nausea, and lipid elevation

  • Serious/opportunistic infection, TB, malignancy, MACE, venous or arterial thrombosis, GI perforation, lymphopenia, neutropenia, anemia, liver injury, and hypersensitivity
