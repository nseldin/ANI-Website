Used To Treat

Induction and/or maintenance of general anesthesia according to agent, age, airway, surgery, vaporizer, and monitored anesthesia plan.

Description

Volatile anesthetics are inhaled liquids delivered as calibrated vapor to produce unconsciousness, amnesia, immobility, and dose-dependent cardiorespiratory depression. Lower blood-gas solubility produces faster wash-in and wash-out: desflurane and sevoflurane generally change depth faster than isoflurane. Airway pungency, sympathetic stimulation, metabolism, carbon-dioxide-absorbent chemistry, and pediatric suitability separate the agents clinically.

Mechanism

No single receptor explains general anesthesia. Volatile agents enhance inhibitory GABA-A and glycine signaling, activate background potassium currents, suppress excitatory nicotinic/NMDA and spinal motor networks, and reduce thalamocortical integration. Minimum alveolar concentration (MAC) compares potency: a lower MAC means greater potency. MAC falls with age, hypothermia, opioids, sedatives and several physiologic states.

Boxed Warning

The class can trigger malignant hyperthermia in susceptible patients, especially with succinylcholine. Rising end-tidal carbon dioxide despite ventilation, unexplained tachycardia, rigidity, acidosis, hyperkalemia and hyperthermia demand immediate trigger discontinuation, 100% oxygen, dantrolene and crisis treatment. Hypotension, respiratory depression, QT effects, hepatic reactions and perioperative hyperkalemia also require continuous monitoring.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify a functioning agent-specific vaporizer, oxygen supply, ventilation, scavenging, fresh carbon-dioxide absorbent, temperature and end-tidal gas monitoring before use.

  • Know the early malignant-hyperthermia pattern: increased CO2 and tachycardia usually appear before a dramatic temperature rise. Do not wait for fever to escalate.

Key Labs

  • Continuous end-tidal CO2, oxygen saturation, inspired/end-tidal anesthetic concentration, ECG, blood pressure and temperature; ABG/VBG, potassium, creatine kinase, lactate, coagulation, urine myoglobin and renal function when malignant hyperthermia or rhabdomyolysis is suspected.

Nclex Traps

  • MAC is an inverse potency measure: lower MAC means more potent.

  • Desflurane's fast offset does not make it a smooth inhaled induction drug; its pungency can provoke coughing and laryngospasm.

  • Malignant hyperthermia is treated on early physiologic evidence, not after late hyperthermia.
