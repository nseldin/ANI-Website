Used To Treat

Rapid treatment or prevention of bronchospasm in reversible obstructive airway disease and prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm. It relieves tightened airway smooth muscle within minutes but does not treat the airway inflammation that drives asthma attacks, so frequent need signals inadequate control rather than a reason to rely on albuterol alone.

Mechanism

Albuterol preferentially binds beta2-adrenergic receptors on airway smooth muscle. The receptor couples to Gs, activates adenylyl cyclase, converts ATP to cyclic AMP, and activates protein kinase A. PKA reduces myosin light-chain kinase activity, lowers calcium-dependent contractile signaling, and promotes potassium-channel opening, so actin-myosin interaction falls and the bronchial wall relaxes. Beta2 receptors on skeletal muscle explain tremor; cardiac beta receptors and reflex effects explain tachycardia. Beta2 stimulation of skeletal-muscle Na+/K+-ATPase shifts potassium intracellularly, while increased glycolysis and lipolysis can increase pyruvate and lactate during intensive treatment. This lactate can drive tachypnea that is mistaken for persistent bronchospasm, so airflow and work of breathing must be reassessed rather than reflexively stacking doses.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Paradoxical bronchospasm may be life-threatening and often appears with first use of a new canister; stop the product and use an alternative if breathing acutely worsens after inhalation. Increasing dose frequency, reduced duration of relief, nighttime symptoms, or failure to respond can indicate deteriorating asthma or COPD. Excessive systemic beta stimulation can cause tachyarrhythmia, ischemia, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, or lactic acidosis.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to albuterol or product components; dry-powder formulations may have product-specific milk-protein restrictions

  • Use caution with significant tachyarrhythmia, ischemic heart disease, hyperthyroidism, seizure disorder, diabetes, or uncorrected hypokalemia

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess breath sounds, air entry, speech, respiratory effort, oxygenation, peak flow when appropriate, heart rate, and response before and after treatment. A silent or quieter chest with fatigue can be ominous.

  • Verify metered-dose, dry-powder, or nebulized formulation and technique. Track canister count and rescue frequency; repeated use is clinical data about control.

Interactions

  • Nonselective beta blockers can block bronchodilation and provoke bronchospasm; cardioselective agents still require clinical judgment.

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, stimulants, other sympathomimetics, and digoxin or arrhythmogenic drugs can alter cardiovascular risk; non-potassium-sparing diuretics magnify hypokalemia.

Key Labs

  • Peak flow or spirometry, heart rate/rhythm, oxygenation, symptoms, and inhaler technique

  • Potassium, glucose, lactate, acid-base status, and ECG during repeated high-dose therapy or suspected toxicity

Nclex Traps

  • Albuterol can improve wheeze while asthma inflammation continues; rescue use does not replace an ICS-containing plan.

  • Tachypnea after many doses may reflect beta2-driven lactic acidosis as well as persistent airflow obstruction.

Description

Albuterol is the racemic, rapid-onset short-acting beta2 agonist used as a rescue bronchodilator. It contains active R-albuterol and much less active S-albuterol. By raising cyclic AMP in airway smooth muscle, it relaxes constricted bronchi and improves airflow within minutes. The opening distinction is essential: albuterol treats bronchospasm, not the mucosal edema, eosinophilic inflammation, and mucus biology that make asthma dangerous. If doses are needed more often, give less relief, or wear off early, the disease may be worsening even when each puff briefly helps. Repeated high doses can shift potassium into cells and increase lactate, tremor, and tachycardia.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor, nervousness, headache, palpitations, sinus tachycardia, muscle cramps, throat irritation, and nausea

  • Hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, lactic acidosis, QT effects, arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, hypersensitivity, or paradoxical bronchospasm
