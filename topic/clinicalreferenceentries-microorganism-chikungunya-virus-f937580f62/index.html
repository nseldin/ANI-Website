Definition

Chikungunya virus is a clinically important virus associated with acute febrile polyarthralgia, persistent inflammatory arthralgia, and rare neurologic or cardiac disease. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Mosquito-bite prevention, vector control, and use of vaccination only under current age-, risk-, and jurisdiction-specific guidance reduce disease. The reviewed route description is arthropod borne transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 1 to 12 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

  • Content

    Chikungunya produces high-level viremia and inflammation in joints, muscle, and connective tissue, sometimes leaving prolonged disabling arthralgia. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Chikungunya virus is associated with acute febrile polyarthralgia, persistent inflammatory arthralgia, and rare neurologic or cardiac disease. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
