Definition

Fasciculations are brief, fine, irregular muscle twitches visible under the skin. They arise when a motor axon and the muscle fibers it controls discharge spontaneously.

Plain Language

A small part of a muscle flickers or twitches under the skin without moving the whole joint.

Why It Matters

Fasciculations can be benign, especially after exercise or stimulant use, but progressive twitching with weakness, muscle wasting, reduced reflexes, swallowing difficulty, or breathing symptoms can signal lower-motor-neuron disease and needs prompt evaluation.

Assessment Method

Inspect relaxed muscles in good or tangential light, including the symptomatic region and tongue when indicated; document distribution, frequency, persistence, cramps, weakness, atrophy, tone, reflexes, sensation, bulbar and respiratory symptoms, progression, exercise, sleep, caffeine, medications, and family history.

Positive Finding

Brief fine irregular contractions ripple or twitch under the skin without producing purposeful joint movement.

Mechanism

A motor axon discharges spontaneously and activates the muscle fibers belonging to its motor unit, creating a visible localized twitch.

Evidence Limitations

Fasciculations can occur in healthy muscle after exercise, stress, poor sleep, or stimulant use; visible fasciculation is not the same as fibrillation detected only by electromyography and does not by itself diagnose motor-neuron disease. A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tremor, myokymia, myoclonus, muscle spasm.
