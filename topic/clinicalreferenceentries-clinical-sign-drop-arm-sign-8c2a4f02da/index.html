Definition

Drop-arm sign is a bedside shoulder sign. It is a clue to large rotator-cuff tear. A positive finding means the arm drops suddenly or cannot be controlled.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Drop-arm sign means the arm drops suddenly or cannot be controlled.

Why It Matters

The observed Drop-arm sign finding can signal large rotator-cuff tear. A major cuff defect cannot center the humeral head and sustain eccentric abduction. Drop-arm sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large rotator-cuff tear

Assessment Method

Passively abduct the arm and ask the patient to lower it slowly.

Positive Finding

The arm drops suddenly or cannot be controlled.

Mechanism

A major cuff defect cannot center the humeral head and sustain eccentric abduction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Drop-arm sign finding raises concern for large rotator-cuff tear because a major cuff defect cannot center the humeral head and sustain eccentric abduction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Drop-arm sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Drop-arm sign to focus evaluation of large rotator-cuff tear and the reviewed alternatives: deltoid palsy, fracture, adhesive capsulitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Passively abduct the arm and ask the patient to lower it slowly. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Drop-arm sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain alone can cause dropping and partial tears may be negative. Therefore, Drop-arm sign cannot confirm or exclude large rotator-cuff tear by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • deltoid palsy

  • fracture

  • adhesive capsulitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as deltoid palsy, fracture, adhesive capsulitis.

False Negative Situations

Drop-arm sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of large rotator-cuff tear.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Observe Drop-arm sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Passively abduct the arm and ask the patient to lower it slowly. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Guard the arm so it does not fall or strike the patient.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Drop-arm sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The arm drops suddenly or cannot be controlled. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Passively abduct the arm and ask the patient to lower it slowly. Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
