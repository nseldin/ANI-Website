Definition

Retinoblastoma develops when a retinal precursor cell loses RB1 tumor-suppressor function, producing a rapidly growing childhood intraocular malignancy that may be hereditary and bilateral. The characteristic pattern is leukocoria in a photograph or examination, strabismus, reduced vision, red painful eye, or bilateral tumors in a young child. Confirm or stage it with urgent dilated examination under anesthesia with ocular ultrasound and retinal imaging; distinguish it from congenital cataract, Coats disease, persistent fetal vasculature, toxocariasis, and retinal detachment by specialist ocular imaging. The priority is to use focal laser, cryotherapy, intra-arterial, intravitreal, or systemic chemotherapy according to eye and tumor stage because vision loss can complicate Retinoblastoma. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Vision change, eye pain, photophobia, foreign-body sensation, redness, drainage, hearing change, vertigo, nasal bleeding, throat swelling, or facial/ear pain anchor eye and ENT assessment.

  • Escalate sudden vision loss, painful red eye, chemical exposure, penetrating eye injury, posterior/uncontrolled nosebleed, airway swelling, or neurologic symptoms.

Diagnostics

  • urgent dilated examination under anesthesia with ocular ultrasound and retinal imaging

  • MRI of brain and orbits to assess optic nerve, extraocular spread, and hereditary trilateral disease

  • genetic counseling and germline RB1 testing

  • Distinguish Retinoblastoma from congenital cataract, Coats disease, persistent fetal vasculature, toxocariasis, and retinal detachment by specialist ocular imaging.

Labs

  • labs are often not primary unless infection/systemic disease is suspected

Treatments

  • use focal laser, cryotherapy, intra-arterial, intravitreal, or systemic chemotherapy according to eye and tumor stage

  • enucleate an eye with advanced visionless or high-risk disease

  • provide lifelong hereditary cancer and family surveillance

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate promptly for leukocoria, a new crossed eye, absent red reflex, unexplained visual behavior change, painful enlarged eye, or family history through an urgent pediatric ophthalmic-oncology referral because early localized disease offers the best survival, eye, and vision outcomes.

  • Document laterality, onset, family history, photographs noticed by caregivers, visual behavior, pain, proptosis, and neurologic symptoms without performing an invasive biopsy because tumor disruption can risk extraocular spread and diagnosis is usually made by specialized examination and imaging.

  • Prepare for examination under anesthesia, ocular ultrasound, and magnetic resonance imaging as directed, monitoring fasting, anesthesia, and recovery safety because complete retinal mapping and assessment of optic-nerve or extraocular extension determine treatment.

  • During systemic, intra-arterial, intravitreal, laser, cryotherapy, radiation, or surgical care, trend blood counts, fever, bleeding, eye pain and redness, vision, vascular access, and treatment-specific toxicity because cure and eye salvage require tightly coordinated multidisciplinary therapy.

  • Coordinate RB1 genetic counseling and examination of both eyes and at-risk relatives, then maintain surveillance for new tumors, recurrence, trilateral disease, vision development, and later cancers because heritable disease affects every retinal cell and lifelong risk extends beyond the treated eye.

Complications

  • vision loss can complicate Retinoblastoma.

  • optic-nerve or extraocular spread

  • metastatic disease can complicate Retinoblastoma.

  • pineal tumor can complicate Retinoblastoma.

  • second malignancies can complicate Retinoblastoma.

Red Flags

  • Leukocoria, absent red reflex, new strabismus, or unexplained loss of visual behavior

  • Proptosis, painful enlarged eye, severe redness, orbital swelling, or suspected extraocular extension

  • Severe headache, vomiting, focal deficit, seizure, or other concern for intracranial disease

  • Fever during immunosuppressive therapy, uncontrolled bleeding, severe eye pain, or sudden post-treatment vision change

Patient Education

  • A white pupil seen in person or repeatedly in flash photographs needs prompt dilated examination; do not wait for pain, because early retinoblastoma is often painless.

  • Keep every eye and genetics appointment even after successful treatment, and ask which relatives need testing; hereditary retinoblastoma can affect both eyes and increase later cancer risk.

Nclex Traps

  • Leukocoria is an urgent cancer warning even when the child seems well

  • Do not biopsy through the eye because tumor seeding can worsen prognosis
