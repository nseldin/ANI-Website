Definition

Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid is a fluid-pattern sign. It is a clue to possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma. A positive finding means a central blood spot is surrounded by a lighter ring.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid means a central blood spot is surrounded by a lighter ring.

Why It Matters

The Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid pattern can signal possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma. Lower-viscosity fluid migrates farther through paper than blood cells. Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma

Assessment Method

Allow drainage to fall onto absorbent material only if it occurs spontaneously.

Positive Finding

A central blood spot is surrounded by a lighter ring.

Mechanism

Lower-viscosity fluid migrates farther through paper than blood cells.

Clinical Interpretation

The Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid pattern raises concern for possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma because lower-viscosity fluid migrates farther through paper than blood cells. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid to focus evaluation of possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma and the reviewed alternatives: irrigation fluid, tears, serous drainage. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Allow drainage to fall onto absorbent material only if it occurs spontaneously. Evidence Limitations

Blood mixed with water or nasal secretions can create the same halo. Therefore, Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid cannot confirm or exclude possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Negative Situations

Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid may be absent even when possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status

Outdated and unreliable; glucose or beta-2 transferrin testing is preferred. Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude possible CSF mixed with blood after craniofacial trauma. Apply the reviewed limitation for Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid: Blood mixed with water or nasal secretions can create the same halo.

Safety Precautions

Observe Halo sign for cerebrospinal fluid with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Allow drainage to fall onto absorbent material only if it occurs spontaneously. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not provoke drainage, pack the nose, or use the halo pattern to clear a skull fracture.
