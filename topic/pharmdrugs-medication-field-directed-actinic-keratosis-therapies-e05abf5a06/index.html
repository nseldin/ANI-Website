Used To Treat

Field treatment of multiple actinic keratoses; selected strengths/regimens also treat carefully defined superficial basal cell carcinoma, and imiquimod treats external anogenital warts.

Description

Field therapy treats visible lesions plus subclinical dysplasia across sun-damaged skin. Imiquimod activates TLR7 on innate immune cells, inducing interferon and cytokine-mediated clearance. Topical fluorouracil is converted inside proliferating cells to metabolites that inhibit thymidylate synthase and disrupt RNA. Both deliberately create inflammation, but the expected sequence, schedule, eligible lesion, anatomic site, pregnancy risk, and follow-up differ substantially.

Mechanism

TLR7 signals through MyD88, IRAK, and NF-kappa-B/interferon-regulatory pathways to induce IFN-alpha, TNF-alpha, and other mediators that recruit cytotoxic immunity. Fluorouracil becomes FdUMP, which traps thymidylate synthase with reduced folate and depletes dTMP, while FUTP incorporation damages RNA. Dysplastic keratinocytes divide rapidly and are preferentially injured, producing erythema, erosion, crusting, and later re-epithelialization.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Topical fluorouracil is contraindicated in pregnancy and DPD deficiency; severe systemic toxicity can occur if catabolism is impaired. Imiquimod can cause intense local reactions and influenza-like systemic symptoms. Neither drug should be used to empirically treat a lesion that may be invasive squamous carcinoma, melanoma, or a non-superficial basal-cell subtype without diagnosis.

Adverse Effects

  • Erythema, erosion, ulceration, crusting, burning, pain, photosensitivity and pigment change

  • Imiquimod: flu-like symptoms, headache, myalgia and lymphadenopathy

  • Fluorouracil: severe mucositis, diarrhea, cytopenia or neurotoxicity with rare systemic exposure/DPD deficiency

Contraindications

  • Fluorouracil: pregnancy, DPD deficiency and serious hypersensitivity

  • Avoid mucosal/ocular exposure and unconfirmed suspicious lesions

  • Imiquimod regimens require immune status, lesion type, size and anatomic-site eligibility

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain the expected inflammatory timeline before treatment so a therapeutic reaction is not mistaken for allergy or stopped too early.

  • Photograph/map the field, protect from UV, prevent transfer to others, and ensure post-treatment examination for residual or invasive disease.

Interactions

  • Occlusion increases topical fluorouracil penetration and toxicity.

  • Other irritants, peels, retinoids and UV exposure amplify skin injury.

  • Systemic brivudine/sorivudine-like DPD inhibitors are dangerous with fluoropyrimidines.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy status and DPD history before fluorouracil

  • CBC/metabolic assessment if systemic toxicity is suspected

  • Biopsy/pathology and serial skin examination for persistent or suspicious lesions

Nclex Traps

  • Inflammation is part of field clearance, but fever, severe diarrhea, mucositis or cytopenic symptoms are not routine skin reactions.

  • Topical therapy does not replace surgery for an accessible invasive or higher-risk skin cancer.

  • Imiquimod is immune activation; fluorouracil is antimetabolite cytotoxicity.
