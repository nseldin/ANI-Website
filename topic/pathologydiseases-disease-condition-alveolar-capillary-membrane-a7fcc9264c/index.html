Plain Meaning

The thin interface where oxygen moves from alveolar air into pulmonary capillary blood and carbon dioxide moves the other direction.

Word Origin

The term names the shared air-blood barrier: alveolus on one side, capillary blood on the other.

Pathology

Gas exchange depends on an extremely thin, dry, well-perfused alveolar-capillary membrane. Edema, inflammation, fibrosis, hemorrhage, or hyaline-membrane injury thickens or floods the barrier, increasing diffusion distance and worsening oxygen transfer. In ARDS, diffuse injury makes the membrane leaky, so protein-rich fluid enters alveoli and oxygenation can become refractory.

Etiology

ARDS, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, pulmonary fibrosis, inhalation injury, aspiration, sepsis-related lung injury, transfusion-related acute lung injury, and other inflammatory or fibrotic lung disorders.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, hypoxemia, exertional desaturation, crackles, increased oxygen need, cyanosis or confusion when severe

  • CO2 may be normal early because carbon dioxide diffuses more easily than oxygen until ventilation fails

Diagnostics

  • ABG/SpO2 and oxygen requirement

  • PaO2/FiO2 ratio in acute lung injury

  • Chest imaging

  • PFTs including DLCO when chronic diffusion impairment is evaluated

  • Clinical context such as ARDS trigger, edema, infection, or fibrosis

Treatments

  • Treat the cause: oxygen, PEEP/recruitment strategy, lung-protective ventilation, proning in severe ARDS when ordered, diuresis when cardiogenic/volume overload contributes, antibiotics/source control for infection, or antifibrotic/anti-inflammatory therapy when indicated

Nursing Priorities

  • Connect diffusion problems with exertional desaturation and low reserve

  • Monitor oxygenation during activity, infection, fluid overload, and procedures

  • Escalate rapidly when a chronic diffusion problem becomes acutely worse

Complications

  • Chronic hypoxemia

  • Pulmonary hypertension

  • Right-heart strain

  • Respiratory failure

  • Refractory hypoxemia

Patient Education

  • Report worsening shortness of breath, new oxygen need, chest pain, fever, or decreased activity tolerance.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal airway does not guarantee normal gas exchange if the membrane is thickened or flooded.

  • DLCO helps assess gas transfer across this interface.

  • ARDS oxygen failure is often a membrane/alveolar flooding problem, not just too little oxygen in the mask.
