Class

Low-fucose human IgG1 bispecific monoclonal antibody against EGFR and MET

Used To Treat

Selected locally advanced or metastatic non-small-cell lung cancers with EGFR exon 19 deletion, EGFR L858R, or EGFR exon 20 insertion under regimen-specific first-line or post-treatment criteria. Depending on the molecular setting it may be paired with lazertinib or carboplatin/pemetrexed, or used alone after platinum therapy.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-alert risks include infusion-related reactions, ILD/pneumonitis, venous thromboembolism with lazertinib, severe dermatologic toxicity, ocular toxicity, and embryo-fetal harm. Withhold immediately for suspected ILD and permanently discontinue confirmed disease. When used with lazertinib, provide labeled anticoagulant prophylaxis for the first 4 months unless contraindicated; vitamin K antagonists are not recommended for that prophylaxis.

Mechanism

One Fab arm binds EGFR and the other binds MET. Amivantamab blocks ligand binding, reduces receptor phosphorylation, and promotes EGFR/MET internalization and degradation. Its low-fucose Fc region binds activating Fc receptors efficiently, supporting NK-cell ADCC, macrophage phagocytosis, and trogocytosis, in which immune cells remove antibody-receptor complexes and portions of tumor-cell membrane. This combines signaling shutdown with physical immune-effector clearance. The same EGFR blockade explains rash and paronychia; the biological cause of the excess VTE observed with lazertinib is not fully established, so prophylaxis is based on clinical event risk rather than a proven single receptor mechanism.

Nursing Essentials

  • For IV therapy, verify full premedication and the split first dose, frequent vital-sign monitoring, infusion-reaction rescue readiness, and the slower restart plan after a mild or moderate reaction. The first infusion is split because initial cytokine-mediated reactions are common and concentrated early, not because half a dose is independently therapeutic.

  • For Rybrevant Faspro, verify the subcutaneous product, abdominal site, product-specific dose and administration time, and observation plan. Never use the IV dose as an SC conversion.

  • When combined with lazertinib, verify non-vitamin-K-antagonist VTE prophylaxis for the first 4 months unless contraindicated. Four months is the labeled prevention window based on observed combination risk; it does not prove VTE risk becomes zero on the next day.

Contraindications

  • A confirmed amivantamab-related ILD/pneumonitis requires permanent discontinuation; new cough, dyspnea, fever, or hypoxia must be evaluated before assuming infection or cancer progression.

  • Serious prior hypersensitivity or an uncontrolled infusion reaction requires label-directed discontinuation and specialist review.

  • Rybrevant IV and Rybrevant Faspro have different components, dose units, schedules, and administration routes; do not convert or substitute them informally.

Interactions

  • Lazertinib changes the risk profile by adding early VTE, skin, liver, and interaction considerations; prophylactic anticoagulation is part of the regimen, not optional background therapy.

  • Carboplatin/pemetrexed adds marrow suppression, renal, GI, and supportive-care requirements.

  • Anticoagulants increase bleeding risk, but omitting prophylaxis can expose the patient to serious VTE; the ordered balance must be individualized rather than solved by automatic avoidance.

Key Labs

  • Tumor EGFR mutation and other required companion-diagnostic results

  • CBC, CMP, liver tests, creatinine, magnesium, potassium, calcium, and hydration status according to regimen

  • Pulse oximetry and chest imaging for respiratory symptoms

  • Focused DVT/PE assessment and bleeding surveillance during prophylaxis

  • Ophthalmic assessment for eye pain, photophobia, or vision change

Nclex Traps

  • Amivantamab is an EGFR/MET bispecific antibody, not an oral EGFR TKI.

  • Most first-dose IV reactions are infusion-related and can occur without classic IgE allergy; stop the infusion, assess, treat, and follow the labeled restart or discontinuation rule.

  • A new cough on targeted lung-cancer therapy is not automatically tumor progression. ILD/pneumonitis can be fatal and requires immediate withholding and evaluation.

Population Risks

  • Patients with prior ILD, poor pulmonary reserve, active clotting or bleeding risk, severe skin disease, or inability to receive prophylactic anticoagulation need close multidisciplinary assessment.

  • Older or frail patients receiving platinum chemotherapy may have less marrow, renal, nutritional, and functional reserve.

  • Pregnancy exposure can harm a fetus; follow current contraception guidance for every regimen component.

Description

Amivantamab is a low-fucose human IgG1 bispecific antibody that grips both EGFR and MET on lung-cancer cells, blocks ligand signaling, promotes receptor degradation, and recruits immune cells to strip or kill the targeted cell. That dual design addresses tumors driven by EGFR and MET-mediated escape, including defined EGFR mutations, but it also produces a distinctive safety pattern: very frequent first-dose IV infusion reactions, EGFR-type skin and nail toxicity, ILD/pneumonitis, and a clinically important early venous-thromboembolism risk when combined with lazertinib. Rybrevant IV and Rybrevant Faspro subcutaneous are different formulations and must not be substituted milligram for milligram.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion-related reaction, especially with the first IV exposure

  • Acneiform rash, xerosis, pruritus, paronychia, fissures, scalp inflammation, and secondary infection

  • Venous thromboembolism including DVT and PE when combined with lazertinib

  • ILD/pneumonitis, cough, dyspnea, and hypoxia

  • Stomatitis, edema, fatigue, musculoskeletal symptoms, ocular surface toxicity, cytopenias, and liver-test abnormalities depending on regimen
