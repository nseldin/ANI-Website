Definition

Haemophilus influenzae is a clinically important bacterium associated with otitis media and sinusitis, pneumonia, epiglottitis, meningitis, and invasive bacteremia. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Haemophilus influenzae is a Gram-negative coccobacillus with a capnophile oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Nasopharyngeal colonization, IgA protease, and a type-specific capsule in encapsulated strains support local respiratory and invasive disease. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Haemophilus influenzae is associated with otitis media and sinusitis, pneumonia, epiglottitis, meningitis, and invasive bacteremia. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
