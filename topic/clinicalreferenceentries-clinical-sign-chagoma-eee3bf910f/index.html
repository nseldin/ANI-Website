Definition

Chagoma is an observed inoculation sign. It is a clue to acute Chagas disease. A positive finding means a firm erythematous swelling or nodule develops at inoculation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chagoma means a firm erythematous swelling or nodule develops at inoculation.

Why It Matters

The observed Chagoma finding can signal acute Chagas disease. Local parasite replication and inflammation create a cutaneous entry lesion. Chagoma cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • acute Chagas disease

Assessment Method

Inspect a suspected vector-entry site.

Positive Finding

A firm erythematous swelling or nodule develops at inoculation.

Mechanism

Local parasite replication and inflammation create a cutaneous entry lesion.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Chagoma finding raises concern for acute Chagas disease because local parasite replication and inflammation create a cutaneous entry lesion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chagoma does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chagoma to focus evaluation of acute Chagas disease and the reviewed alternatives: leishmaniasis, furuncle, allergic bite. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect a suspected vector-entry site. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chagoma as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It resembles common bites and may be absent. Therefore, Chagoma cannot confirm or exclude acute Chagas disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • leishmaniasis

  • furuncle

  • allergic bite

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as leishmaniasis, furuncle, allergic bite.

False Negative Situations

Chagoma may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of acute Chagas disease.
