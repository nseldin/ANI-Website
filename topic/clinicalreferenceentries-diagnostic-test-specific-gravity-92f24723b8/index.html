Summary

Specific gravity is a high-yield Renal/Urologic diagnostic reference. Measures urine concentration and hydration/renal concentrating ability.

Quick Answer

Specific gravity: Measures urine concentration and hydration/renal concentrating ability. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Low

    Meaning

    Dilute urine suggests high fluid intake, impaired concentrating ability, diabetes insipidus, or renal tubular issues depending context.

  • Label

    High

    Meaning

    Concentrated urine suggests dehydration, volume depletion, glycosuria/proteinuria, SIADH context, or contrast/solute effect depending context.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Use with urine output, sodium/osmolality, glucose, kidney function, and hydration status.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Measures urine concentration and hydration/renal concentrating ability.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
