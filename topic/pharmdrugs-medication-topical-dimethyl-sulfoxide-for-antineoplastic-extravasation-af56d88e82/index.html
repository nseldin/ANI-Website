Used To Treat

Selected mitomycin C and mitoxantrone extravasations and, under the ONS/ASCO pathway, anthracycline extravasation when dexrazoxane is unavailable or contraindicated. This focused topical use is distinct from intravesical DMSO products, industrial solvent use, cryopreservation, and unregulated home application because those products, concentrations, routes, and purity controls are not validated for injured oncology tissue.

Description

Topical dimethyl sulfoxide is a small-molecule solvent and free-radical scavenger that penetrates tissue and may promote dispersion of selected extravasated antineoplastics under an oncology protocol. Its solvent and permeability effects can help move drug from tissue while limiting oxidative propagation. The same penetration property means product identity, concentration, skin preparation, application area, drying, and avoidance of contaminants matter. DMSO does not replace immediate aspiration, agent identification, compress care, serial assessment, or timely dexrazoxane when dexrazoxane is the indicated available treatment.

Mechanism

DMSO is a polar aprotic solvent that penetrates skin, changes membrane and solvent interactions, scavenges hydroxyl and other radicals, and can increase movement of solutes through tissue. For selected vesicants, these effects may reduce local concentration and interrupt oxidative propagation of injury. Increased permeability is double edged: it may assist dispersal but can also carry surface contaminants or coapplied chemicals deeper, which explains the need for clean skin, protocol-approved product, open drying, and avoidance of improvised mixtures.

Administration Timing

  • Stop infusion, aspirate through the existing access when directed, identify the agent, and activate the oncology extravasation protocol before topical treatment.

  • Apply only the protocol-specified DMSO formulation, area, frequency, and duration; allow it to dry as directed and avoid occlusive dressings unless the protocol explicitly requires them.

  • Use the drug-specific warm or cold compress plan and serially reassess for days because erythema can improve while deeper injury continues.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm that DMSO is selected for the exact vesicant and that dexrazoxane is unavailable or contraindicated when anthracycline exposure is involved, because antidote choice changes the outcome pathway.

  • Clean and dry the site using the approved method and avoid cosmetics, creams, or contaminated gloves because DMSO can increase transdermal movement of substances.

  • Outline, photograph, and measure the lesion and document pain, blistering, perfusion, sensation, and motion because delayed progression requires early specialty care.

  • Teach that garlic-like breath or taste can follow systemic absorption but increasing pain, blistering, weakness, or numbness is not a benign odor effect.

Key Labs

  • There is no laboratory marker of DMSO success. Serial site dimensions, photographs, pain score, capillary refill, sensation, strength, and range of motion are the principal monitoring data.

  • Obtain CBC, renal and liver function, CK, and agent-specific studies after substantial tissue injury or systemic exposure because chemotherapy absorption and tissue necrosis can add marrow, organ, or muscle injury; do not use normal laboratory results to exclude local necrosis because the injury can remain confined to tissue.

Adverse Effects

  • Local warmth, burning, erythema, pruritus, dryness, dermatitis, blistering, and a garlic-like breath or taste can occur.

  • Systemic nausea, headache, dizziness, or hypersensitivity is possible; the greater danger is failed local rescue with delayed ulceration, infection, necrosis, or functional loss.

Contraindications

  • Do not substitute industrial, veterinary, supplement, or intravesical RIMSO-50 product because its concentration, excipients, purity, and route are not validated for oncology extravasation; the protocol must specify the topical formulation and handling.

  • Do not combine casually with dexrazoxane or apply to the wrong vesicant, contaminated skin, or an occluded site because permeability and agent-specific recommendations can make improvisation harmful.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Progressive pain, swelling, blistering, dusky color, perfusion change, weakness, numbness, ulceration, or systemic infection requires urgent wound, plastic-surgery, or other specialty evaluation.

  • Central-line extravasation, large volume, deep anatomic spread, or uncertain drug identity requires early imaging and specialist planning rather than topical treatment alone.

Interactions

  • Dexrazoxane is the preferred guideline antidote for anthracycline extravasation when available and appropriate; DMSO is an alternative, not an automatic add-on, and combined use lacks established benefit.

  • Other topical medicines, cleaning residues, cosmetics, and occlusive dressings can have altered absorption because DMSO increases permeability; apply only protocol-approved materials.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    A larger surface-area-to-mass ratio and small limb compartments may increase systemic absorption and functional risk; pediatric evidence is sparse and specialist protocol is required.

  • Population

    Patients with fragile skin, neuropathy, or poor circulation

    Note

    Barrier disruption can increase absorption while reduced sensation or perfusion hides progression, so objective serial skin and neurovascular examination is essential.

  • Population

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Human extravasation evidence is limited and DMSO penetrates tissue; balance fetal or infant uncertainty against the morbidity of untreated vesicant injury through oncology and obstetric consultation.

Evidence Limitations

  • ONS/ASCO recommendations are based on very-low-certainty evidence despite the strong rationale to use an available antidote for high-morbidity exposure.

  • Product concentration, regimen, and eligible agents vary across protocols; this card intentionally does not convert DMSO into a universal topical antidote.

Nclex Traps

  • RIMSO-50 is an intravesical product and must not route to topical extravasation instructions because bladder instillation labeling does not establish safe skin concentration or technique for a vesicant injury.

  • DMSO is an alternative when dexrazoxane is unavailable or contraindicated in the anthracycline pathway, not routine combination therapy, because evidence does not establish added benefit and simultaneous use can complicate the agent-specific protocol.

  • Stop and aspirate the infusion before focusing on any topical antidote.

Regulatory Status

OFF-LABEL topical use supported by the 2025 ONS/ASCO antineoplastic-extravasation guideline; no FDA-labeled topical extravasation indication.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): DMSO carries substances through skin. Use only the oncology protocol's product on a clean site, never substitute industrial or intravesical DMSO, and escalate progressive tissue injury immediately.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
