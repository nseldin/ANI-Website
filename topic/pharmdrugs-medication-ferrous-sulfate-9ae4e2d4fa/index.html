  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Used To Treat

Deficiency prevention/treatment, pregnancy nutrition support, anemia support, wound/healing context, or parenteral nutrition depending on the nutrient.

Mechanism

Ferrous sulfate works by replacing iron needed to make hemoglobin and support oxygen-carrying capacity. Oral iron depends on GI absorption and causes constipation/dark stools; IV iron bypasses absorption but requires infusion-reaction monitoring.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for Ferrous sulfate in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

Ferrous sulfate is an iron replacement: it works by replacing iron needed to make hemoglobin and support oxygen-carrying capacity. It is used for deficiency prevention/treatment, pregnancy nutrition support, anemia support, wound/healing context, or parenteral nutrition depending on the nutrient.
