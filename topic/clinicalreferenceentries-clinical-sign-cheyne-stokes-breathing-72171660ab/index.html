Definition

Cheyne-Stokes breathing is an observed respiratory pattern. It is a clue to unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction. A positive finding means respiration gradually waxes and wanes, then pauses in apnea, repeating cyclically.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cheyne-Stokes breathing means respiration gradually waxes and wanes, then pauses in apnea, repeating cyclically.

Why It Matters

The Cheyne-Stokes breathing pattern can signal unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction. Delayed blood-gas feedback causes alternating overshoot and undershoot of ventilatory drive. Cheyne-Stokes breathing cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe several minutes of breathing and oxygenation.

Positive Finding

Respiration gradually waxes and wanes, then pauses in apnea, repeating cyclically.

Mechanism

Delayed blood-gas feedback causes alternating overshoot and undershoot of ventilatory drive.

Clinical Interpretation

The Cheyne-Stokes breathing pattern raises concern for unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction because delayed blood-gas feedback causes alternating overshoot and undershoot of ventilatory drive. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cheyne-Stokes breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cheyne-Stokes breathing to focus evaluation of unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: central sleep apnea, Biot breathing, agonal breathing. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe several minutes of breathing and oxygenation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cheyne-Stokes breathing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Brief sleep-related periodicity can occur and opioids create different patterns. Therefore, Cheyne-Stokes breathing cannot confirm or exclude unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • central sleep apnea

  • Biot breathing

  • agonal breathing

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by central sleep apnea, Biot breathing, agonal breathing without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Cheyne-Stokes breathing may be absent even when unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status

Current characteristic pattern, not a diagnosis. Interpret Cheyne-Stokes breathing in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Brief sleep-related periodicity can occur and opioids create different patterns.

Safety Precautions

Observe Cheyne-Stokes breathing with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe several minutes of breathing and oxygenation. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Document cycle length and associated hypoxemia; escalate new awake periodic breathing.

Nursing Relevance

For Cheyne-Stokes breathing, document which defined components are present or absent, their timing and trend, and whether the positive pattern is met: Respiration gradually waxes and wanes, then pauses in apnea, repeating cyclically. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Observe several minutes of breathing and oxygenation. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, unstable ventilatory feedback with prolonged circulation time or brain dysfunction, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Cheyne-Stokes breathing on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
