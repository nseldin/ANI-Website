Definition

Starry-sky pattern is a histologic sign. It is a clue to high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance. A positive finding means pale tingible-body macrophages punctuate a dark field of proliferating cells.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Starry-sky pattern means pale tingible-body macrophages punctuate a dark field of proliferating cells.

Why It Matters

The Starry-sky pattern laboratory finding can signal high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance. Rapid apoptosis leaves nuclear debris that macrophages ingest, producing light “stars”. Starry-sky pattern cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance

Assessment Method

Review tissue architecture at low power.

Positive Finding

Pale tingible-body macrophages punctuate a dark field of proliferating cells.

Mechanism

Rapid apoptosis leaves nuclear debris that macrophages ingest, producing light “stars”.

Clinical Interpretation

The Starry-sky pattern laboratory finding raises concern for high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance because rapid apoptosis leaves nuclear debris that macrophages ingest, producing light “stars”. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Starry-sky pattern does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Starry-sky pattern to focus evaluation of high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance and the reviewed alternatives: germinal center hyperplasia, lymphoblastic lymphoma, necrosis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review tissue architecture at low power. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Starry-sky pattern as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Multiple benign and malignant processes share the pattern. Therefore, Starry-sky pattern cannot confirm or exclude high cellular turnover with macrophage clearance by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • germinal center hyperplasia

  • lymphoblastic lymphoma

  • necrosis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with germinal center hyperplasia, lymphoblastic lymphoma, necrosis.

False Negative Situations

Starry-sky pattern may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.

Modern Status
