Used To Treat

Time-sensitive postexposure decision support after percutaneous, mucosal, sexual, or perinatal hepatitis B exposure.

Description

This hepatitis B postexposure prophylaxis decision pathway is an exposure-management algorithm that helps prevent hepatitis B infection by using the exposure route and timing, source HBsAg status, and the exposed person's documented vaccination and anti-HBs response to select hepatitis B vaccine, HBIG, both, or neither. A documented vaccine responder with anti-HBs at least 10 mIU/mL after a complete series needs no HBIG. An unvaccinated, incompletely vaccinated, response-unknown, or documented nonresponder follows the CDC table, which can require HBIG, revaccination, or two HBIG doses one month apart. For an infant born to an HBsAg-positive mother, give HBIG 0.5 mL and hepatitis B vaccine in separate limbs within 12 hours. Core needle biopsy is not HBIG, and a hepatitis pathology page cannot replace this timed pathway.

Mechanism

HBIG supplies immediate anti-HBs antibody that neutralizes virus during the vulnerable early window, while vaccine activates B- and T-cell memory for durable protection. Because passive antibody decays, HBIG alone leaves future susceptibility. Because vaccine immunity takes time, vaccine alone may be insufficient immediately after a high-risk source-positive exposure in an unprotected person. Give the two products at separate sites to avoid local interference from passive antibody and preserve the vaccine response while both time horizons are covered.

Administration Timing

  • Wash the exposed site, report the exposure, and document source HBsAg, route, time, vaccine record, and prior anti-HBs immediately; do not delay indicated prophylaxis while retrieving records in parallel.

  • Give indicated adult HBIG 0.06 mL/kg IM as soon as possible, preferably within 24 hours; benefit is unlikely beyond about 7 days for percutaneous or 14 days for sexual exposure.

  • Give an exposed infant HBIG 0.5 mL plus hepatitis B vaccine in separate limbs within 12 hours, then complete the series and postvaccination serology.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use the current CDC table rather than guessing from the number of remembered shots because documented response, not vaccination memory alone, determines management.

  • Record the exact product, measured weight, calculated dose, lot, time, and injection site because those details confirm dose accuracy and preserve traceability. Schedule vaccine completion and appropriately timed serologic follow-up because HBIG protection is temporary and does not create durable immune memory.

  • Provide baseline and follow-up HBV testing and exposure counseling because prophylaxis does not diagnose whether transmission already occurred.

Key Labs

  • Source HBsAg plus exposed-person HBsAg, total anti-HBc, and quantitative anti-HBs as directed define infection and immune response; postvaccination serology is timed after passive antibody no longer confounds interpretation.

Adverse Effects

  • HBIG can cause injection pain, headache, fever, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, or thrombosis; vaccine usually causes local pain and fever but serious allergy is rare.

Contraindications

  • Severe prior immune-globulin reaction limits HBIG and severe vaccine-component allergy limits vaccine, but specialist emergency planning is needed rather than simply omitting protection after a high-risk exposure.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Source-positive exposure without timely prophylaxis, perinatal delay, evidence of infection, anaphylaxis, or a documented two-series nonresponder requires occupational health, infectious-disease, or public-health escalation.

Interactions

  • HBIG can interfere with selected live-virus vaccines, while hepatitis B vaccine complements rather than replaces indicated HBIG.

  • Immunosuppressive therapy can reduce vaccine response, and anticoagulation or thrombocytopenia changes IM-injection technique and risk.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Newborns

    Note

    The first 12 hours are critical because perinatal acquisition has a high risk of chronic infection; separate-limb HBIG and vaccine plus follow-up are required.

  • Population

    Healthcare personnel

    Note

    Occupational decisions depend on documented anti-HBs response and source HBsAg, so systems should preserve records before an exposure occurs.

  • Population

    Immunocompromised or vaccine nonresponders

    Note

    Durable immunity may not develop and repeat HBIG or specialist-directed testing can be necessary after future exposures.

Evidence Limitations

  • Useful timing windows and follow-up schedules vary with exposure route, age, immune status, and local protocol; prompt expert review remains important.

Nclex Traps

  • HBIG is immediate passive protection; vaccine is active long-term protection.

  • Documented anti-HBs response is more informative than a recalled vaccine history.

  • HBIG and vaccine go in separate limbs for the exposed newborn.

Regulatory Status

This is a CDC-aligned educational pathway using FDA-licensed HBIG and hepatitis B vaccines; product selection and testing follow current occupational, perinatal, and public-health guidance.

Boxed Warning

HBV POSTEXPOSURE WARNING: needle-stick, blood-splash, sexual, and perinatal exposures have time-sensitive decisions. Identify source HBsAg and documented immunity now; an exposed newborn of an HBsAg-positive mother needs HBIG 0.5 mL plus vaccine in separate limbs within 12 hours because prompt passive and active protection reduces perinatal transmission and chronic-infection risk.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning names the urgent hazard, explains why it matters, and states the action that prevents delay or medication error.
