Definition

Xanthoma means a yellowish or yellow-orange deposit of lipid, a fat-like material, in the skin, tissue beneath the skin, or a tendon. It can appear as a flat area, bump, plaque, deeper lump, or tendon thickening.

Plain Language

A xanthoma is a visible or palpable collection of lipid in body tissue.

Why It Matters

A xanthoma can be the first visible clue to an important lipid or metabolic disorder. Tendon xanthomas can indicate familial hypercholesterolemia and premature cardiovascular risk, while sudden eruptive xanthomas with abdominal pain can accompany severe triglyceride elevation and pancreatitis.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate the eyelids, palms and creases, extensor surfaces, buttocks, pressure areas, and Achilles and hand extensor tendons; document yellow color, waxy surface, papular, planar, nodular, eruptive, or tendon pattern, onset, pain, family history of premature cardiovascular disease, abdominal symptoms, and metabolic history, then obtain cause-directed lipid and glucose testing.

Positive Finding

Yellow to yellow-orange waxy papules, plaques, or nodules, or firm tendon thickening, appear in a distribution that helps classify the xanthoma subtype.

Mechanism

Lipoproteins or other sterols deposit in tissue and are taken up by macrophages, producing lipid-laden foam cells that form visible or palpable lesions.

Evidence Limitations

Color and morphology vary, several benign and malignant lesions can mimic xanthoma, and neither presence nor absence determines the lipid level; clinical uncertainty may require biopsy or specialty evaluation. Therefore, Xanthoma cannot confirm or exclude localized lipid deposits in skin, subcutaneous tissue, or tendons that may accompany dyslipidemia, familial lipid disorders, diabetes, cholestatic liver disease, or other systemic disease, although some patients have normal lipid values by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as gouty tophus, juvenile xanthogranuloma, sebaceous lesion, histiocytosis.

False Negative Situations

Xanthoma may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of localized lipid deposits in skin, subcutaneous tissue, or tendons that may accompany dyslipidemia, familial lipid disorders, diabetes, cholestatic liver disease, or other systemic disease, although some patients have normal lipid values.
