Definition

Dance sign is a palpated abdominal sign. It is a clue to ileocolic intussusception in a child. A positive finding means the right lower quadrant feels unusually empty, sometimes with a sausage-shaped mass elsewhere.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dance sign means the right lower quadrant feels unusually empty, sometimes with a sausage-shaped mass elsewhere.

Why It Matters

The observed Dance sign finding can signal ileocolic intussusception in a child. Telescoped ileum and cecum leave their usual location and form a more proximal mass. Dance sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ileocolic intussusception in a child

Assessment Method

Gently palpate the right lower abdomen while assessing for a mass and obstruction.

Positive Finding

The right lower quadrant feels unusually empty, sometimes with a sausage-shaped mass elsewhere.

Mechanism

Telescoped ileum and cecum leave their usual location and form a more proximal mass.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Dance sign finding raises concern for ileocolic intussusception in a child because telescoped ileum and cecum leave their usual location and form a more proximal mass. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dance sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dance sign to focus evaluation of ileocolic intussusception in a child and the reviewed alternatives: appendicitis, constipation, malrotation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently palpate the right lower abdomen while assessing for a mass and obstruction. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dance sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The sign is insensitive, especially with guarding or obesity. Therefore, Dance sign cannot confirm or exclude ileocolic intussusception in a child by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • appendicitis

  • constipation

  • malrotation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as appendicitis, constipation, malrotation.

False Negative Situations

Dance sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of ileocolic intussusception in a child.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; ultrasound is preferred. Dance sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude ileocolic intussusception in a child. Apply the reviewed limitation for Dance sign: The sign is insensitive, especially with guarding or obesity.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not delay urgent ultrasound or air-enema pathway to perfect palpation.
