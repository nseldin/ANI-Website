Definition

Ballance sign is a percussion sign. It is a clue to splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum. A positive finding means left flank dullness remains fixed while right-sided dullness may shift.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ballance sign means left flank dullness remains fixed while right-sided dullness may shift.

Why It Matters

A positive Ballance sign response can signal splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum. Clotted blood around the spleen stays fixed while free intraperitoneal blood moves with position. Ballance sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum

Assessment Method

Percuss both flanks during trauma assessment without delaying imaging.

Positive Finding

Left flank dullness remains fixed while right-sided dullness may shift.

Mechanism

Clotted blood around the spleen stays fixed while free intraperitoneal blood moves with position.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ballance sign response raises concern for splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum because clotted blood around the spleen stays fixed while free intraperitoneal blood moves with position. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ballance sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ballance sign to focus evaluation of splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum and the reviewed alternatives: splenomegaly, fecal loading, retroperitoneal mass. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Percuss both flanks during trauma assessment without delaying imaging. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ballance sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus and small or rapidly evolving bleeds make it unreliable. Therefore, Ballance sign cannot confirm or exclude splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • splenomegaly

  • fecal loading

  • retroperitoneal mass

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from splenomegaly, fecal loading, retroperitoneal mass, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ballance sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and insensitive. Ballance sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude splenic rupture with hemoperitoneum. Apply the reviewed limitation for Ballance sign: Body habitus and small or rapidly evolving bleeds make it unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ballance sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Percuss both flanks during trauma assessment without delaying imaging. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not reposition an unstable trauma patient to seek shifting dullness.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Percuss both flanks during trauma assessment without delaying imaging. Base escalation for Ballance sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
