Class

Orthopoxvirus VP37 inhibitor

Used To Treat

FDA-approved treatment of human smallpox disease in adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 3 kg. Use for mpox is not an FDA-approved indication and, when eligible, occurs through the CDC expanded-access investigational pathway rather than routine prescribing.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): never blur smallpox approval with mpox investigational access. Confirm disease, weight, route, meal, renal status, public-health pathway, and possible resistance when disease progresses.

Mechanism

Tecovirimat inhibits the orthopoxvirus VP37 envelope protein, blocking formation and release of extracellular enveloped virions. This limits cell-to-cell and systemic spread rather than directly destroying every intracellular virus, which explains why early treatment and intact supportive care still matter. Resistance can emerge through VP37 changes, especially with prolonged viral replication or inadequate exposure. Food increases oral absorption, so an unfed dose may create a preventable low-exposure state.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm whether the intended disease is smallpox or mpox because approval status, access, evidence, isolation, and public-health reporting differ.

  • Verify weight, route, meal, formulation, renal function, and ability to absorb because low exposure can reduce effect and encourage resistance.

  • Track new lesions, lesion resolution, ocular or neurologic involvement, pain, hydration, secondary infection, adherence, and immune status because antivirals do not replace supportive care.

Contraindications

  • Do not describe tecovirimat as FDA approved for mpox or promise that it shortens mpox lesions.

  • Do not delay isolation, infection control, public-health notification, supportive care, or management of ocular, neurologic, pulmonary, or bacterial complications while obtaining the drug.

Interactions

  • Tecovirimat affects several metabolic pathways, including CYP3A induction and CYP2C8 or CYP2C19 effects, so reconcile narrow-therapeutic-index and glucose-lowering medicines against the current label.

  • Brincidofovir or vaccinia immune globulin decisions are disease- and complication-specific; combination use is not automatically superior and requires public-health guidance.

Key Labs

  • Diagnosis-specific orthopox testing and public-health specimens; do not delay isolation or emergency notification when smallpox is credible.

  • Renal function is especially important for IV formulation decisions; hepatic tests, CBC, cultures, and organ studies follow severity and comorbidity.

Nclex Traps

  • TPOXX is a brand alias, not a second active card.

  • FDA approval is for smallpox, not mpox.

  • Oral doses require a moderate- or high-fat meal for reliable exposure.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children weighing at least 3 kg

    Note

    The smallpox label includes pediatric use, but preparation and weight bands require exact instructions because whole capsules are not appropriate for every child.

  • Population

    Severe renal impairment

    Note

    The IV excipient and route require label-specific renal review; oral and IV formulations are not operationally interchangeable.

  • Population

    Severe immunocompromise

    Note

    Prolonged replication increases progression and resistance risk, so access, adherence, virologic assessment, and combination decisions require CDC expertise.

Description

Tecovirimat, sold as TPOXX, is an orthopoxvirus antiviral that blocks the viral VP37 protein, preventing formation and release of extracellular enveloped virions needed for efficient spread between cells. It is stocked as a smallpox medical countermeasure and was approved under the Animal Rule because naturally occurring smallpox has been eradicated and efficacy trials in infected humans are not feasible. The approved smallpox indication must stay separate from mpox: CDC reports that two randomized studies did not show faster lesion resolution in the populations studied, and U.S. mpox access is limited to eligible patients through expanded-access investigational use. Oral exposure improves with a full moderate- or high-fat meal, while severe illness, inability to absorb, or pediatric size can change the formulation plan.

Administration Timing

  • For suspected smallpox, isolate immediately and contact public health and CDC before waiting for routine confirmation; start countermeasure decisions through the emergency pathway.

  • Give oral capsules or prepared pediatric oral contents with a full moderate- or high-fat meal and use exact weight-based instructions; use IV product only through its renal, preparation, and infusion requirements.

  • For mpox, verify current CDC expanded-access eligibility and documentation because TPOXX is not FDA approved for mpox and routine benefit was not demonstrated in two randomized studies.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, abdominal discomfort, vomiting, and administration-site reactions can occur.

  • IV formulation excipient accumulation and drug interactions require product-specific review; severe allergy or clinical progression requires reassessment.

Escalation Recurrence

  • New lesions during therapy, failure to improve, severe immunocompromise, ocular disease, encephalitis, airway involvement, or suspected resistance requires CDC and specialist escalation.

  • Inability to take or absorb oral doses, severe vomiting, or worsening organ dysfunction requires route and exposure reassessment.

Evidence Limitations

  • Smallpox effectiveness is based on the Animal Rule plus human safety data, not human smallpox efficacy trials.

  • CDC states that two randomized mpox trials did not show faster lesion resolution in studied populations; investigational access should not be presented as proven routine benefit.

Regulatory Status

FDA approved under the Animal Rule for human smallpox in adults and children weighing at least 3 kg; U.S. mpox use is investigational through CDC expanded access for eligible patients.
