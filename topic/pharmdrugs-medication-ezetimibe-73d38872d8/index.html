Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity is a direct contraindication. A statin-ezetimibe combination inherits the statin's contraindications.

  • Avoid casual fibrate combinations in gallbladder disease and do not coadminister a sequestrant without proper spacing.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm whether it is add-on or replacement therapy, the expected LDL reduction, current statin or fibrate, timing around resins, pregnancy context, and follow-up lipid plan.

  • Teach that one 10-mg dose can be taken with or without food. Lack of flushing or immediate sensation does not mean lack of effect; the lipid panel verifies response.

Interactions

  • Give at least two hours before or four hours after a bile-acid sequestrant. Cyclosporine can increase exposure and requires concentration and adverse-effect monitoring.

  • Fibrates can increase biliary cholesterol and cholelithiasis risk. A statin adds muscle and liver risk but also provides purposeful complementary efficacy.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel; hepatic tests when combined with statin or when symptoms occur; CK, creatinine, potassium, and urinalysis for significant muscle injury; gallbladder evaluation for biliary symptoms.

  • No drug level is routine. Unexpected nonresponse should trigger adherence, dose timing, resin binding, secondary-cause, and diagnosis review.

Nclex Traps

  • Ezetimibe blocks NPC1L1 cholesterol uptake; it does not bind bile acids and is not a statin.

  • Dietary cholesterol is only part of the target. Biliary cholesterol recycling explains why the drug works despite a low-cholesterol diet.

Used To Treat

LDL-C reduction in primary hyperlipidemia as monotherapy or, more commonly, as add-on to a maximally tolerated statin; current labeling also includes selected familial hypercholesterolemia and sitosterolemia contexts.

Mechanism

Ezetimibe and its active glucuronide inhibit NPC1L1-mediated endocytosis of free cholesterol into enterocytes. Less cholesterol is packaged into chylomicrons and delivered to liver as remnants. The fall in hepatocyte cholesterol activates SREBP2 and increases LDL-receptor transcription, clearing more LDL and remnant particles. The liver compensates by increasing endogenous synthesis, explaining additive effect with a statin. Enterohepatic recirculation prolongs intestinal exposure. The drug does not meaningfully inhibit absorption of triglycerides or fat-soluble vitamins by itself. Bile-acid sequestrants interrupt recirculation and physically trap drug, while cyclosporine can raise systemic exposure through transporter effects.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: When combined with a statin, apply that statin's muscle, liver, pregnancy, and interaction warnings. Ezetimibe-associated myopathy or liver injury is uncommon but unexplained severe muscle symptoms, dark urine, jaundice, or marked enzyme changes still require evaluation.

Class Pathway

  • Small-intestinal brush-border NPC1L1

  • Reduced dietary and biliary cholesterol uptake

  • Compensatory hepatic LDL-receptor upregulation

Description

Ezetimibe, branded Zetia, is a once-daily cholesterol-absorption inhibitor that blocks NPC1L1 at the intestinal brush border. It reduces entry of both dietary cholesterol and biliary cholesterol recycled through the gut, lowering hepatic cholesterol and increasing LDL-receptor clearance. It usually adds a modest but clinically useful LDL reduction and has cardiovascular outcomes evidence when combined with statin therapy. It is not a general fat blocker, does not cause the calorie malabsorption of orlistat, and remains useful even with low dietary cholesterol because bile continuously supplies cholesterol to the intestinal lumen. When given with a bile-acid sequestrant, it must be taken at least two hours before or four hours after because the resin can bind it and create apparent treatment failure.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal pain, fatigue, arthralgia, sinus or respiratory symptoms, and mild aminotransferase elevation can occur.

  • Myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, serious hypersensitivity, hepatitis, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, and gallbladder disease are uncommon but important, particularly with statin or fibrate combinations.
