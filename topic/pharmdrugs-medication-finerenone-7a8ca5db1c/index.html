Class

Selective nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Under the current U.S. label, reduction of sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and heart-failure hospitalization in adults with CKD associated with type 2 diabetes; and reduction of cardiovascular death, heart-failure hospitalization, and urgent heart-failure visits in adults with heart failure and LVEF at least 40 percent.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hyperkalemia risk rises as kidney function declines and with potassium-raising therapy. Do not initiate above the product's potassium threshold, and measure potassium and eGFR before treatment and about four weeks after initiation or dose adjustment. Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated because they markedly increase finerenone exposure; avoid grapefruit and review CYP3A inducers.

Mechanism

Finerenone binds the mineralocorticoid receptor and prevents aldosterone- and cortisol-driven transcriptional recruitment of inflammatory and profibrotic genes. Its nonsteroidal structure creates a different receptor conformation and cofactor profile from steroidal MRAs and distributes relatively evenly between heart and kidney. In distal nephron cells, reduced mineralocorticoid signaling lowers ENaC and sodium-potassium-ATPase activity, decreasing potassium and hydrogen secretion. In glomerular, interstitial, cardiac, and vascular tissue, blockade reduces oxidative stress, macrophage signaling, collagen deposition, hypertrophy, and remodeling. These effects explain reduced albuminuria and cardiorenal events without requiring strong natriuresis. CYP3A4 produces inactive metabolites; strong inhibitors raise parent exposure and hyperkalemia risk. Potassium and eGFR monitoring at baseline and around four weeks after initiation or dose adjustment connects mechanism directly to safe long-term treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check potassium and eGFR before initiation and at the label- or protocol-defined early interval after a start or dose change, then periodically and during acute illness.

  • Reconcile ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, potassium chloride, salt substitutes, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, and another MRA.

  • Explain that benefit is long-term cardiorenal protection, not immediate fluid removal. Confirm the labeled indication, baseline potassium and eGFR, four-week follow-up plan, and CYP3A medication review.

Contraindications

  • Hyperkalemia, severe renal impairment or anuria, adrenal insufficiency, and duplicate potassium-sparing therapy are major drug-dependent barriers.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated for eplerenone and finerenone; exact potassium and eGFR start thresholds depend on the agent and indication.

  • The current label contraindicates strong CYP3A inhibitors and adrenal insufficiency. Initiation is not recommended below the label's eGFR threshold, and potassium determines starting and holding decisions.

Interactions

  • RAAS blockers, potassium products, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, heparin, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine converge on hyperkalemia.

  • CYP3A inhibitors increase exposure to eplerenone and finerenone; inducers can reduce efficacy.

  • Avoid grapefruit; strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated, moderate or weak inhibitors require closer potassium monitoring, and strong or moderate inducers can reduce efficacy.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, bicarbonate, sodium, pressure, volume status, and indication-specific albuminuria or heart-failure trajectory.

  • Early potassium surveillance matters because toxicity can appear before disease-modifying benefit is clinically visible.

  • Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio helps follow diabetic CKD risk and response but does not replace potassium and eGFR safety monitoring.

Nclex Traps

  • MRA benefit in heart failure or CKD is not simply extra urine; receptor blockade reduces maladaptive inflammation, fibrosis, and remodeling.

  • Potassium-sparing means reduced potassium excretion, not freedom from potassium monitoring.

  • Finerenone is an outcome-modifying nonsteroidal MRA, not an acute loop substitute. Its current heart-failure indication applies to LVEF at least 40 percent.

Description

Finerenone, branded Kerendia, selectively blocks the mineralocorticoid receptor, reducing cardiorenal inflammation and fibrosis while also reducing renal potassium secretion. It is a nonsteroidal MRA, not a rescue diuretic for pulmonary edema, with outcome evidence in diabetic CKD and, under the expanded current U.S. label, heart failure with LVEF at least 40 percent. Unlike spironolactone, it has negligible androgen or progesterone receptor activity and no active metabolites, so gynecomastia is not a defining effect. It is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4; strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated, grapefruit should be avoided, and inducers can reduce effect. Dose selection and continuation depend on eGFR and potassium. The clinical tension is deliberate: blocking harmful receptor signaling protects heart and kidney over time but reduces potassium excretion immediately.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyperkalemia, kidney-function change, hypotension, dizziness, and hyponatremia can occur because mineralocorticoid-dependent sodium uptake and potassium secretion are suppressed.

  • Severe hyperkalemia can cause weakness, conduction block, ventricular arrhythmia, or cardiac arrest; risk rises with CKD, diabetes, older age, RAAS blockade, and potassium products.

  • Hyponatremia and hypotension can occur; gynecomastia and menstrual effects are not characteristic because sex-hormone receptor binding is minimal.
