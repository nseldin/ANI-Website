Definition

Klein sign is a provoked symptom bedside maneuver. It is a clue to mesenteric adenitis versus appendicitis. A positive finding means tenderness shifts medially or leftward in mesenteric adenitis but tends to remain fixed in appendicitis.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Klein sign means tenderness shifts medially or leftward in mesenteric adenitis but tends to remain fixed in appendicitis.

Why It Matters

The provoked Klein sign symptom can signal mesenteric adenitis versus appendicitis. Mobile inflamed mesenteric nodes may change position, whereas a fixed inflamed appendix usually does not. Klein sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • mesenteric adenitis versus appendicitis

Assessment Method

Reassess reported right-lower-quadrant tenderness after the patient turns onto the left side when safe.

Positive Finding

Tenderness shifts medially or leftward in mesenteric adenitis but tends to remain fixed in appendicitis.

The provoked Klein sign symptom raises concern for mesenteric adenitis versus appendicitis because mobile inflamed mesenteric nodes may change position, whereas a fixed inflamed appendix usually does not. Evidence Limitations

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as ovarian pathology, constipation, abdominal wall pain, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Klein sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use positional tenderness to defer imaging in a concerning presentation.
