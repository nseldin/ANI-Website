Used To Treat

HFrEF to improve survival and reduce hospitalization in appropriate symptomatic patients, add-on hypertension, edema from cirrhosis or nephrotic syndrome, and primary hyperaldosteronism under current labeling. It is especially logical in cirrhotic ascites because secondary hyperaldosteronism drives sodium retention.

Mechanism

Spironolactone and its metabolites competitively block intracellular mineralocorticoid receptors in principal cells, cardiomyocytes, vascular tissue, and other aldosterone-responsive sites. In the distal nephron, less receptor-driven SGK1 means NEDD4-2 removes more ENaC from the apical membrane; sodium-potassium-ATPase expression also falls. Less sodium entry makes the lumen less negative, reducing ROMK-mediated potassium and intercalated-cell hydrogen secretion. This explains hyperkalemia and a normal-anion-gap acidosis tendency. In heart and vessels, mineralocorticoid signaling promotes inflammation, collagen deposition, hypertrophy, endothelial dysfunction, and arrhythmogenic remodeling; blocking it improves outcomes even when diuresis is modest. Antiandrogen effects arise because spironolactone antagonizes androgen receptors and alters steroid synthesis. In cirrhosis, opposing secondary hyperaldosteronism directly targets a major mechanism of ascites formation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hyperkalemia can be fatal, especially with CKD, diabetes, older age, acute kidney injury, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, potassium supplements or salt substitutes, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, or another potassium-sparing drug. Check potassium and kidney function before treatment, within about one week after initiation or titration under labeling, and regularly thereafter, with earlier reassessment when risk changes.

Contraindications

  • Hyperkalemia, severe renal impairment or anuria, adrenal insufficiency, and duplicate potassium-sparing therapy are major drug-dependent barriers.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated for eplerenone and finerenone; exact potassium and eGFR start thresholds depend on the agent and indication.

  • The label contraindicates hyperkalemia, Addison disease, and concurrent eplerenone. Clarify severe renal impairment and inability to monitor potassium.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check potassium and eGFR before initiation and at the label- or protocol-defined early interval after a start or dose change, then periodically and during acute illness.

  • Reconcile ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, potassium chloride, salt substitutes, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, and another MRA.

  • Explain that heart-failure benefit comes from blocking remodeling, not just producing urine. Assess breast or sexual adverse effects without embarrassment; switching to a more selective MRA may be clinically appropriate.

Interactions

  • RAAS blockers, potassium products, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, heparin, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine converge on hyperkalemia.

  • CYP3A inhibitors increase exposure to eplerenone and finerenone; inducers can reduce efficacy.

  • Spironolactone can alter digoxin clearance and interfere with some digoxin assays; monitor the clinical and laboratory context rather than one isolated result.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, bicarbonate, sodium, pressure, volume status, and indication-specific albuminuria or heart-failure trajectory.

  • Early potassium surveillance matters because toxicity can appear before disease-modifying benefit is clinically visible.

Nclex Traps

  • MRA benefit in heart failure or CKD is not simply extra urine; receptor blockade reduces maladaptive inflammation, fibrosis, and remodeling.

  • Potassium-sparing means reduced potassium excretion, not freedom from potassium monitoring.

  • Spironolactone acts upstream at the mineralocorticoid receptor and has a delayed genomic effect; amiloride directly closes ENaC.

Description

Spironolactone, branded Aldactone or Carospir, blocks the intracellular mineralocorticoid receptor, reducing aldosterone-driven sodium retention, potassium secretion, inflammation, fibrosis, and cardiac remodeling. It is a steroidal MRA and weak natriuretic whose HFrEF benefit cannot be judged by urine volume alone. The parent drug is rapidly converted to active metabolites, including canrenone and sulfur-containing compounds, so receptor effects develop and resolve more slowly than the short parent half-life suggests. Spironolactone also binds androgen and progesterone receptors, explaining gynecomastia, breast pain, reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, and menstrual effects. Eplerenone is more receptor selective when those effects matter, but neither drug is potassium safe.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyperkalemia, kidney-function change, hypotension, dizziness, and hyponatremia can occur because mineralocorticoid-dependent sodium uptake and potassium secretion are suppressed.

  • Severe hyperkalemia can cause weakness, conduction block, ventricular arrhythmia, or cardiac arrest; risk rises with CKD, diabetes, older age, RAAS blockade, and potassium products.

  • Gynecomastia, breast pain, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, menstrual irregularity, and postmenopausal bleeding reflect androgen and progesterone receptor effects.
