Definition

Enroth sign is an observed periocular sign. It is a clue to thyroid eye disease. A positive finding means periorbital or eyelid edema is present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Enroth sign means periorbital or eyelid edema is present.

Why It Matters

The observed Enroth sign finding can signal thyroid eye disease. Orbital inflammation, venous congestion, and glycosaminoglycan accumulation draw water into soft tissues. Enroth sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid eye disease

Assessment Method

Inspect eyelids and periorbital tissues.

Positive Finding

Periorbital or eyelid edema is present.

Mechanism

Orbital inflammation, venous congestion, and glycosaminoglycan accumulation draw water into soft tissues.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Enroth sign finding raises concern for thyroid eye disease because orbital inflammation, venous congestion, and glycosaminoglycan accumulation draw water into soft tissues. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Enroth sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Enroth sign to focus evaluation of thyroid eye disease and the reviewed alternatives: cellulitis, angioedema, nephrotic syndrome. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect eyelids and periorbital tissues. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Enroth sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Periorbital edema is highly nonspecific. Therefore, Enroth sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cellulitis

  • angioedema

  • nephrotic syndrome

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cellulitis, angioedema, nephrotic syndrome.

False Negative Situations

Enroth sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thyroid eye disease.

Modern Status

Historical eponym for a current finding. Enroth sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease. Apply the reviewed limitation for Enroth sign: Periorbital edema is highly nonspecific.

Safety Precautions

Observe Enroth sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect eyelids and periorbital tissues. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Pain, fever, vision change, or unilateral swelling requires urgent alternative evaluation.
