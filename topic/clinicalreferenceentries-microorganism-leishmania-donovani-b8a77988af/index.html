Definition

Leishmania donovani is a clinically important protozoan parasite associated with visceral leishmaniasis, fever with splenomegaly, and pancytopenic disseminated infection. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Leishmania donovani is associated with visceral leishmaniasis, fever with splenomegaly, and pancytopenic disseminated infection. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
