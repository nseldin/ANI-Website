Definition

HIV infection develops when human immunodeficiency virus infects and depletes CD4 T cells while persistent replication and inflammation weaken cellular immunity. The characteristic pattern is acute febrile seroconversion may resemble mononucleosis, followed by a long asymptomatic phase and later opportunistic disease without treatment. Confirm or stage it with laboratory antigen-antibody screening with confirmatory differentiation testing and nucleic-acid testing when acute infection is possible; distinguish it from EBV or CMV, influenza, primary immunodeficiency, medication lymphopenia, and other causes of weight loss or infection by the complete test algorithm. The priority is to start combination antiretroviral therapy promptly because opportunistic infection can complicate HIV infection. can follow if the process advances.

Pathophysiology

Inflammation is present, often from infection, autoimmune injury, obstruction, or tissue damage depending on the condition.

Signs Symptoms

  • fever may be present but older adults/newborns may be atypical

  • localized pain/swelling/drainage when tissue infection is present

Diagnostics

  • laboratory antigen-antibody screening with confirmatory differentiation testing and nucleic-acid testing when acute infection is possible

  • viral load, CD4 count, resistance, hepatitis, tuberculosis, STI, kidney, liver, and pregnancy assessment

  • repeat testing after a recent exposure when the window period remains

  • Distinguish HIV infection from EBV or CMV, influenza, primary immunodeficiency, medication lymphopenia, and other causes of weight loss or infection by the complete test algorithm.

Labs

  • WBC may rise or fall

  • lactate may rise with sepsis

  • CRP/ESR may rise with inflammation

  • organ labs can worsen in severe infection

Treatments

  • start combination antiretroviral therapy promptly

  • prevent and treat opportunistic infections according to CD4 and exposure

  • support adherence, vaccinations, partner testing, prevention, and undetectable-equals-untransmittable education

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for severe dyspnea, hypoxemia, new focal neurologic deficit, meningismus, persistent fever with instability, profound dehydration, severe rash or mucosal injury, or rapidly declining consciousness; obtain indicated cultures before antibiotics when doing so will not delay treatment because an HIV-related opportunistic infection or antiretroviral toxicity may be life threatening.

  • Confirm the HIV antiretroviral regimen, last doses, adherence barriers, resistance history, allergies, pregnancy potential, and all medicines or supplements, including calcium or magnesium products, because interruptions and chelation or other interactions can cause virologic failure or toxicity.

  • Trend HIV viral load, CD4 count when indicated, complete blood count, kidney and liver function, glucose, and regimen-specific tests because viral suppression and antiretroviral safety require objective follow-up.

  • Screen HIV-related symptoms and obtain ordered cultures for opportunistic infection, tuberculosis, hepatitis, sexually transmitted infection, malignancy, and immune-reconstitution inflammation because declining immunity and treatment recovery can produce distinct urgent syndromes.

  • Verify whether CD4-based opportunistic-infection antibiotic prophylaxis is prescribed and give it on schedule; use standard precautions, protect confidentiality, and avoid stigmatizing language because missed prophylaxis increases preventable infection risk while routine contact does not transmit HIV.

  • Administer prescribed pathogen-directed antibiotic, antifungal, or antiviral therapy promptly for a suspected or confirmed opportunistic infection, then reassess oxygenation, neurologic status, cultures, and kidney and liver function because treatment delay and interactions with antiretroviral therapy can worsen organ injury or toxicity.

Complications

  • opportunistic infection can complicate HIV infection.

  • malignancy can complicate HIV infection.

  • neurologic disease can complicate HIV infection.

  • kidney and cardiovascular disease

  • immune-reconstitution inflammation can complicate HIV infection.

  • treatment interactions can complicate HIV infection.

Red Flags

  • Severe dyspnea or hypoxemia suggesting opportunistic pneumonia

  • New focal deficit, meningismus, seizure, or altered consciousness

  • Persistent fever with hypotension or rapidly worsening illness

  • Severe rash, mucosal injury, jaundice, or other major drug toxicity

Patient Education

  • Explain that daily antiretroviral therapy can produce a durable undetectable viral load and that undetectable equals untransmittable through sex when suppression is confirmed and maintained.

  • Review adherence supports, interaction checks, vaccines, sexual health, follow-up testing, and a direct contact plan before any treatment interruption.

Nclex Traps

  • A negative antibody-only test can miss very early infection

  • An undetectable viral load prevents sexual transmission but does not eliminate other STI prevention needs
