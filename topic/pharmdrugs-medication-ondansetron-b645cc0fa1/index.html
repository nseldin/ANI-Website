Class

Selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist antiemetic

Used To Treat

Prevention or treatment of nausea/vomiting from chemotherapy, radiation, surgery/anesthesia, gastroenteritis, migraine, or other ordered indications when a serotonin-mediated emesis pathway fits.

Mechanism

Blocks serotonin 5-HT3 receptors on GI vagal afferents and in central emesis areas such as the chemoreceptor trigger zone/area postrema, interrupting the serotonin signal that triggers vomiting.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess nausea/vomiting trigger, hydration, bowel pattern, headache, sedation, ECG/QT risk, potassium/magnesium risk, and concurrent QT-prolonging or serotonergic drugs.

  • Constipation and headache are common; palpitations, syncope, severe allergy, or serotonin-toxicity symptoms need urgent review.

Contraindications

  • Clarify congenital long-QT syndrome, significant QT prolongation, serious hypersensitivity, severe electrolyte abnormality, or interacting QT-prolonging therapy.

Interactions

  • QT-prolonging drugs and serotonergic agents increase safety concerns. Apomorphine coadministration is contraindicated in labeling due to severe hypotension/loss of consciousness risk.

Key Labs

  • Potassium and magnesium when QT risk exists.

  • ECG/QT interval when ordered or high-risk.

Nclex Traps

  • Ondansetron is a 5-HT3 blocker, not a dopamine blocker and not a motion-sickness drug. The high-yield safety hook is QT risk plus constipation/headache.

Description

Ondansetron is a selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist antiemetic: it blocks serotonin 5-HT3 receptors on GI vagal afferents and in central emesis areas such as the chemoreceptor trigger zone/area postrema, interrupting the serotonin signal that triggers vomiting. It is used for prevention or treatment of nausea/vomiting from chemotherapy, radiation, surgery/anesthesia, gastroenteritis, migraine, or other ordered indications when a serotonin-mediated emesis pathway fits.
