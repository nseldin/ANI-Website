Class

Dual endothelin ETA/ETB receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (WHO group 1) to improve exercise ability and reduce clinical worsening; selected pediatric PAH use follows specialist labeling.

Boxed Warning

Hepatotoxicity and embryo-fetal toxicity: current TRACLEER labeling requires ALT and AST before treatment and monthly because clinically silent enzyme elevation can precede liver dysfunction or failure. The Bosentan REMS restricts distribution for this liver risk. Exclude pregnancy before treatment, use effective contraception during therapy and for 1 month after stopping, and discontinue promptly if pregnancy occurs. Hormonal contraception alone may be unreliable because CYP induction lowers hormone exposure.

Mechanism

Endothelin-1 activates ETA and smooth-muscle ETB receptors that couple mainly to Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, and intracellular calcium, causing vasoconstriction, hypertrophy, fibrosis, and proliferation. Bosentan competitively blocks both receptor subtypes. Lower smooth-muscle calcium signaling relaxes pulmonary arteries, while reduced growth signaling slows remodeling. Dual ETB blockade can also reduce endothelial endothelin clearance and nitric-oxide/prostacyclin release, so the clinical result reflects the balance of endothelial and smooth-muscle effects rather than simple universal vasodilation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm WHO Group 1 PAH, Bosentan REMS requirements, baseline ALT/AST, bilirubin when indicated, hemoglobin, pregnancy status and contraception, hepatic history, edema/weight, blood pressure, oxygenation, and complete interaction list before the first dose.

  • Obtain aminotransferases every month even when the patient feels well because early liver injury can be asymptomatic. Escalate jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant pain, anorexia, severe fatigue, bilirubin elevation, or symptomatic anemia.

  • Differentiate medication edema from worsening right-heart failure. Acute pulmonary edema after initiation raises concern for pulmonary veno-occlusive disease and requires urgent reassessment rather than routine diuretic teaching alone.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy; concomitant cyclosporine A; concomitant glyburide; or serious hypersensitivity to bosentan/components under current labeling.

  • Avoid initiating with significantly elevated aminotransferases or moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment because baseline liver disease makes toxicity harder to detect and exposure less predictable.

Interactions

  • Bosentan induces CYP2C9 and CYP3A, lowering exposure to hormonal contraceptives and several other substrates. Use an additional reliable contraceptive method rather than hormone-only contraception.

  • Cyclosporine markedly raises bosentan exposure and glyburide adds liver-enzyme risk, so both combinations are contraindicated. Review warfarin, sildenafil, antiretrovirals, azoles, rifamycins, and other CYP-active drugs.

Key Labs

  • ALT and AST before treatment and monthly; bilirubin when liver injury is suspected; hemoglobin/hematocrit at baseline and periodically; pregnancy testing; and PAH functional, oxygenation, BNP/NT-proBNP, and right-heart trends.

Nclex Traps

  • Bosentan is the dual ERA with mandatory monthly aminotransferases and a hepatotoxicity REMS.

  • A negative pregnancy test is not the whole plan: CYP induction can make hormone-only contraception unreliable.

  • Edema can be drug related, right-heart failure, or PVOD-associated pulmonary edema; the assessment determines the response.

Description

Bosentan is an oral dual ETA/ETB endothelin-receptor antagonist that blocks endothelin-1-driven pulmonary vasoconstriction and vascular growth in WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension. This lowers pulmonary vascular resistance and right-ventricular afterload over time. Its identity is inseparable from two boxed risks: potentially severe liver injury requiring baseline and monthly aminotransferases through the Bosentan REMS, and embryo-fetal toxicity requiring pregnancy exclusion and effective contraception. It also lowers hemoglobin, retains fluid, and induces CYP enzymes strongly enough to make hormonal contraception alone unreliable.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, flushing, nasal symptoms, hypotension, edema or fluid retention, and anemia or hemoglobin decline can occur.

  • Serious concerns include aminotransferase elevation, liver failure, pulmonary edema suggesting pulmonary veno-occlusive disease, and fetal harm.
