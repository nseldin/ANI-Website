Definition

Trismus is an observed restriction in how far the mouth can open. Pain, jaw-muscle spasm, inflammation, joint restriction, scarring, infection, trauma, medication effects, tetanus, or other disease can cause it.

Plain Language

Trismus means the jaw cannot open normally.

Why It Matters

Trismus can interfere with eating, swallowing, oral care, examination, and airway access. When it appears with drooling, muffled voice, neck or floor-of-mouth swelling, stridor, fever, or respiratory distress, a deep infection and sudden airway loss must be considered.

Associated Conditions

  • pain, muscle spasm, inflammation, dental or deep-neck infection, temporomandibular disease, trauma, a recent procedure, radiation fibrosis, medication-induced dystonia, tetanus, neurologic disease, or malignancy

Assessment Method

Assess airway, voice, swallowing, drooling, facial and neck swelling, and respiratory effort before asking the patient to move the jaw; if safe, document painless and maximum interincisal opening, pain, symmetry, TMJ findings, dentition, oral and pharyngeal visibility, onset, trauma or procedure, radiation or cancer history, medications, wound history, and tetanus immunization.

Positive Finding

Mouth opening is bilaterally restricted compared with the patient's baseline, often with pain, guarding, stiffness, or sustained contraction of the jaw-closing muscles.

Mechanism

Sustained contraction or protective guarding of the muscles of mastication, local inflammatory involvement, mechanical joint restriction, scar fibrosis, or abnormal neural drive limits mandibular opening.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Trismus finding raises concern for pain, muscle spasm, inflammation, dental or deep-neck infection, temporomandibular disease, trauma, a recent procedure, radiation fibrosis, medication-induced dystonia, tetanus, neurologic disease, or malignancy because sustained contraction or protective guarding of the muscles of mastication, local inflammatory involvement, mechanical joint restriction, scar fibrosis, or abnormal neural drive limits mandibular opening. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Trismus does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Trismus to focus evaluation of pain, muscle spasm, inflammation, dental or deep-neck infection, temporomandibular disease, trauma, a recent procedure, radiation fibrosis, medication-induced dystonia, tetanus, neurologic disease, or malignancy and the reviewed alternatives: temporomandibular joint disorder, mandibular fracture, medication-induced dystonia, deep neck infection. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess airway, voice, swallowing, drooling, facial and neck swelling, and respiratory effort before asking the patient to move the jaw; if safe, document painless and maximum interincisal opening, pain, symmetry, TMJ findings, dentition, oral and pharyngeal visibility, onset, trauma or procedure, radiation or cancer history, medications, wound history, and tetanus immunization. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Trismus as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal opening varies, pain and effort alter measurement, severe trismus can conceal its own cause, and joint ankylosis, fracture, dystonia, deep infection, radiation fibrosis, and malignancy can produce overlapping restriction. Therefore, Trismus cannot confirm or exclude pain, muscle spasm, inflammation, dental or deep-neck infection, temporomandibular disease, trauma, a recent procedure, radiation fibrosis, medication-induced dystonia, tetanus, neurologic disease, or malignancy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • temporomandibular joint disorder

  • mandibular fracture

  • medication-induced dystonia

  • deep neck infection

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as temporomandibular joint disorder, mandibular fracture, medication-induced dystonia, deep neck infection.

False Negative Situations

Trismus may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of pain, muscle spasm, inflammation, dental or deep-neck infection, temporomandibular disease, trauma, a recent procedure, radiation fibrosis, medication-induced dystonia, tetanus, neurologic disease, or malignancy.

Modern Status

Current nonspecific finding; most cases are not tetanus, and the degree and cause cannot be determined from one mouth-opening measurement. Safety Precautions

Observe Trismus with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Assess airway, voice, swallowing, drooling, facial and neck swelling, and respiratory effort before asking the patient to move the jaw; if safe, document painless and maximum interincisal opening, pain, symmetry, TMJ findings, dentition, oral and pharyngeal visibility, onset, trauma or procedure, radiation or cancer history, medications, wound history, and tetanus immunization. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not force the jaw open, insert objects blindly, or delay airway planning for a complete oral examination; drooling, muffled voice, stridor, tongue or floor-of-mouth swelling, toxic appearance, rapidly increasing neck swelling, or respiratory distress requires emergency airway, anesthesia, and otolaryngology support, while a contaminated wound with generalized rigidity or spasms requires urgent tetanus evaluation.
