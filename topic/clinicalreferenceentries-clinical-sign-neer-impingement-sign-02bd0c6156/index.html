Definition

Neer impingement sign is a bedside shoulder-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to subacromial pain. A positive finding means anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced near end-range.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Neer impingement sign means anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced near end-range.

Why It Matters

A positive Neer impingement sign response can signal subacromial pain. Elevation narrows the subacromial space and compresses sensitive bursal or rotator-cuff tissue. Neer impingement sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subacromial pain

Assessment Method

Stabilize the scapula and passively elevate the internally rotated arm.

Positive Finding

Anterolateral shoulder pain is reproduced near end-range.

Mechanism

Elevation narrows the subacromial space and compresses sensitive bursal or rotator-cuff tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Neer impingement sign response raises concern for subacromial pain because elevation narrows the subacromial space and compresses sensitive bursal or rotator-cuff tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Neer impingement sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Neer impingement sign to focus evaluation of subacromial pain and the reviewed alternatives: adhesive capsulitis, biceps pain, cervical referral. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Stabilize the scapula and passively elevate the internally rotated arm. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Neer impingement sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many shoulder disorders hurt in this position. Therefore, Neer impingement sign cannot confirm or exclude subacromial pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • adhesive capsulitis

  • biceps pain

  • cervical referral

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from adhesive capsulitis, biceps pain, cervical referral, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Neer impingement sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current sensitive but nonspecific adjunct. When performed as described, Neer impingement sign can adjust concern for subacromial pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Neer impingement sign: Many shoulder disorders hurt in this position.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Neer impingement sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Stabilize the scapula and passively elevate the internally rotated arm. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force elevation through stiffness or acute injury.
