Definition

Sensory ataxic gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to loss of proprioceptive input. A positive finding means the patient stamps the feet, watches them, and becomes less stable without vision.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Sensory ataxic gait means the patient stamps the feet, watches them, and becomes less stable without vision.

Why It Matters

The observed Sensory ataxic gait finding can signal loss of proprioceptive input. Absent joint-position feedback forces reliance on vision and heavy foot contact for substitute sensory input. Sensory ataxic gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • loss of proprioceptive input

Assessment Method

Observe walking in good light and, only with close support, how balance changes when visual input is reduced.

Positive Finding

The patient stamps the feet, watches them, and becomes less stable without vision.

Mechanism

Absent joint-position feedback forces reliance on vision and heavy foot contact for substitute sensory input.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Sensory ataxic gait finding raises concern for loss of proprioceptive input because absent joint-position feedback forces reliance on vision and heavy foot contact for substitute sensory input. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Sensory ataxic gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Sensory ataxic gait to focus evaluation of loss of proprioceptive input and the reviewed alternatives: cerebellar gait, vestibular gait, functional gait. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe walking in good light and, only with close support, how balance changes when visual input is reduced. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Sensory ataxic gait as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Cerebellar ataxia also widens gait but does not improve as clearly with vision. Therefore, Sensory ataxic gait cannot confirm or exclude loss of proprioceptive input by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cerebellar gait

  • vestibular gait

  • functional gait

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cerebellar gait, vestibular gait, functional gait.

False Negative Situations

Sensory ataxic gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of loss of proprioceptive input.
