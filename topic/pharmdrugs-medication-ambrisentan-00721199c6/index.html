Class

Selective endothelin ETA receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (WHO group 1), alone or with tadalafil, to improve exercise ability and delay clinical worsening.

Boxed Warning

Embryo-fetal toxicity: ambrisentan is contraindicated during pregnancy because animal data show major birth-defect risk. Exclude pregnancy before treatment, use effective contraception before and during therapy and for 1 month after stopping, and discontinue as soon as pregnancy is detected. Current U.S. labeling no longer requires the former ambrisentan REMS; the boxed risk and pregnancy precautions remain fully active.

Mechanism

ETA receptors on pulmonary vascular smooth muscle couple to Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, and intracellular calcium, producing constriction and growth signaling. Ambrisentan preferentially blocks ETA, lowering smooth-muscle calcium and proliferative signaling while relatively sparing ETB. Endothelial ETB helps clear endothelin and release nitric oxide and prostacyclin, although smooth-muscle ETB can constrict; this mixed biology explains why ETA selectivity is meaningful but not a guarantee of superior efficacy or zero edema.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm WHO Group 1 disease rather than IPF-associated Group 3 PH, pregnancy status and contraception, baseline hemoglobin, edema/weight, blood pressure, hepatic status, fertility concerns, and cyclosporine or tadalafil use.

  • Start at the prescribed 5-mg daily regimen and titrate no more often than the labeled interval; do not split, crush, or chew. Check hemoglobin at initiation, after 1 month, and periodically because the decline often begins in the first weeks.

  • Evaluate new edema for drug fluid retention versus worsening right-heart failure. Acute pulmonary edema or worsening oxygenation should trigger PVOD evaluation and possible discontinuation.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, including IPF with WHO Group 3 pulmonary hypertension.

  • Not recommended in moderate or severe hepatic impairment; significant aminotransferase elevation requires cause assessment before treatment.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine approximately doubles ambrisentan exposure, so limit ambrisentan to 5 mg once daily.

  • Tadalafil combination is evidence based for selected PAH patients, but additive vasodilation and edema still require blood-pressure and volume assessment.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing before treatment; hemoglobin/hematocrit at baseline, 1 month, and periodically; liver tests when clinically indicated; and PAH functional, oxygenation, BNP/NT-proBNP, and right-heart trends.

Nclex Traps

  • Ambrisentan is ETA selective, but it still has boxed embryo-fetal toxicity and causes edema and anemia.

  • It is contraindicated in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, even when pulmonary hypertension is present.

  • Cyclosporine limits ambrisentan to 5 mg daily because it raises exposure.

PAH therapy > Endothelin receptor antagonist > Relatively ETA-selective blockade

Description

Ambrisentan is a once-daily, relatively ETA-selective endothelin-receptor antagonist that blocks endothelin-1-driven pulmonary vasoconstriction and smooth-muscle proliferation in adult WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension, alone or with tadalafil. Relative ETA selectivity preserves more endothelial ETB-mediated endothelin clearance and nitric-oxide/prostacyclin release, but it does not guarantee superior efficacy or eliminate edema. Ambrisentan is contraindicated in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, including IPF-associated Group 3 pulmonary hypertension, because the PAH indication must not be generalized to fibrotic-lung-disease pulmonary hypertension. Embryo-fetal toxicity is boxed, hemoglobin commonly falls early, and acute pulmonary edema can reveal pulmonary veno-occlusive disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Peripheral edema, nasal congestion, sinusitis, flushing, palpitations, headache, and anemia or hemoglobin decline are characteristic.

  • Clinically important fluid retention may require a diuretic, evaluation, or discontinuation. Acute pulmonary edema can indicate occult PVOD rather than ordinary ankle edema.
