Class

IV antipseudomonal cephalosporin plus classical beta-lactamase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Susceptible complicated urinary infection, complicated intra-abdominal infection with metronidazole, and hospital-acquired/ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia in adults and indicated pediatric patients. It is especially valuable for selected difficult-to-treat resistant Pseudomonas when susceptibility supports it.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Baseline renal function and rapid renal change can cause failure or toxicity. In trials, patients with creatinine clearance 30 to 50 mL/min had lower cure, emphasizing timely dose adjustment and reassessment.

Mechanism

Ceftolozane's side chains improve outer-membrane behavior, PBP affinity, and stability against Pseudomonas-derived cephalosporinase. PBP inhibition stops elongation and septation; efficacy follows fT>MIC. Tazobactam forms covalent inactive complexes with selected serine beta-lactamases, with its own time-above-threshold exposure supporting protection. Both components are renally eliminated. The pneumonia HABP/VABP dose is higher than cUTI/cIAI dosing because lung infection and critical illness require greater exposure, illustrating why one product name does not imply one universal dose.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm infection indication because pneumonia uses a different exposure regimen than urinary or intra-abdominal disease, then verify renal function, infusion, cultures, and source control.

  • Trend renal function daily in unstable patients and reassess persistent Pseudomonas because resistance can emerge through combined AmpC, efflux, porin, or PBP changes.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to ceftolozane, tazobactam, cephalosporins, penicillins, or other beta-lactams requires avoidance.

  • Do not use alone for complicated intra-abdominal infection because metronidazole supplies the anaerobic component missing from Zerbaxa.

Interactions

  • No dominant CYP interaction defines the product, but nephrotoxins can reduce renal clearance and raise beta-lactam toxicity.

  • Prolonged infusion occupies line time, so compatibility and interruptions must be managed to preserve fT>MIC.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, creatinine/eGFR, urine output, CBC, liver tests, electrolytes, mental status, and infection-specific imaging or respiratory assessment.

  • A Pseudomonas susceptibility result should be interpreted against the exact pneumonia versus nonpneumonia regimen because target exposure differs.

Nclex Traps

  • Zerbaxa is a major DTR Pseudomonas option, but tazobactam does not inhibit every pseudomonal beta-lactamase.

  • Complicated intra-abdominal treatment requires metronidazole for anaerobes.

Description

Ceftolozane/tazobactam, branded Zerbaxa, pairs a cephalosporin engineered for strong intrinsic Pseudomonas activity with tazobactam, a classical beta-lactamase inhibitor. Ceftolozane binds Pseudomonas PBP1b, PBP1c, and PBP3 and remains relatively stable despite several common non-carbapenemase resistance mechanisms, including AmpC changes, efflux, and porin loss. Tazobactam protects against selected class A enzymes and broadens Enterobacterales activity, but does not inhibit most AmpC carbapenemases, KPC, OXA, or MBL enzymes. The drug's antipseudomonal value therefore comes largely from ceftolozane itself, not from tazobactam being a universal shield.

Ceftolozane Tazobactam

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, headache, fever, liver-test elevation, infusion reaction, hypokalemia, candidiasis, and CDI.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, cytopenias, renal injury, encephalopathy, myoclonus, or seizure at excessive cephalosporin exposure.

Class Pathway

  • Ceftolozane high-affinity Pseudomonas PBP binding

  • Relative stability to AmpC/efflux/porin mechanisms

  • Tazobactam protection from selected class A enzymes
