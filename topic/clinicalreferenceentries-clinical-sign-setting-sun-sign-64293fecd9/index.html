Definition

Setting-sun sign is an observed ocular sign. It is a clue to raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant. A positive finding means the eyes deviate downward with visible sclera above the iris.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Setting-sun sign means the eyes deviate downward with visible sclera above the iris.

Why It Matters

The observed Setting-sun sign finding can signal raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant. Pressure on dorsal midbrain vertical-gaze pathways limits upgaze. Setting-sun sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant

Assessment Method

Observe the infant's eye position and scleral show.

Positive Finding

The eyes deviate downward with visible sclera above the iris.

Mechanism

Pressure on dorsal midbrain vertical-gaze pathways limits upgaze.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Setting-sun sign finding raises concern for raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant because pressure on dorsal midbrain vertical-gaze pathways limits upgaze. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Setting-sun sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Setting-sun sign to focus evaluation of raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant and the reviewed alternatives: normal infant gaze, Parinaud syndrome, lid retraction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the infant's eye position and scleral show. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Setting-sun sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Brief benign sunsetting can occur in healthy infants, so persistence and associated signs matter. Therefore, Setting-sun sign cannot confirm or exclude raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal infant gaze

  • Parinaud syndrome

  • lid retraction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal infant gaze, Parinaud syndrome, lid retraction.

False Negative Situations

Setting-sun sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of raised intracranial pressure or hydrocephalus in an infant.

Modern Status

Current pediatric red-flag sign; transient episodes can occur in young infants. Interpret Setting-sun sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Brief benign sunsetting can occur in healthy infants, so persistence and associated signs matter.

Safety Precautions

Observe Setting-sun sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe the infant's eye position and scleral show. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Escalate persistent new sunsetting with vomiting, lethargy, bulging fontanelle, or head-growth change.
