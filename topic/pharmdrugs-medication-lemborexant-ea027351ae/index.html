Used To Treat

Adult insomnia characterized by difficulty with sleep onset, sleep maintenance or both.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current DAYVIGO labeling has no boxed warning. Contraindicated in narcolepsy. Warn about CNS depression, next-day impairment, worsening depression/suicidality, complex sleep behaviors requiring immediate discontinuation, and sleep paralysis/hallucinations/cataplexy-like symptoms. Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors and strong/moderate inducers.

Mechanism

Lemborexant competitively antagonizes both orexin receptors, with active metabolite M10 retaining similar receptor affinity. Reducing orexin tone allows sleep onset and maintenance, while excessive blockade can permit REM features to intrude into wake transitions. Narcolepsy already reflects deficient orexin signaling, so the drug is contraindicated in that disorder.

Nursing Essentials

  • Start with 5 mg no more than once nightly immediately before bed and only when at least 7 hours remain; verify age, hepatic function, CYP3A drugs, alcohol/other depressants, falls, respiratory disease, depression and substance-use risk.

  • Food can delay sleep onset. Assess next-day alertness and driving rather than relying on the patient's sense of being awake, and stop after a complex sleep behavior.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level. Monitor daytime impairment, falls, mood/suicidality, respiratory symptoms, unusual REM-transition events, sleep behaviors and CYP3A interactions.

Nclex Traps

  • Lemborexant is not safer to combine with alcohol simply because it is non-GABAergic; postural stability and memory impairment are additive.

  • Do not administer when less than 7 hours of sleep time remains.

Description

Lemborexant is a Schedule IV dual orexin receptor antagonist taken immediately before bed to suppress wake-promoting orexin A/B signaling at OX1R and OX2R. It is mechanistically different from benzodiazepines and Z-drugs but still causes dose-related next-day somnolence, impaired driving and balance, falls, complex sleep behaviors, sleep paralysis, hallucinations and cataplexy-like symptoms.
