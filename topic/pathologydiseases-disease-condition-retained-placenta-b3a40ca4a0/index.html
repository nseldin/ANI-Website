Definition

Retained placenta develops when all or part of the placenta fails to separate or deliver, preventing effective uterine contraction and sustaining postpartum bleeding. The characteristic pattern is placenta not delivered within the locally defined interval, heavy bleeding, uterine atony, a partially separated placenta, or missing cotyledons. Confirm or stage it with immediate quantified blood-loss, uterine tone, vital-sign, and placenta-completeness assessment; distinguish it from uterine atony without retained tissue, placenta accreta spectrum, laceration, uterine inversion, and concealed rupture by examination and delivery history. The priority is to activate postpartum-hemorrhage resuscitation when bleeding is significant because massive postpartum hemorrhage can complicate Retained placenta. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Vision change, eye pain, photophobia, foreign-body sensation, redness, drainage, hearing change, vertigo, nasal bleeding, throat swelling, or facial/ear pain anchor eye and ENT assessment.

  • Escalate sudden vision loss, painful red eye, chemical exposure, penetrating eye injury, posterior/uncontrolled nosebleed, airway swelling, or neurologic symptoms.

Diagnostics

  • immediate quantified blood-loss, uterine tone, vital-sign, and placenta-completeness assessment

  • bedside ultrasound when retained fragments remain uncertain and bleeding permits

  • CBC, coagulation, fibrinogen, and type and crossmatch during significant hemorrhage

  • Distinguish Retained placenta from uterine atony without retained tissue, placenta accreta spectrum, laceration, uterine inversion, and concealed rupture by examination and delivery history.

Labs

  • platelets and LFTs matter in HELLP

  • urine protein supports preeclampsia but severe features can diagnose without it

  • glucose matters in gestational diabetes and newborn hypoglycemia

Treatments

  • activate postpartum-hemorrhage resuscitation when bleeding is significant

  • perform controlled cord management and manual removal with adequate analgesia by obstetric protocol

  • proceed to operative removal, tamponade, embolization, or surgery when tissue is adherent or bleeding persists

Nursing Priorities

  • Activate the postpartum-hemorrhage protocol, establish large-bore access, obtain blood studies and type and crossmatch, and administer ordered uterotonics when appropriate because resuscitation must proceed while removal is arranged.

  • Recognize failure of placental delivery or an incomplete placenta and quantify ongoing blood loss because retained tissue prevents firm uterine contraction and can cause rapid postpartum hemorrhage.

  • Assess fundal tone, uterine position, lochia, blood pressure, pulse, pain, bladder distention, and shock index trends because visible blood may underestimate concealed or cumulative loss.

  • Keep the patient nothing by mouth, provide analgesia and emotional support, and prepare manual removal or operative management because definitive treatment requires complete placental removal and possible control of abnormal adherence.

  • Escalate immediately for heavy bleeding, uterine inversion, hypotension, tachycardia, altered consciousness, inability to separate the placenta, or coagulopathy because placenta accreta spectrum, uterine injury, or massive hemorrhage may require surgery.

Complications

  • massive postpartum hemorrhage can complicate Retained placenta.

  • shock can complicate Retained placenta.

  • infection can complicate Retained placenta.

  • uterine inversion can complicate Retained placenta.

  • perforation can complicate Retained placenta.

  • hysterectomy can complicate Retained placenta.

Red Flags

  • Heavy ongoing bleeding with a placenta that has not delivered

  • Hypotension, tachycardia, faintness, or altered consciousness

  • Signs of uterine inversion or severe pelvic pain

  • Failure of manual separation or concern for placenta accreta spectrum

Patient Education

  • Explain that retained placental tissue keeps the uterus from contracting effectively, so urgent removal prevents severe bleeding and later infection.

  • After discharge, report fever, foul-smelling lochia, increasing pelvic pain, heavy bleeding, large clots, dizziness, or fainting immediately.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not apply forceful traction against an unseparated placenta

  • A placenta that appears delivered must still be inspected for missing tissue
