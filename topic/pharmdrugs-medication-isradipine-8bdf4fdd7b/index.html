Class

Oral dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker for hypertension

Used To Treat

Hypertension, alone or with a thiazide-type diuretic. Current capsule labeling commonly uses twice-daily dosing. It is not an acute rescue drug and does not provide therapeutic AV-node rate control.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Symptomatic hypotension can occur, especially during initiation or dose escalation. Use cautiously in congestive heart failure because negative inotropy can appear at high exposure even when afterload falls. Edema, palpitations, fatigue, and flushing are dose related. Safety and effectiveness are not established in children. Assess hepatic function, other vasodilators, and the patient's ability to follow a twice-daily schedule rather than assuming every -dipine behaves like once-daily amlodipine.

Mechanism

Isradipine preferentially inhibits Cav1.2 channels in arterial smooth muscle, reducing calcium entry, calcium-calmodulin activation, myosin light-chain kinase activity, and arteriolar contraction. Lower peripheral resistance reduces pressure and afterload. Baroreceptor unloading can increase sympathetic heart rate and produce palpitations or tachycardia, while greater precapillary than postcapillary dilation raises dependent capillary hydrostatic pressure and causes edema. At high exposure, dihydropyridine selectivity is incomplete and negative inotropy can emerge, which explains caution in congestive heart failure despite afterload reduction. Hepatic metabolism and first-pass extraction make older age, liver disease, and interacting drugs relevant to exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify capsule strength, twice-daily schedule, pressure and orthostasis, pulse, edema, chest symptoms, heart-failure baseline, liver function, and adherence barriers.

  • Teach slow position changes, dose consistency, and prompt reporting of syncope, worsening dyspnea, rapid weight gain, or asymmetric painful swelling. Evaluate edema rather than automatically treating it as volume overload.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to isradipine or formulation ingredients.

  • Marked hypotension, unstable heart failure, or severe hepatic dysfunction requires prescriber review and cautious individualized dosing rather than routine escalation.

Interactions

  • Other antihypertensives, diuretics, nitrates, PDE5 inhibitors, alcohol, and volume depletion add hypotension; beta blockers can blunt reflex tachycardia but also reduce cardiac reserve.

  • Review CYP3A inhibitors/inducers and hepatic disease because isradipine is extensively metabolized, even when the older label gives fewer quantified interaction studies than newer agents.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostatic symptoms, pulse, edema and lung findings, weight context, and hepatic/renal function when clinically relevant.

  • No routine serum isradipine level is used; select cardiac, renal, hepatic, or venous testing according to symptoms and the edema/hypotension differential.

Nclex Traps

  • Isradipine is a vascular DHP and may cause tachycardia; it is not an AV-node-slowing antiarrhythmic.

  • Do not assume all DHPs are once daily. Current capsule products are commonly scheduled twice daily, so verify formulation and directions.

Description

Isradipine is an oral dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that lowers blood pressure by relaxing arterioles and reducing systemic vascular resistance. It is a less commonly used maintenance antihypertensive, generally supplied as 2.5- or 5-mg capsules and often dosed twice daily. Its adverse effects map directly to vasodilation: headache, dizziness, flushing, palpitations, tachycardia, and dependent edema. Edema, palpitations, fatigue, and flushing become more common at higher total daily doses, which is why a patient who develops symptoms after titration needs a dose-response review rather than a generic statement that the drug is tolerated. It does not slow the AV node like diltiazem or verapamil.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, dizziness, edema, palpitations, fatigue, flushing, tachycardia, nausea, abdominal discomfort, weakness, and rash.

  • Severe hypotension/syncope, worsening heart failure, arrhythmia, angioedema or serious hypersensitivity, and vasodilatory shock in overdose.
