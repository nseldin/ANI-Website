Used To Treat

Pain, fever, and inflammation. Aspirin also has antiplatelet use when indicated.

Mechanism

Ibuprofen lysine PDA works by inhibiting cyclooxygenase and decreasing prostaglandin E2 signaling that keeps the premature ductus arteriosus open. Less prostaglandin tone promotes ductal constriction, but renal perfusion, platelet function, GI bleeding/NEC risk, urine output, and bilirubin/renal trends matter.

Boxed Warning

Non-aspirin NSAIDs carry boxed warnings for serious cardiovascular thrombotic events and serious GI bleeding, ulceration, and perforation.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • Bleeding, GI pain, renal function, blood pressure, edema, anticoagulant/antiplatelet use, and asthma sensitivity.

Nclex Traps

  • NSAIDs can worsen kidney function, GI bleeding, heart failure fluid retention, and bleeding risk.

Description

Ibuprofen lysine PDA is an NSAID or salicylate: it works by inhibiting cyclooxygenase and decreasing prostaglandin E2 signaling that keeps the premature ductus arteriosus open. It is used for pain, fever, and inflammation. Aspirin also has antiplatelet use when indicated.
