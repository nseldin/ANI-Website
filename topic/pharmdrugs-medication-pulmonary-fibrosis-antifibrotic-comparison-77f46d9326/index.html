Used To Treat

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and product-specific progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease. Nintedanib is labeled for IPF, progressive fibrosing ILD, and slowing pulmonary decline in systemic-sclerosis ILD. Pirfenidone is labeled for IPF in the United States. Neither dissolves mature fibrosis or rapidly improves oxygenation.

Description

Fibrosis is an abnormal wound-repair program in which fibroblasts proliferate, become myofibroblasts, and deposit collagen-rich extracellular matrix that stiffens the lung. Nintedanib inhibits several receptor tyrosine kinases that transmit fibroblast and vascular growth signals. Pirfenidone has antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects, but its precise human molecular target is not established; experimental effects include reduced TGF-beta-driven collagen programs. Both slow average decline rather than reverse scar, so stable lung function can represent treatment benefit even when the patient does not feel dramatically better.

Mechanism

Nintedanib competes at ATP-binding pockets of PDGFR alpha/beta, FGFR1-3, VEGFR1-3, CSF1R, and selected nonreceptor kinases, reducing downstream migration, proliferation, and differentiation signals involved in fibrotic remodeling. VEGF-pathway inhibition also helps explain bleeding, arterial, wound-healing, and gastrointestinal-perforation concerns. Pirfenidone's exact mechanism remains incompletely defined; in experimental systems it reduces fibroblast proliferation, collagen synthesis, and profibrotic mediators including TGF-beta. Because the targets and toxicity pathways differ, diarrhea and vascular risk dominate nintedanib teaching, while photosensitivity, gastrointestinal intolerance, and CYP1A2 exposure dominate pirfenidone teaching.

Boxed Warning

Neither drug has a current U.S. boxed warning. Both can cause serious drug-induced liver injury and require baseline and scheduled liver tests. Nintedanib adds bleeding, arterial thromboembolism, GI perforation, diarrhea/dehydration, embryo-fetal toxicity, and possible nephrotic-range proteinuria. Pirfenidone adds photosensitivity, severe rash including SJS/TEN/DRESS, marked GI intolerance, appetite or weight loss, and CYP1A2 interactions. Neither should create false reassurance during acute hypoxemia or an ILD exacerbation.

Adverse Effects

  • Nintedanib: diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain, appetite loss, weight loss, elevated liver enzymes, bleeding, arterial events, GI perforation, or proteinuria

  • Pirfenidone: nausea, dyspepsia, diarrhea, fatigue, appetite/weight loss, photosensitivity, rash, severe cutaneous reaction, or liver injury

Contraindications

  • Assess liver impairment, pregnancy potential, bleeding or anticoagulation, recent abdominal surgery, and GI-perforation risk before nintedanib.

  • Avoid or modify pirfenidone with strong CYP1A2 inhibition, severe organ impairment, uncontrolled photosensitivity, or confirmed severe cutaneous reaction.

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain that the goal is a slower FVC decline. Continue symptom, oxygen, rehabilitation, vaccination, reflux, transplant, and supportive planning.

  • Trend stool burden, hydration, weight, appetite, liver tests, skin exposure, bleeding, and adherence; early toxicity management helps patients stay on therapy.

Interactions

  • Nintedanib is a P-gp substrate with minor CYP3A4 contribution; inhibitors raise and inducers lower exposure. Anticoagulants and antiplatelets add bleeding risk.

  • Pirfenidone is mainly CYP1A2-metabolized; fluvoxamine and strong inhibitors raise exposure, while smoking induces CYP1A2 and lowers exposure.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, bilirubin, weight, hydration, renal function/proteinuria when indicated, and pregnancy assessment

  • FVC trend, oxygen need, exertional desaturation, symptoms, acute exacerbations, and imaging in the clinical plan

Nclex Traps

  • Antifibrotic means slows progression; it does not mean existing scar disappears.

  • Smoking can lower pirfenidone exposure, while anticoagulation can magnify nintedanib bleeding risk.
