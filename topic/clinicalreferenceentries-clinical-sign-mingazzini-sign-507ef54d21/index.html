Definition

Mingazzini sign is a bedside motor sign. It is a clue to subtle lower-limb weakness. A positive finding means one leg drifts downward or cannot maintain the posture.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mingazzini sign means one leg drifts downward or cannot maintain the posture.

Why It Matters

The observed Mingazzini sign finding can signal subtle lower-limb weakness. Sustained antigravity hip and knee control fails on the weaker side before gross paralysis is obvious. Mingazzini sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • subtle lower-limb weakness

Assessment Method

With the patient supine, flex hips and knees to 90 degrees and ask them to hold both legs elevated.

Positive Finding

One leg drifts downward or cannot maintain the posture.

Mechanism

Sustained antigravity hip and knee control fails on the weaker side before gross paralysis is obvious.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mingazzini sign finding raises concern for subtle lower-limb weakness because sustained antigravity hip and knee control fails on the weaker side before gross paralysis is obvious. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mingazzini sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mingazzini sign to focus evaluation of subtle lower-limb weakness and the reviewed alternatives: hip pain, deconditioning, functional weakness. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient supine, flex hips and knees to 90 degrees and ask them to hold both legs elevated. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Mingazzini sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, fatigue, and limited cooperation can produce apparent drift. Therefore, Mingazzini sign cannot confirm or exclude subtle lower-limb weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip pain

  • deconditioning

  • functional weakness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hip pain, deconditioning, functional weakness.

False Negative Situations

Mingazzini sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of subtle lower-limb weakness.
