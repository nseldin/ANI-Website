Used To Treat

Adult hypertension and edema associated with heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, renal dysfunction, corticosteroid therapy, estrogen therapy, or other specified causes under current labeling. It may remain useful for resistant hypertension in selected CKD patients with close laboratory monitoring.

Mechanism

After reaching the tubular lumen, chlorthalidone inhibits NCC in the early distal convoluted tubule. Sodium-chloride reabsorption falls, increasing distal sodium delivery and RAAS activation; ENaC uptake then increases lumen negativity and potassium or hydrogen secretion, producing hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Lower distal-cell sodium increases basolateral sodium-calcium exchange and TRPV5-mediated calcium entry, reducing urinary calcium. Blocking a diluting segment while preserving the medullary gradient makes water excretion vulnerable, explaining severe hyponatremia. Chlorthalidone's long half-life is not evidence of a different transporter; it reflects pharmacokinetic retention, including carbonic-anhydrase binding within red blood cells. Sustained vascular smooth-muscle changes and sodium balance support antihypertensive action after initial plasma-volume contraction. The long exposure also explains why adverse chemistry can evolve or persist beyond the last dose.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: No U.S. boxed warning. Chlorthalidone's prolonged action can produce sustained hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypotension, hyperuricemia, and kidney-function change. A normal laboratory result immediately after holding the drug does not guarantee that the effect has ended; continue monitoring according to severity and renal function.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and hypersensitivity are core contraindication patterns. Symptomatic hyponatremia, major potassium or magnesium loss, hypotension, or evolving kidney injury requires reassessment.

  • Do not assume every thiazide is ineffective below one eGFR threshold; agent, dose, route, indication, and actual response matter.

  • The label contraindicates anuria and chlorthalidone hypersensitivity; use in severe hepatic disease requires caution because electrolyte shifts can precipitate encephalopathy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check pressure, orthostasis, weight, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, and uric acid when relevant before and after initiation or escalation.

  • Teach earlier-day dosing, slow position changes, photosensitivity precautions, and urgent reporting of confusion, seizure, severe weakness, palpitations, eye pain, or sudden visual change.

  • When switching from HCTZ, do not use a milligram-for-milligram substitution. Account for greater potency and prolonged effect, then reassess after enough time for the new steady exposure.

Interactions

  • Lithium exposure can rise as sodium depletion increases proximal lithium reabsorption; NSAIDs can blunt effect and worsen kidney function.

  • SSRIs, carbamazepine, desmopressin, and high water intake magnify hyponatremia; low potassium increases digoxin-related arrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid, weight, and urine calcium when that is the indication.

  • Check sooner in older adults, low body mass, high water intake, CKD, or interacting medication use because severe sodium decline can appear quickly.

Nclex Traps

  • Thiazides lower urinary calcium while raising uric acid; those effects come from different transport responses.

  • They can cause profound hyponatremia because the diluting segment is blocked while concentrating ability and vasopressin action remain.

  • Chlorthalidone's long action is useful for pressure control but means adverse effects can continue after discontinuation.

Description

Chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like, not chemically thiazide, diuretic that blocks the same distal NCC transporter. It has a very long clinical effect, often extending 48 to 72 hours, because it partitions into erythrocytes and tissues and has an elimination half-life commonly around 40 to 60 hours. That durability can improve round-the-clock blood-pressure control and preserve effect after a missed dose, but it also means hyponatremia, hypokalemia, or hypotension may persist after the tablet is held. Milligram-for-milligram it is generally more potent and longer acting than hydrochlorothiazide, so the two are not directly interchangeable. Chlorthalidone can work in selected advanced CKD hypertension strategies, but lower GFR and longer exposure increase the need to watch creatinine, potassium, magnesium, sodium, glucose, and uric acid.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, volume depletion, hyperuricemia or gout, photosensitivity, and glucose intolerance follow NCC blockade and compensatory distal transport.

  • Severe neurologic hyponatremia, arrhythmia, pancreatitis, severe skin reactions, and acute angle-closure glaucoma or myopia require urgent action.
