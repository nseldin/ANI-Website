Definition

Bamboo-spine sign is an imaging sign. It is a clue to advanced axial spondyloarthritis. A positive finding means thin vertical bony bridges connect adjacent vertebral bodies in a bamboo-like column.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bamboo-spine sign means thin vertical bony bridges connect adjacent vertebral bodies in a bamboo-like column.

Why It Matters

The Bamboo-spine sign imaging pattern can signal advanced axial spondyloarthritis. Chronic enthesitis ossifies outer annulus fibers and spinal ligaments. Bamboo-spine sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • advanced axial spondyloarthritis

Assessment Method

Review spinal radiographs for continuous marginal syndesmophytes.

Positive Finding

Thin vertical bony bridges connect adjacent vertebral bodies in a bamboo-like column.

Mechanism

Chronic enthesitis ossifies outer annulus fibers and spinal ligaments.

Clinical Interpretation

The Bamboo-spine sign imaging pattern raises concern for advanced axial spondyloarthritis because chronic enthesitis ossifies outer annulus fibers and spinal ligaments. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bamboo-spine sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

It is a late sign and does not measure current inflammation. Therefore, Bamboo-spine sign cannot confirm or exclude advanced axial spondyloarthritis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with DISH, degenerative bridging, fluorosis.

False Negative Situations
