Fumarate immunomodulator and Nrf2-pathway activator

Used To Treat

Relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis in adults, including clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease.

Description

Dimethyl fumarate is an oral fumarate immunomodulator prodrug that is rapidly converted to monomethyl fumarate, shifting immune and oxidative-stress signaling partly through Nrf2 antioxidant-pathway activation. It reduces MS activity but can cause intense flushing/GI symptoms, prolonged lymphopenia, opportunistic infection and PML, serious liver injury, and rare anaphylaxis.

Mechanism

The exact MS mechanism is not fully defined. Dimethyl fumarate's active metabolite activates Nrf2-dependent antioxidant-response genes and alters immune-cell activation, cytokine profiles, and lymphocyte survival/trafficking, reducing inflammatory CNS injury.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain CBC/lymphocytes and liver tests before therapy, repeat CBC after six months and every 6-12 months or more often when low, and reassess prolonged lymphopenia.

  • Monitor new neurologic/cognitive/visual deficits for PML, serious infection, shingles, flushing, abdominal pain/diarrhea, weight/hydration, liver injury, and hypersensitivity.

Key Labs

  • CBC with absolute lymphocyte count, liver tests, and clinical/MRI MS surveillance.

Nclex Traps

  • Flushing is common and usually early; persistent lymphopenia is quieter but more dangerous because it raises PML and opportunistic-infection risk.
