Definition

Flaccidity is severely reduced muscle tone in which a relaxed limb feels unusually limp and offers little or no expected resistance to passive movement. It commonly accompanies major weakness or paralysis and may arise from peripheral or lower-motor-neuron dysfunction or an acute spinal-shock phase.

Plain Language

Flaccidity means a muscle or limb is abnormally limp because normal resting muscle tension is greatly reduced.

Why It Matters

Flaccidity helps localize motor-system dysfunction and creates risks from weakness, unstable joints, falls, pressure injury, and loss of cough or swallowing strength. Sudden or rapidly ascending flaccid weakness, breathing difficulty, a new sensory level, or bowel or bladder dysfunction is an emergency.

Assessment Method

Support the limb fully, ask the patient to relax, and move it slowly through safe passive range while comparing sides; assess resting posture, strength, reflexes, sensation, fasciculations, muscle bulk, cranial and respiratory function, onset, medication or toxin exposure, and recent trauma.

Positive Finding

The relaxed limb feels unusually limp and offers little or no expected resistance to passive movement, usually with associated weakness or paralysis.

Mechanism

Loss or severe interruption of motor input and reflex-arc activity reduces the continuous low-level muscle activation that normally creates resting tone.

Evidence Limitations

Sedatives, deep illness, age, joint laxity, pain avoidance, poor support, and incomplete cooperation affect tone assessment; low tone alone does not localize the lesion or distinguish weakness from impaired consciousness. Therefore, Flaccidity cannot confirm or exclude severe loss of muscle tone from lower-motor-neuron, peripheral nerve, neuromuscular, muscle, or acute spinal-shock dysfunction and some severe central injuries by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as sedation, joint hypermobility, poor limb support, transient spinal shock.

False Negative Situations

Flaccidity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe loss of muscle tone from lower-motor-neuron, peripheral nerve, neuromuscular, muscle, or acute spinal-shock dysfunction and some severe central injuries.

Modern Status

Current localization clue, not a diagnosis; acute central injury may produce transient flaccidity before tone changes, while chronic flaccidity with hyporeflexia often points toward a peripheral or lower-motor-neuron process.
