Definition

Shawl sign is an observed rash-distribution sign. It is a clue to dermatomyositis. A positive finding means photosensitive erythema spreads in a shawl-like distribution.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Shawl sign means photosensitive erythema spreads in a shawl-like distribution.

Why It Matters

The observed Shawl sign finding can signal dermatomyositis. Ultraviolet exposure amplifies autoimmune interface injury in exposed skin. Shawl sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dermatomyositis

Assessment Method

Inspect posterior neck, shoulders, upper back, and arms.

Positive Finding

Photosensitive erythema spreads in a shawl-like distribution.

Mechanism

Ultraviolet exposure amplifies autoimmune interface injury in exposed skin.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Shawl sign finding raises concern for dermatomyositis because ultraviolet exposure amplifies autoimmune interface injury in exposed skin. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Shawl sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Shawl sign to focus evaluation of dermatomyositis and the reviewed alternatives: subacute cutaneous lupus, contact dermatitis, sunburn. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect posterior neck, shoulders, upper back, and arms. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Shawl sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sunburn and medication photosensitivity mimic the distribution. Therefore, Shawl sign cannot confirm or exclude dermatomyositis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • subacute cutaneous lupus

  • contact dermatitis

  • sunburn

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as subacute cutaneous lupus, contact dermatitis, sunburn.

False Negative Situations

Shawl sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of dermatomyositis.

Modern Status

Current supportive sign. Interpret Shawl sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Sunburn and medication photosensitivity mimic the distribution.

Safety Precautions

Observe Shawl sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect posterior neck, shoulders, upper back, and arms. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Connect the rash to muscle, lung, swallowing, and malignancy assessment.
