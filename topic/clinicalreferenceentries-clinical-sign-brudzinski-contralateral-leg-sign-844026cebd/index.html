Definition

Brudzinski contralateral leg sign is a bedside meningeal maneuver. It is a clue to meningeal or radicular irritation. A positive finding means the opposite hip and knee flex involuntarily.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Brudzinski contralateral leg sign means the opposite hip and knee flex involuntarily.

Why It Matters

A positive Brudzinski contralateral leg sign response can signal meningeal or radicular irritation. Stretch transmitted through inflamed meninges or nerve roots triggers a protective crossed flexion response. Brudzinski contralateral leg sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • meningeal or radicular irritation

Assessment Method

Flex one hip and knee while observing the opposite leg.

Positive Finding

The opposite hip and knee flex involuntarily.

Mechanism

Stretch transmitted through inflamed meninges or nerve roots triggers a protective crossed flexion response.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Brudzinski contralateral leg sign response raises concern for meningeal or radicular irritation because stretch transmitted through inflamed meninges or nerve roots triggers a protective crossed flexion response. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Brudzinski contralateral leg sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Evidence Limitations

The response is inconsistently defined and lacks dependable diagnostic accuracy. Therefore, Brudzinski contralateral leg sign cannot confirm or exclude meningeal or radicular irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • voluntary mirroring

  • bilateral hip stiffness

  • pain behavior

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from voluntary mirroring, bilateral hip stiffness, pain behavior, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Brudzinski contralateral leg sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; rarely used and poorly validated. Brudzinski contralateral leg sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude meningeal or radicular irritation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Brudzinski contralateral leg sign: The response is inconsistently defined and lacks dependable diagnostic accuracy.
