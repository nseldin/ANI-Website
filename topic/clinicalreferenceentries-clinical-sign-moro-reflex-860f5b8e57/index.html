Definition

Moro reflex is an infant primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response. A positive finding means symmetric arm abduction and extension is followed by adduction and flexion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Moro reflex means symmetric arm abduction and extension is followed by adduction and flexion.

Why It Matters

A positive Moro reflex response can signal brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response. Sudden vestibular and proprioceptive input activates a primitive protective embrace pattern. Moro reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response

Assessment Method

Support the infant securely and allow a small controlled head-and-trunk change without dropping the infant.

Positive Finding

Symmetric arm abduction and extension is followed by adduction and flexion.

Mechanism

Sudden vestibular and proprioceptive input activates a primitive protective embrace pattern.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Moro reflex response raises concern for brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response because sudden vestibular and proprioceptive input activates a primitive protective embrace pattern. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Moro reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Moro reflex to focus evaluation of brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response and the reviewed alternatives: auditory startle, seizure, normal variation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Support the infant securely and allow a small controlled head-and-trunk change without dropping the infant. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Moro reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Crying, sleep state, prematurity, and technique affect symmetry. Therefore, Moro reflex cannot confirm or exclude brainstem-integrated neonatal motor response by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • auditory startle

  • seizure

  • normal variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from auditory startle, seizure, normal variation, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Moro reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current developmental assessment; should integrate by expected age. Interpret Moro reflex in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Crying, sleep state, prematurity, and technique affect symmetry.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Moro reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Support the infant securely and allow a small controlled head-and-trunk change without dropping the infant. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never let the head fall unsupported; asymmetry or persistence needs developmental evaluation.
