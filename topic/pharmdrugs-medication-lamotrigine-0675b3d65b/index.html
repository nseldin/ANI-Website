Used To Treat

Maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder to delay mood episodes after acute stabilization and treatment of selected focal/generalized seizure disorders. It is especially useful against bipolar depressive relapse but is not an established rapid treatment for an acute manic episode.

Mechanism

Lamotrigine binds preferentially to the inactivated state of voltage-sensitive sodium channels, prolonging channel inactivation and reducing sustained high-frequency action potentials. This decreases presynaptic release of glutamate and aspartate; additional calcium-channel and serotonergic effects may contribute, but the exact bipolar mechanism is not fully established. Hepatic UGT glucuronidation clears the drug. Valproate inhibits glucuronidation and roughly prolongs exposure, increasing rash risk; estrogen-containing contraceptives induce glucuronidation and lower levels, while stopping estrogen can rapidly raise lamotrigine unless the dose is adjusted.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Lamotrigine can cause serious and sometimes fatal rashes, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis. Risk is increased by valproate, exceeding the recommended initial dose, or exceeding titration speed; benign and dangerous rashes cannot be predicted reliably at onset, so stop and urgently evaluate a new rash unless it is clearly unrelated. Current labeling also highlights increased risk with HLA-B*1502 in susceptible ancestry.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to lamotrigine or formulation components

  • Do not restart after a serious lamotrigine rash unless a specialist determines benefit clearly outweighs risk

  • Assess major conduction/structural heart disease, interacting valproate, estrogen changes, pregnancy and prolonged missed-dose gaps before dosing

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach the exact titration calendar and never catch up with extra doses; after enough missed days, restarting at the old full dose can recreate high rash risk and requires prescriber guidance.

  • For rash, mucosal sores, facial edema, fever, lymphadenopathy, hepatitis symptoms or systemic illness, hold and urgently evaluate because DRESS/SJS/TEN can begin before widespread skin loss.

Interactions

  • Valproate inhibits clearance and requires a much lower/slower lamotrigine schedule; enzyme inducers such as carbamazepine can lower exposure.

  • Estrogen-containing contraceptives can cut lamotrigine levels during active hormone weeks and levels can rebound during pill-free intervals or after discontinuation.

  • Carbamazepine and other sodium-channel drugs may add dizziness, diplopia and ataxia.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum target replaces clinical response; check level selectively for adherence, pregnancy, interactions or toxicity

  • Liver/CBC/coagulation and inflammatory evaluation when systemic hypersensitivity is suspected

  • Pregnancy, contraceptive and cardiac-risk review

Nclex Traps

  • Lamotrigine prevents bipolar episodes; it is not a rapid rescue drug for acute mania.

  • Slow titration is what prevents concentration-driven rash risk; it is not optional because symptoms are severe.

  • Stopping an estrogen contraceptive can increase lamotrigine exposure even though no lamotrigine dose changed.

Description

Lamotrigine is a sodium-channel-modulating antiseizure drug used as a bipolar maintenance mood stabilizer. By stabilizing rapidly firing neurons and reducing excitatory glutamate release, it helps prevent future mood episodes without the weight, renal, or major metabolic burden of several alternatives. Its defining safety fact is that immune-mediated rash risk rises when the starting dose is too high, titration is too fast, or valproate slows clearance; the deliberately slow schedule is a mechanism-based safety intervention, not administrative caution.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness, diplopia, ataxia, headache, nausea and somnolence or insomnia

  • Serious rash, DRESS/multiorgan hypersensitivity and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis

  • Aseptic meningitis, blood dyscrasias, hepatic injury and suicidal thoughts/behavior

  • Rare cardiac conduction/proarrhythmia concern in clinically important structural or conduction disease
