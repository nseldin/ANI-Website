Used To Treat

Severe carbamazepine poisoning with refractory seizures, life-threatening dysrhythmia, prolonged coma or respiratory depression requiring ventilation, or persistent/rising toxicity despite multiple-dose activated charcoal and supportive care, following EXTRIP criteria.

Description

Carbamazepine overdose can produce coma, seizures, anticholinergic ileus, hypotension, and cardiac sodium-channel blockade. Absorption may be delayed for many hours because tablets clump, extended-release formulations dissolve slowly, and anticholinergic effects stop gut movement. Although carbamazepine is protein bound, severe poisoning saturates binding and modern hemodialysis can remove enough unbound drug to improve the course. EXTRIP recommends extracorporeal treatment for life-threatening dysrhythmia or refractory seizures and suggests it for prolonged ventilated coma or persistent toxicity despite MDAC. Intermittent hemodialysis is preferred, while MDAC should continue when the airway and bowel permit because the two approaches remove drug from different compartments.

Mechanism

High carbamazepine concentrations block neuronal and cardiac fast sodium channels, slowing conduction and destabilizing the brain and myocardium; antimuscarinic effects further delay gut transit. ECTR clears circulating unbound drug, and as that fraction falls, protein-bound and tissue drug re-equilibrate into plasma for additional removal. Modern high-flux HD can therefore work despite baseline protein binding. MDAC adsorbs drug still in the gut and captures drug secreted across the intestinal wall, interrupting enteroenteric recirculation. Combining these mechanisms can reduce both ongoing input and systemic burden. Rebound can follow dialysis because a pharmacobezoar, delayed absorption, or tissue redistribution refills plasma.

Administration Timing

  • Begin airway, seizure, ECG, hypotension, temperature, sodium and glucose management immediately; consider single or multiple-dose charcoal only when airway and bowel safety permit. Obtain serial concentrations but do not wait for a numerical peak when life-threatening clinical criteria are present.

  • Start ECTR for refractory multiple seizures or life-threatening dysrhythmia, and strongly consider it for prolonged coma requiring ventilation, persistent toxicity, or rising/persistently high levels despite MDAC. Consult EXTRIP, Poison Help, nephrology, and critical care.

  • Use intermittent hemodialysis as the preferred modality when feasible. Continue MDAC during ECTR if there is no ileus, obstruction, or unprotected airway, and monitor after stopping for delayed absorption or rebound.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use continuous ECG with QRS and rhythm trending, frequent neurologic examination, seizure precautions, capnography/ventilation, blood pressure, temperature, urine output, and fall/aspiration precautions because toxicity can change rapidly after a long silent absorption phase.

  • Verify formulation, amount, time, coingestants, bowel sounds, last stool, and all charcoal doses; extended-release tablets and anticholinergic ileus explain why an early level can underestimate the eventual peak.

  • During MDAC, protect the airway and watch for vomiting, distention, absent bowel sounds, or obstruction because depressed reflexes and impaired motility can turn charcoal into an aspiration or bowel-obstruction hazard. During ECTR, monitor access, hemodynamics, temperature, electrolytes, and clearance of sedatives or anticonvulsants because the procedure can create physiologic shifts and remove intended medicines.

  • Trend drug level and clinical status together. A concentration can fall while coma persists from sedatives, hypoxic injury, or coingestants, and a temporarily improved level can rebound from the gut.

Key Labs

  • Serial carbamazepine concentrations until clearly falling after the absorption window; ECG/QRS, electrolytes including sodium, potassium, magnesium and calcium, glucose, blood gas, lactate, renal/hepatic function, CBC, CK and pregnancy testing when relevant.

  • During and after ECTR, document pre/post-filter and systemic levels when the protocol uses them, dialysis dose and duration, MDAC timing, seizure burden, ventilator requirements, and post-treatment rebound.

Adverse Effects

  • ECTR risks include hypotension, bleeding or infection at access, electrolyte shifts, hypothermia, dysrhythmia, and unintended medication clearance. MDAC can cause aspiration, obstruction, and fluid/electrolyte problems.

  • Rapid improvement in measured carbamazepine does not protect against aspiration, rhabdomyolysis, pressure injury, or delayed neurologic complications from the preceding coma.

Contraindications

  • ECTR is not indicated for ordinary therapeutic carbamazepine use, an isolated mildly elevated level, or a clinically improving patient. The decision is severity based.

  • MDAC is contraindicated with an unprotected airway, ileus, obstruction, or perforation risk. If charcoal is unsafe, do not delay ECTR that is otherwise indicated.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Refractory seizure, QRS widening or ventricular dysrhythmia, shock, rising lactate, worsening coma, or progressive respiratory failure requires immediate ECTR and critical-care escalation.

  • A post-ECTR rebound or renewed toxicity requires repeat levels, bowel/pharmacobezoar reassessment, continued MDAC when safe, and consideration of another ECTR session.

Evidence Limitations

  • EXTRIP recommendations use low-certainty clinical evidence supported by dialyzability and accumulated case experience. Clinical severity and trend outweigh a single universal concentration threshold.

  • Hemoperfusion can remove protein-bound drug but is less available and has distinct complications; modern intermittent HD is the preferred modality in the EXTRIP recommendation.

Nclex Traps

  • Carbamazepine can peak late because overdose slows its own absorption; one early level is not reassuring.

  • Continue MDAC during ECTR when safe because charcoal limits gut input while dialysis clears blood.

  • Dialysis criteria are severe clinical effects, not therapeutic use or a mildly high level.

Regulatory Status

EXTRIP consensus rescue, not an FDA indication. Use requires nephrology and medical-toxicology selection of modality, timing, MDAC, and stopping criteria.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): ECTR is not indicated for ordinary therapeutic carbamazepine use, an isolated mildly elevated level, or a clinically improving patient. The decision is severity based. Refractory seizure, QRS widening or ventricular dysrhythmia, shock, rising lactate, worsening coma, or progressive respiratory failure requires immediate ECTR and critical-care escalation.
