Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder in adults and approved pediatric age groups and generalized anxiety disorder in adults; other anxiety uses may be specialist-directed/off-label depending on jurisdiction and patient.

Mechanism

Escitalopram binds the primary SERT site and also stabilizes transporter binding through an allosteric site, reducing presynaptic serotonin reuptake. Extracellular serotonin rises quickly, then inhibitory autoreceptors and downstream mood/anxiety networks adapt over repeated dosing; this is why nausea or jitteriness can occur before sustained relief. It is the S-enantiomer that supplies most SERT inhibition, whereas the R-enantiomer in citalopram contributes little therapeutic blockade. Hepatic CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/2D6 participate in metabolism, so poor metabolism, hepatic impairment and interacting drugs can raise exposure and QT/adverse-effect risk.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Dose and interaction review matter in hepatic impairment, CYP2C19 poor metabolism, electrolyte disturbance or other QT risk because higher exposure can prolong repolarization.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI use or serotonergic linezolid/methylene blue outside emergency guidance

  • Pimozide coadministration and product hypersensitivity

  • Assess congenital long-QT syndrome, bradycardia, low potassium/magnesium and multiple QT drugs before high-risk use

Nursing Essentials

  • Reassess anxiety, sleep, activation and suicidality after starting or increasing; therapeutic benefit lags behind early physiologic effects.

  • Taper rather than abruptly stop when possible because rapid loss of SERT blockade can produce dizziness, sensory symptoms, irritability and flu-like discontinuation effects.

Interactions

  • MAOIs and other serotonergic drugs add serotonin syndrome risk.

  • NSAIDs, antiplatelets and anticoagulants add platelet-related bleeding.

  • Diuretics add hyponatremia; CYP2C19 inhibitors and other QT-prolonging drugs can increase exposure/repolarization risk.

Key Labs

  • Sodium in older, diuretic-treated or symptomatic patients

  • Potassium/magnesium and ECG when QT risk is meaningful

  • Mood, suicide/mania screen, sexual function, sleep and bleeding review

Nclex Traps

  • Escitalopram is not simply a lower-dose citalopram tablet; it is the isolated S-enantiomer.

  • A selective receptor profile does not mean no withdrawal, bleeding or hyponatremia.

  • A normal baseline QT does not cancel later electrolyte or interaction risk.

Description

Escitalopram is the pharmacologically active S-enantiomer of racemic citalopram and a highly selective serotonin-transporter inhibitor. It treats depression and generalized anxiety without meaningful direct norepinephrine or dopamine reuptake blockade. The clean receptor profile reduces anticholinergic burden, but it does not remove serotonergic nausea, sexual dysfunction, activation, SIADH/hyponatremia, platelet-related bleeding, withdrawal, or QT vulnerability in a high-risk patient.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, insomnia or somnolence, fatigue, sweating and dry mouth

  • Sexual dysfunction and delayed orgasm/ejaculation

  • Serotonin syndrome, SIADH/hyponatremia, abnormal bleeding and discontinuation syndrome

  • Activation/mania, seizures, angle-closure glaucoma and dose/exposure-related QT prolongation
