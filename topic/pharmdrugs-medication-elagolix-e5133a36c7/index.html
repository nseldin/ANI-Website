Class

Oral nonpeptide pituitary GnRH receptor antagonist for endometriosis pain

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe pain associated with endometriosis, using dose- and risk-specific maximum treatment durations.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current ORILISSA labeling has no boxed warning. Bone mineral density loss is dose- and duration-dependent and may not fully reverse. Contraindicated in pregnancy, known osteoporosis, severe hepatic impairment and with strong OATP1B1 inhibitors. Suicidal behavior/mood worsening, hepatic enzyme elevation and reduced bleeding that masks pregnancy require active surveillance.

Mechanism

By occupying pituitary GnRH receptors, elagolix interrupts pulsatile GnRH signaling without the initial flare of a GnRH agonist. Lower LH/FSH reduces follicular steroidogenesis; less estradiol decreases stimulation, inflammation and nociception around endometriotic lesions. Ovulation is suppressed incompletely, which explains why pregnancy remains possible.

Nursing Essentials

  • Exclude pregnancy before starting or begin within 7 days of menses; use effective nonhormonal contraception during treatment and 28 days after. Verify dose-specific duration, hepatic class, osteoporosis/fracture risk, mood/suicide history and OATP/CYP interactions.

  • Track pelvic pain and dyspareunia, bleeding pattern, vasomotor symptoms, sleep/mood and bone risk. Test for pregnancy when suspected even with amenorrhea or lighter bleeding.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing, BMD/DXA when fracture or bone-loss risk warrants, liver tests for symptoms/risk, mood/suicide assessment and clinical pain/bleeding response.

Nclex Traps

  • Elagolix is not contraception; ovulation can continue and lighter bleeding may delay pregnancy recognition.

  • The 200-mg twice-daily regimen has a shorter maximum duration than 150 mg once daily because hypoestrogenic bone loss is dose dependent.

Description

Elagolix is an oral competitive GnRH receptor antagonist that rapidly lowers pituitary LH and FSH, reduces ovarian estradiol and progesterone, and relieves endometriosis-associated pelvic pain. It does not eradicate ectopic endometrial implants or prevent pregnancy. Dose-dependent hypoestrogenism produces hot flashes and bone loss, so the 150-mg and 200-mg regimens have different goals and strict duration limits.
