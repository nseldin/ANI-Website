Used To Treat

Persistent COPD exacerbation risk despite optimized inhaled therapy, selected by phenotype. Roflumilast is an oral PDE4 inhibitor for severe COPD associated with chronic bronchitis and prior exacerbations. Mepolizumab is an IL-5 antibody now labeled as add-on maintenance for inadequately controlled eosinophilic COPD. Neither is a rescue bronchodilator.

Description

COPD exacerbations arise from different inflammatory patterns, so add-on therapy should not be selected from the diagnosis alone. Roflumilast raises cAMP inside inflammatory cells and is most relevant to severe chronic-bronchitis COPD with recurrent exacerbations, but weight loss and psychiatric toxicity can outweigh benefit. Mepolizumab reduces eosinophil survival and is relevant when the COPD phenotype is eosinophilic. Long-term macrolide strategies work through a different antimicrobial and immunomodulatory route and carry hearing, QT, and resistance concerns. The question is which recurrent-exacerbation mechanism remains after inhaler optimization.

Mechanism

PDE4 normally hydrolyzes cAMP in neutrophils, eosinophils, macrophages, and other inflammatory cells. Roflumilast and its active N-oxide inhibit PDE4, sustain cAMP, activate protein kinase A, and reduce release or trafficking of inflammatory mediators; because this is not direct airway smooth-muscle beta2 stimulation, patients do not feel immediate bronchodilation. Mepolizumab binds IL-5 and prevents IL-5 receptor alpha signaling, reducing eosinophil production and survival. A blood eosinophil signal therefore supports the biologic rationale, whereas chronic bronchitis, severe airflow limitation, and exacerbation history support the PDE4 rationale. Both are layered onto, not substituted for, foundational inhaled and nonpharmacologic care.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning for roflumilast or mepolizumab. Roflumilast can cause suicidal thoughts or behavior, insomnia, anxiety, depression, and clinically important weight loss; weigh patients and reassess mood. Mepolizumab can cause hypersensitivity, herpes zoster, and altered helminth defense. Neither treats acute respiratory deterioration, so worsening dyspnea still requires the acute COPD action plan and evaluation for infection, heart failure, embolism, pneumothorax, or another cause.

Adverse Effects

  • Roflumilast: diarrhea, nausea, reduced appetite, weight loss, headache, insomnia, anxiety, depression, or suicidality

  • Mepolizumab: headache, back pain, injection reaction, hypersensitivity, herpes zoster, and helminth concern

Contraindications

  • Roflumilast is contraindicated in moderate or severe hepatic impairment and requires caution with depression, suicidality, or low body weight.

  • Mepolizumab is contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity and is not acute treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm phenotype and optimized inhaler technique/adherence before adding systemic therapy.

  • Trend exacerbations, oral-steroid or antibiotic courses, weight, appetite, mood, eosinophils, infections, and adverse-effect burden.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inducers reduce roflumilast exposure; dual CYP3A4/1A2 inhibitors can increase it.

  • Corticosteroids must be tapered rather than abruptly stopped after biologic response.

Key Labs

  • Spirometry, exacerbation history, eosinophils, weight/BMI, liver context, mood and sleep

  • Inhaler technique, smoking status, vaccination, oxygenation, and pulmonary rehabilitation participation

Nclex Traps

  • Roflumilast is not a bronchodilator even though it is a COPD drug.

  • Mepolizumab is phenotype-directed; it is not a universal injection for every patient with COPD.
