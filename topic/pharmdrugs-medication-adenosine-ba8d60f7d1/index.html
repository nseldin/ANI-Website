Mechanism

Adenosine works by briefly blocking AV nodal conduction through adenosine receptors, which can terminate AV-node-dependent SVT.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning commonly emphasized; transient asystole/heart block, bronchospasm, and arrhythmia risk require monitoring.

Contraindications

  • Second- or third-degree AV block or sick sinus syndrome without pacemaker.

  • Known hypersensitivity.

  • Use caution with asthma/COPD because bronchospasm can occur.

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give rapid IV push followed by saline flush.

  • Use continuous ECG monitoring.

  • Warn client about brief flushing, chest pressure, or sense of doom.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Caffeine/theophylline may reduce effect.

  • Dipyridamole may increase effect.

  • Carbamazepine may increase heart block risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • No routine lab; assess rhythm, blood pressure, airway history

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • It must be pushed fast because the half-life is only seconds.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Used To Treat

Rapid termination of stable, regular, narrow-complex paroxysmal SVT when AV-node interruption is appropriate; it may also help diagnose some regular tachycardias in monitored emergency care.

Description

Adenosine is an antidysrhythmic and AV nodal blocking agent: it works by briefly blocking AV nodal conduction through adenosine receptors, which can terminate AV-node-dependent SVT. It is used for rapid termination of stable, regular, narrow-complex paroxysmal SVT when AV-node interruption is appropriate; it may also help diagnose some regular tachycardias in monitored emergency care.
