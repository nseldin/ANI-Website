Definition

SLUDGE sign pattern is a clinical toxidrome constellation. It is a clue to muscarinic cholinergic excess. A positive finding means salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, gastrointestinal cramping, and emesis cluster.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive SLUDGE sign pattern means salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, gastrointestinal cramping, and emesis cluster.

Why It Matters

The SLUDGE sign pattern pattern can signal muscarinic cholinergic excess. Acetylcholinesterase inhibition leaves excess acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors. SLUDGE sign pattern cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • muscarinic cholinergic excess

Assessment Method

Assess secretions, pupils, bowel and bladder activity, heart rate, breathing, and mental status.

Positive Finding

Salivation, lacrimation, urination, diarrhea, gastrointestinal cramping, and emesis cluster.

Mechanism

Acetylcholinesterase inhibition leaves excess acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors.

Clinical Interpretation

The SLUDGE sign pattern pattern raises concern for muscarinic cholinergic excess because acetylcholinesterase inhibition leaves excess acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; SLUDGE sign pattern does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use SLUDGE sign pattern to focus evaluation of muscarinic cholinergic excess and the reviewed alternatives: opioid overdose, nicotine poisoning, brainstem injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess secretions, pupils, bowel and bladder activity, heart rate, breathing, and mental status. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating SLUDGE sign pattern as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Mixed exposures and early treatment modify the pattern. Therefore, SLUDGE sign pattern cannot confirm or exclude muscarinic cholinergic excess by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • nicotine poisoning

  • brainstem injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by opioid overdose, nicotine poisoning, brainstem injury without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

SLUDGE sign pattern may be absent even when muscarinic cholinergic excess is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Decontaminate safely and prioritize airway suction, oxygenation, atropine, and toxicology support.
