Plain Meaning

Reopening collapsed or fluid-prone alveoli so they participate in ventilation again.

Word Parts

  • Root

    alveol-

    small air sac

  • Root

    recruitment

    Meaning

    bringing units into active use

Pathology

Alveolar recruitment is the process of opening collapsed or poorly ventilated alveoli. In atelectasis and ARDS, recruitable alveoli can be opened by PEEP, positioning, adequate inspiratory pressure, secretion clearance, and lung-protective ventilation strategies, improving oxygenation by reducing shunt.

Etiology

Atelectasis, ARDS, anesthesia, mucus plugging, surfactant dysfunction, pulmonary edema, pneumonia, shallow breathing from pain, and prolonged immobility.

Signs Symptoms

  • Hypoxemia, low lung volumes, crackles or diminished bases, increased work of breathing, reduced compliance, or rising oxygen requirement when alveoli collapse

Diagnostics

  • SpO2/ABG response

  • PaO2/FiO2 ratio

  • Chest imaging for atelectasis/opacities

  • Ventilator compliance/pressure trends

  • Response after PEEP, suctioning, positioning, or recruitment strategy

Treatments

  • PEEP or CPAP when ordered

  • Lung-protective ventilation

  • Prone positioning for severe ARDS when ordered

  • Incentive spirometry/early mobility when appropriate

  • Pain control, coughing/deep breathing, secretion clearance, and treating the underlying cause

Nursing Priorities

  • Trend oxygenation after positioning, PEEP, suctioning, or mobilization

  • Watch for hypotension or barotrauma when pressures increase

  • Separate recruitable collapse from nonrecruitable consolidation/fibrosis where more pressure may harm

Complications

  • Refractory hypoxemia

  • Atelectrauma from repeated opening/closing

  • Barotrauma if pressure is excessive

  • Ventilator-induced lung injury

Patient Education

  • Deep breathing, mobility, coughing, and ordered devices help keep air sacs open after surgery or illness.

Nclex Traps

  • Recruitment improves oxygenation only when alveoli are recruitable.

  • More pressure can harm if it overdistends already open lung units.

  • PEEP helps oxygenation partly by keeping recruited alveoli open at end-expiration.
