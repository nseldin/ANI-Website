Definition

Oliguria means urine output is abnormally low for the patient's weight, age, time interval, and clinical setting. In many hospitalized adults and children, output below 0.5 milliliters per kilogram per hour for at least six hours is an important threshold.

Plain Language

Oliguria means the patient is making much less urine than expected, not simply urinating less often.

Why It Matters

Oliguria can be an early sign of poor kidney blood flow, acute kidney injury, shock, or urinary obstruction. Verify the measurement and bladder emptying quickly, then assess perfusion, fluid overload, kidney tests, electrolytes, medications, and the trend before choosing fluids or another intervention.

Associated Conditions

  • reduced kidney perfusion from dehydration, hemorrhage, shock, sepsis, or low cardiac output; intrinsic kidney injury; urinary obstruction or retention; medication or toxin effect; or inaccurate collection

Assessment Method

Verify the timed measurement, patient weight, intake, unmeasured voids, catheter position and tubing patency before interpreting the trend; assess bladder fullness or scan when indicated, vital signs, perfusion, weight, edema, lung sounds, urine appearance, recent losses, bleeding, infection, medications, contrast, creatinine, electrolytes, and baseline kidney function.

Positive Finding

Urine output remains below the expected age-, weight-, and context-adjusted range; in many hospitalized adults and children, less than 0.5 milliliters per kilogram per hour for at least six hours is a clinically important threshold.

Mechanism

Decreased renal blood flow lowers glomerular filtration, intrinsic nephron injury reduces filtration or tubular flow, and downstream obstruction prevents produced urine from leaving; each pathway can produce the same low measured output but requires different treatment.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Oliguria finding raises concern for reduced kidney perfusion from dehydration, hemorrhage, shock, sepsis, or low cardiac output; intrinsic kidney injury; urinary obstruction or retention; medication or toxin effect; or inaccurate collection because decreased renal blood flow lowers glomerular filtration, intrinsic nephron injury reduces filtration or tubular flow, and downstream obstruction prevents produced urine from leaving; each pathway can produce the same low measured output but requires different treatment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Oliguria does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Oliguria to focus evaluation of reduced kidney perfusion from dehydration, hemorrhage, shock, sepsis, or low cardiac output; intrinsic kidney injury; urinary obstruction or retention; medication or toxin effect; or inaccurate collection and the reviewed alternatives: collection error, urinary retention, low fluid intake, catheter obstruction. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Oliguria as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

One short collection interval, an unrecorded void, kinked tubing, leaking device, wrong weight, low intake, diuretic timing, or physiologic overnight variation can mislead; creatinine may lag and nonoliguric kidney injury also occurs. Therefore, Oliguria cannot confirm or exclude reduced kidney perfusion from dehydration, hemorrhage, shock, sepsis, or low cardiac output; intrinsic kidney injury; urinary obstruction or retention; medication or toxin effect; or inaccurate collection by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • collection error

  • urinary retention

  • low fluid intake

  • catheter obstruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as collection error, urinary retention, low fluid intake, catheter obstruction.

False Negative Situations

Oliguria may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of reduced kidney perfusion from dehydration, hemorrhage, shock, sepsis, or low cardiac output; intrinsic kidney injury; urinary obstruction or retention; medication or toxin effect; or inaccurate collection.

Modern Status

Current early warning and AKI-severity finding; it is a measured trend rather than a diagnosis and must be interpreted with perfusion, bladder emptying, creatinine, fluid balance, medications, and the clinical trajectory.
