Definition

Scapular winging sign is an observed movement sign. It is a clue to scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury. A positive finding means the medial or lateral scapular border lifts away from the thorax.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Scapular winging sign means the medial or lateral scapular border lifts away from the thorax.

Why It Matters

A positive Scapular winging sign response can signal scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury. Weak serratus anterior, trapezius, or rhomboid fails to anchor the scapula during force transfer. Scapular winging sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury

Assessment Method

Observe the back at rest and during wall push-up or arm elevation.

Positive Finding

The medial or lateral scapular border lifts away from the thorax.

Mechanism

Weak serratus anterior, trapezius, or rhomboid fails to anchor the scapula during force transfer.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Scapular winging sign response raises concern for scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury because weak serratus anterior, trapezius, or rhomboid fails to anchor the scapula during force transfer. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Scapular winging sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Scapular winging sign to focus evaluation of scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury and the reviewed alternatives: structural scapular deformity, shoulder pain, poor posture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the back at rest and during wall push-up or arm elevation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Scapular winging sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus and dyskinesis can mimic true winging. Therefore, Scapular winging sign cannot confirm or exclude scapular stabilizer weakness or nerve injury by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • structural scapular deformity

  • shoulder pain

  • poor posture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from structural scapular deformity, shoulder pain, poor posture, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Scapular winging sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Identify wing direction and nerve pattern; avoid fatiguing a profoundly weak shoulder.
