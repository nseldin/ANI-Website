Boxed Warning

Boxed/high-alert warning focus: bedaquiline has been associated with increased mortality in trials and can prolong QT interval. Use only as part of an appropriate multidrug TB regimen with ECG, electrolyte, liver, interaction, and adherence monitoring.

Class

Diarylquinoline antimycobacterial; mycobacterial ATP synthase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Drug-resistant pulmonary tuberculosis as part of an effective multidrug regimen when susceptibility, eligibility, and specialist/public-health guidance support use.

Description

Bedaquiline is a diarylquinoline TB drug that inhibits the mycobacterial ATP synthase proton pump, starving Mycobacterium tuberculosis of usable energy. The opening safety frame is MDR/RR-TB combination therapy with QT prolongation, hepatotoxicity, long half-life, CYP3A interactions, and ECG/electrolyte monitoring.

Mechanism

Bedaquiline binds mycobacterial ATP synthase and blocks proton-driven ATP production. Energy depletion kills or suppresses TB organisms, including resistant strains, but the same card must make nurses think ECG, potassium/magnesium/calcium, liver tests, and interacting QT/CYP3A drugs.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the regimen includes other active TB drugs; never treat resistant TB with bedaquiline alone.

  • Obtain/monitor ECG and electrolytes as ordered, especially with other QT-prolonging drugs.

  • Monitor liver injury symptoms, adherence, nausea/arthralgia, and public-health follow-up.

Contraindications

  • Clarify significant QT prolongation, ventricular dysrhythmia history, severe liver disease, pregnancy questions, and unavoidable strong CYP3A inducers or multiple QT-prolonging drugs.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP3A inducers such as rifampin when possible because they can reduce exposure. QT-prolonging drugs and hepatotoxic drugs increase monitoring burden.

Key Labs

  • ECG/QTc.

  • Potassium, magnesium, calcium.

  • Liver function tests.

  • TB cultures and susceptibility context.

Nclex Traps

  • Bedaquiline is about TB energy failure, not cell-wall inhibition.

  • The ECG is not decorative; QT prolongation plus low potassium or another QT drug can become torsades risk.
