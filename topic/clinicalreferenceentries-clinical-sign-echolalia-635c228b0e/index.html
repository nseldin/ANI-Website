Definition

Echolalia is an observed speech sign. It is a clue to catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease. A positive finding means the patient repeats words or phrases spoken by another person.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Echolalia means the patient repeats words or phrases spoken by another person.

Why It Matters

The observed Echolalia finding can signal catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease. Language output becomes stimulus bound because generative or inhibitory networks are impaired. Echolalia cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease

Assessment Method

Listen for involuntary repetition during conversation.

Positive Finding

The patient repeats words or phrases spoken by another person.

Mechanism

Language output becomes stimulus bound because generative or inhibitory networks are impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Echolalia finding raises concern for catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease because language output becomes stimulus bound because generative or inhibitory networks are impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Echolalia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Echolalia to focus evaluation of catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease and the reviewed alternatives: palilalia, perseveration, normal childhood repetition. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Listen for involuntary repetition during conversation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Echolalia as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Repetition for clarification or language learning is not pathologic echolalia. Therefore, Echolalia cannot confirm or exclude catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • palilalia

  • perseveration

  • normal childhood repetition

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as palilalia, perseveration, normal childhood repetition.

False Negative Situations

Echolalia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease.

Modern Status

Current descriptive sign with multiple meanings. Interpret Echolalia in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Repetition for clarification or language learning is not pathologic echolalia.

Safety Precautions

Observe Echolalia with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Listen for involuntary repetition during conversation. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Document immediate versus delayed repetition and assess comprehension.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Echolalia observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The patient repeats words or phrases spoken by another person. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Listen for involuntary repetition during conversation. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, catatonia, autism, aphasia, or neurocognitive disease, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Echolalia, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Document immediate versus delayed repetition and assess comprehension. Base escalation for Echolalia on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
