Definition

Hertoghe sign is an observed hair sign. It is a clue to hypothyroidism or atopic disease. A positive finding means the outer third of the eyebrow is thinned or absent.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hertoghe sign means the outer third of the eyebrow is thinned or absent.

Why It Matters

The observed Hertoghe sign finding can signal hypothyroidism or atopic disease. Reduced follicular activity or chronic rubbing causes lateral hair loss. Hertoghe sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hypothyroidism or atopic disease

Assessment Method

Inspect the lateral eyebrows.

Positive Finding

The outer third of the eyebrow is thinned or absent.

Mechanism

Reduced follicular activity or chronic rubbing causes lateral hair loss.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hertoghe sign finding raises concern for hypothyroidism or atopic disease because reduced follicular activity or chronic rubbing causes lateral hair loss. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hertoghe sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hertoghe sign to focus evaluation of hypothyroidism or atopic disease and the reviewed alternatives: frontal fibrosing alopecia, leprosy, overplucking. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the lateral eyebrows. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hertoghe sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Cosmetic removal and normal variation are common. Therefore, Hertoghe sign cannot confirm or exclude hypothyroidism or atopic disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • frontal fibrosing alopecia

  • leprosy

  • overplucking

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as frontal fibrosing alopecia, leprosy, overplucking.

False Negative Situations

Hertoghe sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of hypothyroidism or atopic disease.
