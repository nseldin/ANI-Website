Class

Oral CGRP receptor antagonist gepant for acute and preventive migraine therapy

Used To Treat

Acute migraine with or without aura in adults and preventive treatment of episodic migraine in adults.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current NURTEC ODT labeling has no boxed warning. Serious hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis, dyspnea and delayed rash, can occur days after a dose. Monitor new or worsening hypertension and Raynaud symptoms. Avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors and strong/moderate inducers; delay another dose 48 hours with moderate CYP3A or potent P-gp inhibitors.

Mechanism

Rimegepant occupies the CLR/RAMP1 CGRP receptor complex, preventing CGRP from activating Gs, adenylyl cyclase, and downstream cAMP signaling in trigeminal pain pathways and meningeal neurovascular interfaces. This reduces an important amplifier of migraine nociception without activating the 5-HT1B vasoconstrictor pathway used by triptans. The receptor action explains the therapeutic target, but the label appropriately states that measured pharmacodynamic activity does not fully explain every clinical response.

Nursing Essentials

  • Clarify acute versus preventive schedule before administration: maximum 75 mg in 24 hours, preventive dosing every other day, and safety of more than 18 doses in 30 days is not established. Open the blister with dry hands and do not push the ODT through foil.

  • Assess severe hepatic disease, end-stage renal disease, interacting amiodarone/cyclosporine/quinidine or CYP3A drugs, blood pressure, digital perfusion, hypersensitivity and headache red flags.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to rimegepant or an excipient.

  • Avoid severe hepatic impairment and end-stage renal disease because exposure or unstudied clearance makes the standard regimen unreliable; these are use limitations rather than allergy contraindications.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors because they markedly raise rimegepant exposure, and avoid strong or moderate CYP3A inducers because they can lower exposure enough to lose efficacy.

  • After a moderate CYP3A inhibitor or potent P-gp inhibitor, do not repeat rimegepant within 48 hours. The longer interval allows elevated exposure to decline before another 75-mg dose.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level. Monitor headache days/rescue use, blood pressure, Raynaud symptoms, hypersensitivity and hepatic/renal suitability; review CYP3A and P-gp interactions at every transition.

Nclex Traps

  • Rimegepant can be acute or preventive, but the schedule changes; it is not taken daily for routine prevention.

  • More than 18 doses in 30 days is an unestablished safety boundary, not a known day-19 poisoning threshold. Frequent need should prompt reassessment of diagnosis, control, and prevention.

  • Absence of triptan-like direct vasoconstriction does not mean vascular monitoring is irrelevant.

Description

Rimegepant is a 75-mg orally disintegrating CGRP receptor antagonist that has two distinct adult migraine roles: one dose as needed for an acute attack or one dose every other day for episodic prevention. The maximum is 75 mg in 24 hours, and safety beyond 18 doses in 30 days is not established; this is an evidence boundary that should trigger reassessment of frequent use, not a proven nineteenth-dose toxicity threshold. By blocking trigeminovascular CGRP signaling without direct 5-HT1B-mediated vasoconstriction, it differs from triptans, but current postmarketing warnings still include hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea is the most common acute-treatment adverse effect; nausea, abdominal pain, and dyspepsia occur with preventive use.

  • Serious hypersensitivity may be immediate or delayed for days. Postmarketing hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon require blood-pressure and digital-perfusion assessment rather than reassurance based only on the absence of triptan-like vasoconstriction.
