Definition

Ellis-Damoiseau line is a percussion sign. It is a clue to free-flowing pleural effusion. A positive finding means the dullness forms a curved line highest laterally.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ellis-Damoiseau line means the dullness forms a curved line highest laterally.

Why It Matters

A positive Ellis-Damoiseau line response can signal free-flowing pleural effusion. Pleural pressure and lung recoil distribute free fluid in a meniscus rather than a horizontal bedside boundary. Ellis-Damoiseau line cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • free-flowing pleural effusion

Assessment Method

Map the upper boundary of percussion dullness with the patient upright.

Positive Finding

The dullness forms a curved line highest laterally.

Mechanism

Pleural pressure and lung recoil distribute free fluid in a meniscus rather than a horizontal bedside boundary.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ellis-Damoiseau line response raises concern for free-flowing pleural effusion because pleural pressure and lung recoil distribute free fluid in a meniscus rather than a horizontal bedside boundary. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ellis-Damoiseau line does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ellis-Damoiseau line to focus evaluation of free-flowing pleural effusion and the reviewed alternatives: diaphragmatic elevation, consolidation, mass. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Map the upper boundary of percussion dullness with the patient upright. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ellis-Damoiseau line as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Loculation, adhesions, posture, and obesity distort the curve. Therefore, Ellis-Damoiseau line cannot confirm or exclude free-flowing pleural effusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • diaphragmatic elevation

  • consolidation

  • mass

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from diaphragmatic elevation, consolidation, mass, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ellis-Damoiseau line may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; imaging is more accurate. Ellis-Damoiseau line remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude free-flowing pleural effusion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Ellis-Damoiseau line: Loculation, adhesions, posture, and obesity distort the curve.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ellis-Damoiseau line, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Map the upper boundary of percussion dullness with the patient upright. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use percussion mapping to choose a thoracentesis site without ultrasound.
