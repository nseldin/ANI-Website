Name

Sodium

Range

A common adult serum reference interval is 135-145 mEq/L (the same numeric values as mmol/L), but the reporting laboratory's interval, specimen, and population govern interpretation.

Why

An abnormal sodium result signals an imbalance of water relative to sodium and therefore changes neurologic and fluid assessment; it does not measure dietary salt intake alone. Hyponatremia raises concern for SIADH, thiazide or other medication effect, adrenal insufficiency, or water excess, while hypernatremia points toward free-water loss, diabetes insipidus, or impaired access to water. Promptly assess mental status, seizure activity, volume status, intake and output, weight, glucose, and medicines; cause-directed evaluation can include repeat laboratory testing plus serum osmolality and urine tests for osmolality and sodium as ordered. Escalate immediately for seizure, acute neurologic change, altered consciousness, or rapid deterioration and follow the ordered cause- and symptom-specific correction plan because overly rapid correction can injure the brain. A single sodium value does not diagnose the cause; interpret the trend, symptom timing, glucose and tonicity, volume status, and local range together.

High Causes

  • Free-water loss

  • Diabetes insipidus

  • Dehydration

  • Hypertonic sodium administration

Low Causes

  • SIADH

  • Fluid overload

  • Diuretics

  • Vomiting/diarrhea with replacement by free water

  • Adrenal insufficiency

Critical Concerns

  • Seizures, severe confusion, coma, and rapid sodium shifts are priority concerns.

Nursing Considerations

  • Use seizure precautions and urgent escalation for severe neurologic symptoms according to policy.

  • Assess neurologic status, glucose, tonicity, volume status, weight, and intake and output; review medicines and administered fluids.

  • Follow the ordered cause- and symptom-specific correction plan and monitor the trend; do not independently choose a correction rate.

Nclex Traps

  • The danger is often the water imbalance, its duration, and its effect on the brain rather than dietary sodium or one number by itself.

  • Both overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia and overly rapid lowering of chronic hypernatremia can cause neurologic injury; follow the individualized order and protocol.
