Definition

Bulge sign is a bedside knee-effusion maneuver. It is a clue to small knee effusion. A positive finding means a small fluid bulge reappears along the medial patella.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bulge sign means a small fluid bulge reappears along the medial patella.

Why It Matters

A positive Bulge sign response can signal small knee effusion. Sweeping shifts free synovial fluid around the patella into the visible medial recess. Bulge sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • small knee effusion

Assessment Method

Milk fluid upward on the medial side, then sweep downward laterally and watch medially.

Positive Finding

A small fluid bulge reappears along the medial patella.

Mechanism

Sweeping shifts free synovial fluid around the patella into the visible medial recess.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Bulge sign response raises concern for small knee effusion because sweeping shifts free synovial fluid around the patella into the visible medial recess. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bulge sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bulge sign to focus evaluation of small knee effusion and the reviewed alternatives: soft-tissue edema, prepatellar bursitis, normal synovial fluid. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Milk fluid upward on the medial side, then sweep downward laterally and watch medially. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bulge sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity and periarticular swelling obscure the wave. Therefore, Bulge sign cannot confirm or exclude small knee effusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • soft-tissue edema

  • prepatellar bursitis

  • normal synovial fluid

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from soft-tissue edema, prepatellar bursitis, normal synovial fluid, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Bulge sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current small-effusion adjunct. When performed as described, Bulge sign can adjust concern for small knee effusion, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Bulge sign: Obesity and periarticular swelling obscure the wave.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Bulge sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Milk fluid upward on the medial side, then sweep downward laterally and watch medially. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not confuse subcutaneous swelling with an intra-articular wave.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Bulge sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: A small fluid bulge reappears along the medial patella. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Milk fluid upward on the medial side, then sweep downward laterally and watch medially. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, small knee effusion, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Bulge sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not confuse subcutaneous swelling with an intra-articular wave. Base escalation for Bulge sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
