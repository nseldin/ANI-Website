Definition

Uvular deviation sign is an observed oropharyngeal sign. It is a clue to unilateral peritonsillar mass effect or vagal weakness. A positive finding means the uvula shifts away from a peritonsillar abscess or toward the weak side in vagal palsy depending mechanism.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Uvular deviation sign means the uvula shifts away from a peritonsillar abscess or toward the weak side in vagal palsy depending mechanism.

Why It Matters

The observed Uvular deviation sign finding can signal unilateral peritonsillar mass effect or vagal weakness. A mass physically pushes the uvula, whereas denervated palate fails to elevate and is pulled by the intact side. Uvular deviation sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • unilateral peritonsillar mass effect or vagal weakness

Assessment Method

Inspect uvula and soft-palate elevation without gagging the patient.

Positive Finding

The uvula shifts away from a peritonsillar abscess or toward the weak side in vagal palsy depending mechanism.

Mechanism

A mass physically pushes the uvula, whereas denervated palate fails to elevate and is pulled by the intact side.

Evidence Limitations

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tonsillar tumor, postoperative scar, deep neck infection.

False Negative Situations
