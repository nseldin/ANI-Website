Used To Treat

Adjunctive relief of functional GI spasm/hypermotility, IBS symptoms and selected bladder/secretory disorders; older broad product labeling should not be interpreted as disease-modifying treatment for ulcer, infection, inflammation or obstruction.

Mechanism

Competitive blockade of postganglionic muscarinic receptors suppresses M3-mediated smooth-muscle contraction and secretion and M2-mediated vagal slowing at the SA/AV nodes. It does not block autonomic ganglia at therapeutic doses. Tertiary-amine lipid solubility permits CNS entry, so central delirium and peripheral antimuscarinic findings can occur together.

Contraindications

  • Glaucoma, obstructive uropathy and obstructive GI disease

  • Paralytic ileus, intestinal atony, severe ulcerative colitis or toxic megacolon

  • Myasthenia gravis and unstable cardiovascular status in acute hemorrhage

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the formulation because immediate, sublingual and extended-release products are not administered identically.

  • Screen cognition, eye symptoms, pulse, temperature, bowel sounds/distention, stool/gas passage and urination before use.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergic drugs add delirium, retention, constipation and heat risk.

  • Antacids may reduce absorption of some oral formulations; separate according to labeling.

  • Opposes cholinesterase inhibitors and prokinetics; opioids intensify constipation and retention.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level

  • Heart rate, temperature, mental status, bowel/bladder function and hydration

  • Renal function can matter because much drug is excreted unchanged; evaluate acute pathology rather than ordering a nonspecific level

Nclex Traps

  • Hyoscyamine is an antimuscarinic, not an anti-inflammatory ulcer-healing drug.

  • Hot, dry, tachycardic, confused and mydriatic is a coherent mechanism-based toxicity pattern.

  • Sublingual absorption can be rapid, so formulation and duplicate anticholinergic exposure matter.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Avoid glaucoma, obstructive uropathy or GI disease, paralytic ileus/intestinal atony, severe ulcerative colitis or toxic megacolon, myasthenia gravis and unstable cardiovascular status. Fever/heat exposure can become dangerous because sweating is inhibited. New eye pain/halos, inability to void, absent stool/gas, tachyarrhythmia or delirium requires urgent reassessment.

Description

Hyoscyamine is the levo-isomer of atropine and a tertiary-amine antimuscarinic that blocks acetylcholine at smooth muscle, cardiac tissue and exocrine glands, reducing GI/bladder spasm and secretions. CNS penetration and nonselective peripheral blockade create a classic anticholinergic toxicity pattern: confusion, mydriasis, tachycardia, dry hot skin, urinary retention and ileus.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, thirst, blurred vision, mydriasis, photophobia and reduced sweating

  • Tachycardia/palpitations, constipation/ileus and urinary retention

  • Drowsiness, dizziness, agitation, hallucinations, confusion or delirium
