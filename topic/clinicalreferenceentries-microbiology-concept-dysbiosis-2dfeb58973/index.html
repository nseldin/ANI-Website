  • Content

    Dysbiosis is a context-dependent disruption in the composition, location, or function of a microbial community that is associated with impaired ecological stability or host health. It is not a single organism, disease, or universally standardized laboratory diagnosis.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    Antibiotics, infection, inflammation, diet, altered anatomy, immune dysfunction, and other pressures can remove competitors, change nutrients, or alter barrier conditions. The resulting community may lose protective functions, permit expansion of opportunists, or produce a different metabolite pattern. The host disease can also cause the microbial change, so direction of causation must be tested rather than assumed.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    A well-established example is antibiotic disruption that reduces colonization resistance and permits Clostridioides difficile to expand. In many chronic conditions, however, a changed community is an association and may be a consequence, contributor, or both.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Use the concept to explain mechanisms and research findings, not to replace a specific diagnosis. Patient care should focus on the established disease, medication exposures, hydration and nutrition, infection risk, and evidence-based prevention or treatment. Broad claims that one supplement or cleanse 'resets' every microbiome are not supported.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Definitions and measurement thresholds vary by body site and method. Reduced diversity is not always harmful, increased diversity is not always beneficial, and stool is an imperfect proxy for mucosal communities. A result requires comparison with an appropriate population and clinical question.
