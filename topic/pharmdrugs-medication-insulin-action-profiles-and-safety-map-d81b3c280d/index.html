Used To Treat

Basal insulin suppresses fasting hepatic glucose release between meals and overnight. Prandial insulin covers carbohydrate entering from a meal. Correction insulin treats an unexpected elevation using an individualized sensitivity factor. Type 1 diabetes usually needs continuous basal coverage plus mealtime insulin; type 2 regimens are individualized.

Description

Insulin action profiles compare rapid, short, intermediate, long, and ultra-long receptor agonists by onset, peak, and duration because their concentration curves lower glucose at different times. Lispro and aspart are rapid prandial analogs; regular insulin is slower and is the standard insulin that can be given IV for emergencies such as DKA. NPH is an intermediate protamine suspension with a visible cloudiness and a clinically relevant peak. Glargine and degludec are long or ultra-long basal analogs designed for relatively flat background coverage. Basal insulin cannot cover a carbohydrate-heavy meal quickly, and rapid insulin cannot safely provide an entire day of basal need.

Mechanism

Every insulin activates the insulin-receptor tyrosine kinase, triggering IRS/PI3K/Akt signaling, GLUT4 movement to skeletal-muscle and adipose-cell membranes, glycogen and lipid synthesis, and suppression of hepatic gluconeogenesis and ketogenesis. The molecules differ mainly in absorption engineering: lispro/aspart substitutions reduce self-association, regular insulin forms hexamers that dissociate more slowly, protamine delays NPH absorption, glargine precipitates after injection at physiologic pH, and degludec forms subcutaneous multihexamers. These depot differences create onset and peak patterns; they do not change the receptor target.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: All insulins can cause fatal hypoglycemia and shift potassium into cells. Product, concentration, device, timing, meal, activity, renal function, and injection site must be verified before every dose; U-100, U-200, U-300, and U-500 products are not casual volume-for-volume substitutions.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypoglycemia with neuroglycopenia, seizure, coma, injury, or death

  • Hypokalemia, weight gain, edema, injection-site reaction, lipohypertrophy, lipoatrophy, and localized amyloidosis

  • Hyperglycemia or DKA from omitted basal insulin, pump failure, wrong product, spoiled insulin, or repeated injection into abnormal tissue

Contraindications

  • Do not administer during an active hypoglycemic episode.

  • Do not mix glargine or degludec with another insulin; follow exact product instructions for any permitted NPH mixture.

  • Do not substitute concentrations, pens, pumps, syringes, or routes without an explicit conversion and monitoring plan.

Nursing Essentials

  • Match the glucose pattern to the component: fasting highs suggest basal review, postmeal highs suggest meal dose/timing review, and overnight lows may mean excessive basal or an evening mismatch.

  • Inspect and rotate sites; repeated injections into lipohypertrophy or amyloid tissue produce erratic absorption, while suddenly moving to normal tissue can precipitate hypoglycemia.

Interactions

  • Beta blockers can mask adrenergic warning signs; the patient may still develop sweating, confusion, or neuroglycopenia.

  • Steroids and sympathomimetics often raise needs; renal decline, poor intake, exercise, alcohol, or resolution of stress can lower needs.

  • Pioglitazone plus insulin increases edema and heart-failure risk.

Key Labs

  • Point-of-care glucose/CGM trend and A1c

  • Potassium in high-dose IV therapy or patients at risk

  • Meal intake, renal/hepatic function, injection sites, device settings, and hypoglycemia awareness

Nclex Traps

  • Never hold all basal insulin automatically in type 1 diabetes because ketogenesis can begin even while the patient is NPO.

  • Clear-before-cloudy applies only to specifically permitted regular/rapid plus NPH mixtures; long-acting analogs are not mixed.

  • The word long-acting does not mean interchangeable milligram or unit kinetics across glargine U-100, glargine U-300, and degludec.
