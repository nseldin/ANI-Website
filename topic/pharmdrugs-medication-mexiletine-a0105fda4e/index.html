Class

Class IB sodium-channel blocker antiarrhythmic

Used To Treat

Documented life-threatening ventricular dysrhythmias, especially sustained ventricular tachycardia, when specialist-directed oral class IB therapy is appropriate.

Boxed Warning

Antiarrhythmic mortality warning: class I rhythm suppression has not improved survival in non-life-threatening ventricular ectopy and related drugs increased mortality after myocardial infarction. Reserve mexiletine for serious ventricular dysrhythmias; initiation and titration require ECG and clinical supervision.

Mechanism

Mexiletine blocks fast inward sodium current during phase 0, with greater binding to open or inactivated channels and rapid unbinding between beats. It lowers action-potential upstroke and excitability in ventricular/Purkinje tissue and often shortens action-potential duration, which explains its class IB identity and similarity to lidocaine.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend ECG/rhythm, QRS/PR, heart rate, blood pressure, ventricular ectopy, dizziness, tremor, ataxia, nausea, and seizure symptoms.

  • Assess liver disease and interacting CYP1A2/CYP2D6 drugs; taking with food may reduce GI intolerance.

Key Labs

  • ECG/rhythm, liver tests when indicated, electrolytes including potassium/magnesium, and drug level only when specifically ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Mexiletine is oral lidocaine-like class IB therapy for serious ventricular rhythms, not routine treatment of harmless PVCs.

Description

Mexiletine is the orally active relative of lidocaine: a class IB antiarrhythmic that preferentially suppresses abnormal firing in depolarized or ischemic ventricular tissue. It is not a benign drug for routine premature beats; proarrhythmia, conduction disease, neurologic toxicity, and liver metabolism make monitored use essential.
