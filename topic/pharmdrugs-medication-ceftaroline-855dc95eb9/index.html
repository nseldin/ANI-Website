Used To Treat

FDA-labeled acute bacterial skin and skin-structure infection, including susceptible MRSA, and community-acquired bacterial pneumonia caused by listed susceptible organisms. It is not a Pseudomonas drug.

Mechanism

Phosphatases rapidly convert the water-soluble fosamil prodrug to ceftaroline. The active drug acylates PBPs and blocks peptidoglycan transpeptidation. MRSA PBP2a normally keeps its active site relatively closed and has low affinity for most beta-lactams; ceftaroline binding promotes an allosteric change that opens access and permits inhibitory engagement. Similar high affinity for altered pneumococcal PBPs supports activity against resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae. Renal elimination requires dose adjustment. Sustained marrow exposure during prolonged courses can suppress neutrophils, which is why CBC monitoring becomes more important as duration extends.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Ceftaroline has no current U.S. boxed warning. Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity, C. difficile colitis, direct-Coombs seroconversion and hemolytic anemia, renal accumulation with encephalopathy, and neutropenia during prolonged therapy are major hazards. MRSA activity applies to susceptible isolates and labeled infection context; it does not turn ceftaroline into empiric coverage for every resistant hospital pathogen.

Contraindications

  • Known serious hypersensitivity to ceftaroline or another cephalosporin; severe related beta-lactam reactions require specialist assessment.

  • Do not use as a Pseudomonas, atypical, Enterococcus, ESBL, or carbapenemase solution merely because it covers MRSA.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify that MRSA or another listed organism and infection site fit the evidence, obtain cultures, calculate renal function, and examine the planned duration.

  • Trend CBC with differential during prolonged or off-label deep-infection courses; investigate new fever, sore throat, or mucosal infection because neutropenia can appear after initial clinical improvement.

Interactions

  • Formal CYP interactions are minimal because ceftaroline is not substantially CYP metabolized, but nephrotoxins can reduce clearance and increase CNS exposure.

  • Other marrow-suppressive drugs can increase the clinical consequence of prolonged ceftaroline-associated neutropenia.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, renal function, CBC with differential, liver tests when indicated, direct antiglobulin/hemolysis evaluation for new anemia, and stool testing for significant diarrhea.

  • Trend infection-site response and bacteremia clearance; a positive Coombs test alone is not hemolysis, but falling hemoglobin with LDH/bilirubin changes needs action.

Nclex Traps

  • Ceftaroline covers susceptible MRSA because it binds PBP2a; it does not cover Pseudomonas.

  • Community-acquired pneumonia labeling includes susceptible MSSA, not MRSA pneumonia as a blanket labeled indication; organism and site still matter.

Description

Ceftaroline is the active IV cephalosporin generated from the ceftaroline-fosamil prodrug that binds ordinary bacterial penicillin-binding proteins plus altered MRSA PBP2a and resistant pneumococcal PBP2x, blocking cell-wall cross-linking in susceptible organisms. That unusual PBP affinity allows a beta-lactam mechanism to treat susceptible MRSA skin infection while retaining useful streptococcal and selected respiratory gram-negative activity. It does not reliably cover Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, ESBL or carbapenemase producers, Enterococcus, atypicals, or broad anaerobes. 'Fifth generation' therefore means a special target exception, not universal broadness.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, rash, pruritus, infusion reaction, fever, positive direct Coombs test, and transient liver-test changes.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, immune hemolytic anemia, neutropenia/agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, encephalopathy, and seizures.
