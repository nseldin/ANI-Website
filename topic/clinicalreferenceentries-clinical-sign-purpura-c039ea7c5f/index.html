Definition

Purpura is an observed skin and mucosal bleeding sign. It is a clue to blood that has leaked into skin or mucous membranes because of platelet, coagulation, vascular, inflammatory, traumatic, or medication-related causes. A positive finding means flat or raised red, purple, or brown lesions remain visible with pressure and are larger than pinpoint petechiae but may include a range of hemorrhagic sizes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Purpura means flat or raised red, purple, or brown lesions remain visible with pressure and are larger than pinpoint petechiae but may include a range of hemorrhagic sizes.

Why It Matters

The observed Purpura finding can signal blood that has leaked into skin or mucous membranes because of platelet, coagulation, vascular, inflammatory, traumatic, or medication-related causes. Extravasated red blood cells remain trapped in tissue, so pressure cannot empty the color as it can in a dilated blood vessel; inflammation can make lesions raised or palpable. Purpura cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Evidence Limitations

Terminology overlaps by lesion size and causes are diverse; skin tone, age of bleeding, pressure injury, and lighting affect visibility. Therefore, Purpura cannot confirm or exclude blood that has leaked into skin or mucous membranes because of platelet, coagulation, vascular, inflammatory, traumatic, or medication-related causes by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as vasculitis, thrombocytopenia, traumatic bruising, vascular lesions.

False Negative Situations

Purpura may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of blood that has leaked into skin or mucous membranes because of platelet, coagulation, vascular, inflammatory, traumatic, or medication-related causes.
