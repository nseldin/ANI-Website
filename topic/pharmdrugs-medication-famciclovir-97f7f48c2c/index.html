Class

Oral diacetyl prodrug of penciclovir for HSV and VZV DNA-polymerase inhibition

Used To Treat

Herpes zoster, recurrent herpes labialis or genital herpes, and chronic genital-herpes suppression in immunocompetent adults according to the current label. It does not cure latent infection.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Acute kidney injury and excessive exposure can occur when renal dosing is not adjusted. Confusion, dizziness, somnolence, or hallucination can be more consequential in older or renally impaired patients. Rare severe skin, hypersensitivity, hepatic, or hematologic reactions require prompt assessment. The drug is not established as a cure for latent HSV/VZV or for every immunocompromised indication.

Mechanism

After absorption, esterases remove acetyl groups and oxidize famciclovir to penciclovir. HSV or VZV thymidine kinase phosphorylates penciclovir to the monophosphate, and host kinases form penciclovir triphosphate. The triphosphate competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate at viral DNA polymerase and becomes incorporated into DNA. Because extension can sometimes continue after incorporation, the dominant effect is prolonged polymerase inhibition and marked slowing of chain elongation rather than obligatory immediate termination. The active metabolite persists for hours in infected cells, supporting episodic regimens. Thymidine-kinase-negative or altered viruses can resist both penciclovir/famciclovir and acyclovir/valacyclovir; polymerase mutations can add cross-resistance.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach prodrome recognition so episodic therapy can begin at tingling, burning, or earliest lesion onset, when replication is most active.

  • Explain that symptoms and shedding may outlast the dose and that transmission precautions remain necessary even when lesions are absent.

Contraindications

  • Prior hypersensitivity to famciclovir or penciclovir cream.

  • Adjust dose for renal function and confirm the labeled indication; episodic one-day or single-dose regimens are not interchangeable across infection sites.

Interactions

  • Probenecid and other drugs affecting renal tubular secretion may raise penciclovir exposure.

  • Review nephrotoxins and medications contributing to dizziness or confusion in older patients.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR before choosing dose in renal disease or older age; liver tests and CBC if prolonged therapy or toxicity warrants them

  • Lesion onset and evolution, recurrence frequency, neurologic or ocular involvement, immune status, adherence, and evidence of resistance

Nclex Traps

  • Famciclovir becomes penciclovir, not acyclovir.

  • Its triphosphate inhibits DNA polymerase but is not the same obligate chain terminator as acyclovir triphosphate.

Description

Famciclovir is an oral prodrug rapidly converted to penciclovir, a guanosine analogue activated preferentially in HSV- or VZV-infected cells. Penciclovir triphosphate has a long intracellular half-life and competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase, slowing viral genome elongation. Unlike acyclovir triphosphate, incorporated penciclovir is not an obligate immediate chain terminator because it retains a 3-prime hydroxyl group, but DNA synthesis still becomes inefficient. This distinction explains how related drugs can share thymidine-kinase resistance yet differ in intracellular persistence. Famciclovir works best when started early in a recurrence because it can stop new replication but cannot erase latency or reverse established neural injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain, dizziness, fatigue, and pruritus

  • Renal accumulation, confusion or hallucination, severe skin/hypersensitivity reaction, thrombocytopenia, and liver-test abnormalities
