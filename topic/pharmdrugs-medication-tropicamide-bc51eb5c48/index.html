Class

Short-duration ophthalmic antimuscarinic mydriatic with cycloplegic activity

Used To Treat

Pupil dilation for fundus examination and short cycloplegia for selected refraction or procedures.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Screen for narrow anterior chamber/angle-closure risk. Psychotic reactions, behavioral disturbance and vasomotor or cardiorespiratory collapse have been reported with anticholinergic eye drugs in children. Use punctal occlusion and the minimum effective dose.

Mechanism

Tropicamide competitively blocks muscarinic receptors in the iris sphincter and ciliary body, creating unopposed radial dilation and loss of near-focus accommodation. Clinical effect begins within minutes and usually resolves over hours, though complete recovery can take longer. Nasolacrimal absorption explains dry mouth, tachycardia and rare CNS effects.

Nursing Essentials

  • Provide sunglasses/photophobia guidance and advise against driving until vision is clear. Distinguish expected blur from severe eye pain, halos, headache, nausea or vomiting that suggest acute angle closure.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity and known/suspected narrow-angle or angle-closure glaucoma unless directed within an ophthalmic plan.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics add systemic toxicity. Pilocarpine opposes the effect. Phenylephrine adds mydriasis and can add cardiovascular risk.

Key Labs

  • Pupil and accommodative recovery

  • Intraocular pressure/angle assessment when indicated

  • Pulse, blood pressure, temperature and mental status if systemic toxicity occurs

Nclex Traps

  • Tropicamide is shorter acting, not risk free.

  • Mydriasis plus severe eye pain/nausea is not an expected exam aftereffect.

  • The 1% product has stronger cycloplegic effect than 0.5%.

Description

Tropicamide is a relatively short-acting ophthalmic antimuscarinic that dilates the pupil and temporarily weakens accommodation. It is convenient for routine fundus examination because recovery is generally faster than with cyclopentolate or atropine, but the 1% product produces stronger cycloplegia. It can still precipitate angle closure and cause systemic anticholinergic delirium or cardiorespiratory effects, especially in children.

Adverse Effects

  • Stinging, photophobia, blurred near vision, elevated IOP/acute angle closure, dry mouth, flushing, tachycardia, fever, confusion, agitation and rare pediatric cardiorespiratory collapse.
