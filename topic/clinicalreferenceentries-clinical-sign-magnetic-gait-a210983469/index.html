Definition

Magnetic gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction. A positive finding means the feet appear stuck to the floor with short shuffling steps and start hesitation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Magnetic gait means the feet appear stuck to the floor with short shuffling steps and start hesitation.

Why It Matters

The observed Magnetic gait finding can signal frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction. Frontal-subcortical network dysfunction impairs gait initiation and scaling despite preserved basic strength. Magnetic gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe initiation, stride length, turning, and foot clearance with fall protection.

Positive Finding

The feet appear stuck to the floor with short shuffling steps and start hesitation.

Mechanism

Frontal-subcortical network dysfunction impairs gait initiation and scaling despite preserved basic strength.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Magnetic gait finding raises concern for frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction because frontal-subcortical network dysfunction impairs gait initiation and scaling despite preserved basic strength. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Magnetic gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Magnetic gait to focus evaluation of frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: parkinsonian gait, severe arthritis, functional gait. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe initiation, stride length, turning, and foot clearance with fall protection. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Magnetic gait as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Parkinson disease and fear of falling can look similar. Therefore, Magnetic gait cannot confirm or exclude frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • parkinsonian gait

  • severe arthritis

  • functional gait

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as parkinsonian gait, severe arthritis, functional gait.

False Negative Situations

Magnetic gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of frontal-subcortical gait dysfunction.

Current descriptive clue; not specific for normal-pressure hydrocephalus. Interpret Magnetic gait in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Parkinson disease and fear of falling can look similar.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not diagnose hydrocephalus without the broader syndrome and neuroimaging.
