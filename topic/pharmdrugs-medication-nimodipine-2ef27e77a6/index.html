Class

Cerebrovascular-active oral dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker for aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage

Used To Treat

Improvement of neurologic outcome by reducing the incidence and severity of ischemic deficits after subarachnoid hemorrhage from a ruptured intracranial berry aneurysm. The capsule label regimen is 60 mg enterally every 4 hours for 21 consecutive days, begun within 96 hours of the hemorrhage; use the exact current product and protocol.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - DO NOT ADMINISTER NIMODIPINE INTRAVENOUSLY OR BY ANY PARENTERAL ROUTE. Death, cardiac arrest, cardiovascular collapse, severe hypotension, and bradycardia have occurred when capsule contents were injected. Nimodipine is for oral or enteral administration only. Use a properly labeled enteral syringe and feeding-tube procedure when the patient cannot swallow, and prevent any connection to IV tubing. Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated because dangerous hypotension can occur; strong inducers can markedly reduce efficacy. Monitor blood pressure and neurologic status through every dose rather than withholding casually, because missed therapy can surrender outcome benefit while uncontrolled hypotension can also reduce cerebral perfusion.

Mechanism

Nimodipine inhibits L-type calcium entry into vascular smooth muscle and has a relatively strong cerebral vascular effect, possibly aided by high lipophilicity and CNS penetration. Less intracellular calcium reduces calmodulin-myosin light-chain kinase signaling and vascular contraction. After aneurysmal SAH, delayed cerebral ischemia involves vasoconstriction, microvascular dysfunction, cortical spreading depolarizations, inflammation, thrombosis, and impaired autoregulation; nimodipine's clinical benefit may therefore involve microcirculatory and neuronal protection beyond large-artery diameter. It undergoes extensive CYP3A4 first-pass metabolism and averages only about 13% oral bioavailability. Cirrhosis, strong CYP3A inhibitors, and grapefruit can sharply raise exposure and hypotension, while inducers can reduce exposure and neurologic protection.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify aneurysmal SAH indication, start time, exact product, 4-hour schedule, blood pressure target, hepatic function, bowel function, and all CYP3A drugs. Coordinate temporary dose modifications only through the neurocritical-care plan when hypotension threatens cerebral perfusion.

  • For enteral-tube delivery, use the product-specific oral/enteral method and an enteral-only syringe, flush the tube as directed, label every syringe clearly, and keep the preparation physically separated from IV medications.

Contraindications

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as selected macrolides, azole antifungals, HIV protease inhibitors, and nefazodone are contraindicated because exposure and hypotension can rise substantially.

  • Never administer IV, IM, subcutaneous, or by another parenteral route. Strong inducers such as rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, and St. John's wort should generally be avoided because efficacy can fall.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated; moderate/weak inhibitors and grapefruit can raise exposure and prolong hypotension. Grapefruit effect can persist for days after the last intake.

  • Strong CYP3A inducers markedly reduce concentration and efficacy. Other antihypertensives, PDE5 inhibitors, alpha blockers, and diuretics add hypotension.

Key Labs

  • Frequent BP/HR and neurologic checks, cerebral-perfusion context, liver function, bowel function, and medication-interaction review. Routine nimodipine serum levels are not used.

  • Trend the clinical outcome the drug protects: new focal deficit, reduced consciousness, delayed cerebral ischemia, infarction, and hemodynamic tolerance rather than expecting angiographic vasospasm alone to define benefit.

Nclex Traps

  • Nimodipine improves neurologic outcome after aneurysmal SAH; it is not simply a generic antihypertensive and not proven to work only by visibly reversing large-vessel spasm.

  • The capsule contains a liquid, but that liquid is still enteral medication. Drawing it into the wrong syringe and injecting it can be fatal.

Description

Nimodipine is an oral or enteral, highly lipophilic dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker used to improve neurologic outcome after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage. It is not a routine hypertension drug and it must never be injected. Its greater cerebral-artery effect and blood-brain-barrier penetration may reduce delayed ischemic injury, but benefit is not explained simply by visibly opening a vasospastic artery: trials improved neurologic outcomes even though angiography did not clearly prove prevention or reversal of vasospasm. The practical priority is uninterrupted scheduled enteral dosing while preventing treatment-limiting hypotension and avoiding CYP3A interactions. Frequent every-4-hour dosing reflects rapid early elimination despite an 8- to 9-hour terminal half-life.

Adverse Effects

  • Decreased blood pressure, headache, nausea, diarrhea, edema, rash, and abnormal liver tests can occur.

  • Fatal cardiovascular collapse after parenteral administration, severe hypotension, bradycardia, ileus/pseudo-obstruction, hepatic accumulation, and loss of neurologic benefit from strong CYP3A induction or missed doses.
