Definition

Broadbent sign is an observed precordial sign. It is a clue to adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly. A positive finding means systolic retraction occurs near the left posterior axillary line.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Broadbent sign means systolic retraction occurs near the left posterior axillary line.

Why It Matters

The observed Broadbent sign finding can signal adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly. Pericardial adhesions or a displaced enlarged heart pull the chest wall inward during contraction. Broadbent sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly

Assessment Method

Observe and palpate the left posterior-lateral chest during systole.

Positive Finding

Systolic retraction occurs near the left posterior axillary line.

Mechanism

Pericardial adhesions or a displaced enlarged heart pull the chest wall inward during contraction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Broadbent sign finding raises concern for adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly because pericardial adhesions or a displaced enlarged heart pull the chest wall inward during contraction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Broadbent sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Broadbent sign to focus evaluation of adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly and the reviewed alternatives: normal intercostal motion, postoperative scar, respiratory retraction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe and palpate the left posterior-lateral chest during systole. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Broadbent sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor reproducibility and body habitus limit detection. Therefore, Broadbent sign cannot confirm or exclude adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal intercostal motion

  • postoperative scar

  • respiratory retraction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal intercostal motion, postoperative scar, respiratory retraction.

False Negative Situations

Broadbent sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly.

Modern Status

Historical and rarely useful. Broadbent sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly. Apply the reviewed limitation for Broadbent sign: Poor reproducibility and body habitus limit detection.

Safety Precautions

Observe Broadbent sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe and palpate the left posterior-lateral chest during systole. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not confuse respiratory retraction with pulse-synchronous systolic movement.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Broadbent sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Systolic retraction occurs near the left posterior axillary line. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Observe and palpate the left posterior-lateral chest during systole. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, adhesive pericarditis or marked cardiomegaly, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Broadbent sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.

Common Pitfalls
