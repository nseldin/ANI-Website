Used To Treat

Type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes when insulin is needed, DKA/HHS protocols, and hyperkalemia shifting therapy with glucose.

Mechanism

Insulin sliding scale works by activating insulin receptors so glucose moves into cells and potassium shifts intracellularly. Onset, peak, and meal timing determine the hypoglycemia risk window.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • Blood glucose, hypoglycemia symptoms, potassium in DKA/hyperkalemia protocols, meal timing, and injection-site rotation.

Nclex Traps

  • Hypoglycemia is the immediate danger. Potassium must be watched closely in DKA treatment.

Description

Insulin sliding scale is an insulin: it works by activating insulin receptors so glucose moves into cells and potassium shifts intracellularly. It is used for type 1 diabetes, type 2 diabetes when insulin is needed, DKA/HHS protocols, and hyperkalemia shifting therapy with glucose.
