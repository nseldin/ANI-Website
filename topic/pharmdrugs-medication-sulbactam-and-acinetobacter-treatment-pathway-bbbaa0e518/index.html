Used To Treat

Susceptible Acinetobacter baumannii-calcoaceticus complex infection, including labeled sulbactam/durlobactam treatment of adult HABP/VABP and sulbactam-based guideline strategies for carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii.

Description

Sulbactam is unusual among older beta-lactamase inhibitors because it directly kills susceptible Acinetobacter by binding PBP1 and PBP3, enzymes required for cell-wall synthesis. In ampicillin/sulbactam, the ampicillin treats susceptible partner organisms while sulbactam contributes the clinically important Acinetobacter activity. Carbapenem-resistant A. baumannii often produces OXA-type and other serine beta-lactamases that destroy sulbactam. Durlobactam was designed to inhibit broad class A, C, and D Acinetobacter enzymes and protect sulbactam long enough to reach its PBP targets. The Xacduro product is therefore not two passive inhibitors: sulbactam is the antibacterial engine and durlobactam is its enzyme shield.

Mechanism

Sulbactam acylates Acinetobacter PBP1 and PBP3, disrupting elongation and septation. Durlobactam is a diazabicyclooctane that reversibly acylates susceptible serine beta-lactamases, including many ADC class C and OXA class D enzymes. It does not inhibit class B metallo-beta-lactamases and has no useful antibacterial activity alone. Severe CRAB infection also reflects biofilm, devices, ventilator exposure, tissue necrosis, and limited host reserve, so current guidance commonly uses a sulbactam-containing backbone with a second active agent in important disease rather than treating one MIC as the whole problem.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Xacduro is labeled for susceptible Acinetobacter HABP/VABP in adults, not every infection or every Gram-negative organism. Severe beta-lactam hypersensitivity and renal exposure require active review.

Adverse Effects

  • Liver-test abnormalities, diarrhea, anemia, hypokalemia, hypersensitivity, CDI, and infusion-related complications can occur with sulbactam/durlobactam.

  • Treatment failure can reflect an MBL, altered PBPs, permeability/efflux, high burden, abscess or device source, or inadequate partner strategy rather than absence of durlobactam exposure alone.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to sulbactam, durlobactam, or other beta-lactams is a contraindication to Xacduro under the current label.

  • Do not interpret the word inhibitor as meaning sulbactam lacks direct antibacterial activity against Acinetobacter; that misconception can lead to poor regimen design.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the organism is Acinetobacter baumannii-calcoaceticus complex, review susceptibility and resistance mechanism, assess pneumonia and ventilator source, and coordinate renal-adjusted coadministration because both components must arrive in the studied relationship.

  • Trend oxygenation, hemodynamics, respiratory cultures, source control, creatinine, liver tests, CBC, and potassium because response and toxicity are multi-organ signals.

Interactions

  • Xacduro is co-packaged and preparation is product specific; do not substitute arbitrary separate-component doses.

  • Concurrent nephrotoxic salvage agents can narrow the safety margin and indirectly alter sulbactam/durlobactam clearance.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, carbapenemase or resistance testing where available, creatinine/eGFR, liver tests, CBC, potassium, oxygenation, and pneumonia imaging or source assessment.

  • For sulbactam, fT>MIC predicts activity; for durlobactam, exposure relative to the combination MIC predicts protection, so coordinated dosing matters.

Nclex Traps

  • Sulbactam directly binds Acinetobacter PBPs; durlobactam protects it from serine beta-lactamases.

  • Durlobactam alone is not an antibiotic and does not inhibit metallo-beta-lactamases.

Source Note

Current U.S. Xacduro label updated November 2025 and IDSA 2024 AMR guidance.

    Pediatric indication, meningitis role, formulation, dose, interval, renal maturation, and maximum dose differ by agent and age. Use the exact label or pediatric infectious-disease protocol because beta-lactams are not safely scaled from an adult regimen by intuition.

  • Type

    Label

    Older adult caution

    Note

    Older adults can have sharply reduced renal clearance despite an unremarkable serum creatinine. Estimate kidney function, reassess it during acute illness, and connect new confusion, myoclonus, or seizure to possible beta-lactam accumulation rather than assuming delirium is unrelated.

  • Type

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Many beta-lactams have substantial pregnancy and lactation experience, but infection severity, allergy history, maternal organ function, placental exposure, neonatal bilirubin handling, and the exact agent still matter. Treat serious infection promptly with an evidence-supported regimen rather than applying one class-wide pregnancy rule.
