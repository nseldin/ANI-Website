Definition

Liver failure develops when massive acute hepatocyte loss or end-stage chronic liver dysfunction impairs detoxification, synthesis, metabolism, bile flow, and circulatory regulation. The characteristic pattern is jaundice, coagulopathy, hypoglycemia, encephalopathy, ascites, bleeding, infection, or organ failure, with acute failure defined by new coagulopathy and encephalopathy without prior cirrhosis. Confirm or stage it with serial INR, glucose, bilirubin, transaminases, ammonia context, CBC, kidney, electrolytes, lactate, and blood gas; distinguish it from sepsis-associated cholestasis, biliary obstruction, isolated hepatitis, hemolysis, and medication sedation by synthetic function, imaging, cause testing, and history. The priority is to treat acetaminophen or other reversible cause immediately because cerebral edema can complicate Liver failure. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Abdominal pain pattern, distention, nausea/vomiting, diarrhea or constipation, stool/emesis blood, jaundice, dysphagia, poor intake, dehydration, or guarding are the useful GI clues.

  • Escalate rigid abdomen, peritonitis signs, GI bleeding, shock, severe dehydration, fever with jaundice, or worsening mental status.

Diagnostics

  • serial INR, glucose, bilirubin, transaminases, ammonia context, CBC, kidney, electrolytes, lactate, and blood gas

  • toxin, viral, autoimmune, ischemic, pregnancy, vascular, and metabolic cause testing

  • ultrasound with vascular flow and urgent transplant evaluation for acute or decompensated high-risk disease

  • Distinguish Liver failure from sepsis-associated cholestasis, biliary obstruction, isolated hepatitis, hemolysis, and medication sedation by synthetic function, imaging, cause testing, and history.

Labs

  • hemoglobin may fall with bleeding

  • AST/ALT and bilirubin may rise with hepatic/biliary injury

  • lipase rises in pancreatitis

  • albumin and INR reflect liver synthetic function

Treatments

  • treat acetaminophen or other reversible cause immediately

  • protect glucose, airway, brain, kidney, infection, bleeding, and nutrition while avoiding hepatotoxins

  • refer early to a transplant center rather than waiting for irreversible multiorgan failure

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for rapidly declining consciousness, severe hypoglycemia, active bleeding, rising lactate, oliguria, seizure, or worsening acidosis; activate rapid response, protect the airway, and prepare ordered blood products when major bleeding is present because liver failure can deteriorate into shock, cerebral edema, and multiorgan failure while intensive-care and transplant-center consultation may be urgent.

  • Perform frequent neurologic checks and assess asterixis because rising ammonia and cerebral edema can progress from subtle confusion to coma.

  • Monitor glucose, international normalized ratio, bilirubin, liver enzymes, lactate, creatinine, sodium, potassium, and urine output to detect metabolic and multiorgan failure.

  • Use bleeding precautions and aspiration precautions, and avoid unnecessary invasive procedures, because impaired synthesis and encephalopathy increase hemorrhage and airway risk.

  • Administer ordered dextrose for hypoglycemia, lactulose for hepatic encephalopathy, antimicrobials for suspected infection, and an etiology-specific antidote such as acetylcysteine when indicated because liver failure requires simultaneous correction of its cause and immediate complications.

Complications

  • cerebral edema can complicate Liver failure.

  • hemorrhage can complicate Liver failure.

  • sepsis can complicate Liver failure.

  • hepatorenal syndrome can complicate Liver failure.

  • respiratory failure can complicate Liver failure.

  • hypoglycemia can complicate Liver failure.

  • death can complicate Liver failure.

Red Flags

  • Rapidly declining consciousness or new seizure

  • Severe hypoglycemia or worsening metabolic acidosis

  • Active bleeding with worsening coagulation findings

  • Rising lactate, oliguria, or another failing organ

Patient Education

  • Teach patients to avoid unapproved medicines, supplements, alcohol, and acetaminophen-containing combinations because impaired liver clearance changes toxicity risk.

  • Explain that confusion, unusual sleepiness, vomiting blood, black stool, jaundice, or decreasing urine requires urgent evaluation.

Nclex Traps

  • INR reflects synthetic failure but does not predict every bleeding event

  • A falling aminotransferase level can accompany worsening failure when viable hepatocytes are lost
