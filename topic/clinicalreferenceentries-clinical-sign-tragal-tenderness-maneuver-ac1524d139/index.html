Definition

Tragal tenderness maneuver is a provoked pain bedside maneuver. It is a clue to acute otitis externa. A positive finding means marked canal pain is reported or produces protective withdrawal.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tragal tenderness maneuver means marked canal pain is reported or produces protective withdrawal.

Why It Matters

A positive Tragal tenderness maneuver response can signal acute otitis externa. Movement and pressure stress inflamed external auditory canal skin. Tragal tenderness maneuver cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • acute otitis externa

Assessment Method

Press the tragus gently and move the pinna while asking about pain.

Positive Finding

Marked canal pain is reported or produces protective withdrawal.

Mechanism

Movement and pressure stress inflamed external auditory canal skin.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Tragal tenderness maneuver response raises concern for acute otitis externa because movement and pressure stress inflamed external auditory canal skin. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tragal tenderness maneuver does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tragal tenderness maneuver to focus evaluation of acute otitis externa and the reviewed alternatives: mastoiditis, TMJ pain, foreign body. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Press the tragus gently and move the pinna while asking about pain. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tragal tenderness maneuver as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Young children may react nonspecifically and middle-ear disease can coexist. Therefore, Tragal tenderness maneuver cannot confirm or exclude acute otitis externa by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • mastoiditis

  • TMJ pain

  • foreign body

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from mastoiditis, TMJ pain, foreign body, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Tragal tenderness maneuver may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Tragal tenderness maneuver, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Press the tragus gently and move the pinna while asking about pain. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not insert instruments into a swollen canal without visualization and training.
