Definition

Clasp-knife response is an observed tone sign. It is a clue to upper motor neuron spasticity. A positive finding means high initial resistance suddenly gives way.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Clasp-knife response means high initial resistance suddenly gives way.

Why It Matters

The observed Clasp-knife response finding can signal upper motor neuron spasticity. Velocity-dependent stretch-reflex overactivity is followed by inhibitory tendon and interneuronal effects. Clasp-knife response cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • upper motor neuron spasticity

Assessment Method

Passively move a joint through range after ensuring no injury.

Positive Finding

High initial resistance suddenly gives way.

Mechanism

Velocity-dependent stretch-reflex overactivity is followed by inhibitory tendon and interneuronal effects.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Clasp-knife response finding raises concern for upper motor neuron spasticity because velocity-dependent stretch-reflex overactivity is followed by inhibitory tendon and interneuronal effects. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Clasp-knife response does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Clasp-knife response to focus evaluation of upper motor neuron spasticity and the reviewed alternatives: rigidity, contracture, guarding. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Passively move a joint through range after ensuring no injury. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Clasp-knife response as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain and fixed contracture can prevent the classic release. Therefore, Clasp-knife response cannot confirm or exclude upper motor neuron spasticity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as rigidity, contracture, guarding.

False Negative Situations

Clasp-knife response may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of upper motor neuron spasticity.
