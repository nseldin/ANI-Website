Nursing Essentials

  • Give with or immediately after meals and ensure chewable tablets are chewed completely or crushed; use the oral powder correctly when prescribed.

  • Monitor constipation, abdominal pain/distention, vomiting, bowel obstruction risk, phosphorus response, calcium/PTH, and separation from interacting oral drugs.

Key Labs

  • Serum phosphorus, calcium, PTH, bicarbonate/nutrition context, and renal/dialysis trends.

Nclex Traps

  • Lanthanum works in the gut only when taken with phosphate-containing food. It is radiopaque and may appear on abdominal imaging.

Used To Treat

Hyperphosphatemia in chronic kidney disease, especially dialysis, as an adjunct to dietary phosphate control.

Description

Lanthanum carbonate is a non-calcium phosphate binder taken with food. In the acidic upper GI tract it releases lanthanum ions that bind dietary phosphate into insoluble complexes excreted in stool, lowering phosphorus without adding calcium; GI obstruction/perforation risk and correct chewing are the practical dangers.

Mechanism

Trivalent lanthanum has high affinity for phosphate across the GI pH range and forms poorly absorbed lanthanum-phosphate complexes. Because the effect occurs inside the gut, the dose must accompany meals and does not remove phosphate already circulating in blood.

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for lanthanum carbonate in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.
