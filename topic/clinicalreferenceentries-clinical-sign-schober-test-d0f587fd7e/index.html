Definition

Schober test is a bedside spinal-mobility maneuver. It is a clue to reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis. A positive finding means the distance increases less than expected for the method.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Schober test means the distance increases less than expected for the method.

Why It Matters

A positive Schober test response can signal reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis. Inflammation, fibrosis, or structural fusion limits lumbar-segment flexion. Schober test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis

Assessment Method

Mark standardized points over the lumbar spine and remeasure their separation during forward flexion.

Positive Finding

The distance increases less than expected for the method.

Mechanism

Inflammation, fibrosis, or structural fusion limits lumbar-segment flexion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Schober test response raises concern for reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis because inflammation, fibrosis, or structural fusion limits lumbar-segment flexion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Schober test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Schober test to focus evaluation of reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis and the reviewed alternatives: lumbar fusion, obesity, hip restriction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Mark standardized points over the lumbar spine and remeasure their separation during forward flexion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Schober test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, body habitus, landmark error, and effort affect results. Therefore, Schober test cannot confirm or exclude reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lumbar fusion

  • obesity

  • hip restriction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from lumbar fusion, obesity, hip restriction, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Schober test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current measurement adjunct; method and reference ranges vary. When performed as described, Schober test can adjust concern for reduced lumbar flexion, often in axial spondyloarthritis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Schober test: Age, body habitus, landmark error, and effort affect results.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Schober test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Mark standardized points over the lumbar spine and remeasure their separation during forward flexion. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use a consistent modified protocol and do not force painful flexion.
