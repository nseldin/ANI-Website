Summary

Cardioversion is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Delivers synchronized electrical energy to convert selected unstable or persistent tachydysrhythmias while timing the shock with the cardiac cycle.

Quick Answer

Cardioversion: Delivers synchronized electrical energy to convert selected unstable or persistent tachydysrhythmias while timing the shock with the cardiac cycle. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Delivers synchronized electrical energy to convert selected unstable or persistent tachydysrhythmias while timing the shock with the cardiac cycle.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess rhythm, hemodynamic stability, anticoagulation status when atrial fibrillation/flutter timing matters, consent if elective, sedation readiness, oxygen, suction, IV access, and defibrillator synchronization.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Monitor airway after sedation, rhythm, blood pressure, chest discomfort, skin burns, neuro status, recurrence of dysrhythmia, and anticoagulation plan when applicable.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Loss of pulse, hypotension, respiratory depression after sedation, stroke symptoms, recurrent unstable tachydysrhythmia, or worsening chest pain.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Cardioversion is synchronized. Defibrillation is unsynchronized. NCLEX loves that distinction.
