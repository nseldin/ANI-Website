Mechanism

Methotrexate enters cells through reduced-folate carriers and is polyglutamated by folylpolyglutamate synthetase, trapping active forms intracellularly. Because methotrexate polyglutamates inhibit the enzyme ATIC, AICAR accumulates. AICAR then inhibits AMP deaminase and adenosine deaminase, which raises extracellular adenosine. Adenosine activates A2A receptors and raises cyclic AMP in inflammatory cells; therefore neutrophil adhesion, oxidant release, TNF, and other cytokine programs fall. Folate-pathway inhibition also affects the enzymes dihydrofolate reductase and thymidylate synthase, especially at antineoplastic exposure, which reduces tetrahydrofolate-dependent nucleotide synthesis. Renal elimination is major; therefore dehydration, kidney injury, third-space fluid, or competing tubular transport can prolong exposure and convert a tolerated dose into marrow, mucosal, hepatic, pulmonary, or renal toxicity.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: embryo-fetal toxicity, serious hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, severe adverse reactions including death, and risk of fatal medication errors. For rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile arthritis, and psoriasis, methotrexate is taken once weekly; accidental daily dosing has caused death. Monitor closely for bone marrow suppression, infection, GI ulceration, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary toxicity including pneumonitis/interstitial lung disease, severe skin reaction, renal toxicity, and secondary malignancy. Stop and urgently evaluate new dyspnea, dry cough, fever, mouth ulcers, bleeding, profound diarrhea, jaundice, or rash rather than waiting for the next routine laboratory visit.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy for non-neoplastic indications, prior serious hypersensitivity, major marrow suppression or immunodeficiency, severe liver disease or alcohol-use risk, and inability to follow weekly dosing/monitoring require avoidance or specialist reassessment.

  • Clarify kidney function, pleural effusion/ascites, active infection, lung symptoms, vaccination, folate status, and every prescription or over-the-counter interaction before giving.

Nursing Essentials

  • Write and teach the exact weekday; reconcile tablets by strength and total weekly dose. A patient who says 'I take it every day' needs immediate clarification before another dose.

  • Folic acid commonly reduces mucosal, GI, liver, and hematologic toxicity but does not neutralize overdose, eliminate monitoring, or make pregnancy safe.

Interactions

  • Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole and other antifolates can cause profound marrow suppression; NSAIDs, penicillins, PPIs, probenecid, nephrotoxins, and drugs competing for renal secretion can increase exposure in susceptible settings.

  • Alcohol and other hepatotoxins add liver injury; live vaccines may cause infection during immunosuppression; nitrous oxide intensifies functional folate inhibition.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and serial CBC, creatinine/eGFR, AST/ALT, albumin, hepatitis context, pregnancy assessment, and product/indication-specific chest or infection evaluation

  • Mouth, skin, stool, bleeding, respiratory symptoms, fever, disease activity, adherence, dose day, and interacting medication review

Nclex Traps

  • Inflammatory-disease methotrexate is commonly once weekly, not once daily. Daily dosing is a medical emergency risk even when each tablet is small.

  • Leucovorin is used for selected toxicity or high-dose rescue; routine folic acid supplementation is not the same intervention.

Used To Treat

Once-weekly treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, and severe psoriasis when standard therapies are inadequate, plus very different dose schedules in selected malignancies. The indication determines dose, route, rescue strategy, and monitoring; an oncology regimen must never be inferred from a rheumatoid prescription or vice versa.

Description

Methotrexate is a folate-antagonist antimetabolite and once-weekly inflammatory-arthritis DMARD that forms intracellular polyglutamates, inhibits ATIC, and raises anti-inflammatory adenosine signaling. Those polyglutamates persist and alter folate and purine pathways. In rheumatoid disease, accumulation of AICAR and increased extracellular adenosine reduce neutrophil, macrophage, and lymphocyte signaling over weeks. At higher oncology exposures, inhibition of dihydrofolate reductase and thymidylate/purine synthesis limits DNA production in rapidly dividing cells. This dose-context difference explains why the same drug can be a weekly DMARD, a chemotherapy agent, and a source of fatal daily-versus-weekly errors.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, fatigue, stomatitis, mucositis, transaminase elevation, alopecia, photosensitivity, and cytopenias

  • Fatal myelosuppression or infection, hepatic fibrosis, pneumonitis, GI ulceration/bleeding, renal injury, severe cutaneous reaction, lymphoproliferative disease, and fetal harm
