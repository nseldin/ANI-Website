Name

Magnesium

Range

Mayo Clinic Laboratories lists 1.7-2.3 mg/dL as one serum-magnesium interval for people older than 17 years, but age, specimen, method, laboratory, kidney function, medicines, supplements, and treatment context matter; use the printed interval and local critical-value policy. Do not use a general laboratory reference interval as an obstetric magnesium-sulfate treatment target. Parenteral magnesium monitoring follows the specific product label, indication, order, clinical findings, and facility protocol.

Why

An abnormal magnesium result signals disturbed neuromuscular and cardiac function and can make potassium or calcium abnormalities harder to interpret and correct. Low magnesium raises concern for gastrointestinal or renal loss, malnutrition or alcohol use disorder, uncontrolled diabetes, or medicines such as diuretics and proton-pump inhibitors, and can accompany weakness, cramps, dysrhythmia, or seizure. High magnesium most often raises concern for impaired kidney elimination or magnesium exposure from parenteral therapy, supplements, laxatives, or antacids and can progress to decreased reflexes, respiratory depression, hypotension, bradycardia, or cardiac arrest. Promptly assess rhythm and ECG, weakness or cramps, tremor, reflexes, mental status, respirations, blood pressure, kidney function and urine output, gastrointestinal losses, nutrition, and every magnesium-containing medicine or product. Cause-directed evaluation can include repeat serum magnesium, potassium, calcium, phosphorus, glucose, kidney function, ECG, and urine magnesium or other testing as ordered. During parenteral magnesium therapy, follow the label and protocol for serum and clinical monitoring, withhold further dosing and escalate when patellar reflexes are absent, evaluate respiratory depression immediately, and ensure the ordered injectable calcium rescue is available. Escalate immediately for seizure, serious dysrhythmia, loss of reflexes, respiratory compromise, hemodynamic instability, or rapid deterioration. One serum magnesium value does not diagnose the cause, prove normal total-body stores, or by itself establish magnesium-sulfate toxicity. In a malnourished patient starting nutrition, a new fall in magnesium, phosphorus, or potassium during the first five days after substantially increased energy intake raises concern for refeeding syndrome under the ASPEN framework and requires ordered multidisciplinary assessment rather than diagnosis from one mineral value.

High Causes

  • Impaired renal elimination or kidney failure

  • Parenteral magnesium therapy

  • Magnesium-containing supplements, laxatives, antacids, or other medicines

  • Dehydration or selected endocrine conditions in the appropriate context

Low Causes

  • Chronic diarrhea, malabsorption, or another gastrointestinal loss

  • Malnutrition or alcohol use disorder

  • Renal loss from uncontrolled diabetes or selected medicines

  • Diuretics, proton-pump inhibitors, or selected antibiotics

  • Refeeding-related intracellular shift when the complete nutrition and electrolyte pattern fits

Critical Concerns

  • Loss of patellar or other deep-tendon reflexes, respiratory depression, hypotension, bradycardia, or cardiac arrest during parenteral magnesium therapy is an urgent toxicity pattern.

  • Seizure, serious dysrhythmia, respiratory compromise, hemodynamic instability, or rapid deterioration with an abnormal magnesium result requires immediate escalation.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess rhythm, ECG, neuromuscular findings, reflexes, respirations, mental status, blood pressure, kidney function, urine output, gastrointestinal losses, nutrition, and all magnesium-containing products.

  • Trend magnesium with potassium, calcium, phosphorus, glucose, and kidney function and follow the ordered product-specific magnesium, refeeding, or electrolyte protocol; do not independently select a replacement dose or obstetric target.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal serum magnesium does not exclude depleted body stores, and refractory potassium or calcium abnormalities should prompt magnesium review.

  • During magnesium-sulfate therapy, clinical toxicity findings and renal elimination matter; do not rely on a memorized universal therapeutic range or use the drug as the primary antihypertensive in preeclampsia.
