Class

Long-acting lipoglycopeptide antibiotic

Used To Treat

Single-dose IV treatment of acute bacterial skin and skin-structure infection caused by susceptible gram-positive organisms, including MRSA.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for oritavancin in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Oritavancin binds peptidoglycan stem peptides and bridging segments to inhibit transglycosylation and transpeptidation, while its lipophilic structure disrupts membrane integrity and causes depolarization. The result is concentration-dependent killing of susceptible gram-positive bacteria.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document the dose/time prominently and communicate coagulation-test interference across transitions of care.

  • Do not use IV unfractionated heparin for 120 hours after oritavancin; monitor infusion reaction, osteomyelitis symptoms, C. difficile diarrhea, allergy, and skin response.

Key Labs

  • Cultures, CBC, liver/renal context, and coagulation assays interpreted by their known interference windows; aPTT may be falsely high for 120 hours.

Nclex Traps

  • A high aPTT after oritavancin may be reagent interference, not anticoagulation. IV heparin is contraindicated during the five-day aPTT interference window.

Description

Oritavancin is a long-acting lipoglycopeptide antibiotic with three linked actions: it blocks peptidoglycan polymerization, blocks cross-linking, and disrupts the gram-positive membrane. Its most unusual nursing hazard is laboratory interference: it falsely prolongs aPTT and other phospholipid-dependent coagulation tests, making IV unfractionated heparin unsafe to monitor for five days after a dose.
