Used To Treat

Second-line short-term and non-continuous chronic atopic-dermatitis treatment when other topical prescription therapy fails or is inadvisable.

Description

Topical tacrolimus and pimecrolimus are steroid-sparing immunomodulators for atopic dermatitis. They inhibit calcineurin signaling inside activated T cells, reducing cytokine transcription without causing corticosteroid skin atrophy. That makes them useful on thin or sensitive areas when appropriately prescribed, but they carry a boxed warning because long-term safety is not established and rare lymphoma and skin-malignancy reports have occurred without proof of causation.

Mechanism

Tacrolimus binds FKBP12; pimecrolimus binds macrophilin-12. Each drug-protein complex inhibits the calcium/calmodulin-dependent phosphatase calcineurin. NFAT therefore remains phosphorylated in the cytoplasm instead of entering the nucleus, reducing IL-2 and other T-cell cytokine transcription. Burning often occurs initially because inflamed skin and sensory signaling remain active before immune suppression controls the dermatitis.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Long-term safety of topical calcineurin inhibitors has not been established. Rare malignancies, including skin cancer and lymphoma, have been reported, although causality is unproven. Avoid continuous long-term use, limit application to involved atopic-dermatitis areas, and do not use in children younger than 2 years.

Adverse Effects

  • Application-site burning, warmth, stinging or pruritus

  • Viral skin infections including herpes simplex, eczema herpeticum, varicella or zoster

  • Lymphadenopathy, folliculitis and rare hypersensitivity

  • Theoretical/observational malignancy concern addressed by the boxed warning

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to the drug or vehicle

  • Not indicated below age 2 or in immunocompromised patients

  • Avoid malignant/premalignant lesions, active untreated infection, Netherton syndrome, severely impaired barrier, and occlusion

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the lesion is atopic dermatitis rather than infection, malignancy, or a steroid-masked eruption.

  • Teach intermittent use on active areas, sun protection, and reassessment if not improving by 6 weeks.

Interactions

  • Avoid UV phototherapy or deliberate tanning because long-term skin-cancer risk is uncertain.

  • Systemic CYP3A inhibitors matter most when large areas or severe barrier failure increase absorption.

  • Do not combine casually with systemic immunosuppression.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level for standard limited-area use

  • Skin and lymph-node examination for persistent lesions or lymphadenopathy

  • Drug level, renal assessment, or specialist review only when unusually extensive absorption is plausible

Nclex Traps

  • No skin atrophy does not mean no warning; these drugs have a malignancy boxed warning.

  • They are second-line, intermittent atopic-dermatitis drugs, not general rash creams.

  • Burning can be expected early, but vesicles, crusting, fever, or eye involvement require assessment.
