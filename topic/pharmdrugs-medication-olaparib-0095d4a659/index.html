Class

PARP1/2/3 inhibitor and PARP-trapping antineoplastic

Used To Treat

Selected biomarker-defined or maintenance settings in ovarian, fallopian-tube, peritoneal, HER2-negative breast, pancreatic, and prostate cancers, alone or in labeled combinations.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Monitor CBC for prolonged cytopenia and evaluate suspected MDS/AML with hematology and marrow/cytogenetics. Interrupt and evaluate new dyspnea, cough, or fever for pneumonitis; assess DVT/PE symptoms, especially with abiraterone-based prostate therapy.

Mechanism

Catalytic PARP inhibition prevents poly-ADP-ribosylation and repair-protein recruitment, while PARP-DNA trapping obstructs replication. The resulting double-strand breaks normally require BRCA-dependent homologous recombination; HRR-deficient tumor cells cannot repair them accurately and undergo genomic collapse and apoptosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify biomarker and cancer-specific regimen; tablet and historic capsule dosing are not milligram-equivalent.

  • Review strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, renal function for dose reduction, pregnancy prevention, and signs of anemia, infection, bleeding, thrombosis, or pneumonitis.

Key Labs

  • CBC baseline and monthly or per protocol, creatinine/eGFR, liver context, pregnancy testing when applicable, and pulmonary or thromboembolic evaluation for symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Persistent cytopenia is not always routine chemotherapy suppression; MDS/AML must be considered.

  • PARP inhibitor benefit depends on the exact disease/biomarker context, not simply the presence of any DNA-repair mutation.

Description

Olaparib is an oral PARP1/2/3 inhibitor that blocks single-strand DNA repair and traps PARP on DNA, producing replication-fork collapse and synthetic lethality in homologous-recombination-deficient tumors such as many BRCA1/2-mutated cancers. Marrow suppression, therapy-related MDS/AML, pneumonitis, venous thromboembolism in prostate regimens, CYP3A interactions, and fetal toxicity require longitudinal surveillance.
