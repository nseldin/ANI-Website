Class Example Names

  • Dicyclomine

Used To Treat

Short-term relief of functional bowel or IBS-related smooth-muscle spasm and selected bladder/secretory symptoms; these drugs treat symptoms, not inflammatory, infectious, ischemic, obstructive or malignant disease.

Description

Dicyclomine and hyoscyamine are tertiary-amine antimuscarinics that reduce acetylcholine-driven GI smooth-muscle contraction and secretion. Dicyclomine also has a direct musculotropic antispasmodic action. Because tertiary amines enter the brain and muscarinic receptors serve the eye, heart, bladder, glands and bowel, the same mechanism can cause delirium, blurred vision, tachycardia, urinary retention, constipation and dangerous heat intolerance.

Mechanism

Competitive blockade of postganglionic muscarinic receptors reduces parasympathetic M3-mediated smooth-muscle contraction and glandular secretion; cardiac M2 blockade can accelerate rate. CNS penetration impairs attention, memory and thermoregulation. Reduced sweating prevents evaporative cooling, and reduced bowel/bladder tone can convert partial obstruction into retention or ileus.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Never use symptom suppression to cover an acute surgical abdomen. Both drugs are unsafe with GI or urinary obstruction, glaucoma, myasthenia gravis, severe ulcerative colitis/toxic megacolon risk, or unstable cardiovascular states. Dicyclomine is contraindicated in infants younger than 6 months and in nursing mothers because severe respiratory/CNS toxicity has occurred in infants.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, blurred vision, mydriasis, photophobia, tachycardia, constipation and urinary retention

  • Drowsiness, dizziness, confusion, agitation, hallucinations or delirium

  • Decreased sweating, fever and heat prostration; ileus or toxic megacolon in susceptible bowel disease

Contraindications

  • Glaucoma, obstructive uropathy, obstructive GI disease, paralytic ileus, severe ulcerative colitis/toxic megacolon and myasthenia gravis

  • Unstable cardiovascular status in acute hemorrhage

  • Dicyclomine: infants younger than 6 months and nursing mothers; clarify reflux esophagitis and tachyarrhythmia

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess pain pattern, distention, bowel sounds, vomiting, stool/gas passage, urinary retention, eye history, cognition, temperature and medication burden before giving.

  • Teach hydration and heat precautions, slow position change, oral care and prompt reporting of inability to void, severe constipation, eye pain/halos, confusion or fever.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, first-generation antihistamines, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics and antiparkinsonian drugs add delirium, retention and heat risk.

  • Antacids may alter hyoscyamine absorption; separate according to the formulation label.

  • Anticholinergics oppose prokinetic or cholinesterase-inhibitor effects.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level; monitor heart rate, temperature, mental status, bowel/bladder function and hydration

  • Renal/hepatic function when accumulation or vulnerability is plausible

  • Evaluate for obstruction, glaucoma or acute inflammatory/surgical pathology rather than ordering a nonspecific drug level

Nclex Traps

  • Dry mouth plus blurred vision, urinary retention and confusion is a mechanism-linked anticholinergic pattern.

  • Fever during anticholinergic therapy may reflect inability to sweat, not infection alone.

  • Antispasmodic relief does not establish IBS and must not delay evaluation of obstruction, ischemia, infection or toxic megacolon.
