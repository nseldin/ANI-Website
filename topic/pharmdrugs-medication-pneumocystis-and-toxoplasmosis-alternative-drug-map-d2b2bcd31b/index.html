Used To Treat

Prevention or treatment of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and toxoplasmosis when first-line trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is unsuitable or when a guideline-supported combination is preferred. Regimens are not interchangeable: dapsone alone and aerosolized pentamidine do not protect against toxoplasmosis, while pyrimethamine must be paired with leucovorin and usually another antiparasitic.

Description

These drugs occupy different steps and must be assembled into complete regimens. Pyrimethamine inhibits protozoal dihydrofolate reductase; sulfadiazine or dapsone inhibits dihydropteroate synthase upstream, producing sequential folate blockade. Leucovorin bypasses host dihydrofolate reductase and rescues human marrow from predictable folate depletion, so it is part of therapy rather than an optional vitamin. Dapsone's oxidant hydroxylamine metabolite can cause hemolysis and methemoglobinemia, especially in G6PD deficiency. Pentamidine is a toxic non-folate alternative that disrupts multiple macromolecular pathways and can injure pancreatic islets, kidneys, marrow, and cardiac conduction. Atovaquone collapses mitochondrial electron transport but has variable food-dependent absorption.

Mechanism

Microbes synthesize folate de novo. Sulfonamides and dapsone mimic para-aminobenzoic acid and competitively inhibit dihydropteroate synthase, reducing dihydrofolate production. Pyrimethamine then inhibits parasite dihydrofolate reductase, preventing regeneration of tetrahydrofolate needed for thymidylate and purine synthesis. Human cells obtain folate from diet but still use dihydrofolate reductase; high-dose pyrimethamine can therefore suppress marrow. Folinic acid, or leucovorin, enters downstream as reduced folate and rescues host nucleotide synthesis without reliably reversing the complete parasite-directed combination. Atovaquone inhibits cytochrome bc1 and mitochondrial potential. Pentamidine binds nucleic acids and disrupts RNA, protein, and phospholipid synthesis, although no single dominant Pneumocystis target fully explains its activity.

Boxed Warning

No one class box covers these drugs, but the regimen-specific hazards are serious. Pyrimethamine can cause potentially fatal marrow suppression unless leucovorin and CBC monitoring are used. Dapsone can cause agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, hemolysis, methemoglobinemia, and severe hypersensitivity. IV pentamidine can cause profound hypotension, dysglycemia that may evolve from hypoglycemia to diabetes, pancreatitis, renal failure, electrolyte loss, and QT-related ventricular dysrhythmia. Check G6PD before dapsone or primaquine and do not infer safety from a normal pulse oximeter when methemoglobinemia is possible.

Adverse Effects

  • Cytopenias, rash, hepatitis, GI intolerance, hemolysis and methemoglobinemia from folate or oxidant drugs

  • Pentamidine-associated hypotension, nephrotoxicity, pancreatitis, glucose disturbance, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hypokalemia, and arrhythmia

Contraindications

  • Resolve G6PD status before dapsone or primaquine; avoid pyrimethamine without leucovorin and a complete partner regimen.

  • Select by disease severity: aerosolized pentamidine is a prophylaxis option, not adequate treatment for extrapulmonary disease, and does not protect against toxoplasmosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • For moderate-to-severe PCP, recognize the oxygenation threshold for adjunctive corticosteroids and select a regimen supported for that severity.

  • Track the complete combination, not just one familiar drug. Missing leucovorin or the partner agent turns a memorized list into unsafe incomplete therapy.

Interactions

  • Other oxidants or marrow suppressants compound dapsone/pyrimethamine toxicity; trimethoprim and sulfonamides add folate-pathway pressure.

  • Atovaquone exposure falls with rifampin or rifabutin and rises with food; pentamidine adds risk with nephrotoxins, pancreatotoxic drugs, and QT-prolonging agents.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, G6PD activity, renal and liver function, glucose, calcium, magnesium, potassium, ECG, amylase/lipase when indicated

  • Oxygenation, A-a gradient when relevant, disease severity, oral absorption and food intake, rash, neuropathy, dark urine, cyanosis with a saturation gap, and treatment response

Nclex Traps

  • Leucovorin is required marrow rescue with pyrimethamine; folic acid is not an equivalent substitute in this setting.

  • Dapsone alone or aerosolized pentamidine can prevent PCP in selected patients but does not provide toxoplasmosis protection.
