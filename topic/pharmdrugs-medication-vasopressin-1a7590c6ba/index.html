Used To Treat

Adjunct vasopressor support for vasodilatory shock, central diabetes insipidus when the ordered product/route matches, and selected bleeding or variceal protocols.

Mechanism

Stimulates V1 receptors on vascular smooth muscle to cause vasoconstriction and raise MAP. V2 receptor activity in the kidney increases water reabsorption, which matters most in diabetes insipidus and hyponatremia risk.

Boxed Warning

High-alert safety focus: ischemia, reduced cardiac output in vulnerable clients, hyponatremia/water intoxication, and mismatching the shock versus diabetes-insipidus indication.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to vasopressin.

  • Clarify coronary artery disease/ischemia, mesenteric or peripheral vascular disease, severe hyponatremia, or low-output heart failure risk before non-emergency use.

Nursing Essentials

  • For shock, monitor MAP, perfusion, ECG, chest pain, abdominal pain, mottling/cool extremities, urine output, lactate, and signs of ischemia.

  • For diabetes insipidus, monitor intake/output, urine specific gravity or osmolality as ordered, daily weight, sodium, and neurologic changes.

  • Do not titrate or substitute vasopressin like a catecholamine unless the protocol specifically says so.

Interactions

  • Other vasopressors increase ischemia risk.

  • Drugs that promote hyponatremia or water retention can increase low-sodium risk.

  • Lithium, demeclocycline, and some diuretics can reduce antidiuretic response depending on context.

Key Labs

  • MAP and perfusion markers.

  • Serum sodium and osmolality when water balance is relevant.

  • Urine output, urine specific gravity/osmolality, creatinine/eGFR, and lactate as ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Vasopressin is not just another norepinephrine drip; it is a non-catecholamine vasopressor with water-balance effects.

  • Improved MAP with new chest pain, abdominal pain, mottled skin, or falling urine output means reassess for ischemia.

Description

Vasopressin is an antidiuretic hormone analog with two exam-defining receptor effects: V1 stimulation causes vasoconstriction to raise MAP, while V2 stimulation increases renal collecting-duct water reabsorption. The first-read danger split is ischemia from vasoconstriction versus hyponatremia/fluid overload from water retention.
