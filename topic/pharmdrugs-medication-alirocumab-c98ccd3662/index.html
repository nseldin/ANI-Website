Used To Treat

Reduction of major adverse cardiovascular events in adults at increased risk and LDL-C reduction in label-defined adults and pediatric patients with primary hyperlipidemia or familial hypercholesterolemia, with current indication wording and age criteria applied.

Mechanism

Alirocumab binds free PCSK9, thereby preventing its interaction with the LDL receptor. Without PCSK9, endosomal LDL dissociates and the receptor returns to the surface instead of being delivered to lysosomes. Repeated receptor use increases clearance of LDL and other ApoB particles, which explains why the laboratory effect is much larger than blocking one round of LDL uptake. Antibody proteolysis, not hepatic CYP or renal filtration, clears the drug, so classic CYP interactions are not expected. LDL can vary near the end of a four-week interval in some patients because antibody concentration falls, so dose adjustment is based on measured response and current label instructions. Functional receptor availability remains necessary; a patient with no LDL-receptor activity will have a limited pathway response and may need receptor-independent HoFH treatment because there is no receptor for the antibody to preserve.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity, including hypersensitivity vasculitis, angioedema, and reactions requiring hospitalization, has occurred. Discontinue and urgently evaluate systemic allergy or airway symptoms.

Contraindications

  • History of serious hypersensitivity to alirocumab or an excipient is the principal contraindication.

  • Do not interchange pens, strengths, schedules, or monthly and biweekly regimens without a deliberate prescription and response plan.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm dose, frequency, indication, last injection, LDL response, storage, warming time, device integrity, site rotation, complete injection, and sharps disposal.

  • For monthly dosing, follow the current device instructions and missed-dose algorithm exactly; do not compress or double doses by intuition.

Interactions

  • No meaningful CYP-mediated interactions are expected because antibodies are cleared by proteolysis rather than cytochrome enzymes. Continue indicated background lipid therapy unless the prescriber changes it.

  • Other injectable therapies, allergy history, and immunologic disease still matter because they change reaction recognition and adherence planning.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel at a time that reflects the chosen dosing interval; no routine alirocumab level is used.

  • Unexpected response should prompt administration, storage, access, adherence, diagnosis, and receptor-function review before automatic dose escalation.

Nclex Traps

  • Alirocumab and evolocumab share a mechanism but have different labels, devices, strengths, schedules, and age indications.

  • Alirocumab is an antibody against PCSK9 protein, not an RNA drug and not a statin.

Description

Alirocumab, branded Praluent, is a subcutaneous fully human monoclonal antibody that binds circulating PCSK9 and prevents PCSK9-mediated destruction of hepatic LDL receptors. More receptors therefore recycle to the cell surface and remove LDL particles, producing a large additional LDL reduction. It has cardiovascular outcomes evidence and a current U.S. indication for reducing major adverse cardiovascular events in adults at increased risk because sustained particle clearance reduces plaque-related events. Common starting regimens are 75 mg every two weeks or 300 mg every four weeks, with adjustment based on LDL response and the exact indication. It is not the same molecule or device as evolocumab and is not the same platform as inclisiran, so schedules and outcome claims cannot simply be copied. Minimal CYP metabolism means few classic drug interactions, but a dose can still fail through wrong storage, incomplete injection, missed administration, or a receptor-negative HoFH phenotype.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site pain, erythema, pruritus or swelling, influenza-like symptoms, myalgia, and upper-respiratory complaints can occur.

  • Serious allergic reactions are uncommon. Technique and adherence problems can mimic pharmacologic failure.
