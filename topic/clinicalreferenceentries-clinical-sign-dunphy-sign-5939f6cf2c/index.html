Definition

Dunphy sign is a provoked symptom-sign. It is a clue to localized peritoneal irritation. A positive finding means coughing sharply worsens localized abdominal pain.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dunphy sign means coughing sharply worsens localized abdominal pain.

Why It Matters

The provoked Dunphy sign symptom can signal localized peritoneal irritation. A sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure moves inflamed parietal peritoneum. Dunphy sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • localized peritoneal irritation

Assessment Method

Ask a stable patient to cough once and report whether pain changes or localizes.

Positive Finding

Coughing sharply worsens localized abdominal pain.

Mechanism

A sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure moves inflamed parietal peritoneum.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Dunphy sign symptom raises concern for localized peritoneal irritation because a sudden rise in intra-abdominal pressure moves inflamed parietal peritoneum. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dunphy sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dunphy sign to focus evaluation of localized peritoneal irritation and the reviewed alternatives: diverticulitis, pelvic inflammation, muscle strain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask a stable patient to cough once and report whether pain changes or localizes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dunphy sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Any peritoneal inflammation or abdominal-wall pain can be positive. Therefore, Dunphy sign cannot confirm or exclude localized peritoneal irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • diverticulitis

  • pelvic inflammation

  • muscle strain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as diverticulitis, pelvic inflammation, muscle strain, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Dunphy sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; nonspecific. When performed as described, Dunphy sign can adjust concern for localized peritoneal irritation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Dunphy sign: Any peritoneal inflammation or abdominal-wall pain can be positive.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Dunphy sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask a stable patient to cough once and report whether pain changes or localizes. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not repeatedly provoke severe pain or use a negative cough test to exclude appendicitis.

Nursing Relevance

Record the patient's description of Dunphy sign, the exact trigger, distribution, duration, and associated objective findings; the reviewed positive response is: Coughing sharply worsens localized abdominal pain. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask a stable patient to cough once and report whether pain changes or localizes. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, localized peritoneal irritation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Dunphy sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not repeatedly provoke severe pain or use a negative cough test to exclude appendicitis. Base escalation for Dunphy sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
