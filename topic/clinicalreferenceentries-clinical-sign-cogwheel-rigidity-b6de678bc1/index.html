Definition

Cogwheel rigidity is an observed tone sign. It is a clue to parkinsonism with superimposed tremor. A positive finding means resistance has rhythmic ratcheting interruptions.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cogwheel rigidity means resistance has rhythmic ratcheting interruptions.

Why It Matters

The observed Cogwheel rigidity finding can signal parkinsonism with superimposed tremor. Continuous parkinsonian rigidity is modulated by an underlying tremor, creating stepwise resistance. Cogwheel rigidity cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • parkinsonism with superimposed tremor

Assessment Method

Passively flex and extend the relaxed wrist or elbow.

Positive Finding

Resistance has rhythmic ratcheting interruptions.

Mechanism

Continuous parkinsonian rigidity is modulated by an underlying tremor, creating stepwise resistance.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Cogwheel rigidity finding raises concern for parkinsonism with superimposed tremor because continuous parkinsonian rigidity is modulated by an underlying tremor, creating stepwise resistance. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cogwheel rigidity does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cogwheel rigidity to focus evaluation of parkinsonism with superimposed tremor and the reviewed alternatives: lead-pipe rigidity, spasticity, paratonia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Passively flex and extend the relaxed wrist or elbow. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cogwheel rigidity as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Voluntary tension and examiner rhythm can simulate ratcheting. Therefore, Cogwheel rigidity cannot confirm or exclude parkinsonism with superimposed tremor by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lead-pipe rigidity

  • spasticity

  • paratonia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as lead-pipe rigidity, spasticity, paratonia.

False Negative Situations

Cogwheel rigidity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of parkinsonism with superimposed tremor.

Modern Status
