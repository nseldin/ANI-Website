Definition

Parachute reflex is an infant protective-response sign. It is a clue to developing cortical postural protection. A positive finding means the arms extend forward with fingers spread.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Parachute reflex means the arms extend forward with fingers spread.

Why It Matters

The observed Parachute reflex finding can signal developing cortical postural protection. Visual, vestibular, and proprioceptive input activates a learned protective extension response. Parachute reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • developing cortical postural protection

Assessment Method

While securely supporting the infant, tilt the body forward toward a surface without releasing the infant.

Positive Finding

The arms extend forward with fingers spread.

Mechanism

Visual, vestibular, and proprioceptive input activates a learned protective extension response.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Parachute reflex finding raises concern for developing cortical postural protection because visual, vestibular, and proprioceptive input activates a learned protective extension response. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Parachute reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Parachute reflex to focus evaluation of developing cortical postural protection and the reviewed alternatives: Moro reflex, voluntary reaching, asymmetry from plexus injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: While securely supporting the infant, tilt the body forward toward a surface without releasing the infant. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Parachute reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fear, vision, and technique affect response. Therefore, Parachute reflex cannot confirm or exclude developing cortical postural protection by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Moro reflex

  • voluntary reaching

  • asymmetry from plexus injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Moro reflex, voluntary reaching, asymmetry from plexus injury.

False Negative Situations

Parachute reflex may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of developing cortical postural protection.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Maintain full support and never allow the infant to fall.
