Used To Treat

Local candidiasis, systemic yeast and mold infections, and selected endemic mycoses with an agent chosen for spectrum, site, formulation, exposure, interactions, and organ safety.

Description

Azoles coordinate the heme iron in fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, a CYP51 enzyme that removes a methyl group during ergosterol synthesis. Blocking that step depletes functional ergosterol and accumulates abnormal 14-methyl sterols, disrupting membrane proteins, permeability, growth, and replication. Most azoles are primarily fungistatic against Candida, so immune function and source control still matter. Their shared target does not create a shared spectrum: fluconazole lacks reliable mold activity, voriconazole is central for Aspergillus but not Mucorales, and isavuconazole or posaconazole can cover both Aspergillus and Mucorales. Imidazoles such as topical clotrimazole or miconazole are mainly local treatments, while oral ketoconazole is restricted by severe systemic toxicity.

Mechanism

The interaction problem follows from imperfect enzyme selectivity. Azoles can inhibit human CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, transporters, or steroid-synthesis enzymes to different degrees, while inducers can lower azole exposure and cause breakthrough infection. Many also inhibit cardiac repolarization pathways or raise concentrations of another QT-active drug, so hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, bradycardia, congenital long-QT states, and polypharmacy amplify torsades risk. Isavuconazole is the notable exception that dose-dependently shortens QT and is contraindicated in familial short-QT syndrome. QT direction alone does not determine safety because hepatic injury, interactions, and exposure variability remain. The correct action is an agent-specific interaction review, not the vague statement that all azoles are 'hard on the liver.'

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Warnings differ by agent. Oral ketoconazole carries boxed hepatotoxicity and dangerous interaction warnings; itraconazole carries boxed heart-failure and interaction warnings. Other azoles can still cause severe hepatic injury, QT effects, embryo-fetal harm, or life-threatening interactions without a boxed warning.

Adverse Effects

  • Hepatotoxicity, nausea, rash, and interaction-mediated toxicity occur across several agents. Voriconazole adds visual, neuropsychiatric, photosensitivity, periostitis, and skin-cancer concerns with prolonged exposure.

  • Itraconazole can cause negative inotropy and edema; isavuconazole shortens QT; ketoconazole can suppress adrenal and gonadal steroid synthesis.

Contraindications

  • Do not memorize one interaction list for the entire class. Check the exact azole against the current medication list because direction, magnitude, and contraindicated partners differ.

  • Do not infer susceptibility from genus alone. Species, prior azole exposure, local resistance, and susceptibility testing can reverse the expected choice.

Nursing Essentials

  • Reconcile prescribed, over-the-counter, herbal, transplant, oncology, anticoagulant, antiarrhythmic, anticonvulsant, statin, and psychotropic medicines before the first dose and at every transition.

  • Assess baseline and serial liver tests, ECG/electrolytes when indicated, formulation administration, adherence, and drug concentrations for agents or infections where exposure is unpredictable and failure is costly.

Interactions

  • Rifampin, rifabutin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St. John's wort, and other inducers can reduce selected azoles. Calcineurin/mTOR inhibitors, warfarin, DOACs, statins, benzodiazepines, antiarrhythmics, and kinase inhibitors can rise with selected azoles.

  • Gastric pH and food strongly affect some formulations: itraconazole capsules need acid and food, its solution behaves differently, and posaconazole suspension is not interchangeable with delayed-release tablets.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, potassium, magnesium, creatinine, ECG when risk exists, organism susceptibility, and trough concentration for selected itraconazole, voriconazole, or posaconazole treatment.

  • A normal level does not overcome organism resistance or absent source control; an abnormal level should be interpreted with timing, formulation, adherence, interactions, and hepatic function.

Nclex Traps

  • Isavuconazole shortens QT; most clinically discussed azole electrophysiology concerns involve QT prolongation.

  • Posaconazole suspension and delayed-release tablets are not dose-for-dose interchangeable.

Source Note

Current U.S. fluconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, Cresemba, itraconazole, and ketoconazole labels plus IDSA aspergillosis guidance.
