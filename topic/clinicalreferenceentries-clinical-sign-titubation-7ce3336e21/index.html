Definition

Titubation is an observed clinical sign. It is a clue to midline cerebellar dysfunction. A positive finding means the head or trunk rocks rhythmically, usually at low frequency.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Titubation means the head or trunk rocks rhythmically, usually at low frequency.

Why It Matters

The observed Titubation finding can signal midline cerebellar dysfunction. Vermian or cerebellar control failure destabilizes axial posture. Titubation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • midline cerebellar dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe the seated or standing patient for rhythmic oscillation of the head or trunk.

Positive Finding

The head or trunk rocks rhythmically, usually at low frequency.

Mechanism

Vermian or cerebellar control failure destabilizes axial posture.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Titubation finding raises concern for midline cerebellar dysfunction because vermian or cerebellar control failure destabilizes axial posture. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Titubation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Titubation to focus evaluation of midline cerebellar dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: essential head tremor, dystonic tremor, vestibular sway. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the seated or standing patient for rhythmic oscillation of the head or trunk. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Titubation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Tremor phenomenology overlaps and the sign is not etiologically specific. Therefore, Titubation cannot confirm or exclude midline cerebellar dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • essential head tremor

  • dystonic tremor

  • vestibular sway

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as essential head tremor, dystonic tremor, vestibular sway.

False Negative Situations

Titubation may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of midline cerebellar dysfunction.
