Definition

Inferior wall MI is an inferior-wall myocardial infarction—cardiac ischemic necrosis caused by acute right-coronary or circumflex arterial occlusion; it can also injure the right ventricle or atrioventricular node. The characteristic pattern is ischemic chest discomfort or an equivalent with ST elevation in II, III, and aVF, reciprocal change, bradycardia, or hypotension. Confirm or stage it with immediate 12-lead ECG with serial tracings and right-sided leads when right-ventricular involvement is possible; distinguish it from pericarditis, early repolarization, pulmonary embolism, aortic catastrophe, and abdominal disease by reciprocal ECG change, troponin, imaging, and symptoms. The priority is to activate immediate reperfusion with percutaneous intervention or protocol-based fibrinolysis when appropriate because right-ventricular failure can complicate Inferior wall MI. can follow if the process advances.

Mechanistically, acute right-coronary or circumflex occlusion infarcts the inferior myocardium and may involve the right ventricle or atrioventricular node.

Signs Symptoms

  • Chest pain, dyspnea, syncope, palpitations, hypotension, weak/absent pulses, cool skin, altered mentation, or low urine output depending on the perfusion problem.

  • Worsening trend matters: new ECG change, rising troponin/BNP, escalating oxygen need, or falling blood pressure changes priority.

Diagnostics

  • immediate 12-lead ECG with serial tracings and right-sided leads when right-ventricular involvement is possible

  • serial cardiac troponin

  • urgent coronary angiography for reperfusion planning

  • Distinguish Inferior wall MI from pericarditis, early repolarization, pulmonary embolism, aortic catastrophe, and abdominal disease by reciprocal ECG change, troponin, imaging, and symptoms.

Labs

  • troponin may rise with myocardial injury

  • BNP may rise with heart failure strain

  • potassium and magnesium shifts increase dysrhythmia risk

  • creatinine matters before contrast and medication dosing

Treatments

  • activate immediate reperfusion with percutaneous intervention or protocol-based fibrinolysis when appropriate

  • give antiplatelet and anticoagulant therapy according to the reperfusion plan

  • support right-ventricular preload and bradyarrhythmia while avoiding preload-reducing drugs when hypotension is present

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for persistent ischemic pain, new heart block, severe bradycardia, hypotension, syncope, or ventricular arrhythmia because urgent pacing, reperfusion, or circulatory support may be required.

  • Obtain serial 12-lead electrocardiograms with right-sided leads when indicated because inferior infarction may involve the right ventricle and alter fluid and medication tolerance.

  • Administer antiplatelet and reperfusion therapies as ordered while checking bleeding risks because rapid coronary reperfusion limits myocardium lost to ischemia.

  • Monitor chest pain, blood pressure, heart rate, atrioventricular conduction, rhythm, and signs of right-sided failure to detect bradyarrhythmia or preload-sensitive shock.

  • Trend the disease-specific findings of ischemic chest discomfort or an equivalent with ST elevation in II, III, and aVF, reciprocal change, bradycardia, or hypotension and escalate immediately for right-ventricular failure can complicate Inferior wall MI. because acute right-coronary or circumflex occlusion infarcts the inferior myocardium and may involve the right ventricle or atrioventricular node.

Complications

  • right-ventricular failure can complicate Inferior wall MI.

  • high-grade heart block can complicate Inferior wall MI.

  • papillary-muscle rupture can complicate Inferior wall MI.

  • ventricular arrhythmia can complicate Inferior wall MI.

  • cardiogenic shock can complicate Inferior wall MI.

Red Flags

  • Persistent chest pain or evolving electrocardiographic changes

  • New high-grade atrioventricular block or severe bradycardia

  • Hypotension, syncope, or signs of right-ventricular failure

  • Ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation

Patient Education

  • Teach the patient to call emergency services for recurrent pressure, dyspnea, sweating, nausea, or faintness rather than driving to care.

  • Explain the purpose of antiplatelet therapy, cardiac rehabilitation, risk-factor control, and adherence after reperfusion.

Nclex Traps

  • Inferior infarction with hypotension requires right-sided leads before routine nitrates

  • Do not dismiss epigastric symptoms, especially with a diagnostic ECG
