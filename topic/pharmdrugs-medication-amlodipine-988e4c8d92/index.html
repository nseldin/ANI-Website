Mechanism

Amlodipine stabilizes inhibited states of Cav1.2 L-type channels in arterial smooth muscle. Less calcium enters during depolarization, calcium-calmodulin activation falls, myosin light-chain kinase phosphorylates less myosin, and arterioles relax. Lower systemic vascular resistance reduces blood pressure and left-ventricular afterload; coronary dilation and reduced oxygen demand support antianginal benefit. Because venules dilate less than arterioles, capillary hydrostatic pressure rises downstream, especially in dependent legs, and fluid filters into tissue. Slow absorption, high protein binding, extensive hepatic metabolism, and a 30- to 50-hour half-life explain gradual onset, once-daily dosing, delayed steady state, and prolonged toxicity. Amlodipine does not meaningfully slow the AV node at usual doses, so it cannot substitute for diltiazem or verapamil in rapid atrial fibrillation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Symptomatic hypotension can occur, particularly with severe aortic stenosis, although the gradual onset makes abrupt hypotension less likely during routine oral use. Starting or increasing amlodipine can worsen angina or precipitate myocardial infarction in severe obstructive coronary disease. Titrate slowly in severe hepatic impairment because clearance falls and half-life can reach about 56 hours. Edema is dose related: in the NORVASC trials it rose from 1.8% at 2.5 mg to 10.8% at 10 mg. Severe overdose can cause delayed, prolonged vasodilatory shock and pulmonary edema and requires emergency toxicology care.

Contraindications

  • Known serious hypersensitivity to amlodipine. Severe aortic stenosis, very low pressure, shock, or unstable coronary symptoms require careful clinician risk assessment.

  • Amlodipine is not contraindicated merely because edema occurs, but the dose, alternative causes, tolerability, and treatment goal must be reassessed rather than reflexively escalating a diuretic.

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure blood pressure and orthostatic symptoms, assess chest-pain pattern, examine edema symmetry and lung/JVP findings, and ask whether swelling began after initiation or dose escalation. Do not judge benefit or intolerance before accounting for its slow approach to steady state.

  • Teach once-daily adherence, not to double after a missed dose without instructions, oral/dental hygiene for gingival overgrowth, and prompt review for syncope, worsening chest pain, dyspnea, rapid weight gain, or asymmetric painful swelling.

Interactions

  • Moderate or strong CYP3A inhibitors can raise amlodipine exposure and increase hypotension/edema; monitor when inhibitors or inducers are started or stopped. Sildenafil and other vasodilators can add hypotension.

  • Amlodipine increases simvastatin exposure; current NORVASC labeling limits simvastatin to 20 mg daily. It can also increase cyclosporine or tacrolimus exposure, so transplant-drug troughs require monitoring.

Key Labs

  • Routine serum drug levels are not used. Monitor pressure, pulse, orthostasis, edema and respiratory findings; assess renal perfusion after large pressure changes and liver function when hepatic disease or injury is suspected.

  • When combined with simvastatin, assess muscle symptoms and CK if clinically indicated; with cyclosporine/tacrolimus, follow troughs and toxicity according to protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Amlodipine lowers afterload but is not the AV-node-slowing CCB. It does not replace diltiazem or verapamil for rate control.

  • Ankle edema often reflects precapillary dilation, not automatic heart failure. Evaluate the whole client before labeling it or treating it as volume overload.

Description

Amlodipine is a slow-onset, long-acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that relaxes arterioles, lowers systemic vascular resistance and afterload, and improves coronary blood flow without therapeutically suppressing the AV node. Its 30- to 50-hour terminal half-life produces smooth once-daily pressure control and limits abrupt reflex tachycardia, but it also means dose changes take roughly a week to reach steady state and adverse effects or overdose can persist for days. The signature adverse effect is dose-related ankle edema caused by precapillary arteriolar dilation rather than automatic total-body fluid overload. This explains why lungs may remain clear and why simply adding a diuretic may reduce plasma volume without fixing the capillary pressure imbalance.

Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and children 6 years and older under the label; chronic stable angina, vasospastic angina, and selected coronary artery disease outcomes. It is a maintenance drug, not a rapid rescue treatment for hypertensive emergency or an AV-node rate-control drug.

Adverse Effects

  • Dose-related peripheral edema, headache, flushing, dizziness, palpitations, fatigue, nausea, abdominal pain, somnolence, and gingival hyperplasia.

  • Severe hypotension, syncope, worsening angina/MI, hepatic injury, angioedema or severe hypersensitivity, and prolonged vasoplegic/cardiogenic shock in overdose.
