Class

Topical calcineurin-inhibitor T-cell immunomodulator for inflammatory dry eye

Used To Treat

Product-specific increase in tear production or treatment of dry-eye disease associated with ocular-surface inflammation; concentration and formulation differ across products.

Description

Cyclosporine ophthalmic is a local calcineurin inhibitor that reduces T-cell cytokine signaling on the ocular surface and lacrimal functional unit. By interrupting inflammatory damage, it can improve tear production and surface health over weeks to months. It is not an instant lubricant. Burning on instillation is common, and contact lenses must be removed; systemic cyclosporine toxicities are not expected at ordinary ocular exposure, but formulation-specific technique matters.

Mechanism

Cyclosporine binds cyclophilin, and the complex inhibits calcineurin phosphatase. NFAT remains phosphorylated outside the nucleus, reducing IL-2 and other T-cell activation signals. Less ocular-surface inflammation supports goblet-cell and lacrimal function. Slow tissue recovery explains delayed benefit and why early stinging does not prove treatment failure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: No boxed warning and negligible routine systemic blood concentrations, but hypersensitivity, contamination and ocular infection/injury still matter. Do not administer while wearing contact lenses. Significant pain, discharge, photophobia or vision loss is not routine dry-eye burning and requires evaluation.

Adverse Effects

  • Ocular burning/stinging, hyperemia, discharge, tearing, pain, foreign-body sensation, itching and transient blurred vision.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to cyclosporine or formulation components. Clarify active ocular infection, contact-lens use and whether another cause of dry eye requires treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact product concentration and preparation instructions; emulsions may require mixing as labeled. Teach that regular twice-daily or product-specific dosing over weeks to months is needed and lubricants should be spaced from it.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Topical anti-inflammatory agents or punctal plugs may alter observed tear-production response in product studies. Separate other drops. Routine systemic cyclosporine interaction monitoring is not required for ordinary ophthalmic exposure.

Key Labs

  • Ocular-surface examination, tear production/staining and visual acuity

  • Assessment for infection or corneal epithelial disease

  • No routine serum cyclosporine level for topical ocular therapy

Nclex Traps

  • Cyclosporine eye drops are delayed immune-modulating therapy, not immediate artificial tears.

  • Do not apply while contact lenses are in place.

  • Ocular dosing does not require transplant-style serum trough monitoring.
