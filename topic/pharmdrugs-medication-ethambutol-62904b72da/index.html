Class

First-line antitubercular medication

Used To Treat

Active tuberculosis as part of combination therapy while susceptibility is being clarified or when ordered.

Mechanism

Ethambutol inhibits mycobacterial arabinosyltransferases involved in arabinogalactan synthesis. Disrupting arabinogalactan impairs the mycolyl-arabinogalactan-peptidoglycan cell-wall complex, increasing wall permeability and limiting growth of actively multiplying TB bacilli.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess baseline and ongoing vision changes, especially blurred vision or trouble distinguishing red and green.

  • Teach the client to report visual changes immediately.

Contraindications

  • Clarify optic neuritis, inability to report visual symptoms, or severe prior hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Aluminum-containing antacids can reduce absorption if taken too close to ethambutol.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and periodic visual acuity/color discrimination assessment when ordered.

  • Renal function matters because dose adjustment may be needed.

Nclex Traps

  • Ethambutol equals eye safety in NCLEX thinking. Vision changes are not a minor side effect.

Description

Ethambutol is a first-line antitubercular arabinosyltransferase inhibitor that weakens mycobacterial arabinogalactan cell-wall construction in active TB regimens; its signature toxicity is optic neuritis with decreased visual acuity or red-green color discrimination. It helps protect RIPE therapy while susceptibility is clarified, but vision changes are a stop-and-escalate clue.
