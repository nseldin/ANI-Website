Used To Treat

Time-sensitive decision support after meaningful varicella or disseminated-zoster exposure in a person with uncertain or absent immunity.

Description

The varicella postexposure prophylaxis decision pathway helps protect susceptible people after meaningful exposure by matching evidence of immunity, live-vaccine safety, and severe-disease risk to active vaccination or passive VARIZIG. An immune person usually needs no postexposure product. A susceptible vaccine-eligible person should receive varicella vaccine as soon as possible, ideally within 3 to 5 days, because active immunity may prevent or reduce disease. A susceptible high-risk person for whom live vaccine is contraindicated should receive VARIZIG as soon as possible, ideally within 96 hours and through day 10. The pathway is not treatment of established varicella or shingles.

Mechanism

Postexposure vaccine stimulates active adaptive immunity before viral dissemination, while VARIZIG supplies immediate passive anti-VZV antibody when the patient cannot safely wait for or receive live vaccine. Passive antibody wanes and can lengthen incubation, so surveillance after VARIZIG extends through day 28. Exposure to localized shingles usually requires direct contact with uncovered lesions, whereas airborne exposure matters for varicella or disseminated zoster; clarifying exposure prevents both missed prophylaxis and unnecessary product use.

Administration Timing

  • Immediately document exposure type, date, source disease, immunity evidence, pregnancy, immune status, age, and vaccine eligibility because both vaccine and VARIZIG are time sensitive.

  • Give varicella vaccine as soon as possible, ideally within 3-5 days, to a susceptible eligible person; give VARIZIG ideally within 96 hours and through day 10 to a susceptible high-risk person with a live-vaccine contraindication.

  • Monitor or exclude exposed susceptible personnel according to CDC timing, extending surveillance through days 8-28 after exposure when VARIZIG was given.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify documented vaccine doses, laboratory immunity, or provider-verified disease history rather than relying on a vague recollection because unnecessary immune globulin is scarce and missed susceptibility is dangerous.

  • Coordinate infection prevention, obstetrics, pediatrics, or immunology because exposure definitions and severe-risk eligibility differ by setting.

  • Teach immediate reporting of fever or rash and avoidance of high-risk contacts because prophylaxis can fail and contagiousness begins before the rash is obvious.

Key Labs

  • Rapid VZV IgG can clarify immunity when results will return without delaying prophylaxis; PCR of lesions diagnoses active disease but is not a reason to postpone a time-sensitive exposed-person decision.

Adverse Effects

  • Vaccine can cause injection-site reactions, fever, or rash and is live; VARIZIG can cause injection pain, hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, or thrombosis.

Contraindications

  • Live varicella vaccine is contraindicated in pregnancy and selected severe immunocompromise because its live attenuated virus creates a theoretical fetal risk or a risk of vaccine-virus disease in a severely weakened host; VARIZIG is contraindicated after a severe systemic reaction to human immune globulin because re-exposure can provoke another serious hypersensitivity reaction.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Fever, rash, respiratory symptoms, neurologic change, neonatal exposure, or breakthrough disease in an immunocompromised patient requires immediate isolation, diagnostic testing, and active-treatment consultation.

Interactions

  • Recent immune globulin can blunt live-vaccine response and requires current spacing guidance.

  • Antivirals around live varicella vaccination may reduce vaccine-virus replication, while immunosuppressants determine vaccine eligibility and disease severity.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Live vaccine is contraindicated because its live attenuated virus creates a theoretical fetal risk; a susceptible patient with meaningful exposure should receive rapid obstetric assessment for VARIZIG eligibility.

  • Population

    Immunocompromised patients

    Note

    Severe disseminated disease can occur despite prophylaxis, so VARIZIG, extended monitoring, isolation, and early antiviral planning are important.

  • Population

    Newborns and premature infants

    Note

    Maternal rash timing, gestational age, birth weight, and inpatient status determine eligibility and require neonatal expertise.

Evidence Limitations

  • Exposure definitions and immunity evidence depend on setting and host factors; postexposure products reduce risk but do not guarantee prevention.

Nclex Traps

  • Varicella vaccine and VARIZIG are selected by immunity, risk, and contraindication rather than given together to everyone.

  • VARIZIG surveillance extends to day 28 because passive antibody can lengthen the incubation period.

  • Shingles vaccine and treatment of active shingles are different questions.

Regulatory Status

This is a CDC-aligned educational pathway using FDA-licensed varicella vaccine and VARIZIG; exact eligibility belongs to current public-health and specialist guidance.

Boxed Warning

VARICELLA EXPOSURE WARNING: pregnancy, immunocompromise, or neonatal exposure can become time critical. Determine immunity and vaccine eligibility now; use vaccine within 3-5 days when eligible or VARIZIG as soon as possible through day 10 when high risk and vaccine is contraindicated.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning names the urgent hazard, explains why it matters, and states the action that prevents delay or medication error.
