Used To Treat

Hypocalcemia and calcium replacement; myocardial membrane stabilization in dangerous hyperkalemia; magnesium-toxicity reversal; antacid or supplementation use; and intestinal phosphate binding depending on the exact salt, dose, and route.

Description

Calcium salt drugs deliver calcium ions or exploit the salt's GI chemistry for distinct purposes: IV chloride/gluconate rapidly stabilize excitable cardiac and neuromuscular membranes, oral citrate/carbonate replace calcium, and acetate/carbonate can bind intestinal phosphate. The salt and route are not interchangeable because elemental-calcium content, tissue injury, acid dependence, sodium/phosphate compatibility, and clinical urgency differ.

Mechanism

Dissociated calcium ions support cardiac conduction, excitation-contraction coupling, neurotransmission, coagulation, and bone mineralization. Raising extracellular calcium restores the cardiac membrane threshold-potential gap that protects the myocardium during severe hyperkalemia without removing potassium; in the gut, selected calcium salts either supply absorbable calcium or form poorly absorbed phosphate complexes that leave in stool.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify the exact salt, concentration, route, elemental-calcium dose, indication, IV access, infusion rate, and compatibility before administration; calcium chloride is more calcium-dense and caustic than calcium gluconate.

  • Trend ECG and symptoms in emergencies, ionized/serum calcium, potassium, magnesium, phosphate, renal function, IV site, bowel effects, kidney-stone risk, and separation from tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, levothyroxine, iron, and bisphosphonates.

Key Labs

  • Ionized or corrected calcium, potassium, magnesium, phosphate, creatinine/eGFR, ECG when acute instability exists, and clinical response.

Nclex Traps

  • IV calcium in hyperkalemia stabilizes the cardiac membrane but does not lower serum potassium; shifting and elimination therapy must still occur.

  • Calcium chloride and calcium gluconate are not milligram-for-milligram substitutes, and calcium acetate is primarily a phosphate binder rather than generic calcium replacement.
