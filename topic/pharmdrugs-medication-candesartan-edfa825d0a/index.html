Class

Cilexetil ester-prodrug ARB converted to selective active candesartan

Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric ages and treatment of NYHA class II-IV heart failure with left-ventricular systolic dysfunction to reduce cardiovascular death and heart-failure hospitalization according to labeling.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Candesartan cilexetil is contraindicated in pregnancy. The same developmental RAAS dependence explains why use below one year of age is unsafe.

Mechanism

Candesartan cilexetil is rapidly hydrolyzed at its ester linkage during absorption to active candesartan. Candesartan selectively and tightly blocks AT1 receptors, interrupting angiotensin-II-driven vascular calcium signaling, aldosterone release, sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, and cardiac remodeling. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers intraglomerular pressure and can raise creatinine during poor inflow; reduced aldosterone raises potassium. It does not inhibit ACE or directly increase bradykinin, so cough is less characteristic. In HFrEF, lower afterload and less neurohormonal remodeling reduce recurrent decompensation and cardiovascular events over time. The mechanism does not produce an immediate diuretic bolus, so symptom benefit and outcome benefit should not be confused.

Nursing Essentials

  • For heart failure, begin from the prescribed low dose and titrate at the planned interval while trending pressure, potassium, renal function, volume, and symptoms.

  • Verify that the product is candesartan cilexetil, the prodrug; the body converts it to candesartan during absorption.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, aliskiren coadministration in diabetes, serious hypersensitivity, and use for hypertension in children younger than one year.

  • Clarify severe pressure reduction, potassium elevation, AKI, or bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Interactions

  • Potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class; heart-failure MRA use adds deliberate potassium surveillance.

  • Do not interpret old evidence for ACE inhibitor plus candesartan as routine permission for modern duplicate RAAS blockade; current guideline therapy and patient context govern.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, urine output, weight/congestion, and ventricular-function context.

  • Pediatric monitoring requires age-appropriate pressure, renal, electrolyte, and growth assessment.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Candesartan cilexetil is the prodrug; candesartan is active. The suffix cilexetil signals an absorption-enabling ester.

  • Its heart-failure benefit is reduction of death and hospitalization over time, not rapid removal of pulmonary edema.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Candesartan is the active AT1 blocker produced when the oral prodrug candesartan cilexetil is hydrolyzed during gastrointestinal absorption. It blocks angiotensin-II vasoconstriction and aldosterone signaling without directly inhibiting bradykinin metabolism. Its defining evidence distinction is a labeled heart-failure outcome role: in adults with NYHA class II-IV systolic dysfunction, it reduces cardiovascular death and heart-failure hospitalization. The heart-failure target dose is approached gradually because pressure, potassium, and renal tolerance must be established; doubling a dose before the full hemodynamic response is known can produce hypotension or kidney injury. Pediatric hypertension labeling begins at age one, while infants under one year should not receive it because RAAS blockade can disrupt kidney development. The prodrug formulation and outcome indication should both appear immediately on the card.

Adverse Effects

  • In heart failure, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal-function change occur more often than in uncomplicated hypertension because perfusion reserve and interacting therapy differ.

  • Dizziness, fatigue, and back pain can occur; severe renal failure or potassium dysrhythmia is the high-risk extension.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
