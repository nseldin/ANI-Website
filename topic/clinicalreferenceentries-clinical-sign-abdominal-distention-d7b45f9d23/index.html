Definition

Abdominal distention is an observed increase in abdominal size or fullness. The abdomen may look enlarged, rounded, taut, or asymmetric because of gas, fluid, stool, an enlarged organ, a mass, pregnancy, or body habitus.

Plain Language

Abdominal distention means the belly looks larger or more swollen than expected. It is not the same as bloating, which is the person's feeling of fullness and may occur without visible enlargement.

Why It Matters

New or rapidly worsening distention can signal bowel obstruction, ileus, ascites, urinary retention, bleeding, or peritonitis. Severe pain, persistent vomiting, inability to pass stool or gas, guarding, rigidity, bleeding, fainting, or unstable vital signs requires urgent evaluation.

Assessment Method

With the patient supine and appropriately exposed, inspect the contour and symmetry from the foot and side of the bed, compare with baseline, measure girth consistently when trending is ordered, auscultate before palpation, and assess pain, vomiting, bowel movements, flatus, urine output, weight change, vital signs, and pregnancy possibility.

Positive Finding

The abdomen is visibly fuller, enlarged, protuberant, taut, or asymmetric compared with the patient's baseline.

Mechanism

Gas, fluid, stool, an enlarged organ, a mass, pregnancy, or increased tissue volume pushes the abdominal wall outward; the time course and associated findings help distinguish the cause.

Evidence Limitations

Baseline body habitus, pregnancy, positioning, respiration, recent meals, and inconsistent girth landmarks affect interpretation; visible distention does not reveal whether the contents are gas, fluid, stool, tissue, or an enlarged organ. Therefore, Abdominal distention cannot confirm or exclude increased abdominal volume from gas, fluid, stool, an enlarged organ, a mass, pregnancy, or body habitus; an acute painful pattern can accompany obstruction, ileus, ascites, urinary retention, hemorrhage, or peritonitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as obesity, pregnancy, abdominal wall hernia, organ enlargement.

False Negative Situations

Abdominal distention may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of increased abdominal volume from gas, fluid, stool, an enlarged organ, a mass, pregnancy, or body habitus; an acute painful pattern can accompany obstruction, ileus, ascites, urinary retention, hemorrhage, or peritonitis.
