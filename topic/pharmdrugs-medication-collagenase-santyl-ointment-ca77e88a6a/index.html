Class

Topical enzymatic debriding agent

Description

Collagenase Santyl is a topical enzyme used to remove collagen that anchors necrotic tissue to a wound bed. It is not the injectable collagenase product used for Dupuytren contracture or Peyronie disease. Enzymatic debridement is useful when selective, gradual removal is preferred, but it does not replace assessment of perfusion, pressure, infection, moisture balance, nutrition, or the need for urgent surgical debridement.

Mechanism

Collagenase derived from Clostridium histolyticum cleaves native collagen in necrotic tissue. Eschar and devitalized tissue remain attached partly through collagen fibers; breaking those fibers loosens the nonviable material so it can separate with cleansing and dressing changes. The enzyme requires a compatible moist wound environment. Heavy metals and some antiseptics can inactivate it, which explains why product compatibility matters.

Used To Treat

Debridement of chronic dermal ulcers and severely burned areas when enzymatic debridement is clinically appropriate.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. The major safety issue is delaying source control or surgical evaluation in an infected, ischemic, or rapidly worsening wound while relying on gradual enzymatic debridement.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to collagenase or a product component.

  • Do not apply as though it treats infection, restores perfusion, or replaces urgent sharp or surgical debridement when necrosis, sepsis, compartment threat, or deep rapidly spreading infection requires immediate action.

Adverse Effects

  • Transient erythema, irritation, pain, or maceration around the wound when ointment contacts healthy skin.

  • Rare systemic hypersensitivity; worsening infection reflects inadequate wound control rather than an expected therapeutic effect.

Interactions

  • Silver, mercury, and other heavy-metal ions in some antiseptics can reduce enzyme activity. Strongly acidic or alkaline products and incompatible cleansers can also inactivate collagenase.

  • If an incompatible antiseptic was used, cleanse the wound thoroughly with a compatible solution before application.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the wound type, perfusion, infection status, depth, tunneling, drainage, odor, pain, pressure cause, and debridement goal before treatment.

  • Apply only to necrotic tissue, protect periwound skin, use the ordered dressing, and document wound dimensions and tissue composition so lack of progress is recognized.

  • Escalate spreading erythema, crepitus, fever, disproportionate pain, exposed deep structures, ischemia, or systemic illness rather than continuing routine dressing care.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level is used

  • Culture only when clinical infection warrants it; a surface swab does not substitute for assessment

  • Glucose, nutrition, perfusion, inflammatory markers, and imaging are selected from the wound context

Administration

  • Cleanse the wound, remove loose material, and apply a thin layer once daily or as ordered. A clinician may crosshatch thick eschar when appropriate.

  • Stop when debridement is complete and healthy granulation tissue is established, or sooner if the plan changes because of infection, surgery, or intolerance.

Patient Education

  • Explain that the ointment removes dead tissue gradually; it is not an antibiotic and cannot fix pressure or poor blood flow.

  • Keep pressure-relief, nutrition, glucose, smoking-cessation, and dressing instructions because those factors determine whether the cleaned wound can heal.

Nclex Traps

  • Santyl is selective enzymatic debridement, not an antibiotic, perfusion treatment, or substitute for urgent surgical source control.

  • Silver and other heavy-metal antiseptics can inactivate collagenase; compatibility belongs in the dressing-plan check.

Routes

  • Topical application to the wound bed only; this product is not injectable.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Serious hypersensitivity is rare.

  • Progressive wound infection, ischemia, or sepsis is a clinical emergency that may be masked or delayed if gradual enzymatic debridement is continued without reassessment.

Toxicity Management

  • Remove excess ointment and cleanse the area with a compatible solution if accidental exposure or severe irritation occurs. Treat hypersensitivity supportively and escalate infected, ischemic, or rapidly worsening wounds for source control rather than adding more enzyme.

Special Populations

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established in the label.

  • Pregnancy, lactation, renal disease, and hepatic disease do not have route-specific dosing data; systemic absorption is expected to be limited, but wound severity and the need for definitive debridement still drive care.

  • Older adults often have perfusion, nutrition, diabetes, pressure, or vascular factors that must be corrected for debridement to lead to healing.
