Used To Treat

Serious infections caused by susceptible penicillinase-producing staphylococci, particularly MSSA bacteremia, endocarditis, bone/joint, skin/soft-tissue, and other deep infection when culture and site support it.

Mechanism

A bulky isoxazolyl ring sterically shields the beta-lactam core from many staphylococcal beta-lactamases while preserving PBP binding. MRSA changes the target rather than merely increasing penicillinase: PBP2a's closed active site has low affinity for oxacillin. This explains why beta-lactamase inhibitors do not restore oxacillin against MRSA and why ceftaroline, which can allosterically open PBP2a, is a special exception. Oxacillin undergoes renal and nonrenal handling, so organ trends and high-dose exposure both matter.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe allergy, hepatotoxicity, neutropenia, interstitial nephritis, hypokalemia, and sodium/volume load can develop; prolonged therapy requires scheduled surveillance rather than symptom-only monitoring.

Contraindications

  • Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity requires avoidance or specialist-directed strategy.

  • Do not interpret an oxacillin-resistant S. aureus report as a request to add a beta-lactamase inhibitor; it usually signals PBP2a-mediated MRSA.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use culture-directed oxacillin as definitive therapy and repeat blood cultures in invasive S. aureus because clearance is a key source-control signal.

  • Schedule CBC, liver, renal, potassium, and sodium/fluid review during prolonged therapy because toxicity can be clinically silent before the lab trend appears.

Interactions

  • Oxacillin can alter warfarin response through illness and enzyme effects; trend INR rather than assuming one direction in every patient.

  • Separate aminoglycoside preparation unless compatibility is established because beta-lactam contact can reduce aminoglycoside activity.

Key Labs

  • Culture and oxacillin/methicillin susceptibility, serial blood cultures, CBC, AST/ALT/bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, and sodium/fluid status.

  • mecA or PBP2a testing clarifies target resistance; beta-lactamase testing alone does not define MRSA.

Nclex Traps

  • Oxacillin susceptibility is a marker for MSSA beta-lactam susceptibility; oxacillin resistance usually means MRSA.

  • Penicillinase resistance protects the ring but does not overcome PBP2a.

Description

Oxacillin is a narrow IV, penicillinase-resistant isoxazolyl beta-lactam antibiotic that binds native PBPs in susceptible MSSA, blocks transpeptidation, and causes bactericidal cell-wall failure. Its bulky side chain resists hydrolysis by staphylococcal penicillinase, but it does not overcome the low-affinity PBP2a produced by mecA/mecC. The laboratory term 'oxacillin resistant' is therefore used as a surrogate for PBP2a-mediated methicillin resistance, which predicts resistance to most beta-lactams even if oxacillin itself was not planned. Oxacillin has little reason to remain after cultures identify a nonstaphylococcal target. Hepatic injury, neutropenia, interstitial nephritis, hypokalemia, and sodium exposure become especially important during prolonged high-dose therapy.

Adverse Effects

  • Rash, urticaria, anaphylaxis, diarrhea/CDI, phlebitis, fever, transaminase elevation or hepatitis, and GI symptoms.

  • Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, interstitial nephritis, creatinine rise, hypokalemia, and rare neurotoxicity at excessive exposure.
