Used To Treat

Multiple myeloma and selected hematologic or oncologic indications; individual agents also have specific uses such as del(5q) myelodysplastic syndrome, lymphomas, or Kaposi sarcoma.

Description

Immunomodulatory imide drugs bind cereblon, retargeting a cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase to destroy transcription factors such as Ikaros and Aiolos. This is targeted protein degradation, not vague immune stimulation: it directly alters myeloma-cell survival while increasing T-cell/NK-cell activity and reducing proinflammatory signaling. Severe teratogenicity and venous/arterial thrombosis are defining class hazards.

Mechanism

Drug-bound cereblon changes substrate recognition by the CRL4-CRBN ubiquitin ligase. Ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of Ikaros/Aiolos and related substrates changes gene transcription, suppresses malignant plasma-cell growth, enhances IL-2-dependent T/NK activity, and contributes antiangiogenic effects.

Boxed Warning

Embryo-fetal toxicity is profound because these are thalidomide analogues; restricted REMS, pregnancy testing, contraception, semen/blood-donation rules, and dispensing controls are mandatory. Lenalidomide also carries boxed hematologic toxicity; lenalidomide and pomalidomide carry boxed venous and arterial thromboembolism warnings with risk-based prophylaxis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify REMS authorization and reproductive-risk requirements before every dispense; never open or handle damaged capsules casually.

  • Trend CBC, renal/hepatic function, thrombosis and bleeding risk, neuropathy, rash/DRESS/SJS-TEN, infection, and the ordered antithrombotic plan.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing per REMS, CBC with differential/platelets, creatinine/eGFR for lenalidomide dosing, liver tests, and clinical thrombosis assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • REMS precautions apply to males as well as females because drug can be present in semen and fetal exposure must be prevented.

  • Antithrombotic prophylaxis reduces but does not eliminate DVT, PE, MI, or stroke risk.
