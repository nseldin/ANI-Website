Class

Oral L-valyl prodrug of ganciclovir for CMV DNA-polymerase inhibition

Used To Treat

Induction and maintenance treatment of CMV retinitis in selected adults and prevention of CMV disease in specified high-risk transplant populations, including protocol-specific pediatric recipients. It is not a milligram-for-milligram substitute for oral ganciclovir or IV ganciclovir.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: severe leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, bone-marrow failure, and aplastic anemia can occur. The drug also carries warnings for impaired fertility, fetal toxicity, mutagenicity, and carcinogenicity. Avoid crushing or breaking tablets because the contents are hazardous. Use effective contraception for label-specified intervals and monitor CBC and renal function repeatedly rather than only at baseline.

Mechanism

Valganciclovir is absorbed through peptide transport and hydrolyzed to ganciclovir. UL97 phosphotransferase adds the first phosphate; host kinases create ganciclovir triphosphate. The active metabolite competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate at CMV UL54 DNA polymerase, is incorporated into viral DNA, and slows or terminates elongation. UL97 mutation reduces activation and often creates resistance while leaving foscarnet susceptibility; UL54 mutation can affect ganciclovir and other polymerase inhibitors. Ganciclovir is cleared primarily by the kidney, so valganciclovir dose tables use creatinine clearance, not a one-size schedule. The prodrug's higher bioavailability is why 900 mg valganciclovir cannot be equated to 900 mg old oral ganciclovir.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify current weight, creatinine clearance, CBC, dose, formulation, induction versus maintenance phase, and transplant protocol at each transition of care.

  • Use hazardous-drug precautions. Caregivers should avoid skin or mucous-membrane contact with broken tablets or reconstituted solution and follow spill instructions.

Contraindications

  • Clinically significant hypersensitivity to valganciclovir, ganciclovir, or a component.

  • Do not use tablet strength when the calculated pediatric or renal dose requires oral solution; do not substitute products on a milligram-for-milligram basis.

Interactions

  • Mycophenolate, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, zidovudine, chemotherapy, and other marrow suppressants add cytopenia risk.

  • Nephrotoxins raise ganciclovir exposure; imipenem-cilastatin can add seizure risk. Probenecid and tubular-secretion competition may alter clearance.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and platelets, creatinine/eGFR or creatinine clearance, liver tests, pregnancy assessment, and protocol-specific CMV PCR

  • Vision and retinal findings for retinitis, infection/bleeding symptoms, graft function, neurologic status, adherence, oral absorption, and resistance concern

Nclex Traps

  • Valganciclovir is better absorbed oral ganciclovir, not a less myelosuppressive drug.

  • Renal dose adjustment affects both efficacy and marrow safety; an unchanged dose can become toxic when kidney function falls.

Description

Valganciclovir is an oral L-valyl ganciclovir prodrug that esterases rapidly convert to ganciclovir; CMV UL97 and host kinases then form a triphosphate that inhibits UL54 DNA polymerase and suppresses viral DNA elongation. This prodrug design produces far greater oral bioavailability than old oral ganciclovir capsules and permits effective outpatient CMV exposure. The delivery advantage does not remove ganciclovir's marrow, reproductive, mutagenic, fetal, or renal toxicity. Tablets and oral solution require dose precision, and renal function can change quickly after transplant. A patient who becomes neutropenic or develops acute kidney injury may move from adequate antiviral exposure to dangerous accumulation without a change in prescribed tablets.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, fever, tremor, headache, insomnia, and transplant-associated graft or infection complications

  • Neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, marrow failure, renal accumulation, seizures, severe skin reaction, fertility impairment, and fetal harm
