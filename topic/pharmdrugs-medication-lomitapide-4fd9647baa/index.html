Class

Oral microsomal triglyceride transfer-protein inhibitor for homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia

Used To Treat

Adjunct to a low-fat diet, exercise, and other LDL-lowering therapies to reduce LDL-C in adult and pediatric patients age 2 years and older with clinically or laboratory diagnosed HoFH under the current 2026 U.S. label. It is not established for ordinary hypercholesterolemia.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - RISK OF HEPATOTOXICITY: Lomitapide can cause aminotransferase elevation and hepatic steatosis. Steatosis may progress to steatohepatitis and cirrhosis. Measure liver tests before treatment, before each dose increase, and regularly thereafter; reduce or stop for significant abnormalities. Because of this risk, JUXTAPID is available only through a restricted REMS and should be prescribed only for confirmed HoFH.

Mechanism

MTP transfers triglyceride, cholesterol ester, and phospholipid to nascent ApoB in the endoplasmic reticulum. In enterocytes this creates chylomicrons; in hepatocytes it creates VLDL. Lomitapide binds and inhibits MTP, so ApoB cannot acquire enough lipid, is targeted for proteasomal degradation, and fewer particles are secreted. Less VLDL means less IDL and LDL downstream, bypassing the need for LDL-receptor clearance. Triglyceride that cannot leave accumulates in liver, directly explaining steatosis and possible steatohepatitis or cirrhosis. Reduced chylomicron formation leaves dietary fat in the gut, producing dose- and fat-dependent GI symptoms and reducing vitamins A, D, E, K and essential fatty acids. CYP3A4 extensively metabolizes lomitapide, so inhibitors can sharply increase exposure and liver toxicity; the drug also increases simvastatin, lovastatin, and warfarin exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm HoFH diagnosis, REMS enrollment, baseline liver testing and hepatic assessment, pregnancy test and contraception, low-fat diet below 20 percent of energy, vitamin E and essential-fatty-acid supplementation, and complete CYP3A, statin, and warfarin review.

  • Give once daily with water at least two hours after the evening meal; swallow capsules whole. Food, especially fat, worsens GI effects, and taking it away from the meal reduces the luminal fat-load interaction.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, moderate or severe hepatic impairment, active liver disease or unexplained persistent abnormal liver tests, and concurrent strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors are major contraindications.

  • Safety and effectiveness are not established for hypercholesterolemia without HoFH. Do not use as a general escalation drug for common LDL elevation.

Interactions

  • Strong and moderate CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated; weak inhibitors require dose restriction and careful timing or monitoring. Avoid grapefruit. CYP3A inducers can reduce efficacy.

  • Lomitapide raises simvastatin and lovastatin exposure, requiring dose limits. It can raise warfarin exposure, so INR must be checked after initiation and dose changes. Separate other oral medicines when severe vomiting or diarrhea threatens absorption.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin before treatment, before dose increases, and regularly; lipid panel and ApoB; hepatic fat assessment as directed; INR with warfarin; vitamins A/D/E/K, essential fatty acids, nutrition, weight, and pregnancy testing.

  • Symptoms and bilirubin or INR matter alongside aminotransferases because impaired hepatic function is more serious than isolated enzyme leakage.

Nclex Traps

  • Lomitapide lowers LDL without requiring LDL receptors by preventing VLDL particle assembly. It is not a statin or PCSK9 inhibitor.

  • Hepatic fat is not an unrelated side effect; it is the direct consequence of blocking triglyceride export from hepatocytes.

Description

Lomitapide, branded Juxtapid, is an oral microsomal triglyceride transfer-protein (MTP) inhibitor reserved for homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. MTP normally loads triglyceride onto nascent ApoB so enterocytes can secrete chylomicrons and hepatocytes can secrete VLDL; blocking it causes poorly lipidated ApoB to be degraded, reducing VLDL production and downstream LDL even when LDL receptors barely function. That same mechanism traps triglyceride inside hepatocytes and impairs intestinal fat transport, causing hepatic steatosis, aminotransferase elevation, diarrhea, nausea, weight loss, and fat-soluble-vitamin or essential-fatty-acid deficiency. It carries a boxed hepatotoxicity warning and is available only through JUXTAPID REMS. The current 2026 label includes patients age 2 years and older with HoFH. A low-fat diet, daily vitamin E and essential fatty-acid supplementation, liver monitoring, pregnancy prevention, and extensive CYP3A interaction management are core treatment, not optional advice.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain or distension, constipation, flatulence, weight loss, fatigue, headache, and reduced fat-soluble nutrient absorption are common or important.

  • Hepatotoxicity, marked hepatic steatosis, severe dehydration from GI loss, increased INR or bleeding with warfarin, myopathy through statin interactions, and embryo-fetal harm are serious risks.
