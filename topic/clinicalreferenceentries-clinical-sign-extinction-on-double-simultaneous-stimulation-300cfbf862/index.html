Definition

Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation is a bedside cortical sensory sign. It is a clue to contralateral parietal attention dysfunction. A positive finding means both single stimuli are felt but the contralesional one is missed during simultaneous stimulation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation means both single stimuli are felt but the contralesional one is missed during simultaneous stimulation.

Why It Matters

The observed Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation finding can signal contralateral parietal attention dysfunction. Competing bilateral input overwhelms impaired hemispatial attention networks. Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • contralateral parietal attention dysfunction

Assessment Method

Touch matching sites on each side separately and then simultaneously with eyes closed.

Positive Finding

Both single stimuli are felt but the contralesional one is missed during simultaneous stimulation.

Mechanism

Competing bilateral input overwhelms impaired hemispatial attention networks.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation finding raises concern for contralateral parietal attention dysfunction because competing bilateral input overwhelms impaired hemispatial attention networks. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation to focus evaluation of contralateral parietal attention dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: hemianesthesia, delirium, visual or auditory neglect. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Touch matching sites on each side separately and then simultaneously with eyes closed. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Primary sensory loss and inattention invalidate the comparison. Therefore, Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation cannot confirm or exclude contralateral parietal attention dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hemianesthesia

  • delirium

  • visual or auditory neglect

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hemianesthesia, delirium, visual or auditory neglect.

False Negative Situations

Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of contralateral parietal attention dysfunction.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Prove each side is sensed alone before calling simultaneous omission extinction.
