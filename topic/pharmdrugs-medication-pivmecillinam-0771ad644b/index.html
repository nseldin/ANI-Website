Class

Oral pivaloyloxymethyl prodrug of the PBP2-selective amidinopenicillin mecillinam

Used To Treat

Adult women with uncomplicated urinary tract infection caused by susceptible Escherichia coli, Proteus mirabilis, or Staphylococcus saprophyticus under the U.S. Pivya label. It is not a general oral drug for pyelonephritis, bacteremia, complicated UTI, or every urinary organism.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Pivmecillinam is contraindicated in patients with primary or secondary carnitine deficiency disorders and porphyria. Severe beta-lactam hypersensitivity can occur.

Mechanism

Intestinal absorption is followed by esterase cleavage of the pivoxil ester, releasing active mecillinam and pivalate. Mecillinam crosses the Gram-negative outer membrane and preferentially acylates PBP2 rather than the broader PBP pattern of many penicillins. This target profile explains strong urinary Enterobacterales activity and synergy patterns with other beta-lactams, but also limited relevance for many Gram-positive organisms. Pivalate is conjugated with carnitine for excretion; repeated or prolonged pivalate-containing drug exposure can lower carnitine, which is why inherited carnitine-transport/metabolism disorders and long courses matter even though the labeled uUTI course is short.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm uncomplicated lower UTI rather than pyelonephritis by assessing fever, flank pain, vomiting, sepsis, pregnancy context, obstruction risk, and culture because tissue-invasive disease needs a different exposure strategy.

  • Review pivalate-containing medicines and carnitine disorders, then teach completion and escalation for fever, flank pain, worsening symptoms, or no improvement because those findings question the diagnosis or susceptibility.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to pivmecillinam, mecillinam, penicillins, cephalosporins, or other beta-lactams requires avoidance under the label.

  • Primary or secondary carnitine deficiency, porphyria, and conditions that impair swallowing or GI passage are important labeled barriers because the prodrug/carnitine pathway and tablet delivery create specific harm.

Interactions

  • Valproate and other pivalate-containing drugs can compound carnitine depletion; review the complete medication list rather than only antibiotics.

  • Probenecid can reduce renal beta-lactam secretion and alter exposure, though routine coadministration is not the treatment goal.

Key Labs

  • Urine culture and susceptibility when indicated, pregnancy testing when relevant, renal function for overall assessment, and carnitine only when clinical context or prolonged/repeated pivalate exposure makes depletion plausible.

  • Routine drug levels are not used; persistent symptoms require culture, adherence, anatomy, tissue invasion, and alternative diagnosis review.

Nclex Traps

  • Pivmecillinam is the oral prodrug; mecillinam is the active PBP2-binding antibacterial.

  • An uncomplicated-cystitis indication does not make it a pyelonephritis or sepsis drug.

Description

Pivmecillinam, branded Pivya, is an oral beta-lactam antibiotic prodrug for susceptible uncomplicated cystitis: esterases convert it to mecillinam, which preferentially binds PBP2 in Gram-negative bacilli and blocks lateral peptidoglycan wall growth. Because PBP2 maintains rod shape, its inhibition converts susceptible bacteria toward spherical forms, destabilizes the wall, and leads to death. The pivaloyloxymethyl ester improves oral absorption, while high urinary mecillinam exposure creates the focused lower-UTI role. U.S. approval is for adult women with uUTI caused by designated susceptible organisms, so a convenient oral tablet should not be extrapolated to febrile pyelonephritis, obstruction, male complicated infection, or bloodstream disease without evidence.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, vulvovaginal candidiasis, headache, and rash are common important effects.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, CDI, liver injury, cytopenias, and carnitine depletion with repeated/prolonged pivalate exposure are uncommon but important.
