Used To Treat

Short-term inpatient reduction of severe hypertension when rapid, titratable arterial vasodilation is needed.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for fenoldopam in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Selective peripheral D1-like receptor stimulation activates adenylyl cyclase and raises cAMP in arteriolar smooth muscle, producing vasodilation and increased renal blood flow/natriuresis. Baroreflex sympathetic activation can increase heart rate as pressure falls.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use continuous BP and rhythm monitoring during titration; assess rate of pressure reduction, perfusion, urine output, chest pain, headache, flushing, and reflex tachycardia.

  • Trend potassium and use caution with glaucoma or raised intraocular pressure; protect the solution from inappropriate preparation or line errors.

Key Labs

  • Frequent blood pressure, ECG/heart rate, potassium, renal function, urine output, and perfusion/neurologic status.

Nclex Traps

  • Fenoldopam is D1 vasodilation, not a renal-dose dopamine infusion. A falling BP with rising heart rate fits reflex tachycardia.

Description

Fenoldopam is a rapid IV D1-receptor agonist that lowers blood pressure by dilating systemic and renal arteries. Unlike dopamine, it does not rely on alpha or beta stimulation; the price of fast afterload reduction is reflex tachycardia, headache/flushing, hypotension, hypokalemia, and increased intraocular pressure. Why is fenoldopam short-term and limited to an in-hospital infusion of up to 48 hours? It is a rapidly titratable bridge for severe hypertension, not a chronic outpatient blood-pressure regimen. Its minutes-long half-life makes pressure effects quickly reversible when the infusion is changed, but continued exposure can sustain hypotension, reflex tachycardia, hypokalemia, and increased intraocular pressure. Therefore continuous blood-pressure and perfusion assessment plus transition to a durable oral plan are part of the treatment; exceeding 48 hours is outside the studied label rather than a calendar point at which one new toxicity suddenly begins.
