Class

Intralesional bacterial collagenase combination

Description

Xiaflex contains two purified collagenases that are injected directly into a pathologic collagen cord or plaque. It is completely different from Santyl wound ointment. Local collagen digestion can release a Dupuytren contracture or weaken a Peyronie plaque, but the same tissue-cleaving action can injure a tendon, ligament, urethra, corporal tissue, blood vessel, or nerve if the product is placed incorrectly or the treated area is stressed too soon.

Mechanism

Collagenase AUX-I and AUX-II cleave collagen at complementary sites. Local injection disrupts the collagen structure of a palpable Dupuytren cord or Peyronie plaque; the required later manipulation or modeling then changes the deformity. The effect is intentionally local and destructive, so precise anatomic placement, product reconstitution, post-procedure restrictions, and rapid recognition of rupture are central to safe use.

Used To Treat

Adult Dupuytren contracture with a palpable cord and adult Peyronie disease with a palpable plaque and curvature of at least 30 degrees at treatment initiation.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING for Peyronie disease: corporal rupture and other serious penile injury, including severe hematoma, can occur. Peyronie treatment is restricted through the Xiaflex REMS program and must be performed by trained, certified clinicians and sites.

Contraindications

  • Peyronie plaque involving the penile urethra because collagen digestion can damage that structure.

  • History of hypersensitivity to Xiaflex or another therapeutic collagenase.

  • Review anticoagulants, coagulation disorders, infection, prior anatomy-altering surgery, and target-site integrity because bleeding or misplaced injection can produce severe injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Dupuytren treatment: swelling, bruising, injection-site hemorrhage, pain, lymph-node tenderness, skin tear, tendon or ligament rupture, nerve injury, and complex regional pain.

  • Peyronie treatment: penile hematoma, swelling, pain, corporal rupture, other serious penile injury, severe hypersensitivity, and syncope.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs can increase procedure-related bleeding; follow the exact label and specialist plan rather than stopping therapy independently.

  • Do not substitute Santyl or any other collagenase formulation; concentration, route, indication, preparation, and risk controls are not interchangeable.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication, palpable target, trained injector, REMS requirements for Peyronie disease, anticoagulant plan, correct reconstitution, dose, side, and post-injection procedure.

  • After Dupuytren treatment, assess circulation, sensation, skin integrity, swelling, and tendon function. After Peyronie treatment, urgently assess popping sensation, sudden detumescence, severe swelling, hematoma, hematuria, or inability to void.

  • Reinforce activity and manipulation restrictions exactly; premature force can convert a controlled local collagen effect into rupture.

Key Labs

  • No serum drug level

  • Coagulation and platelet evaluation when bleeding risk warrants it

  • Focused neurovascular, tendon, urinary, and penile assessment according to indication

Administration

  • Intralesional injection only after reconstitution with the supplied diluent by a clinician experienced with the target anatomy.

  • Indication-specific finger extension or penile modeling occurs at defined times; do not improvise or allow patient self-manipulation outside instructions.

Patient Education

  • Seek urgent care for a pop, sudden loss of erection, severe bruising or swelling, inability to urinate, new numbness, loss of tendon function, or breathing/facial swelling.

  • Follow splinting, activity, sexual-abstinence, and modeling directions exactly because healing tissue remains vulnerable after collagen disruption.

Nclex Traps

  • Xiaflex is a trained-clinician intralesional product and must never be confused with topical Santyl.

  • A penile pop, sudden detumescence, severe hematoma, or loss of tendon function is an urgent structural-injury signal, not an expected minor injection reaction.

Routes

  • Intralesional injection administered only by a trained clinician using indication-specific technique.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Penile fracture or other serious penile injury, including corporal rupture and severe hematoma, during Peyronie treatment.

  • Tendon rupture, ligament injury, nerve injury, skin laceration, serious bleeding, or anaphylaxis.

Toxicity Management

  • There is no chemical antidote. Immobilize or protect the treated structure, assess neurovascular status and bleeding, and obtain urgent surgical or urologic evaluation for suspected rupture, fracture, severe hematoma, tendon injury, or compartment-threatening swelling.

Special Populations

  • The approved indications are adult Dupuytren contracture and adult Peyronie disease; pediatric use is not established.

  • Pregnancy and lactation are not expected treatment contexts, but exposure data are limited.

  • Review anticoagulants and bleeding disorders before injection; renal or hepatic adjustment is not the central safety issue because harm is primarily local tissue disruption and hypersensitivity.
