Definition

Crowe sign is an observed freckling sign. It is a clue to neurofibromatosis type 1. A positive finding means clusters of freckles are present in intertriginous areas.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Crowe sign means clusters of freckles are present in intertriginous areas.

Why It Matters

The observed Crowe sign finding can signal neurofibromatosis type 1. Biallelic NF1 loss in melanocytes creates pigment lesions in skin folds uncommon for ordinary sun freckles. Crowe sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • neurofibromatosis type 1

Assessment Method

Inspect axillary and inguinal folds with consent.

Positive Finding

Clusters of freckles are present in intertriginous areas.

Mechanism

Biallelic NF1 loss in melanocytes creates pigment lesions in skin folds uncommon for ordinary sun freckles.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Crowe sign finding raises concern for neurofibromatosis type 1 because biallelic NF1 loss in melanocytes creates pigment lesions in skin folds uncommon for ordinary sun freckles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Crowe sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Crowe sign to focus evaluation of neurofibromatosis type 1 and the reviewed alternatives: lentigines, postinflammatory pigment, Noonan syndrome. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect axillary and inguinal folds with consent. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Crowe sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Young children may not yet show it and other RASopathies can overlap. Therefore, Crowe sign cannot confirm or exclude neurofibromatosis type 1 by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lentigines

  • postinflammatory pigment

  • Noonan syndrome

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as lentigines, postinflammatory pigment, Noonan syndrome.

False Negative Situations

Crowe sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of neurofibromatosis type 1.
