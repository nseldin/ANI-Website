Definition

Aaron sign is a referred-pain maneuver. It is a clue to possible appendiceal irritation. A positive finding means pain or distress is referred to the epigastrium or precordium.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Aaron sign means pain or distress is referred to the epigastrium or precordium.

Why It Matters

A positive Aaron sign response can signal possible appendiceal irritation. Visceral afferent convergence may refer cecal-appendiceal stimulation to upper abdominal regions. Aaron sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • possible appendiceal irritation

Assessment Method

Apply steady pressure at McBurney region while asking where discomfort is felt.

Positive Finding

Pain or distress is referred to the epigastrium or precordium.

Mechanism

Visceral afferent convergence may refer cecal-appendiceal stimulation to upper abdominal regions.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Aaron sign response raises concern for possible appendiceal irritation because visceral afferent convergence may refer cecal-appendiceal stimulation to upper abdominal regions. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Aaron sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Aaron sign to focus evaluation of possible appendiceal irritation and the reviewed alternatives: peptic pain, anxiety, diffuse peritonitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply steady pressure at McBurney region while asking where discomfort is felt. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Aaron sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Poor validation and subjective referral make it unsuitable for diagnosis. Therefore, Aaron sign cannot confirm or exclude possible appendiceal irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • peptic pain

  • anxiety

  • diffuse peritonitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from peptic pain, anxiety, diffuse peritonitis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Aaron sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable. Aaron sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude possible appendiceal irritation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Aaron sign: Poor validation and subjective referral make it unsuitable for diagnosis.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Aaron sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply steady pressure at McBurney region while asking where discomfort is felt. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not prolong focal pressure merely to seek referred discomfort.

Nursing Relevance

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
