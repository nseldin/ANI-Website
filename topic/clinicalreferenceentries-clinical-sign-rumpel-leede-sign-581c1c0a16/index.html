Definition

Rumpel-Leede sign is a tourniquet-provoked skin sign. It is a clue to capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia. A positive finding means numerous petechiae appear below the cuff.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rumpel-Leede sign means numerous petechiae appear below the cuff.

Why It Matters

The observed Rumpel-Leede sign finding can signal capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia. Venous congestion raises capillary pressure beyond the tolerance of fragile vessels or inadequate platelet plugs. Rumpel-Leede sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia

Assessment Method

Apply standardized cuff pressure for a limited interval and inspect distal skin.

Positive Finding

Numerous petechiae appear below the cuff.

Mechanism

Venous congestion raises capillary pressure beyond the tolerance of fragile vessels or inadequate platelet plugs.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Rumpel-Leede sign finding raises concern for capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia because venous congestion raises capillary pressure beyond the tolerance of fragile vessels or inadequate platelet plugs. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Rumpel-Leede sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Rumpel-Leede sign to focus evaluation of capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia and the reviewed alternatives: traumatic petechiae, vasculitis, normal fragility. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply standardized cuff pressure for a limited interval and inspect distal skin. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Rumpel-Leede sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pressure, duration, skin tone, age, and baseline petechiae limit accuracy. Therefore, Rumpel-Leede sign cannot confirm or exclude capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • traumatic petechiae

  • vasculitis

  • normal fragility

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as traumatic petechiae, vasculitis, normal fragility.

False Negative Situations

Rumpel-Leede sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; not a standalone bleeding test. Rumpel-Leede sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude capillary fragility or thrombocytopenia. Apply the reviewed limitation for Rumpel-Leede sign: Pressure, duration, skin tone, age, and baseline petechiae limit accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Observe Rumpel-Leede sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Apply standardized cuff pressure for a limited interval and inspect distal skin. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Avoid in severe bleeding risk, fragile skin, vascular access limbs, or lymphedema.
