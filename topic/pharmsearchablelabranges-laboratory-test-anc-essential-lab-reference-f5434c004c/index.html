Name

ANC

Range

ANC is reported as cells/µL or × 10^9/L. A count below 500 cells/µL (0.5 × 10^9/L) marks high infection risk in cancer-care guidance, but reference intervals, neutropenia grading, medicine hold parameters, and fever pathways vary with age, population, treatment, expected duration, and protocol. In ASCO/IDSA adult cancer guidance, fever is a single oral temperature of at least 38.3°C (101°F) or at least 38.0°C (100.4°F) sustained for 1 hour; use the patient's oncology or facility instructions because this definition is not a universal rule for every population or setting.

Why

A low absolute neutrophil count result signals reduced neutrophil defense and increasing risk of serious bacterial or fungal infection as the count falls and the duration lengthens. Important causes include cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiation, marrow failure or infiltration, severe infection, autoimmune disease, nutritional deficiency, and medicines such as clozapine; an elevated result can accompany acute infection or inflammation, corticosteroids, or physiologic stress. Promptly assess temperature using the ordered method, vital signs and perfusion, chills, oral mucosa, skin and line sites, respiratory and urinary symptoms, abdominal or perirectal symptoms without invasive rectal examination when contraindicated, medicine and treatment timing, recent cultures and antibiotics, and the ANC trend and expected duration. Cause-directed evaluation can include repeat CBC with differential, blood cultures and other site-directed cultures, metabolic and organ-function laboratory testing, and imaging or marrow evaluation as ordered. Escalate immediately for fever meeting the patient's neutropenia pathway, hypothermia with illness, sepsis, shock or hemodynamic instability, respiratory distress, altered consciousness, or rapid deterioration; adults with cancer-related febrile neutropenia require prompt risk assessment and initial empirical antibacterial therapy within 1 hour of triage under ASCO/IDSA guidance. One ANC value does not diagnose infection or its cause and a normal or recovering count does not exclude serious infection; interpret symptoms, trend, nadir timing, neutropenia duration, treatment, comorbidities, and the applicable protocol together.

Aliases

High Causes

  • Acute infection/inflammation

  • Steroid effect

  • Stress response

Low Causes

  • Chemotherapy or radiation

  • Clozapine or another medicine effect

  • Bone-marrow failure or infiltration

  • Severe infection

  • Autoimmune neutropenia

  • Selected nutritional deficiencies

Critical Concerns

  • Fever meeting the patient's neutropenia pathway, hypothermia with illness, sepsis, shock or hemodynamic instability, respiratory distress, altered consciousness, or rapid deterioration requires immediate escalation.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess temperature, vital signs and perfusion, mouth, skin and line sites, respiratory, urinary and abdominal symptoms, treatment and medicine timing, and ANC trend; institute protective measures according to the ordered protocol.

  • Follow the patient's cancer-treatment or facility fever instructions and obtain cultures and other evaluation promptly as ordered without delaying protocol-directed empirical antibiotics.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not wait for pus, marked local inflammation, or a dramatic fever; neutropenia can blunt usual infection findings.

  • ANC is an infection-risk measure, not proof that infection is present or absent; fever definitions and medication hold parameters are protocol- and population-specific.
