Definition

Waddling gait is an observed gait sign. It is a clue to bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness. A positive finding means the pelvis alternately drops and the trunk shifts over each stance leg.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Waddling gait means the pelvis alternately drops and the trunk shifts over each stance leg.

Why It Matters

The observed Waddling gait finding can signal bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness. Weak bilateral hip abductors require compensatory trunk lean to keep the center of mass over the supporting foot. Waddling gait cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness

Assessment Method

Observe pelvic motion and trunk sway while walking with fall protection.

Positive Finding

The pelvis alternately drops and the trunk shifts over each stance leg.

Mechanism

Weak bilateral hip abductors require compensatory trunk lean to keep the center of mass over the supporting foot.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Waddling gait finding raises concern for bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness because weak bilateral hip abductors require compensatory trunk lean to keep the center of mass over the supporting foot. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Waddling gait does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Waddling gait to focus evaluation of bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness and the reviewed alternatives: lumbar lordosis, hip pain, normal late-pregnancy gait. Evidence Limitations

Pregnancy, obesity, and skeletal deformity may resemble the pattern. Therefore, Waddling gait cannot confirm or exclude bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lumbar lordosis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as lumbar lordosis, hip pain, normal late-pregnancy gait.

False Negative Situations

Waddling gait may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of bilateral proximal hip-girdle weakness.
