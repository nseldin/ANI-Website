Used To Treat

Adult hypertension and edema associated with heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, corticosteroid or estrogen therapy, and selected renal disorders under current labeling. It is also used in selected hypercalciuric stone-prevention and nephrogenic-diabetes-insipidus strategies.

Mechanism

HCTZ is secreted into the tubular lumen and inhibits NCC, reducing sodium-chloride entry into distal convoluted-tubule cells. Lower intracellular sodium strengthens basolateral sodium-calcium exchange, drawing calcium into blood and favoring apical TRPV5 calcium entry; mild volume contraction also increases proximal calcium reclamation. Urinary calcium therefore falls. More sodium reaches collecting-duct ENaC, where lumen negativity drives potassium and hydrogen secretion, while RAAS activation from contraction adds aldosterone signaling. This explains hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis. Because the distal convoluted tubule is a principal diluting segment, its blockade reduces free-water excretion while the medullary gradient remains intact, explaining potentially severe hyponatremia. Reduced proximal urate secretion and volume-mediated reabsorption raise uric acid. Long-term vascular sodium and calcium changes help sustain pressure reduction after the initial volume contraction stabilizes.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe thiazide-associated hyponatremia can cause confusion, seizure, coma, or death and may develop soon after initiation or when water intake, kidney function, or interacting drugs change. Current labeling also advises skin protection and surveillance because cumulative HCTZ exposure is associated with increased non-melanoma skin-cancer risk, especially squamous-cell carcinoma.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and hypersensitivity are core contraindication patterns. Symptomatic hyponatremia, major potassium or magnesium loss, hypotension, or evolving kidney injury requires reassessment.

  • Do not assume every thiazide is ineffective below one eGFR threshold; agent, dose, route, indication, and actual response matter.

  • The label contraindicates anuria and hypersensitivity to hydrochlorothiazide or other sulfonamide-derived drugs; reaction history still requires clinical characterization.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check pressure, orthostasis, weight, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, and uric acid when relevant before and after initiation or escalation.

  • Teach earlier-day dosing, slow position changes, photosensitivity precautions, and urgent reporting of confusion, seizure, severe weakness, palpitations, eye pain, or sudden visual change.

  • Reconcile combination products such as lisinopril/HCTZ or losartan/HCTZ to prevent duplicate thiazide dosing. Teach sun protection and reporting of changing skin lesions.

Interactions

  • Lithium exposure can rise as sodium depletion increases proximal lithium reabsorption; NSAIDs can blunt effect and worsen kidney function.

  • SSRIs, carbamazepine, desmopressin, and high water intake magnify hyponatremia; low potassium increases digoxin-related arrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid, weight, and urine calcium when that is the indication.

  • Check sooner in older adults, low body mass, high water intake, CKD, or interacting medication use because severe sodium decline can appear quickly.

Nclex Traps

  • Thiazides lower urinary calcium while raising uric acid; those effects come from different transport responses.

  • They can cause profound hyponatremia because the diluting segment is blocked while concentrating ability and vasopressin action remain.

  • HCTZ reduces urinary calcium but can still cause hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hyperuricemia, and photosensitivity. One favorable ion effect does not make the drug electrolyte neutral.

Description

Hydrochlorothiazide, often abbreviated HCTZ, is a conventional thiazide that lowers blood pressure and produces modest sodium-water loss by blocking NCC in the early distal convoluted tubule. It is not simply a weaker furosemide: it acts later in the nephron, preserves urinary calcium, and has a greater tendency to impair urine dilution and cause hyponatremia. Oral diuresis begins within about two hours, peaks near four hours, and can persist for roughly six to twelve hours. The early pressure effect reflects lower sodium and plasma volume; chronic blood-pressure control also reflects reduced peripheral vascular resistance. HCTZ is common in combination tablets, so duplicate exposure can be missed. Cumulative use is associated with a small increased risk of non-melanoma skin cancer, particularly squamous-cell carcinoma, likely related to photosensitization and cumulative ultraviolet injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, volume depletion, hyperuricemia or gout, photosensitivity, and glucose intolerance follow NCC blockade and compensatory distal transport.

  • Severe neurologic hyponatremia, arrhythmia, pancreatitis, severe skin reactions, and acute angle-closure glaucoma or myopia require urgent action.

  • Cumulative photosensitization is associated with non-melanoma skin cancer risk; erectile dysfunction and lipid effects may also occur.
