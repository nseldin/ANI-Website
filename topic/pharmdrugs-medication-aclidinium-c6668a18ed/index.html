Used To Treat

Twice-daily maintenance treatment of COPD. Aclidinium prevents cholinergic bronchoconstriction over time and is not an initial treatment for acute bronchospasm or sudden breathing difficulty.

Mechanism

M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle use Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, and intracellular calcium to activate contraction. Aclidinium competitively and reversibly blocks muscarinic receptors, preventing acetylcholine from generating this calcium signal and producing prolonged site-specific bronchodilation. The molecule is a quaternary ammonium ester. In plasma it is rapidly hydrolyzed chemically and by butyrylcholinesterase into inactive alcohol and acid metabolites, reducing duration of systemic exposure compared with the airway receptor effect. This pharmacokinetic feature explains a relatively low systemic burden; it does not change the core antimuscarinic warnings or make extra doses appropriate during acute dyspnea.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Aclidinium is not rescue therapy. Discontinue and use alternative treatment for paradoxical bronchospasm. Immediate hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis or angioedema, can occur. Eye pain, halos, blurred vision, or red eye may indicate acute angle closure; difficult or painful urination may indicate retention. Severe milk-protein allergy is a contraindication because the powder contains lactose.

Contraindications

  • Severe hypersensitivity to milk proteins, aclidinium, or an excipient

  • Use caution with narrow-angle glaucoma, prostatic hyperplasia, bladder-neck obstruction, or other anticholinergics

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach load-press-inhale-hold technique and confirm the control window changes and click occurs; inspiratory flow determines delivery.

  • Discard at the labeled interval after opening or when the device locks at zero, and ensure a separate rescue inhaler is available.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics add ocular, urinary, bowel, cardiac, and drying effects.

  • Check combination inhalers for duplicate LAMA or LABA ingredients before adding Tudorza.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, exacerbations, spirometry, device technique, urinary output, and ocular symptoms

  • No routine drug level; renal impairment did not require labeled adjustment, but clinical anticholinergic toxicity still matters

Nclex Traps

  • Rapid plasma hydrolysis limits systemic persistence; it does not make aclidinium short acting in the lung.

  • Pressair is breath actuated and maintenance only, not a nebulized rescue medicine.

Description

Aclidinium is a twice-daily inhaled long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) delivered by the breath-actuated Pressair dry-powder device. It blocks airway M3 receptors, reducing acetylcholine-driven smooth-muscle contraction and helping airways remain open between doses. Once drug reaches plasma, ester hydrolysis rapidly converts it to inactive metabolites, which limits systemic persistence but does not eliminate dry mouth, glaucoma, urinary retention, or hypersensitivity risk. Its device provides visual and audible feedback after a successful inhalation, making inspiratory technique part of the dose. Aclidinium is COPD maintenance, not asthma rescue, and the lactose carrier creates a severe milk-protein-allergy restriction.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nasopharyngitis, cough, dry mouth, diarrhea, toothache, or sinus symptoms

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, hypersensitivity, acute angle-closure glaucoma, urinary retention, tachycardia, or additive anticholinergic toxicity
