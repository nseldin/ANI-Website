Used To Treat

A decision card for distinguishing systemic azoles that share CYP51 inhibition but differ in Candida, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus, Mucorales, endemic-fungus, urine, and CNS usefulness.

Description

The systemic triazole comparison map distinguishes fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, and isavuconazole by fungal spectrum, formulation, tissue distribution, metabolism, QT effect, interactions, and need for drug-level monitoring. All inhibit fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP51), reduce ergosterol synthesis, and impair membrane function, but organism susceptibility and human pharmacokinetics make them clinically noninterchangeable. Fluconazole is highly bioavailable, water soluble, renally cleared, and penetrates urine and cerebrospinal fluid well; it is useful for susceptible Candida and cryptococcal consolidation but has no reliable Aspergillus or Mucorales activity. Itraconazole covers several endemic fungi and Aspergillus but has variable absorption, major CYP3A interactions, poor CSF exposure, and a heart-failure warning. Voriconazole is a principal treatment for invasive Aspergillus and penetrates CNS, but CYP2C19 variability, nonlinear adult pharmacokinetics, visual effects, hepatotoxicity, photosensitivity, and interactions make exposure monitoring valuable. Posaconazole covers Candida, Aspergillus, and Mucorales; its delayed-release tablet, suspension, powder, and IV formulations cannot be casually substituted. Isavuconazonium is a water-soluble prodrug of isavuconazole for invasive aspergillosis and mucormycosis; it shortens QT and has fewer formulation barriers than voriconazole, but still has CYP3A interactions and hepatic risk.

Mechanism

Spectrum differences arise from target affinity, cell entry, efflux, and fungal resistance, while clinical differences arise from absorption and host metabolism. Fluconazole exposure is predictable enough that routine levels are uncommon. Itraconazole depends on formulation, gastric acidity, food, and CYP3A metabolism, so levels can expose silent underdosing. Voriconazole metabolism becomes saturable in adults: a modest dose increase can cause a disproportionate concentration rise, which explains both breakthrough infection at low levels and neurohepatic toxicity at high levels. Posaconazole suspension relies heavily on food and GI function, whereas delayed-release tablets improve exposure; therefore the label forbids simple substitution. Isavuconazonium is rapidly cleaved to active isavuconazole and an inactive product, enabling oral and cyclodextrin-free IV delivery.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: No comparison table replaces organism identification, susceptibility, current labeling, and interaction review. Giving the 'right class' at the wrong exposure can select resistance and allow invasive disease to progress.

Adverse Effects

  • Shared concerns include hepatic injury, GI effects, rash, and interactions. Agent-specific patterns include itraconazole edema/heart failure, voriconazole visual-neurologic and phototoxic effects, posaconazole QT prolongation, and isavuconazole QT shortening.

  • Treatment failure can result from intrinsic resistance, acquired ERG11/CYP51 change, efflux, nonadherence, malabsorption, an inducer, inadequate source control, or an infected compartment the drug reaches poorly.

Contraindications

  • Do not use voriconazole for mucormycosis or fluconazole for invasive aspergillosis merely because both are triazoles.

  • Do not open or crush formulations unless the exact label allows it; administration changes can destroy delayed release or alter exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record exact formulation, meal and acid requirements, loading regimen, route transitions, interacting drugs, trough timing when ordered, and the susceptibility result that justifies step-down.

  • At every handoff, say both drug and formulation. 'Posaconazole 300 mg' without formulation can conceal a clinically dangerous substitution.

Interactions

  • All systemic triazoles require a medication review, but CYP pathways and magnitude differ. Voriconazole and fluconazole involve CYP2C19/2C9 as well as CYP3A, while itraconazole, posaconazole, and isavuconazole are particularly important CYP3A partners.

  • Acid suppressants and tube feeds affect selected oral formulations; severe diarrhea, mucositis, graft-versus-host disease, or altered GI anatomy can make an apparently correct oral dose unreliable.

Key Labs

  • Liver tests, electrolytes, renal function for fluconazole dosing and IV excipients where relevant, ECG when indicated, species/susceptibility, and selected trough levels after steady state or when efficacy, toxicity, absorption, or interaction is uncertain.

  • Therapeutic ranges are assay-, organism-, site-, and guideline-dependent. Interpret a trough with infectious-disease and pharmacy expertise rather than treating one number as universal.

Nclex Traps

  • Fluconazole reaches urine; echinocandins generally do not produce useful urinary concentrations.

  • Isavuconazonium is the prodrug; isavuconazole is the active azole.

Source Note
