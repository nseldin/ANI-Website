Used To Treat

Oral or intrathecal baclofen toxicity causing profound coma, hypotonia, hyporeflexia, apnea, bradycardia, hypotension, hypothermia, myoclonus, seizure, or a brain-death-like examination.

Description

Baclofen is a GABA-B receptor agonist and muscle relaxant that suppresses excitatory neurotransmitter release; overdose can produce profound yet reversible neurologic and respiratory depression. Coma, absent or depressed brainstem reflexes, flaccidity, hyporeflexia, apnea, bradycardia, hypothermia, and hypotension can mimic catastrophic brain injury or brain death; myoclonus and seizures can paradoxically coexist because different neural networks are affected. Baclofen is cleared mainly by the kidneys, so ordinary therapeutic dosing can accumulate in kidney impairment. Intrathecal-pump programming error, refill error, catheter problem, or reservoir overdose can deliver a concentrated central dose and requires immediate pump expertise. There is no reliable antagonist. Ventilation, circulation, temperature support, seizure care, and time are the foundation. EXTRIP distinguishes acute overdose in normal kidney function, where routine dialysis adds uncertain benefit, from therapeutic toxicity in kidney impairment with ventilated coma, where intermittent hemodialysis is suggested. Removing baclofen quickly from a chronically treated patient can uncover dangerous withdrawal, so recovery monitoring includes both toxicity and rebound hyperexcitability.

Mechanism

Baclofen is a GABA-B receptor agonist. Presynaptic activation reduces calcium influx and excitatory neurotransmitter release, while postsynaptic potassium efflux hyperpolarizes neurons. Excess signaling suppresses spinal reflexes, arousal, respiratory drive, autonomic tone, and muscle tone, producing flaccid coma and apnea. Supportive ventilation maintains gas exchange while kidney elimination lowers the CNS concentration. The drug is small, has modest protein binding, and is dialyzable, but patients with normal kidneys after a one-time acute overdose often clear it with high-quality supportive care; dialysis then adds procedure risk without proven outcome gain. In kidney impairment, endogenous clearance may be extremely slow, making hemodialysis more valuable. Chronic receptor adaptation explains why abrupt concentration reduction or pump interruption can cause rebound spasticity, agitation, hallucination, hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, seizure, and organ failure.

Administration Timing

  • Call 911 for difficult arousal, slow or absent breathing, seizure, profound weakness, or suspected pump malfunction. Support ventilation immediately, check glucose and temperature, obtain ECG and kidney function, stop further oral or pump delivery only with an urgent plan, and call Poison Help.

  • Provide lung-protective ventilation, hemodynamic and temperature support, and benzodiazepines for seizures. Contact the intrathecal-pump team urgently for interrogation and specialist reservoir or cerebrospinal-fluid management; do not manipulate a pump without trained personnel.

  • Use EXTRIP context for dialysis: severe acute overdose with normal kidneys generally receives standard care alone, whereas therapeutic toxicity in kidney impairment with coma requiring mechanical ventilation supports intermittent hemodialysis. Monitor closely for withdrawal as toxicity resolves or dialysis lowers the concentration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify oral versus intrathecal exposure, last dose, pump model, refill and programming history, reservoir concentration, kidney function, dialysis schedule, chronic duration, co-sedatives, and any recent interruption that could later produce withdrawal.

  • Trend respiratory effort and end-tidal carbon dioxide, brainstem and limb reflexes, tone, myoclonus, seizure, temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, ECG, urine output, skin integrity, and sedation-free neurologic changes. Prevent pressure injury, aspiration, corneal injury, and venous thrombosis during prolonged coma.

  • Document exact timing of pump actions and dialysis relative to arousal. As consciousness returns, watch for new spasticity, autonomic instability, agitation, hallucination, fever, rising CK, or seizure rather than assuming every change is recurrent overdose.

Key Labs

  • Obtain bedside glucose, electrolytes, bicarbonate, creatinine and urea, blood gas, CK, liver studies, pregnancy testing when relevant, and ECG because these tests identify reversible mimics, impaired clearance, rhabdomyolysis, acid-base injury, and conduction complications. Obtain neuroimaging, EEG, infection studies, or other tests when history or recovery does not fit isolated baclofen because an atypical trajectory raises concern for another cause of coma or seizure.

  • Serum baclofen testing is often delayed and does not define CNS concentration or brain-death exclusion by itself. Serial examination must account for dose, kidney function, temperature, acid-base status, co-sedatives, and adequate observation.

Adverse Effects

  • Toxicity can cause aspiration, apnea, prolonged ventilation, coma, hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, seizure, rhabdomyolysis, kidney injury, and death, while the depth of examination can lead to premature prognostic error.

  • Dialysis can cause hypotension and access complications and may precipitate withdrawal; pump manipulation can cause infection, catheter damage, underdose, or additional overdose if performed incorrectly.

Contraindications

  • Do not diagnose irreversible neurologic death until baclofen and other depressants are adequately excluded under formal neurologic-death standards, because profound toxic coma with depressed brainstem reflexes can still be reversible. A negative routine toxicology screen does not exclude baclofen because standard panels usually do not test for it.

  • Do not give flumazenil or naloxone as a substitute for ventilation in isolated baclofen toxicity, and do not apply routine dialysis to every acute overdose without kidney and EXTRIP context.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Apnea, deep coma, seizure, hemodynamic instability, intrathecal exposure, kidney failure, unexpectedly prolonged course, or emerging withdrawal requires ICU, toxicology, nephrology, neurology, and pump-specialist care. Transfer early if pump interrogation or dialysis is unavailable.

Interactions

  • Opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol, sleep medicines, and anesthetics deepen respiratory and CNS depression. Kidney-toxic illness or medicines reduce baclofen clearance indirectly.

  • Abrupt interruption of chronic oral or intrathecal baclofen can cause life-threatening withdrawal; toxic-dose reduction and safe maintenance restart require one coordinated plan rather than independent teams stopping and restarting drug.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Kidney disease

    Note

    Therapeutic doses can accumulate into ventilated coma; medication reconciliation and renal-dose prevention are as important as dialysis rescue.

  • Population

    Intrathecal-pump users

    Note

    A tiny programming, concentration, refill, or catheter error can produce rapid central overdose or withdrawal and needs immediate pump expertise.

  • Population

    Pregnancy and newborns

    Note

    Stabilize maternal ventilation first; chronic exposure can also create neonatal withdrawal, requiring obstetric and neonatal planning.

Evidence Limitations

  • EXTRIP recommendations are based on very-low-quality evidence. The distinction between acute overdose and therapeutic accumulation in kidney disease improves decision quality, but dialysis timing, serum-level interpretation, and pump interventions remain individualized.

Nclex Traps

  • Profound baclofen coma can mimic brain death and may still be reversible.

  • Dialysis is most supported for therapeutic toxicity with kidney impairment and ventilated coma, not every acute overdose.

  • Rapid clearance or pump interruption can uncover life-threatening baclofen withdrawal.

Regulatory Status

No specific antidote exists. Intermittent hemodialysis is an EXTRIP-supported specialist intervention for selected kidney-impairment toxicity; intrathecal pump management must follow device and specialty protocols.

Boxed Warning

BACLOFEN-TOXICITY WARNING: call 911 for difficult arousal, apnea, seizure, or profound weakness and ventilate promptly. Deep reversible coma can mimic brain death; consider kidney function and pump error, use dialysis selectively under EXTRIP guidance, and monitor for dangerous withdrawal as drug effect clears.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning identifies the emergency, explains the mechanism of harm, and separates immediate stabilization from specialist antidote or elimination decisions.
