Definition

Murphy sign is a bedside abdominal maneuver. It is a clue to acute cholecystitis. A positive finding means inspiration stops abruptly because of focal pain.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Murphy sign means inspiration stops abruptly because of focal pain.

Why It Matters

A positive Murphy sign response can signal acute cholecystitis. The descending inflamed gallbladder contacts the examiner's fingers and irritates the parietal peritoneum. Murphy sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • acute cholecystitis

Assessment Method

Palpate beneath the right costal margin while the patient inspires.

Positive Finding

Inspiration stops abruptly because of focal pain.

Mechanism

The descending inflamed gallbladder contacts the examiner's fingers and irritates the parietal peritoneum.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Murphy sign response raises concern for acute cholecystitis because the descending inflamed gallbladder contacts the examiner's fingers and irritates the parietal peritoneum. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Murphy sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Murphy sign to focus evaluation of acute cholecystitis and the reviewed alternatives: hepatitis, peptic ulcer, pleurisy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate beneath the right costal margin while the patient inspires. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Murphy sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Analgesia, age, diabetes, and severe illness can blunt pain; specificity varies. Therefore, Murphy sign cannot confirm or exclude acute cholecystitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hepatitis

  • peptic ulcer

  • pleurisy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hepatitis, peptic ulcer, pleurisy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Murphy sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; ultrasound confirmation is usually required. When performed as described, Murphy sign can adjust concern for acute cholecystitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Murphy sign: Analgesia, age, diabetes, and severe illness can blunt pain; specificity varies.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Murphy sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Palpate beneath the right costal margin while the patient inspires. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Compare left and right and do not press forcefully on a guarding abdomen.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Murphy sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Inspiration stops abruptly because of focal pain. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Palpate beneath the right costal margin while the patient inspires. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, acute cholecystitis, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Murphy sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Compare left and right and do not press forcefully on a guarding abdomen. Base escalation for Murphy sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
