Class Example Names

  • Permethrin

  • Ivermectin

Used To Treat

Scabies and head lice with formulation-, age-, pregnancy-, outbreak-, and resistance-specific regimens; ivermectin also has unrelated oral nematode and topical rosacea indications.

Description

Permethrin and ivermectin disrupt parasite neural ion-channel signaling and paralyze arthropods through channels humans either regulate differently or do not possess. Permethrin is a pyrethroid that delays arthropod voltage-gated sodium-channel repolarization; 5% cream is a first-line scabicide and 1% products treat lice. Ivermectin binds invertebrate glutamate-gated chloride channels, hyperpolarizing nerve and muscle; topical lotion treats lice and oral ivermectin is used off-label for scabies in selected patients or outbreaks.

Mechanism

Persistent sodium influx from permethrin produces delayed repolarization, repetitive firing, paralysis, and parasite death. Ivermectin opens glutamate-gated chloride channels, increasing chloride conductance and flaccid paralysis. Mammals lack these glutamate channels and therapeutic ivermectin crosses the intact human blood-brain barrier poorly. Neither mechanism immediately removes eggs, environmental fomites, or the host inflammatory reaction to dead mites.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Correct whole-body/scalp application and contact management are essential. Persistent itch for 2-4 weeks after scabies treatment can be post-scabetic inflammation rather than failure, but new burrows or untreated contacts suggest reinfestation. Oral ivermectin requires special caution with Loa loa exposure because heavy microfilaremia can precipitate severe encephalopathy.

Adverse Effects

  • Burning, stinging, pruritus, erythema or scalp/skin irritation

  • Ivermectin: dizziness, GI symptoms, rash, hypotension and inflammatory Mazzotti-type reactions

  • Rare neurotoxicity with excessive exposure or unusual blood-brain-barrier vulnerability

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity and formulation-specific pediatric age restrictions

  • Avoid mucosa/eyes and clarify pregnancy/lactation before systemic ivermectin

  • Assess Loa loa exposure, severe crusted disease, immunocompromise and household/outbreak context

Nursing Essentials

  • Treat close contacts and decontaminate clothing/bedding according to protocol; a correctly treated patient can be reinfested by an untreated household.

  • Teach exact coverage and contact time, including scalp/face in infants or older adults when directed.

Interactions

  • Ivermectin is a CYP3A4/P-gp substrate; strong inhibitors and CNS depressants can increase toxicity concern.

  • Multiple pediculicides can compound irritation without correcting application failure.

  • Warfarin INR changes have been reported with ivermectin and warrant monitoring when clinically relevant.

Key Labs

  • No routine permethrin level

  • Stool/skin/eye parasite testing and Loa loa evaluation when epidemiologically indicated

  • Neurologic status, blood pressure, liver function and INR when systemic ivermectin risk warrants

Nclex Traps

  • Post-scabetic itching does not automatically mean live mites remain.

  • Permethrin 5% is scabies treatment; lower-strength lice products are not interchangeable.

  • Oral ivermectin's U.S. label is for strongyloidiasis/onchocerciasis; scabies use is clinically established but off-label.
