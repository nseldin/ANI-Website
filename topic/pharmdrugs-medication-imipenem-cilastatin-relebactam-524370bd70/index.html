Class

IV carbapenem plus human renal DHP-I inhibitor plus class A/C bacterial beta-lactamase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Susceptible complicated urinary, complicated intra-abdominal, hospital-acquired, and ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia under the current U.S. label. It is an important option for selected KPC-producing Enterobacterales and resistant Pseudomonas when the mechanism and MIC fit.

Description

Imipenem/cilastatin/relebactam, branded Recarbrio, is a three-component IV beta-lactam antibiotic: imipenem binds PBPs and blocks bacterial wall cross-linking, cilastatin inhibits human renal dehydropeptidase I to prevent kidney metabolism of imipenem, and relebactam inhibits selected class A and C beta-lactamases to protect imipenem from hydrolysis. The three components have distinct roles: only imipenem directly kills bacteria, cilastatin solves human drug metabolism, and relebactam solves selected bacterial resistance. Relebactam covers KPC and AmpC but does not reliably inhibit OXA-48-like or metallo-beta-lactamases, and the product retains imipenem's seizure and valproate risks.

Mechanism

Protected imipenem enters susceptible Gram-negative bacteria and acylates multiple PBPs. Relebactam reversibly carbamylates the active-site serine of KPC and AmpC-like enzymes, while cilastatin independently blocks human DHP-I at the proximal-tubule brush border. This distinction explains why cilastatin cannot restore a resistant MIC and relebactam cannot prevent renal imipenem breakdown. Renal function controls all three exposures. In resistant Pseudomonas, relebactam can help when AmpC plus porin changes drive resistance, but MBLs, OXA enzymes, severe efflux, or target changes can still defeat the combination.

Nursing Essentials

  • At preparation and handoff, name all three jobs because confusing cilastatin with relebactam can conceal either renal metabolism or microbial resistance failure.

  • Verify culture/MIC, carbapenemase, renal function, CNS history, valproate, infusion, and source control before each major regimen decision.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, KPC/AmpC or carbapenemase testing, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, mental status, and valproate concentration when exposed.

  • Persistent infection warrants repeat microbiology because enzyme mutation, porin/efflux change, or source-control failure can emerge.

Nclex Traps

  • Cilastatin blocks a human kidney enzyme; relebactam blocks selected bacterial enzymes.

  • Recarbrio covers selected KPC/AmpC mechanisms, not MBL or OXA-48-like resistance by class name alone.

Imipenem Cilastatin Relebactam

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, liver-test elevation, phlebitis, rash, candidiasis, cytopenias, allergy, and CDI.

  • Confusion, hallucinations, tremor, myoclonus, nonconvulsive status, generalized seizure, and breakthrough seizure from valproate depletion.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to any component or related beta-lactams requires avoidance according to the current label.

  • Do not use for a known MBL or OXA-48-like producer without susceptibility-supported rationale because relebactam lacks dependable inhibitor activity.

Interactions

  • Imipenem markedly lowers valproate/divalproex; ganciclovir and other proconvulsant drugs can compound seizure risk.

  • Probenecid can increase component exposure and is generally not intentionally combined.

    Pediatric caution

    Note

    Pediatric indication, meningitis role, formulation, dose, interval, renal maturation, and maximum dose differ by agent and age. Use the exact label or pediatric infectious-disease protocol because beta-lactams are not safely scaled from an adult regimen by intuition.

  • Type

    Label

    Older adult caution

    Note

    Older adults can have sharply reduced renal clearance despite an unremarkable serum creatinine. Estimate kidney function, reassess it during acute illness, and connect new confusion, myoclonus, or seizure to possible beta-lactam accumulation rather than assuming delirium is unrelated.

  • Type

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Many beta-lactams have substantial pregnancy and lactation experience, but infection severity, allergy history, maternal organ function, placental exposure, neonatal bilirubin handling, and the exact agent still matter. Treat serious infection promptly with an evidence-supported regimen rather than applying one class-wide pregnancy rule.
