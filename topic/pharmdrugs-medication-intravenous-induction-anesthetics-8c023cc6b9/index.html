Used To Treat

Rapid induction of unconsciousness for general anesthesia or selected procedural/critical-care sedation; the choice depends on hemodynamics, airway, analgesia need, neurologic goals, adrenal risk, recovery profile, and monitoring environment.

Description

Intravenous induction anesthetics rapidly produce hypnosis, but they are not interchangeable. Propofol and etomidate enhance GABA-A inhibition; ketamine blocks NMDA signaling and supplies dissociative analgesia; methohexital and thiopental are ultrashort barbiturates. The useful first question is not simply how fast the patient sleeps, but what each agent does to blood pressure, ventilation, pain, cerebral physiology, adrenal steroid synthesis, and recovery.

Mechanism

Increasing GABA-A chloride conductance hyperpolarizes neurons and disconnects cortical arousal, whereas NMDA blockade interrupts excitatory glutamatergic integration and pain transmission. Rapid onset reflects fast brain delivery. Rapid awakening after a bolus often reflects redistribution from brain to muscle and fat before metabolic elimination is complete, so repeated doses or infusions can accumulate despite a short first awakening.

Boxed Warning

Several agents carry formulation-specific requirements for continuous respiratory/cardiac monitoring and immediate airway-resuscitation capability. All can cause apnea, obstruction, aspiration, hypotension or dysrhythmia, and none should be treated as a routine anxiolytic. Propofol adds contamination and infusion-syndrome risk; etomidate suppresses adrenal steroid synthesis; barbiturates are unsafe in porphyria; ketamine has a distinct sympathetic and emergence profile.

Nursing Essentials

  • Prepare oxygen, suction, bag-mask ventilation, intubation equipment, ECG, blood pressure, capnography when available, and a clinician dedicated to continuous observation before administration.

  • Know whether the chosen drug provides analgesia. Propofol, etomidate, methohexital, and thiopental do not reliably treat procedural pain; unconsciousness does not prove comfort.

Key Labs

  • Continuous oxygenation, ventilation/capnography, ECG and blood pressure; glucose and pregnancy context when relevant; acid-base, lactate, potassium, creatine kinase, triglycerides and renal function for prolonged propofol exposure; cortisol context after etomidate in critical illness.

Nclex Traps

  • A patient who is motionless or amnestic may still need analgesia.

  • A short awakening after a bolus can reflect redistribution, not complete elimination.

  • Etomidate's relative hemodynamic stability does not make it physiologically neutral.
