Class

Antimalarial partner drug

Used To Treat

Uncomplicated malaria due to susceptible Plasmodium species when paired with artemether.

Mechanism

Lumefantrine works by using agent-specific parasite targets: artemisinins create peroxide-mediated blood-stage injury, quinine/chloroquine impair heme detoxification, 8-aminoquinolines target liver forms, antifolates impair folate/DNA pathways, and atovaquone targets mitochondrial electron transport. Species, resistance, G6PD status, QT risk, retinal monitoring, food-dependent absorption, and adherence determine safety.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food or milk when ordered because fat improves absorption.

  • Assess QT risk, interacting medications, vomiting after doses, and symptom response.

Contraindications

  • Clarify significant QT prolongation risk or interacting QT-prolonging therapy.

Interactions

  • QT-prolonging drugs and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers can be important.

Key Labs

  • Electrolytes if QT risk, ECG if ordered, and malaria response markers if available.

Nclex Traps

  • Food improves absorption. Vomiting soon after a dose may mean the client did not absorb therapy.

Description

Lumefantrine is an antimalarial partner drug: it works by using agent-specific parasite targets: artemisinins create peroxide-mediated blood-stage injury, quinine/chloroquine impair heme detoxification, 8-aminoquinolines target liver forms, antifolates impair folate/DNA pathways, and atovaquone targets mitochondrial electron transport. It is used for uncomplicated malaria due to susceptible Plasmodium species when paired with artemether.
