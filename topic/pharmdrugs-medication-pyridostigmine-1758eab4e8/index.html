Contraindications

  • Mechanical intestinal or urinary obstruction is a labeled contraindication for routine use because increased smooth-muscle activity can worsen obstruction.

  • Do not use pyridostigmine after nerve-agent exposure or as a substitute for atropine and pralidoxime.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and formulation because myasthenia regimens, immediate-release tablets, older pretreatment schedules, and the 105 mg extended-release product are not interchangeable.

  • Assess strength together with secretions, bowel activity, pupils, heart rate, breathing, and fasciculations because excessive cholinergic effect can look like worsening neuromuscular disease.

  • Teach the operational transition from pretreatment to rescue because taking pyridostigmine after exposure can worsen toxicity.

Interactions

  • Other cholinergic drugs, beta blockers, and medicines that slow conduction can add bradycardia or secretions, while antimuscarinics can mask muscarinic warning signs without correcting weakness.

  • Alcohol is prohibited with the 105 mg extended-release soman product, while empty-stomach or low-fat, low-calorie administration changes expected exposure; neuromuscular blockers also interact unpredictably with acetylcholinesterase inhibition.

Key Labs

  • No serum concentration routinely guides dosing; respiratory mechanics and clinical strength matter in myasthenia, while exposure signs drive nerve-agent rescue.

  • ECG, oxygenation, blood gas, electrolytes, and cholinesterase activity may support severe toxicity evaluation but must not delay antidotes.

Nclex Traps

  • Pyridostigmine is soman pretreatment, not postexposure rescue.

  • After exposure, stop it and use atropine plus pralidoxime.

  • Myasthenic and cholinergic weakness require assessment of secretions and dosing history, not strength alone.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Adult military personnel at soman risk

    Note

    The 105 mg extended-release indication is operationally restricted and must begin at least several hours before exposure with the required meal and immediate access to postexposure antidotes.

  • Population

    Asthma, bradyarrhythmia, or gastrointestinal disease

    Note

    More acetylcholine can worsen bronchospasm, conduction slowing, secretions, or motility, so clinical monitoring is essential.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, children, or civilians

    Note

    The current 105 mg soman pretreatment indication is not a general population prophylaxis; specialist and product-specific authority are required.

Used To Treat

Improvement of neuromuscular transmission in myasthenia gravis under formulation-specific labeling and, for the 105 mg extended-release product, pretreatment of adult military personnel at high risk of soman exposure. It is not a postexposure antidote.

Mechanism

Pyridostigmine carbamylates acetylcholinesterase reversibly, slowing acetylcholine breakdown. In myasthenia gravis, more acetylcholine reaches remaining nicotinic receptors and improves end-plate depolarization. Before soman exposure, reversible carbamylation prevents some enzyme from being irreversibly phosphonylated and rapidly aged; later hydrolysis releases functional enzyme. After exposure, however, additional acetylcholinesterase inhibition can worsen secretions, bronchospasm, bradycardia, fasciculations, and weakness, which explains why pyridostigmine is pretreatment only and why atropine plus pralidoxime are still required.

Boxed Warning

PRETREATMENT-ONLY WARNING: pyridostigmine does not protect fully and can worsen cholinergic toxicity after exposure. Stop it at exposure signs and immediately use atropine, pralidoxime, decontamination, airway support, and seizure treatment.

Description

Pyridostigmine is a reversible peripheral acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that increases acetylcholine to improve myasthenic strength and, in its 105 mg extended-release formulation, temporarily protects some enzyme from soman before exposure. In soman defense, reversible enzyme occupancy shields a fraction of acetylcholinesterase from irreversible soman binding, leaving protected enzyme that can function after pyridostigmine dissociates. The 105 mg extended-release product is taken once daily and must be started at least several hours before potential soman exposure. Take it with either a medium-fat, medium-calorie meal or a high-fat, high-calorie meal. Do not take it on an empty stomach or with a low-fat, low-calorie meal. Do not take this product with alcohol. At exposure signs, stop it and immediately use atropine and pralidoxime; it cannot rescue an exposed person by itself.

Adverse Effects

  • Abdominal cramping, diarrhea, nausea, salivation, sweating, urinary urgency, miosis, bradycardia, and muscle twitching reflect excess cholinergic activity.

  • Severe bronchial secretions, bronchospasm, bradyarrhythmia, weakness, or cholinergic crisis can threaten ventilation.

Administration Timing

  • For the 105 mg extended-release soman indication, start at least several hours before potential exposure and use only as directed adult military pretreatment.

  • Take one dose daily with either a medium-fat, medium-calorie meal or a high-fat, high-calorie meal. Do not take it on an empty stomach, with a low-fat, low-calorie meal, or with alcohol because exposure can become unreliable or unsafe.

  • Do not continue longer than 14 days unless the responsible medical authority reevaluates ongoing high risk. At the first sign of exposure, stop pyridostigmine and immediately begin atropine, pralidoxime, decontamination, airway support, and seizure treatment.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Increasing weakness, inability to handle secretions, declining vital capacity, hypoxemia, or uncertainty between myasthenic and cholinergic crisis requires emergency airway and neurology assessment.

  • Any credible soman symptoms require immediate rescue-system activation, decontamination, and evacuation.

Evidence Limitations

  • Soman effectiveness is supported under the Animal Rule because controlled human nerve-agent exposure trials are unethical.

  • Benefits and dosing from one formulation or indication cannot be transferred to another pyridostigmine product.

Regulatory Status

Pyridostigmine products have formulation-specific indications; the current 105 mg extended-release product is FDA approved under the Animal Rule as pretreatment for adult military personnel at high risk of soman exposure.
