Definition

Basophilic stippling is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis. A positive finding means multiple blue granules are distributed through erythrocytes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Basophilic stippling means multiple blue granules are distributed through erythrocytes.

Why It Matters

The Basophilic stippling laboratory finding can signal aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis. Impaired RNA degradation leaves ribosomal aggregates visible in mature cells. Basophilic stippling cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis

Assessment Method

Review a well-stained peripheral smear.

Positive Finding

Multiple blue granules are distributed through erythrocytes.

Mechanism

Impaired RNA degradation leaves ribosomal aggregates visible in mature cells.

Clinical Interpretation

The Basophilic stippling laboratory finding raises concern for aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis because impaired RNA degradation leaves ribosomal aggregates visible in mature cells. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Basophilic stippling does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Basophilic stippling to focus evaluation of aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis and the reviewed alternatives: Pappenheimer bodies, reticulocytosis, artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review a well-stained peripheral smear. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Basophilic stippling as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fine stippling can be nonspecific and stain artifact occurs. Therefore, Basophilic stippling cannot confirm or exclude aggregated ribosomal RNA in disordered erythropoiesis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Pappenheimer bodies

  • reticulocytosis

  • artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with Pappenheimer bodies, reticulocytosis, artifact.

False Negative Situations

Basophilic stippling may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
