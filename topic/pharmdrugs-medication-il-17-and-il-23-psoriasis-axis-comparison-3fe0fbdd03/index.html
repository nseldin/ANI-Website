Used To Treat

Product-specific plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, enthesitis-related arthritis, hidradenitis suppurativa, Crohn disease, and ulcerative colitis. Secukinumab directly blocks IL-17A. Ustekinumab blocks p40 shared by IL-12 and IL-23. Guselkumab blocks the IL-23-specific p19 subunit. The distinction changes immune effects and approved uses.

Description

IL-23 sustains pathogenic Th17 cells, which release IL-17A and related cytokines that recruit neutrophils, stimulate keratinocytes, and drive skin, enthesis, and joint inflammation. Secukinumab neutralizes the downstream effector IL-17A, often improving psoriasis rapidly but weakening mucocutaneous Candida defense and potentially worsening inflammatory bowel disease. Ustekinumab binds p40, interrupting both IL-12-driven Th1/NK signaling and IL-23-driven Th17 maintenance. Guselkumab binds p19, selectively blocking IL-23 while leaving IL-12 intact. A drug can therefore be excellent for skin disease yet inappropriate for a patient whose bowel phenotype points in the opposite direction.

Mechanism

IL-23 is a p19/p40 heterodimer that binds IL-23R plus IL-12R beta1 and activates JAK2/TYK2-STAT3 signaling, maintaining Th17 cells and their IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 output. IL-12 is a p35/p40 heterodimer that also uses p40 but promotes Th1 and NK-cell interferon-gamma responses. Ustekinumab sequesters shared p40 and prevents both cytokines from engaging IL-12R beta1. Guselkumab sequesters IL-23 p19 and preserves IL-12 signaling. IL-17A binds IL-17RA/RC on epithelial and stromal cells, activating ACT1-dependent NF-kappaB and chemokine programs; secukinumab neutralizes IL-17A itself. The downstream target explains candidiasis and bowel caution, while upstream p19 blockade changes the sustaining signal.

Boxed Warning

None of these three products currently has a U.S. boxed warning, but all can cause serious hypersensitivity and infection. Evaluate for tuberculosis and complete appropriate vaccines before treatment. Avoid live vaccines. Secukinumab requires particular caution with inflammatory bowel disease and mucocutaneous Candida; ustekinumab carries a rare posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome warning; guselkumab requires liver monitoring in specified inflammatory-bowel regimens and patients at risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, injection reaction, hypersensitivity, TB or other infection, and product-specific liver abnormalities

  • Secukinumab: candidiasis, IBD worsening, or eczematous eruption; ustekinumab: rare PRES; guselkumab: possible hepatotoxicity in IBD treatment

Contraindications

  • Prior severe hypersensitivity and clinically important active infection require resolution; screen TB and vaccination status before use.

  • Active or high-risk IBD weighs against IL-17 blockade, while neurologic symptoms or liver risk alter monitoring for the other targets.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask about chronic diarrhea, blood in stool, abdominal pain, oral or genital candidiasis, neurologic symptoms, infection, and vaccine plans before selecting or continuing therapy.

  • Track skin, nail, joint, enthesis, spine, and bowel outcomes separately because one domain can improve while another remains active.

Interactions

  • Do not combine biologic cytokine inhibitors or add a JAK inhibitor without a specialist plan; additive immunosuppression raises infection risk.

  • Cytokine normalization may alter CYP activity for some substrates, so narrow-index medications deserve review when inflammation changes substantially.

Key Labs

  • TB and infection evaluation, liver tests where indicated, vaccination review, and disease-domain assessments

  • CBC/liver context, bowel symptoms, Candida examination, neurologic assessment, and product-specific response

Nclex Traps

  • Ustekinumab blocks shared p40; guselkumab blocks IL-23-specific p19; secukinumab blocks the downstream IL-17A effector.

  • IL-17 supports mucosal antifungal defense, which explains candidiasis risk rather than making it an unrelated adverse effect.
