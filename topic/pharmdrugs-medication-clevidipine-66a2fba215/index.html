Used To Treat

Reduction of blood pressure in adults when oral therapy is not feasible or desirable, especially when minute-to-minute titration and rapid offset are useful. It is a short-term monitored infusion that requires a transition plan to longer-acting therapy.

Mechanism

Clevidipine inhibits Cav1.2 L-type channels in arterial smooth muscle. Less calcium-calmodulin signaling reduces myosin light-chain kinase activity, arterioles relax, systemic vascular resistance falls, and blood pressure drops without direct therapeutic AV-node blockade. The parent drug contains an ester linkage that esterases in blood and extravascular tissues rapidly hydrolyze to an inactive carboxylic-acid metabolite. The initial elimination phase removes about 85% to 90% of activity with a half-life near one minute, making clearance unlikely to depend substantially on renal or hepatic function. Rapid arterial dilation can trigger baroreceptor-mediated tachycardia, and the short offset creates rebound hypertension when the infusion is stopped before an oral replacement has begun working.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cleviprex is contraindicated with allergy to soybeans, soy products, eggs, or egg products; defective lipid metabolism; and severe aortic stenosis. Maintain strict asepsis because the lipid emulsion can support microbial growth. Use within 12 hours of stopper puncture and discard the remainder. The label recommends no more than 1,000 mL or an average 21 mg/hour in 24 hours because of lipid load, and experience beyond 72 hours is limited. Monitor for hypotension, reflex tachycardia, negative inotropy/heart failure, and rebound hypertension for at least 8 hours after prolonged infusion when no other therapy has replaced it.

Contraindications

  • Allergy to soybean/soy products or eggs/egg products; defective lipid metabolism such as pathologic hyperlipemia, lipoid nephrosis, or acute pancreatitis with hyperlipidemia; and severe aortic stenosis.

  • Do not use without continuous monitoring, a calibrated infusion device, a dedicated compatible line, and an oral/longer-acting transition plan when ongoing control is needed.

Nursing Essentials

  • Start and titrate only to the ordered condition-specific target with continuous BP/HR monitoring. The label starts at 1 to 2 mg/hour, permits initial doubling at 90-second intervals, then uses smaller changes every 5 to 10 minutes as target approaches; most patients respond at 4 to 6 mg/hour.

  • Invert the vial gently to homogenize the emulsion, inspect it, use aseptic technique and a calibrated pump, do not dilute or share the medication line, record puncture time, discard at 12 hours, and include clevidipine in the total daily lipid/calorie calculation.

Interactions

  • Clevidipine and its metabolites do not meaningfully inhibit or induce CYP enzymes at clinical concentrations, so pharmacokinetic interaction potential is low.

  • Other antihypertensives, nitrates, anesthetics, and volume depletion add hypotension. Concurrent propofol, parenteral nutrition, or another lipid emulsion adds lipid and calories even when there is no direct drug-drug interaction.

Key Labs

  • Continuous pressure and pulse, perfusion, neurologic status, urine output, ECG when indicated, and heart-failure findings. After stopping a prolonged infusion without replacement, continue pressure monitoring for rebound.

  • Review triglycerides, pancreatitis/lipid-metabolism history, total lipid and calorie intake, and cumulative 24-hour volume during prolonged or high-rate therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • Cleviprex is the milky soy/egg lipid-emulsion CCB. Its 12-hour rule means discard the punctured vial, not merely rotate a peripheral IV site.

  • Rapid esterase clearance permits tight titration but does not protect against rebound. Clevidipine is not a beta blocker and does not prevent beta-blocker-withdrawal tachycardia or ischemia.

Description

Clevidipine is an ultra-short-acting IV dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker supplied as a milky lipid emulsion. It relaxes arterioles and lowers afterload, while blood and extravascular tissue esterases hydrolyze its ester bond so rapidly that the initial half-life is about one minute. Pressure can therefore be adjusted at 90-second intervals early in titration and begins to recover quickly when the infusion stops. The formulation is as important as the molecule: soybean oil and egg-derived phospholipids create soy/egg-allergy and lipid-metabolism contraindications; each milliliter contributes about 0.2 g lipid and 2 kcal; the punctured vial must be discarded after 12 hours; and the line should not carry other medications. This is why clevidipine is not simply faster nicardipine.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, reflex tachycardia, flushing, and infusion-related discomfort can occur.

  • Severe hypotension, myocardial ischemia, worsening heart failure, rebound hypertension, hypertriglyceridemia or pancreatitis concern in susceptible patients, hypersensitivity, and contamination-related infection.
