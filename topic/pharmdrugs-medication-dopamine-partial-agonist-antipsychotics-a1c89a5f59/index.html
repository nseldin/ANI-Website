Used To Treat

Schizophrenia and drug-specific bipolar, adjunctive depression, autism-irritability, Tourette, or agitation indications.

Description

A partial agonist occupies dopamine receptors but produces less signaling than dopamine itself. In a dopamine-rich mesolimbic synapse it competes with dopamine and lowers net signaling; in a dopamine-poor pathway it supplies limited receptor activity instead of turning the receptor fully off. This functional stabilization helps explain lower average prolactin elevation, parkinsonism, and metabolic burden than several antagonist antipsychotics, but it does not mean the drugs are neutral or stimulating in a simple way.

Mechanism

Aripiprazole partially activates D2 and D3 receptors, partially activates 5-HT1A, and antagonizes 5-HT2A. Brexpiprazole has lower intrinsic D2 activity and relatively stronger 5-HT1A/5-HT2A actions; cariprazine is D3-preferring and has long-lived active metabolites. Partial D2 stimulation can leave motor circuits restless rather than rigid, producing akathisia. Dopamine effects in reward circuits can also unmask compulsive gambling, shopping, eating or sexual behavior. Long half-lives mean both benefit and adverse effects can change slowly after a dose adjustment.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; dopamine partial agonists is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Aripiprazole and brexpiprazole used as antidepressant adjuncts also carry antidepressant suicidality warning context. Monitor akathisia and impulse-control disorders explicitly.

Adverse Effects

  • Akathisia, restlessness, insomnia or nausea

  • Impulse-control disorders and activation/mania

  • EPS, NMS, tardive dyskinesia and metabolic effects remain possible

  • Long-lived metabolites can delay steady state and recovery from adverse effects

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity

  • Assess dementia, suicidality, impulse-control, metabolic, seizure and cardiovascular risk

  • Do not confuse partial agonism with a dopamine agonist treatment for Parkinson disease

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask whether the patient feels driven to move; visible pacing alone does not distinguish akathisia from agitation.

  • Ask patient and family about new gambling, shopping, binge eating or sexual urges because shame can hide the signal.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2D6 or CYP3A4 inhibitors raise aripiprazole/brexpiprazole exposure; strong CYP3A4 inducers lower it.

  • Cariprazine is CYP3A4 dependent and accumulates through long-lived metabolites.

  • Other dopamine blockers can erase the partial-agonist distinction and add EPS.

Key Labs

  • Metabolic panel and AIMS/EPS despite lower average burden

  • Impulse-control and akathisia screening

  • Dose/interactions reviewed against the long effective half-life

Nclex Traps

  • Akathisia can look like worsening mania or anxiety.

  • Partial agonist means lower intrinsic receptor activity than dopamine, not half a dose.

  • Clinical changes can lag dose changes because active metabolites persist.
