Class

Used To Treat

Susceptible urinary and respiratory infections. It is often paired with sulfamethoxazole, but the trimethoprim component has its own nursing concerns.

Mechanism

Trimethoprim works by blocking sequential bacterial folate synthesis: sulfonamides inhibit dihydropteroate synthase and trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. Rash/SJS, sulfa allergy history, hyperkalemia, renal effects, photosensitivity, marrow suppression, and warfarin interaction matter.

Nursing Essentials

  • Watch for rash, bone marrow suppression, hyperkalemia, kidney function changes, and infection response.

  • Teach hydration and to report rash, sore throat, fever, unusual bruising, or severe diarrhea.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe folate deficiency anemia, significant bone marrow suppression, severe renal/hepatic impairment, or sulfonamide allergy when the ordered product also contains sulfamethoxazole.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, and potassium supplements can increase hyperkalemia risk. Warfarin effect may increase.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, creatinine, CBC if prolonged therapy or high-risk client.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal-looking potassium can trend upward. Hyperkalemia risk matters more in kidney disease or when combined with potassium-raising drugs.

Description

Trimethoprim is an antifolate antibiotic: it works by blocking sequential bacterial folate synthesis: sulfonamides inhibit dihydropteroate synthase and trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. It is used for susceptible urinary and respiratory infections. It is often paired with sulfamethoxazole, but the trimethoprim component has its own nursing concerns.
