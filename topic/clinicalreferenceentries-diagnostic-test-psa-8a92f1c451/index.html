Summary

PSA (prostate-specific antigen) is a high-yield Oncology diagnostic reference. Prostate-specific antigen used in prostate cancer screening/monitoring context.

Quick Answer

PSA (prostate-specific antigen): Prostate-specific antigen used in prostate cancer screening/monitoring context. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Lower / stable

    Meaning

    PSA is not elevated or is stable for the client context; prostate cancer is less supported but not impossible.

  • Label

    High / rising

    Meaning

    Elevated or rapidly rising PSA can reflect prostate cancer, BPH, prostatitis, urinary retention, recent ejaculation/procedure, or instrumentation.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Interpret with age, prostate exam, symptoms, infection, medications, trend, and urology guidance.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Prostate-specific antigen used in prostate cancer screening/monitoring context.

    • Before / preparation

    • Confirm the purpose of screening, age/risk eligibility, prep requirements, and whether abnormal results require diagnostic confirmation.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Teach that screening estimates risk; it does not always diagnose. Promote follow-up and reduce fear with clear next steps.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Positive screening with symptoms, high-risk history, severe abnormality, or failure to follow up on a positive result.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Do not treat a screen as a final diagnosis unless the test is designed and interpreted that way.
