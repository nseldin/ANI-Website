Definition

Hirsutism means coarse, pigmented terminal hair grows in an androgen-dependent pattern where that amount or distribution is unexpected for the patient, commonly on the face, chest, abdomen, back, or inner thighs.

Plain Language

Coarse dark hair grows in areas that are especially sensitive to androgen hormones.

Why It Matters

Gradual isolated hirsutism is common and may be idiopathic or related to polycystic ovary syndrome, but rapid progression or virilization can signal marked androgen excess from a medication, adrenal disorder, or ovarian or adrenal tumor. The assessment must be respectful and must never equate body hair with hygiene, gender identity, or personal worth.

Associated Conditions

  • polycystic ovary syndrome, idiopathic follicular sensitivity, medications or androgen exposure, nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome, or an ovarian or adrenal androgen-secreting tumor

Assessment Method

With permission and privacy, assess onset, tempo, distribution, grooming history, menstrual or reproductive pattern, acne, scalp hair loss, voice change, muscle change, medications and supplements, and other endocrine findings; use a validated pattern score only when appropriate for the patient and setting.

Positive Finding

Coarse pigmented terminal hair is increased in androgen-dependent areas where that pattern is unexpected for the patient, such as the face, chest, abdomen, back, or inner thighs.

Mechanism

Higher androgen production or exposure, altered androgen metabolism, or greater follicular sensitivity converts fine vellus hair into coarse terminal hair in androgen-responsive follicles.

Clinical Interpretation

The Hirsutism pattern raises concern for polycystic ovary syndrome, idiopathic follicular sensitivity, medications or androgen exposure, nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome, or an ovarian or adrenal androgen-secreting tumor because higher androgen production or exposure, altered androgen metabolism, or greater follicular sensitivity converts fine vellus hair into coarse terminal hair in androgen-responsive follicles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hirsutism does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hirsutism to focus evaluation of polycystic ovary syndrome, idiopathic follicular sensitivity, medications or androgen exposure, nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome, or an ovarian or adrenal androgen-secreting tumor and the reviewed alternatives: hypertrichosis, familial hair pattern, medication-related hair growth, cosmetic or grooming variation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hirsutism as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Hair pattern varies with genetics, age, ancestry, grooming, medication, and patient preference; visual scoring is imperfect and hair amount does not reliably measure androgen concentration. Therefore, Hirsutism cannot confirm or exclude polycystic ovary syndrome, idiopathic follicular sensitivity, medications or androgen exposure, nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome, or an ovarian or adrenal androgen-secreting tumor by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hypertrichosis

  • familial hair pattern

  • medication-related hair growth

  • cosmetic or grooming variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by hypertrichosis, familial hair pattern, medication-related hair growth, cosmetic or grooming variation without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Hirsutism may be absent even when polycystic ovary syndrome, idiopathic follicular sensitivity, medications or androgen exposure, nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing syndrome, or an ovarian or adrenal androgen-secreting tumor is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status
