Class

PARP1/2 inhibitor and PARP-trapping antineoplastic

Used To Treat

Maintenance treatment of selected HRD-positive advanced ovarian/fallopian-tube/primary-peritoneal cancers after first-line platinum response and selected recurrent germline-BRCA-mutated disease after platinum response.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Monitor CBC weekly for the first month, monthly for the next 11 months, then periodically; stop and evaluate prolonged cytopenias for MDS/AML. Monitor blood pressure and heart rate closely, and urgently evaluate headache, seizure, vision change, confusion, or severe hypertension for PRES.

Mechanism

Niraparib inhibits PARP catalytic activity and stabilizes PARP-DNA complexes. Unrepaired single-strand lesions and stalled forks become double-strand breaks; homologous-recombination-deficient cells cannot accurately repair them, producing apoptosis. Off-target monoamine-transporter inhibition may contribute to pulse and blood-pressure effects.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use baseline weight and platelet count for the individualized starting-dose decision when the regimen requires it.

  • Trend nausea, fatigue, bruising/bleeding, infection, BP/pulse, neurologic symptoms, pregnancy risk, and adherence; uncontrolled hypertension needs treatment and possible dose modification.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets on the labeled schedule, BP and heart rate, creatinine/eGFR, liver context, pregnancy testing when applicable, and brain imaging/neurologic workup for suspected PRES.

Nclex Traps

  • Niraparib's hypertension/PRES pattern differentiates it from other PARP inhibitors.

  • A falling platelet count early in therapy can be dose-limiting before anemia becomes dominant.

Description

Niraparib is an oral PARP1/2 inhibitor that blocks enzymatic DNA repair and increases PARP-DNA trapping, generating lethal replication damage in susceptible ovarian cancers. It is distinguished within the class by early thrombocytopenia and clinically important hypertension/tachycardia, with additional MDS/AML, PRES, myelosuppression, and fetal risks.
