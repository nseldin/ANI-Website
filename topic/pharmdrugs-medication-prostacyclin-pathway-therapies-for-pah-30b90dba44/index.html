Used To Treat

Pulmonary arterial hypertension through continuous IV or subcutaneous infusion, inhalation, oral extended-release therapy, or oral/temporary IV selective IP-receptor agonism. Inhaled treprostinil also treats selected pulmonary hypertension associated with interstitial lung disease, and IV iloprost has a separate severe-frostbite indication.

Description

Prostacyclin-pathway drugs restore a vasodilator, antiplatelet, and antiproliferative signal that is deficient in PAH. Epoprostenol, treprostinil, and iloprost are prostacyclin analogs; selexipag is a non-prostanoid prodrug whose active metabolite selectively activates the IP receptor. Route is part of the pharmacology: epoprostenol has an extremely short effect and requires uninterrupted IV delivery, treprostinil spans IV/SQ/inhaled/oral products, iloprost uses repeated inhalation for PAH, and selexipag is titrated to an individual tolerated dose.

Mechanism

IP receptors on vascular smooth muscle and platelets couple to Gs. Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, raises cAMP, and activates protein kinase A, which reduces calcium-dependent contractile signaling and platelet activation. Lower pulmonary vascular resistance reduces right-ventricular afterload; platelet inhibition explains bleeding risk, and systemic vasodilation explains hypotension, flushing, and headache. Receptor activation fades according to each formulation's exposure, which is why interruption risk is most immediate with continuous ultra-short-acting epoprostenol.

Boxed Warning

The class has no single boxed warning, but abrupt withdrawal is a life-threatening pathway hazard for continuous epoprostenol and infused treprostinil. Loss of receptor stimulation permits pulmonary vascular tone to rebound while the failing right ventricle suddenly faces greater afterload. Keep backup pumps, batteries, medication, lines, and an emergency transition plan. Hypotension, bleeding, pulmonary edema, and route-specific device or airway complications remain clinically important.

Adverse Effects

  • Mechanism-related effects include headache, flushing, jaw pain, nausea, diarrhea, dizziness, hypotension, syncope, and bleeding.

  • Route-specific effects include central-line infection or thrombosis with IV therapy, severe SQ site pain, cough and bronchospasm with inhaled therapy, and GI intolerance with oral therapy.

Contraindications

  • Product contraindications differ. Epoprostenol is contraindicated in severe left-ventricular systolic heart failure, pulmonary edema, or hypersensitivity; selexipag is contraindicated with strong CYP2C8 inhibitors; other products may list no formal contraindication but still require hemodynamic and bleeding assessment.

  • Pulmonary edema after initiation can reveal pulmonary veno-occlusive disease because arterial dilation increases flow into an obstructed venous circuit.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs can add to platelet-inhibition bleeding risk; other vasodilators add hypotension.

  • Treprostinil and selexipag have CYP2C8 interaction rules. Formulations cannot be substituted milligram-for-milligram or breath-for-tablet because delivery and exposure differ.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify exact molecule, brand, formulation, route, concentration, device, schedule, titration step, interruption plan, and backup supplies before administration.

  • Do not stop an infusion to troubleshoot. Maintain drug delivery through a verified backup route or pump according to the emergency protocol while the line or device problem is corrected.

  • Monitor blood pressure, syncope, oxygenation, right-heart symptoms, bleeding, jaw pain, diarrhea, infusion sites and lines, inhaled-airway symptoms, adherence, and functional response.

Key Labs

  • No class serum concentration guides dosing. Follow hemodynamics and oxygenation, CBC and bleeding assessment, liver function for treprostinil exposure context, infection evaluation for central access, and PAH functional/right-heart measures.

Nclex Traps

  • Epoprostenol interruption is a medical emergency, not a routine late dose.

  • Treprostinil products share an ingredient but not interchangeable administration instructions.

  • Selexipag activates the IP receptor but is not a prostacyclin analog.

  • Inhaled therapy still has systemic hypotension and bleeding risk.
