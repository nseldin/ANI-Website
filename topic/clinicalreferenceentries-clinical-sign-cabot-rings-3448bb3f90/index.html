Definition

Cabot rings is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to severe dyserythropoiesis. A positive finding means thin red-violet ring or figure-eight structures appear in erythrocytes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cabot rings means thin red-violet ring or figure-eight structures appear in erythrocytes.

Why It Matters

The Cabot rings laboratory finding can signal severe dyserythropoiesis. Residual mitotic-spindle or nuclear-envelope material persists after abnormal erythroid maturation. Cabot rings cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe dyserythropoiesis

Assessment Method

Review peripheral smear.

Positive Finding

Thin red-violet ring or figure-eight structures appear in erythrocytes.

Mechanism

Residual mitotic-spindle or nuclear-envelope material persists after abnormal erythroid maturation.

Clinical Interpretation

The Cabot rings laboratory finding raises concern for severe dyserythropoiesis because residual mitotic-spindle or nuclear-envelope material persists after abnormal erythroid maturation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cabot rings does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

They are rare and can be confused with parasites or stain artifact. Therefore, Cabot rings cannot confirm or exclude severe dyserythropoiesis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • malaria

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with malaria, Howell-Jolly bodies, artifact.

False Negative Situations

Cabot rings may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
