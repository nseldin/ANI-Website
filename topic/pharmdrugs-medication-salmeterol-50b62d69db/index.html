Used To Treat

Twice-daily maintenance treatment of asthma only with concurrent inhaled corticosteroid therapy, prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm in selected patients, and maintenance bronchodilation in COPD. Salmeterol is not indicated for relief of acute bronchospasm.

Mechanism

The saligenin head of salmeterol activates beta2 receptors while its long aryloxyalkyl side chain anchors in a hydrophobic exosite and cell membrane. Receptor Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, raises cyclic AMP, and activates protein kinase A, which reduces myosin light-chain kinase and calcium-dependent contraction. Lipophilic retention and repeated receptor engagement prolong bronchodilation. Access to the active receptor conformation is slower than with formoterol or albuterol, explaining the delayed onset. Systemic beta2 activation can produce tremor, tachycardia, intracellular potassium shift, hyperglycemia, and QT effects. The drug relaxes muscle but does not repress inflammatory gene transcription, which explains the mandatory ICS partner in asthma.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING for SEREVENT DISKUS: LABA monotherapy without an inhaled corticosteroid increases asthma-related death risk. Use salmeterol for asthma only as add-on therapy when an ICS-containing regimen is required, and discontinue standalone LABA if the patient is no longer receiving the ICS. The boxed warning does not turn salmeterol into a rescue medicine; acute bronchospasm requires a rapid-acting reliever and urgent assessment when severe.

Contraindications

  • Asthma treatment without concomitant long-term inhaled corticosteroid therapy

  • Primary treatment of status asthmaticus or acute episodes; severe milk-protein allergy or product hypersensitivity for the lactose powder

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the patient takes an ICS-containing asthma controller and has a separate rapid reliever. Do not accept Serevent as the only inhaler for asthma.

  • Track nighttime symptoms, rescue use, peak flow, adherence, device technique, and cardiovascular effects; reassess deterioration rather than adding doses.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can markedly increase salmeterol exposure and cardiovascular toxicity.

  • Other LABAs or sympathomimetics duplicate toxicity; nonselective beta blockers oppose bronchodilation; diuretics can magnify hypokalemia.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, peak flow or spirometry, rescue frequency, heart rate/rhythm, and inhaler technique

  • Potassium, glucose, and ECG when high exposure, interacting drugs, or toxicity is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Salmeterol is a maintenance LABA with slow onset; it is not interchangeable with rapid-onset formoterol for MART.

  • LABA monotherapy is dangerous in asthma even when symptoms seem better.

Description

Salmeterol is a long-acting beta2 agonist that keeps airway smooth muscle relaxed for about 12 hours but begins too slowly to be a rescue inhaler. Its long lipophilic side chain interacts with an accessory region near the beta2 receptor, helping the active head repeatedly engage the receptor while the molecule remains in the membrane-receptor environment. In asthma, salmeterol must never be used without an inhaled corticosteroid because bronchodilation can reduce symptoms while eosinophilic inflammation and attack risk continue. In COPD, LABA monotherapy is an accepted maintenance pathway because the disease biology and evidence differ. A patient acutely short of breath needs a rapid reliever, not extra Serevent doses.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, tremor, palpitations, tachycardia, muscle cramps, throat irritation, cough, or nervousness

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, QT prolongation, arrhythmia, ischemia, hypersensitivity, or asthma-related harm with monotherapy
