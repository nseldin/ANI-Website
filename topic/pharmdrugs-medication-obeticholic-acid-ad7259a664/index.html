Class

Withdrawn farnesoid-X-receptor agonist bile-acid synthesis suppressor

Used To Treat

Formerly approved in the United States for primary biliary cholangitis with inadequate ursodiol response or ursodiol intolerance; the accelerated approval was withdrawn on November 24, 2025, so this is a historical recognition and safety card rather than a current treatment recommendation.

Boxed Warning

Historical U.S. boxed warning and current-status alert: Obeticholic acid was associated with hepatic decompensation and failure, sometimes fatal or requiring transplant, in PBC patients with cirrhosis. Prior labeling contraindicated decompensated cirrhosis, previous decompensation, and compensated cirrhosis with portal hypertension. Its U.S. accelerated approval was withdrawn November 24, 2025; do not present it as currently available routine PBC therapy.

Mechanism

Intestinal FXR induces fibroblast growth factor 19, which signals to the liver to suppress CYP7A1, the rate-limiting enzyme in classical bile-acid synthesis. Hepatic FXR also induces small heterodimer partner and bile-acid export/transport programs. Reducing synthesis can lower cholestatic bile-acid burden, but excessive exposure or impaired hepatic handling in cirrhosis can worsen hepatic injury.

Nursing Essentials

  • Reconcile historical exposure and document that the U.S. indication is withdrawn before answering a medication-option question.

  • For a patient previously exposed, assess pruritus, jaundice, ascites, edema, GI bleeding, encephalopathy and liver synthetic function.

Contraindications

  • Historical: decompensated cirrhosis, prior decompensation or compensated cirrhosis with portal hypertension

  • Complete biliary obstruction

  • Current U.S. prescribing is inappropriate after withdrawal of the PBC approval

Interactions

  • Bile-acid sequestrants reduce absorption and historically required wide dose separation.

  • Warfarin INR could decrease and required monitoring.

  • CYP1A2 substrate exposure could increase; prior labeling required interaction review.

Key Labs

  • Bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, AST/ALT, albumin and INR

  • Platelets and portal-hypertension/decompensation assessment

  • Lipid panel including HDL-C and symptom burden such as pruritus

Nclex Traps

  • A familiar brand name does not mean the drug remains an active U.S. option.

  • Biomarker improvement under accelerated approval did not guarantee proven long-term clinical benefit.

  • Pruritus is common, but jaundice, ascites, bleeding or confusion are hepatic-decompensation alarms.

Description

Obeticholic acid is a semisynthetic bile-acid analog and potent farnesoid X receptor agonist that suppressed hepatic bile-acid synthesis and increased transport/disposal signaling. Its U.S. accelerated PBC approval was withdrawn in 2025 after the benefit-risk and confirmatory-evidence process; the drug remains important to recognize in prior exposure and safety histories without being recommended as current routine therapy.

Adverse Effects

  • Often severe or dose-limiting pruritus, fatigue and abdominal discomfort

  • Hepatic decompensation/failure, jaundice, ascites, variceal bleeding and encephalopathy

  • Reduced HDL-C and other lipid changes
