Definition

Biot breathing is an older, inconsistently defined label for an irregular respiratory pattern in which groups of breaths are interrupted by unpredictable apnea. The observed rate, depth, rhythm, and pauses should be documented instead of assuming one neurologic location.

Plain Language

Breathing occurs in irregular groups with sudden, unpredictable pauses.

Why It Matters

This pattern can accompany severe neurologic illness, brain injury, meningitis, opioid toxicity, or failing ventilation. Airway and ventilation take priority over naming the eponym, and the pattern alone cannot reliably identify where the nervous system is injured.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

Observe breathing continuously.

Positive Finding

Groups of breaths with irregular depth are interrupted by unpredictable apnea.

Mechanism

Damage to medullary rhythm generation disrupts regular respiratory timing.

Clinical Interpretation

The Biot breathing pattern raises concern for medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness because damage to medullary rhythm generation disrupts regular respiratory timing. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Biot breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Biot breathing to focus evaluation of medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness and the reviewed alternatives: Cheyne-Stokes breathing, cluster breathing, agonal respirations. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe breathing continuously. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Biot breathing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Literature often conflates Biot and ataxic breathing. Therefore, Biot breathing cannot confirm or exclude medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Cheyne-Stokes breathing

  • cluster breathing

  • agonal respirations

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by Cheyne-Stokes breathing, cluster breathing, agonal respirations without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Biot breathing may be absent even when medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status

Historical term with inconsistent definitions; describe the pattern precisely. Biot breathing remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness. Apply the reviewed limitation for Biot breathing: Literature often conflates Biot and ataxic breathing.

Safety Precautions

Observe Biot breathing with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe breathing continuously. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use objective rate, depth, pauses, and neurologic status instead of the eponym alone.

Nursing Relevance

For Biot breathing, document which defined components are present or absent, their timing and trend, and whether the positive pattern is met: Groups of breaths with irregular depth are interrupted by unpredictable apnea. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Observe breathing continuously. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, medullary dysfunction or severe neurologic illness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
