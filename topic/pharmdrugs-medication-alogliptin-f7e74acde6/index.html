Used To Treat

Type 2 diabetes as an oral incretin-based glucose-lowering therapy.

Mechanism

Alogliptin works by inhibiting dipeptidyl peptidase-4, the enzyme that breaks down endogenous incretin hormones. More intact GLP-1 and GIP increases glucose-dependent insulin release and reduces glucagon with low hypoglycemia risk unless combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

Boxed Warning

No universal boxed warning; pancreatitis, severe joint pain, hypersensitivity, and heart failure warning for selected agents are high-yield.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity

  • renal impairment may require adjustment

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor glucose/A1c, pancreatitis symptoms, renal dosing, and heart failure symptoms for selected agents.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Insulin/sulfonylureas increase hypoglycemia risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • glucose/A1c

  • renal function

  • lipase if pancreatitis symptoms

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • Severe abdominal pain can be pancreatitis; do not just treat nausea.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Description

Alogliptin is a DPP-4 inhibitor antidiabetic: it works by inhibiting dipeptidyl peptidase-4, the enzyme that breaks down endogenous incretin hormones. It is used for type 2 diabetes as an oral incretin-based glucose-lowering therapy.
