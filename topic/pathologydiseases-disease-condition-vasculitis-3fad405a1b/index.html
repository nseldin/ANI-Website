Definition

Vasculitis develops when immune, infectious, drug, or malignant injury inflames vessel walls and causes stenosis, thrombosis, aneurysm, hemorrhage, and organ ischemia according to vessel size. The characteristic pattern is constitutional symptoms plus palpable purpura, neuropathy, kidney injury, lung hemorrhage, headache, pulse loss, abdominal ischemia, or other multisystem vascular clues. Confirm or stage it with CBC, inflammatory markers, urinalysis, creatinine, liver, complement, autoimmune, infection, and medication evaluation; distinguish it from infection, embolism, atherosclerosis, thrombotic microangiopathy, connective-tissue disease, and drug reaction by cultures, vessel pattern, tissue, and organ distribution. The priority is to stabilize threatened lung, kidney, brain, gut, eye, or limb ischemia because rapid kidney failure can complicate Vasculitis. can follow if the process advances.

Pathophysiology

Inflammation is present, often from infection, autoimmune injury, obstruction, or tissue damage depending on the condition.

Signs Symptoms

  • fever may be present but older adults/newborns may be atypical

  • localized pain/swelling/drainage when tissue infection is present

Diagnostics

  • CBC, inflammatory markers, urinalysis, creatinine, liver, complement, autoimmune, infection, and medication evaluation

  • organ-specific vascular imaging

  • biopsy of an accessible active site when classification changes immunosuppression

  • Distinguish Vasculitis from infection, embolism, atherosclerosis, thrombotic microangiopathy, connective-tissue disease, and drug reaction by cultures, vessel pattern, tissue, and organ distribution.

Labs

  • WBC may rise or fall

  • lactate may rise with sepsis

  • CRP/ESR may rise with inflammation

  • organ labs can worsen in severe infection

Treatments

  • stabilize threatened lung, kidney, brain, gut, eye, or limb ischemia

  • remove an infectious or drug cause

  • use disease-specific glucocorticoid and steroid-sparing immunosuppression only after classification and infection assessment

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate for vision change, hemoptysis, hypoxemia, new neurologic deficit, absent pulse, severe abdominal pain, oliguria, or shock because retinal, pulmonary, cerebral, limb, bowel, or renal ischemia is time-critical.

  • Map constitutional, skin, joint, nerve, airway, lung, kidney, gastrointestinal, ocular, and vascular symptoms because vasculitis is a disease family whose urgency and treatment depend on vessel size and threatened organ.

  • Trend blood pressure, pulses, urine output and sediment, creatinine, blood count, inflammatory markers, oxygenation, and subtype-specific studies because ischemia, hemorrhage, or glomerular injury may progress silently.

  • Administer glucocorticoid, immunosuppressive, antimicrobial, antithrombotic, or prophylactic therapy exactly for the confirmed subtype because treating infection-related and autoimmune vasculitis as interchangeable can worsen the cause.

  • Monitor fever, blood counts, glucose, bone protection, vaccination status, and opportunistic-infection symptoms, and obtain cultures promptly when infection is suspected, because sustained immune suppression controls vessel injury while creating serious preventable infectious and metabolic complications.

Complications

  • rapid kidney failure can complicate Vasculitis.

  • pulmonary hemorrhage can complicate Vasculitis.

  • stroke can complicate Vasculitis.

  • blindness can complicate Vasculitis.

  • aneurysm rupture can complicate Vasculitis.

  • bowel infarction can complicate Vasculitis.

  • neuropathy can complicate Vasculitis.

  • treatment infection can complicate Vasculitis.

Red Flags

  • Sudden visual loss, diplopia, severe new headache, or jaw claudication

  • Hemoptysis, rapidly worsening dyspnea, hypoxemia, or diffuse pulmonary bleeding

  • New focal neurologic deficit, absent pulse, cool limb, or severe asymmetric pain

  • Oliguria, rapidly rising creatinine, severe abdominal pain, bleeding, or shock

Patient Education

  • Know the exact vasculitis subtype and involved organs because the word vasculitis alone does not define treatment, prognosis, or emergency risks.

  • Report fever or infection exposure promptly while immunosuppressed and never stop corticosteroids suddenly because both infection and adrenal crisis can become dangerous.

Nclex Traps

  • Vasculitis is a family of diseases, not a single positive antibody

  • Immunosuppression can be disastrous when an occult infection is the true cause
