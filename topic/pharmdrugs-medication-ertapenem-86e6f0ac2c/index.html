Used To Treat

Susceptible complicated intra-abdominal, skin/diabetic foot, community-acquired pulmonary, complicated urinary, pelvic, and selected bloodstream infections, plus surgical prophylaxis. It is often used for ESBL-producing Enterobacterales when once-daily outpatient therapy is appropriate, but not for Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, or Enterococcus coverage.

Mechanism

Ertapenem acylates PBPs and produces time-dependent wall failure. A benzoic-acid side chain increases albumin binding and prolongs systemic half-life, explaining once-daily dosing. High binding also means severe hypoalbuminemia can increase free fraction, clearance, distribution, and exposure variability, so critical illness may not behave like stable outpatient pharmacokinetics. Renal elimination requires dose reduction, and accumulation can antagonize CNS inhibitory signaling. Like every carbapenem, ertapenem can rapidly lower valproate concentrations.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Once daily does not mean low risk. Renal accumulation can cause hallucinations, myoclonus, encephalopathy, or seizures, and valproate concentrations can fall profoundly.

Contraindications

  • Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity is a contraindication; IM ertapenem reconstituted with lidocaine has additional amide-local-anesthetic contraindications.

  • Do not use when Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Enterococcus, MRSA, atypicals, or meningitis organisms require coverage because once-daily convenience does not fill spectrum gaps.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm cultures exclude the major spectrum gaps before narrowing to ertapenem, then verify renal function, albumin/critical illness context, CNS history, valproate, and outpatient access plan.

  • Teach immediate reporting of confusion, hallucinations, jerking, or seizure because outpatient once-daily therapy can hide accumulating neurotoxicity between visits.

Interactions

  • Ertapenem markedly lowers valproate/divalproex; probenecid prolongs exposure and is not routinely combined.

  • Nephrotoxic co-therapy can reduce clearance and increase CNS exposure.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, albumin in critical illness, mental status, and valproate concentration if co-exposure occurs.

  • A urine or wound isolate should be interpreted with the syndrome because colonization does not justify prolonged broad carbapenem therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • Ertapenem is the once-daily carbapenem that does not reliably cover Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, or Enterococcus.

  • Its convenience makes it useful for selected outpatient ESBL therapy, not universally preferable to narrower oral options.

Description

Ertapenem, branded Invanz, is a highly protein-bound once-daily carbapenem beta-lactam antibiotic that binds multiple PBPs and blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking in susceptible bacteria. It resists many ESBL and AmpC enzymes and provides broad activity against susceptible Enterobacterales, streptococci, MSSA, and anaerobes. Its defining limitation is weak or absent dependable activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter species, and Enterococcus. That gap can be beneficial stewardship when those organisms are not threats because ertapenem avoids unnecessary nonfermenter pressure while preserving convenient exposure. It is not a meningitis drug and can still cause neurotoxicity, especially with renal impairment, low albumin, older age, or CNS disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, headache, infusion reaction, rash, liver-test elevation, thrombocytosis, candidiasis, and CDI.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, delirium, hallucination, myoclonus, seizure, cytopenias, interstitial nephritis, and transaminase injury can occur.
