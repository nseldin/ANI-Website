Used To Treat

Product-specific maintenance bronchodilation in COPD and, only when combined with an inhaled corticosteroid in an appropriate product and prescribed regimen, asthma maintenance or anti-inflammatory reliever/MART treatment. Standalone formoterol does not treat acute deterioration and must not be used alone in asthma.

Mechanism

Formoterol binds beta2 receptors with high intrinsic efficacy. Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, cyclic AMP rises, and protein kinase A reduces myosin light-chain kinase activity and calcium-dependent contraction, rapidly relaxing airway smooth muscle. The molecule's polar groups allow relatively fast access through airway fluid, while lipophilic portions partition into the cell membrane and slowly return to the receptor, extending action. Systemic receptor stimulation shifts potassium into skeletal muscle and increases tremor, heart rate, glucose, and lactate. Formoterol does not directly switch off inflammatory transcription; the inhaled corticosteroid in an AIR/MART inhaler supplies that protection with each symptom-driven dose.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Standalone formoterol products remain unsafe as asthma monotherapy and are not indicated for acute bronchospasm. An ICS-formoterol inhaler may be a reliever only when the exact product, strength, patient, and regimen are prescribed for AIR/MART. Excessive use can cause arrhythmia, QT effects, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, or paradoxical bronchospasm, and worsening symptoms require reassessment.

Contraindications

  • Do not use without an inhaled corticosteroid in asthma

  • Do not use as primary treatment for status asthmaticus or acute deterioration; do not use with hypersensitivity to product components

Nursing Essentials

  • Name the complete product: standalone nebulized formoterol and ICS-formoterol inhalers have different roles. Verify the written reliever and maximum-dose plan.

  • Assess rescue frequency, heart rate, potassium risk, inhaler or nebulizer technique, and whether worsening disease is being masked.

Interactions

  • Other LABAs, sympathomimetics, MAO inhibitors, tricyclics, and QT-prolonging drugs add cardiovascular risk.

  • Nonselective beta blockers oppose bronchodilation; non-potassium-sparing diuretics can magnify hypokalemia.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, spirometry or peak flow, exacerbations, reliever use, heart rate/rhythm, and technique

  • Potassium, glucose, lactate, and ECG during high exposure or suspected toxicity

Nclex Traps

  • Formoterol is rapid-onset despite being long acting.

  • Rapid onset supports ICS-formoterol reliever logic; it does not permit formoterol-only asthma rescue.

Description

Formoterol is unusual among LABAs because it begins bronchodilation rapidly like a rescue beta agonist yet remains active for about 12 hours. Its molecular balance of water solubility and lipophilicity lets some drug reach beta2 receptors quickly while another fraction remains in airway membranes as a depot. This property allows low-dose ICS-formoterol combinations to serve as anti-inflammatory relievers or MART under validated plans. It does not make standalone nebulized formoterol an asthma rescue drug: products such as Perforomist are COPD maintenance treatments, and any LABA used in asthma must be paired with an inhaled corticosteroid.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor, nervousness, headache, palpitations, tachycardia, muscle cramps, nausea, or insomnia

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, QT prolongation, arrhythmia, ischemia, or hypersensitivity
