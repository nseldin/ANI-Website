Definition

Dimple sign is a palpation skin sign. It is a clue to dermatofibroma. A positive finding means the center depresses inward.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dimple sign means the center depresses inward.

Why It Matters

A positive Dimple sign response can signal dermatofibroma. A fibrous dermal lesion is tethered to surrounding collagen, so lateral pressure pulls it down. Dimple sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dermatofibroma

Assessment Method

Pinch or compress the lesion laterally.

Positive Finding

The center depresses inward.

Mechanism

A fibrous dermal lesion is tethered to surrounding collagen, so lateral pressure pulls it down.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Dimple sign response raises concern for dermatofibroma because a fibrous dermal lesion is tethered to surrounding collagen, so lateral pressure pulls it down. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dimple sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dimple sign to focus evaluation of dermatofibroma and the reviewed alternatives: melanoma, DFSP, insect-bite scar. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Pinch or compress the lesion laterally. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dimple sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Some dermatofibromas do not dimple and malignant lesions can appear similar. Therefore, Dimple sign cannot confirm or exclude dermatofibroma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • melanoma

  • DFSP

  • insect-bite scar

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from melanoma, DFSP, insect-bite scar, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Dimple sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current supportive clue; not definitive. Interpret Dimple sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Some dermatofibromas do not dimple and malignant lesions can appear similar.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Dimple sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Pinch or compress the lesion laterally. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Changing, atypical, or large lesions need evaluation despite a dimple response.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Dimple sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The center depresses inward. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Pinch or compress the lesion laterally. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, dermatofibroma, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Dimple sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Changing, atypical, or large lesions need evaluation despite a dimple response. Base escalation for Dimple sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
