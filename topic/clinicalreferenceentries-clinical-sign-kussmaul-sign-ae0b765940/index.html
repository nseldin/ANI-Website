Definition

Kussmaul sign is an observed venous-pressure sign. It is a clue to impaired right-ventricular filling. A positive finding means jugular venous pressure rises or fails to fall with inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Kussmaul sign means jugular venous pressure rises or fails to fall with inspiration.

Why It Matters

The observed Kussmaul sign finding can signal impaired right-ventricular filling. Increased venous return enters a right ventricle that cannot accommodate volume, backing pressure into the neck veins. Kussmaul sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • impaired right-ventricular filling

Assessment Method

Observe jugular venous pressure during quiet inspiration with the torso appropriately elevated.

Positive Finding

Jugular venous pressure rises or fails to fall with inspiration.

Mechanism

Increased venous return enters a right ventricle that cannot accommodate volume, backing pressure into the neck veins.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Kussmaul sign finding raises concern for impaired right-ventricular filling because increased venous return enters a right ventricle that cannot accommodate volume, backing pressure into the neck veins. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Kussmaul sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Kussmaul sign to focus evaluation of impaired right-ventricular filling and the reviewed alternatives: tension pneumothorax, massive pulmonary embolism, measurement artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe jugular venous pressure during quiet inspiration with the torso appropriately elevated. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Kussmaul sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It may be absent in disease and respiratory effort or tricuspid disease can confuse the waveform. Therefore, Kussmaul sign cannot confirm or exclude impaired right-ventricular filling by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tension pneumothorax

  • massive pulmonary embolism

  • measurement artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tension pneumothorax, massive pulmonary embolism, measurement artifact.

False Negative Situations

Kussmaul sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of impaired right-ventricular filling.

Safety Precautions

Observe Kussmaul sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe jugular venous pressure during quiet inspiration with the torso appropriately elevated. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not confuse this with Kussmaul breathing, which is a respiratory pattern.
