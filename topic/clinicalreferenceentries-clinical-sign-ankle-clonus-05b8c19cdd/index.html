Definition

Ankle clonus is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability. A positive finding means rhythmic alternating plantarflexion and dorsiflexion repeats, especially when sustained.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ankle clonus means rhythmic alternating plantarflexion and dorsiflexion repeats, especially when sustained.

Why It Matters

A positive Ankle clonus response can signal marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability. Sudden muscle stretch triggers self-reinforcing hyperexcitable stretch-reflex cycles. Ankle clonus cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability

Assessment Method

With the knee supported, briskly dorsiflex the foot and maintain gentle pressure.

Positive Finding

Rhythmic alternating plantarflexion and dorsiflexion repeats, especially when sustained.

Mechanism

Sudden muscle stretch triggers self-reinforcing hyperexcitable stretch-reflex cycles.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ankle clonus response raises concern for marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability because sudden muscle stretch triggers self-reinforcing hyperexcitable stretch-reflex cycles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ankle clonus does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ankle clonus to focus evaluation of marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability and the reviewed alternatives: physiologic brisk reflex, ankle tremor, rigidity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the knee supported, briskly dorsiflex the foot and maintain gentle pressure. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ankle clonus as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Brief unsustained beats vary with anxiety and age. Therefore, Ankle clonus cannot confirm or exclude marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • physiologic brisk reflex

  • ankle tremor

  • rigidity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from physiologic brisk reflex, ankle tremor, rigidity, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ankle clonus may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current upper-motor-neuron sign; a few beats may be normal in some people. Interpret Ankle clonus in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Brief unsustained beats vary with anxiety and age.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ankle clonus, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the knee supported, briskly dorsiflex the foot and maintain gentle pressure. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force an injured ankle and report sustained versus unsustained clonus.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: With the knee supported, briskly dorsiflex the foot and maintain gentle pressure. Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
