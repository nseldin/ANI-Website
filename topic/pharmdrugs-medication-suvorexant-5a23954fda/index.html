Class

Dual OX1R/OX2R orexin receptor antagonist hypnotic

Used To Treat

Adult insomnia characterized by difficulty with sleep onset, sleep maintenance or both.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current BELSOMRA labeling has no boxed warning. It is contraindicated in narcolepsy. CNS depression and next-day impairment increase with dose, inadequate sleep time, alcohol or other depressants; discontinue immediately after sleep-walking, sleep-driving or another complex behavior. Monitor depression/suicidality and compromised respiratory function.

Mechanism

Orexin neurons normally stabilize wakefulness and prevent inappropriate REM-state intrusion. Suvorexant occupies both orexin receptors, lowering wake-promoting signaling so sleep can begin and persist. Because loss of orexin signaling is central to narcolepsy, receptor blockade explains both the narcolepsy contraindication and dose-related REM-transition adverse effects.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give no more than once nightly within 30 minutes of bed with at least 7 hours before planned awakening; use the lowest effective dose and review strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors, alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, fall risk, sleep apnea/COPD and obesity exposure effects.

  • Assess next-day driving before discharge or teaching, and explain sleep paralysis/hallucinations so they are reported rather than mistaken for psychosis; investigate cataplexy-like weakness and persistent insomnia.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level. Monitor alertness/driving, falls, respiratory symptoms, mood/suicidality, sleep behaviors, REM-transition symptoms and CYP3A/CNS-depressant exposure.

Nclex Traps

  • At least 7 hours in bed is required but does not guarantee next-day competence, especially at 20 mg or with interactions.

  • Orexin blockade is contraindicated in narcolepsy; it does not treat narcoleptic sleepiness.

Description

Suvorexant is a Schedule IV dual orexin receptor antagonist that suppresses hypothalamic wake drive by blocking orexin A and B at OX1R and OX2R. It promotes sleep without directly enhancing GABA-A receptors, but can still impair alertness and driving the next day, cause complex sleep behaviors, and produce sleep paralysis, vivid sleep-transition hallucinations or cataplexy-like leg weakness.
