Used To Treat

Osteoporosis at high or very high fracture risk when anabolic treatment is appropriate, followed by antiresorptive therapy to preserve gained bone.

Description

Teriparatide and romosozumab build bone through different signals. Daily intermittent teriparatide pulses the PTH1 receptor, stimulating osteoblast recruitment and survival more than resorption. Romosozumab neutralizes osteocyte-derived sclerostin, releasing Wnt signaling so formation rises while resorption falls. Both are time-limited because their anabolic advantage wanes, not because the skeleton is fully cured.

Mechanism

Intermittent PTH1R Gs-cAMP/PKA and Gq signaling increases osteoblast lineage activity; continuous PTH exposure would favor RANKL and resorption, explaining why daily pulses differ from hyperparathyroidism. Sclerostin normally binds LRP5/6 and restrains canonical Wnt-beta-catenin signaling. Romosozumab removes that brake, but formation markers peak early and decline, so labeling limits therapy to 12 monthly doses. Subsequent antiresorptive therapy prevents remodeling from erasing gains.

Boxed Warning

Romosozumab carries a BOXED WARNING for possible increased myocardial infarction, stroke and cardiovascular death; do not initiate after MI or stroke within the previous year and stop if one occurs. Teriparatide no longer has a U.S. boxed warning, but osteosarcoma-risk populations remain excluded.

Adverse Effects

  • Teriparatide: transient hypercalcemia, nausea, leg cramps and orthostatic symptoms

  • Romosozumab: arthralgia, headache, injection reactions and hypocalcemia

  • Both require later antiresorptive planning; romosozumab also has ONJ/AFF and cardiovascular concerns

Contraindications

  • Teriparatide: avoid bone metastases, skeletal malignancy, prior skeletal radiation, Paget disease/open epiphyses and hypercalcemic disorders

  • Romosozumab: hypocalcemia and serious hypersensitivity; do not start within 1 year of MI/stroke

  • Neither is routine low-risk osteopenia therapy

Nursing Essentials

  • Choose pathway based on fracture urgency, cardiovascular risk, calcium status, cancer/radiation history and ability to complete follow-on therapy.

  • Teach initial teriparatide doses seated/lying and that a full romosozumab monthly dose requires two injections.

Interactions

  • Teriparatide hypercalcemia can increase digoxin toxicity concern.

  • Other calcium-raising therapies increase teriparatide hypercalcemia.

  • Potent antiresorptive sequencing requires a planned protocol rather than casual overlap or a treatment gap.

Key Labs

  • Calcium, 25-OH vitamin D, renal function and PTH context

  • Bone density and fracture history

  • Cardiovascular history for romosozumab and skeletal malignancy/radiation/Paget history for teriparatide

Nclex Traps

  • Intermittent PTH can build bone even though chronic high PTH resorbs bone.

  • Romosozumab is limited to 12 doses because its anabolic effect wanes.

  • Stopping an anabolic without antiresorptive consolidation allows gains to fade.
