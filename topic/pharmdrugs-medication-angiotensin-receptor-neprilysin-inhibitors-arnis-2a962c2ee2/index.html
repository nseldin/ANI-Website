Used To Treat

Chronic heart failure according to current sacubitril/valsartan labeling and guideline-defined phenotype. In symptomatic HFrEF, ARNI therapy is recommended to reduce morbidity and mortality when appropriate; use in other ejection-fraction ranges is more selective.

Description

ARNI means angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor. The available therapy combines sacubitril, a prodrug whose active metabolite inhibits neprilysin, with valsartan, an ARB. Neprilysin normally degrades natriuretic peptides and several other vasoactive peptides. Inhibition increases natriuresis, vasodilation, cyclic-GMP signaling, and antifibrotic effects. Neprilysin also degrades angiotensin II, so inhibiting it alone could increase an opposing vasoconstrictor signal; valsartan blocks AT1 and prevents that counter-response. The combination is disease modifying rather than merely a diuretic: it changes maladaptive neurohormonal signaling and reduces heart-failure events in appropriate patients. Its practical dangers follow the same biology: hypotension from vasodilation, hyperkalemia and renal changes from AT1 blockade, fetal toxicity from RAAS disruption, and angioedema from vasoactive-peptide accumulation.

Mechanism

Sacubitril is converted by esterases to LBQ657, which inhibits neprilysin. Atrial and B-type natriuretic peptides activate particulate guanylyl cyclase receptors, raising cyclic GMP and promoting natriuresis, diuresis, vasodilation, reduced sympathetic tone, and less hypertrophy or fibrosis. Valsartan selectively blocks AT1-mediated vasoconstriction, aldosterone, sodium retention, and remodeling. BNP is a neprilysin substrate and may rise after treatment, while NT-proBNP is not directly degraded by neprilysin and may better track changing wall stress. Bradykinin and other peptides can also increase, explaining the contraindication with ACE inhibition, the 36-hour washout, and avoidance in patients with angioedema history.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Sacubitril/valsartan contains valsartan and directly blocks fetal RAAS signaling. Stop promptly when pregnancy is detected.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, dizziness, hyperkalemia, cough, renal-function change, and angioedema.

  • Severe airway angioedema, acute kidney injury, dangerous hyperkalemia, shock, and fetal toxicity.

Contraindications

  • ACE inhibitor use or use within the previous 36 hours; history of angioedema related to ACE inhibitor or ARB; and aliskiren use in diabetes.

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and duplicate ARB therapy. Clarify severe hypotension, hyperkalemia, or evolving AKI.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the last ACE-inhibitor dose, previous angioedema, pressure, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, and whether another ARB is hidden in the medication list.

  • Teach twice-daily adherence, daily weight and heart-failure symptoms, pressure/orthostasis, potassium-product avoidance unless prescribed, and emergency reporting of facial, tongue, or throat swelling.

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors are contraindicated and require 36-hour separation; separate ARBs duplicate valsartan. Potassium products, MRAs, NSAIDs, lithium, and diuretics require deliberate monitoring.

  • PDE5 inhibitors and other vasodilators can add hypotension. Renal impairment and volume depletion magnify pressure and kidney effects.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume, weight, urine output, and heart-failure trajectory.

  • BNP may be affected by neprilysin inhibition; NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate and is often easier to interpret mechanistically during therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • ARNI is not ACE inhibitor plus ARB. It is neprilysin inhibitor plus ARB, and adding an ACE inhibitor is dangerous.

  • The 36-hour interval prevents overlapping peptide-degradation blockade and angioedema; it is not merely a dose-timing preference.
