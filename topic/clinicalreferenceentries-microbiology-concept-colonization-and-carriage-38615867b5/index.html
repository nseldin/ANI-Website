Definition

Colonization means a microorganism is present and reproducing on or in the body without causing the tissue injury and clinical response that define infection. Carriage describes the state of harboring that organism, often without symptoms. A colonized person can still transmit some organisms or later develop infection if barriers or defenses change.

    Definition

  • Content

    Colonization succeeds when the organism can attach, obtain nutrients, tolerate local conditions, and coexist with host defenses without producing enough invasion or damage to cause disease. Infection begins when organism behavior, inoculum, host vulnerability, or barrier disruption shifts the balance toward tissue invasion, toxin injury, or a damaging inflammatory response.

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Examples include nasal carriage of Staphylococcus aureus, gastrointestinal carriage of some resistant gram-negative organisms, and asymptomatic colonization with Clostridioides difficile. The same positive result can mean colonization in one specimen and infection in another, so body site and symptoms are essential.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Do not treat a laboratory result in isolation. Assess fever, pain, local inflammation, organ dysfunction, specimen quality, imaging, inflammatory findings, and whether the sampled site is normally sterile. Unnecessary treatment of colonization exposes patients to adverse effects and selection pressure while failing to address the true cause of symptoms.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Colonization is not equivalent to contamination: contamination is introduced during collection or processing, whereas colonization exists in the patient. It is also not a permanent label. Carriage may be intermittent, testing can miss it, and management depends on the organism, care setting, procedure, and current guidance.
