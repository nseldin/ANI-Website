Class

Selective serotonin 5-HT2A inverse agonist atypical antipsychotic

Used To Treat

Hallucinations and delusions associated with Parkinson disease psychosis, without direct dopamine D2 blockade.

Boxed Warning

Increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis. Pimavanserin is not approved for dementia psychosis unless hallucinations and delusions are related to Parkinson disease.

Mechanism

Pimavanserin acts primarily as an inverse agonist/antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, with lower affinity for 5-HT2C and negligible dopamine, muscarinic, histamine, or adrenergic receptor binding. The exact link to improvement in Parkinson hallucinations/delusions is not fully defined.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the psychosis indication and rule out delirium, infection, medication toxicity, sleep disorder, or sensory impairment.

  • Monitor ECG/QT context, potassium/magnesium, bradycardia, syncope, edema, confusion, falls, and strong CYP3A interactions.

Key Labs

  • ECG/QTc when risk is present, potassium, magnesium, medication review, and mental/motor-status trend.

Nclex Traps

  • Pimavanserin is unusual because it treats psychosis without D2 blockade; that helps motor symptoms but does not remove QT or mortality risk.

Description

Pimavanserin treats Parkinson psychosis through serotonin 5-HT2A inverse agonism rather than dopamine D2 blockade, so it is less likely to worsen parkinsonian motor symptoms than conventional antipsychotics. It still prolongs QT and carries the class mortality warning for elderly clients with dementia-related psychosis outside the approved Parkinson-related indication.
