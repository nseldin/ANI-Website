Summary

D-dimer is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Helps rule out clotting disorders such as PE/DVT in low-risk clients; positive is nonspecific.

Quick Answer

D-dimer: Helps rule out clotting disorders such as PE/DVT in low-risk clients; positive is nonspecific. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    In a low or appropriate pretest-probability client, a negative D-dimer can help rule out venous thromboembolism.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    A positive D-dimer means fibrin formation/breakdown is increased, but it is nonspecific. Clot, surgery, pregnancy, infection, cancer, trauma, inflammation, liver disease, and age can raise it.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Do not diagnose PE or DVT from a positive D-dimer alone. If clinical suspicion is moderate/high or symptoms are dangerous, imaging and escalation matter more than waiting on this test.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Helps rule out clotting disorders such as PE/DVT in low-risk clients; positive is nonspecific.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Positive D-dimer is nonspecific. Infection, surgery, pregnancy, cancer, and inflammation can elevate it.
