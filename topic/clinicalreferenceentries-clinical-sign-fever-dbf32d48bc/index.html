Definition

Fever is a measured rise in body temperature caused by the brain temporarily raising the body's temperature target, usually during infection, inflammation, or another immune response. It is different from uncontrolled hyperthermia.

Plain Language

The body deliberately turns its temperature target up, so the person may shiver while heating and sweat when the target falls again.

Why It Matters

Fever can be an important clue to infection, inflammation, medication reaction, malignancy, or another illness, but its urgency depends on age, immune status, symptoms, measurement site, and trend. Fever with shock signs, severe breathing difficulty, confusion, seizure, stiff neck, a nonblanching rash, profound immune suppression, or rapid decline needs urgent evaluation.

Assessment Method

Measure temperature with a validated device at an appropriate site and record the value, units, site, method, time, recent hot or cold intake, antipyretics, age, immune status, exposures, symptoms, and trend while assessing airway, breathing, circulation, hydration, mental status, rash, pain, and focal findings.

Positive Finding

Measured body temperature is above the expected range for the patient's age, site, time, and clinical context because the thermoregulatory set point has risen; chills may occur while temperature rises and sweating while it falls.

Mechanism

Immune mediators stimulate prostaglandin signaling that raises the hypothalamic temperature set point, so vasoconstriction, shivering, behavior, and later heat loss move body temperature toward the new target.

Evidence Limitations

Device accuracy, measurement site, technique, circadian timing, age, antipyretics, recent drinks, environment, and immune suppression can alter the reading; serious infection can occur without fever. Therefore, Fever cannot confirm or exclude regulated response to infection, inflammation, immune activation, tissue injury, medication or transfusion reaction, malignancy, or another pyrogenic process by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hyperthermia, normal circadian rise, environmental heat exposure, medication effect.

False Negative Situations

Fever may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of regulated response to infection, inflammation, immune activation, tissue injury, medication or transfusion reaction, malignancy, or another pyrogenic process.

Modern Status

Current vital-sign finding; no single cutoff, body site, or touch assessment defines every fever, and fever must be distinguished from unregulated hyperthermia.
