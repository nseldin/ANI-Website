Range

An adult triglyceride result measures blood fat in mg/dL. Common MedlinePlus categories are healthy <150 mg/dL, borderline high 150-199 mg/dL, high 200-499 mg/dL, and very high >=500 mg/dL. These are interpretation categories, not universal treatment or emergency cutoffs; use the performing laboratory's report, age-specific guidance, symptoms, and the applicable clinical protocol. A lipid panel may be fasting or nonfasting, so follow the ordered collection instructions. A fasting repeat may be used when triglycerides are elevated or an inherited lipid disorder is suspected.

Why

An elevated triglyceride result signals increased triglyceride-rich lipoprotein burden and is associated with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), meaning plaque-related heart, brain, or peripheral-artery disease; very high levels also raise acute-pancreatitis risk. Promptly assess the collection and fasting context, upper-abdominal pain that may spread to the back, vomiting, fever, heart rate, breathing, alcohol use, diabetes control, thyroid disease, kidney or liver disease, pregnancy, medicines, diet, family history, and prior pancreatitis. Cause-directed evaluation can include a repeat fasting lipid panel, LDL-C and non-HDL-C, selective ApoB, glucose or A1C, TSH, liver and kidney tests, and lipase or imaging when symptoms suggest pancreatitis. Escalate urgently when a very high result accompanies severe or worsening upper-abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, fever, tachycardia, shortness of breath, jaundice, or a seriously ill appearance. Pancreatitis-prevention treatment thresholds differ by guideline and context: the 2020 Endocrine Society guideline addresses adults with fasting triglycerides over 500 mg/dL, while the 2026 AHA/ACC guideline emphasizes triglyceride-lowering therapy especially at >=1000 mg/dL. One triglyceride result does not diagnose pancreatitis, identify its cause, establish one universal treatment trigger, or replace the full lipid and ASCVD-risk assessment.

High Causes

  • Insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, obesity, or poorly controlled diabetes

  • High intake of added sugar, refined carbohydrate, excess calories, or alcohol

  • Hypothyroidism

  • Kidney or liver disease

  • Pregnancy-related or other hormonal context

  • Inherited hypertriglyceridemia or chylomicronemia

  • Medicines such as selected diuretics, estrogens, retinoids, glucocorticoids, antipsychotics, or HIV therapies, depending on the exact drug and patient

Low Causes

  • Low triglycerides are uncommon and usually not urgent; malnutrition, malabsorption, hyperthyroidism, chronic illness, or medication and nutrition context may contribute.

Critical Concerns

  • A very high triglyceride result alone is not the diagnosis of pancreatitis and is not automatically an emergency; symptoms and clinical stability determine immediate urgency.

  • Severe or worsening upper-abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, fever, tachycardia, dyspnea, jaundice, or a seriously ill appearance with very high triglycerides requires urgent pancreatitis evaluation.

  • Do not turn 500 mg/dL or 1000 mg/dL into one universal treatment rule; the fasting state, trend, cause, pregnancy status, pancreatitis history, symptoms, and applicable guideline or protocol matter.

Nursing Considerations

  • Verify whether fasting was ordered, the time and content of the last meal, alcohol exposure, current medicines, and whether the sample or report notes lipemia or another limitation.

  • Assess abdominal symptoms and stability first, then review the full lipid profile, glucose or A1C, TSH, liver and kidney tests, pregnancy context, family history, and prior pancreatitis; anticipate repeat fasting testing or cause-directed pancreatic evaluation when ordered.

  • Reinforce the clinician-directed plan for nutrition, alcohol avoidance or limitation, activity, weight, diabetes control, smoking cessation, and prescribed lipid therapy, then trend the response rather than treating one number in isolation.

Nclex Traps

  • Triglycerides are both an ASCVD-risk marker and, at very high levels, a pancreatitis-risk clue; the acute priority comes from the symptom-and-stability pattern, not the number alone.

  • Fasting is not universal for every lipid panel. Follow the order and repeat fasting testing when the clinical question or guideline calls for it.
