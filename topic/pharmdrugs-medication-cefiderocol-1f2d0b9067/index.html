Class

IV siderophore cephalosporin for susceptible aerobic Gram-negative infection

Used To Treat

Adults with susceptible complicated urinary tract infection including pyelonephritis and hospital-acquired/ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia under the U.S. label, particularly when limited alternatives exist for resistant aerobic Gram-negative pathogens such as Enterobacterales, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, or Stenotrophomonas.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: An increase in all-cause mortality was observed in a critically ill carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative trial. Use for susceptible labeled infection, monitor response closely, and reassess early deterioration or persistent cultures.

Mechanism

The catechol side chain coordinates ferric iron. Energy-dependent iron uptake overcomes the usual porin barrier and concentrates drug in the periplasm, where the beta-lactam core acylates PBPs. Efficacy follows unbound fT>MIC; the labeled three-hour infusion increases time above MIC compared with a one-hour infusion. Renal elimination means both impaired and augmented clearance require dose changes. In a trial of critically ill patients with carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative infection, all-cause mortality was numerically higher with cefiderocol than best available therapy, especially in pneumonia, bloodstream infection, or sepsis; the cause was not established, so organism fit and close clinical response monitoring are essential rather than assuming the novel entry mechanism guarantees success.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify species, MIC, infection source, alternatives, renal function including augmented clearance, three-hour infusion, and source control because every element determines whether the Trojan-horse concept becomes adequate bedside exposure.

  • Trend respiratory/hemodynamic response and repeat cultures when indicated; persistent or worsening infection should prompt resistance, source, dose, and alternative review rather than waiting for course completion.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to cefiderocol, cephalosporins, or related beta-lactams requires avoidance according to phenotype.

  • Do not use for Gram-positive or anaerobic infection because the iron-transport design and labeled microbiology focus on aerobic Gram-negative organisms.

Interactions

  • No major CYP interaction defines cefiderocol, but nephrotoxic co-therapy can reduce clearance and raise beta-lactam neurotoxicity risk.

  • Line compatibility and the prolonged infusion schedule must be coordinated because interruption shortens fT>MIC.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC using validated testing, creatinine/eGFR and urine output, CBC, liver tests, potassium, mental status, and source-specific imaging or oxygenation.

  • Susceptibility testing can be technically sensitive to iron conditions, so discordant clinical response warrants microbiology consultation and repeat testing when appropriate.

Nclex Traps

  • Cefiderocol uses iron uptake to enter; it does not treat iron deficiency and is not an iron supplement.

  • Activity against some MBL producers does not make every carbapenem-resistant isolate susceptible.

Class Pathway

  • Chelate ferric iron

  • Use bacterial siderophore transport to cross outer membrane

  • Release in periplasm and bind PBPs

Description

Cefiderocol, branded Fetroja, is an IV siderophore cephalosporin that couples a cephalosporin PBP inhibitor to an iron-chelating catechol group. Bacteria under iron limitation actively import the ferric-drug complex through siderophore transporters, giving cefiderocol a 'Trojan horse' route across the Gram-negative outer membrane. In the periplasm it dissociates from iron and binds PBPs, especially PBP3, stopping wall synthesis. The molecule is stable against many serine and metallo-beta-lactamases, which creates activity against some organisms resistant to other beta-lactams. Susceptibility still varies because transporter loss, heteroresistance, enzyme combinations, efflux, and PBP changes can defeat it.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, infusion-site reaction, constipation, rash, candidiasis, cough, liver-test elevation, hypokalemia, nausea, and CDI can occur.

  • Serious hypersensitivity, cytopenias, seizures or encephalopathy with excessive beta-lactam exposure, and microbiologic failure or emergent resistance are important risks.
