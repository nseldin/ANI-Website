Definition

Bulla means a clearly outlined fluid-filled blister larger than 10 millimeters, or 1 centimeter, across. It is the larger counterpart of a vesicle.

Plain Language

A bulla is a large blister filled with clear, cloudy, or blood-tinged fluid.

Why It Matters

Bulla describes a lesion shape, not its cause. One may follow friction or a burn, but widespread painful bullae, skin peeling, mouth or eye involvement, fever, or a new medication can signal a severe drug reaction or autoimmune blistering emergency.

Associated Conditions

  • epidermal or subepidermal separation caused by burns, friction, bites, contact dermatitis, medication reactions, autoimmune blistering disease, infection, or inherited skin fragility

Assessment Method

Inspect without unnecessary rupture, measure the blister, and document location, distribution, tense or flaccid roof, fluid appearance, surrounding skin, pain or itch, mucosal, eye, or genital involvement, recent medications and exposures, fever, and infection-control or burn concerns.

Positive Finding

A circumscribed elevated blister greater than 10 millimeters across contains clear, serous, cloudy, or blood-tinged fluid and is larger than a vesicle.

Mechanism

Injury, inflammation, toxin effects, or loss of adhesion separates epidermal layers or the epidermis from the dermis, allowing fluid to collect in the new space.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bulla finding raises concern for epidermal or subepidermal separation caused by burns, friction, bites, contact dermatitis, medication reactions, autoimmune blistering disease, infection, or inherited skin fragility because injury, inflammation, toxin effects, or loss of adhesion separates epidermal layers or the epidermis from the dermis, allowing fluid to collect in the new space. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bulla does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Size boundaries overlap, fragile bullae may rupture into erosions or crust before examination, and fluid color or roof tension alone does not establish the cause or cleavage level. Therefore, Bulla cannot confirm or exclude epidermal or subepidermal separation caused by burns, friction, bites, contact dermatitis, medication reactions, autoimmune blistering disease, infection, or inherited skin fragility by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as vesicle, pustule, friction blister, edema blister, cyst.

False Negative Situations

Bulla may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of epidermal or subepidermal separation caused by burns, friction, bites, contact dermatitis, medication reactions, autoimmune blistering disease, infection, or inherited skin fragility.

Modern Status

Current descriptive morphology, not a diagnosis; widespread painful bullae, epidermal detachment, mucosal involvement, or a new medication exposure can indicate a severe blistering emergency. Observe Bulla with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect without unnecessary rupture, measure the blister, and document location, distribution, tense or flaccid roof, fluid appearance, surrounding skin, pain or itch, mucosal, eye, or genital involvement, recent medications and exposures, fever, and infection-control or burn concerns.
