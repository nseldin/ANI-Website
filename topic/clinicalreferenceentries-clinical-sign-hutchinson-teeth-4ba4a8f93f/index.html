Definition

Hutchinson teeth is an observed dental sign. It is a clue to late congenital syphilis. A positive finding means incisors are small, widely spaced, and notched at the cutting edge.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hutchinson teeth means incisors are small, widely spaced, and notched at the cutting edge.

Why It Matters

The observed Hutchinson teeth finding can signal late congenital syphilis. Treponemal disruption of tooth-bud development alters enamel and crown shape. Hutchinson teeth cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • late congenital syphilis

Assessment Method

Inspect permanent upper central incisors.

Positive Finding

Incisors are small, widely spaced, and notched at the cutting edge.

Mechanism

Treponemal disruption of tooth-bud development alters enamel and crown shape.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hutchinson teeth finding raises concern for late congenital syphilis because treponemal disruption of tooth-bud development alters enamel and crown shape. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hutchinson teeth does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hutchinson teeth to focus evaluation of late congenital syphilis and the reviewed alternatives: peg teeth, enamel hypoplasia, tooth wear. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect permanent upper central incisors. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hutchinson teeth as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Other developmental dental abnormalities can mimic the shape. Therefore, Hutchinson teeth cannot confirm or exclude late congenital syphilis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • peg teeth

  • enamel hypoplasia

  • tooth wear

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as peg teeth, enamel hypoplasia, tooth wear.

False Negative Situations

Hutchinson teeth may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of late congenital syphilis.
