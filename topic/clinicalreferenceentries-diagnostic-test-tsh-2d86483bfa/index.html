Summary

TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) is a high-yield Endocrine diagnostic reference. Pituitary thyroid-stimulating hormone; primary screening anchor for thyroid function.

Quick Answer

TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone): Pituitary thyroid-stimulating hormone; primary screening anchor for thyroid function. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    High TSH

    Meaning

    Usually suggests primary hypothyroidism when free T4 is low or low-normal; pituitary response is trying to stimulate the thyroid.

  • Label

    Low TSH

    Meaning

    Usually suggests hyperthyroidism or excess thyroid hormone when free T4/T3 are high; can also reflect pituitary/hypothalamic disease or medication effects.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    TSH is a screen anchor, but acute illness, pregnancy, pituitary disease, amiodarone, biotin, and timing of thyroid medication can affect interpretation.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Pituitary thyroid-stimulating hormone; primary screening anchor for thyroid function.

    • Before / preparation

    • Check timing, fasting status, current endocrine medications, steroid use, pregnancy status when relevant, and whether dynamic testing requires serial specimens.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect the result to symptoms, glucose safety, thyroid/adrenal crisis risk, and medication teaching.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Severe hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, thyroid storm/myxedema cues, adrenal crisis, altered mental status, dehydration, or unstable vital signs.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Endocrine tests often require correct timing. Wrong timing can make the result misleading.
