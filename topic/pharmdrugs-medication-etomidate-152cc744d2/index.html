Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to etomidate or formulation components. Clarify severe adrenal insufficiency, septic shock, porphyria history, and whether another induction strategy offers a better risk-benefit profile; never use it as an unmonitored sedative or prolonged infusion.

Nursing Essentials

  • Prepare full airway monitoring despite relative cardiovascular stability. Distinguish etomidate myoclonus from purposeful movement or seizure; hypnosis does not supply analgesia.

  • In critical illness, document the etomidate dose/time and communicate it when evaluating refractory hypotension, cortisol testing or steroid decisions. Avoid repeated/prolonged dosing unless a specialist has explicitly justified it.

Interactions

  • Opioids, benzodiazepines and other anesthetics increase respiratory depression. Drugs or disease that suppress the adrenal axis can compound steroid deficiency. Neuromuscular-blocker use can hide myoclonus and awareness unless sedation and analgesia are independently assured.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG, blood pressure, oxygenation and ventilation

  • Hemodynamics and vasopressor trajectory

  • Cortisol/adrenal evaluation only when clinically indicated; electrolytes and glucose in suspected adrenal dysfunction

Nclex Traps

  • Etomidate is hemodynamically sparing, not hemodynamically harmless.

  • Myoclonus after etomidate is common and does not automatically equal a seizure.

  • No analgesia means painful stimulation still requires a pain-control plan.

Class

Hemodynamically sparing IV GABA-A sedative-hypnotic induction anesthetic

Used To Treat

Single-dose induction of general anesthesia, especially when severe hypotension risk makes cardiovascular stability valuable; etomidate causes hypnosis without analgesia and is not intended as a prolonged infusion.

Mechanism

Etomidate positively modulates beta-2/beta-3-containing GABA-A receptors, increasing inhibitory chloride conductance and rapidly suppressing consciousness. It has little direct vasodilating or myocardial-depressant effect at induction doses. Transient myoclonus likely reflects uneven suppression and disinhibition of subcortical motor pathways rather than purposeful movement. Separately, its imidazole structure binds adrenal 11-beta-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) and related steroidogenic enzymes, reducing conversion to cortisol and aldosterone; that endocrine mechanism is independent of hypnosis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Use only where airway, ventilation and cardiovascular rescue are immediately available. Etomidate is not approved for prolonged infusion because cumulative adrenal suppression and excess mortality were observed with that practice. A single dose can lower cortisol and aldosterone; interpret persistent shock and vasopressor need in context rather than assuming hemodynamic stability at injection means no later physiologic cost.

Description

Etomidate is an ultrashort IV GABA-A induction anesthetic that suppresses consciousness while usually preserving blood pressure and cardiac output better than propofol or barbiturates. That hemodynamic advantage can help selected unstable patients, but the tradeoff is predictable inhibition of adrenal cortisol and aldosterone synthesis, plus frequent myoclonus and injection pain. One induction dose can suppress steroid production for hours, which matters most in sepsis, shock or adrenal disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Transient skeletal myoclonus, injection pain, apnea or hypoventilation, nausea/vomiting, laryngospasm, hiccups, blood-pressure changes, and temporary adrenal corticosteroid suppression.
