Used To Treat

Adult schizophrenia; acute bipolar I manic/mixed episodes; bipolar I maintenance adjunct to lithium/valproate. Intramuscular formulation treats acute agitation in schizophrenia and has different administration rules.

Mechanism

D2 antagonism reduces mesolimbic psychosis and can cause EPS/prolactin effects; 5-HT2A antagonism modulates dopamine release. Ziprasidone also has 5-HT1A agonist and serotonin/norepinephrine transporter inhibitory activity that may contribute to mood effects. It blocks cardiac delayed-rectifier potassium current, slowing ventricular repolarization and increasing QTc; bradycardia, congenital long QT, structural heart disease, low potassium/magnesium and other QT drugs amplify torsades risk. Food increases bioavailability, so a missed meal changes pharmacokinetics more than a minor adherence detail.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Ziprasidone is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Its greater QT-prolonging capacity than several alternatives must influence drug selection. It is contraindicated in known QT prolongation, recent acute MI, uncompensated heart failure and specified QT-prolonging combinations.

Contraindications

  • Known QT prolongation/congenital long-QT syndrome, recent acute MI or uncompensated heart failure

  • Specified QT-prolonging drugs and product hypersensitivity

  • Correct clinically important low potassium/magnesium before treatment

Nursing Essentials

  • Give every oral dose with an adequate meal and document whether apparent nonresponse follows fasting administration.

  • Treat syncope, new palpitations or seizure-like collapse as a possible rhythm problem and obtain ECG/electrolyte assessment.

Interactions

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs are contraindicated or should be avoided according to labeling.

  • CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers alter exposure, though QT risk remains the dominant interaction screen.

  • Diuretics, vomiting and diarrhea increase risk through low potassium/magnesium.

Key Labs

  • ECG when cardiac risk exists

  • Potassium and magnesium before/through treatment when depletion risk exists

  • AIMS/EPS and standard metabolic monitoring

Nclex Traps

  • Lower metabolic risk does not mean lower overall risk.

  • A dose swallowed without enough food may deliver much less drug.

  • Syncope is not automatically orthostasis in a QT-prolonging drug.

Description

Ziprasidone blocks dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors to treat schizophrenia and acute bipolar mania, with relatively low average weight/lipid burden but clinically important QT-prolongation risk. Oral absorption roughly doubles with food and should occur with a meal of about 500 calories; taking it without adequate food can create low, erratic exposure that looks like treatment failure. Lower metabolic burden therefore trades against meal adherence and cardiac-risk complexity rather than making ziprasidone universally safer.

Adverse Effects

  • QT prolongation, palpitations, syncope and rare torsades

  • Somnolence, dizziness, akathisia and other EPS

  • Nausea and relatively modest average weight change

  • NMS, tardive dyskinesia, DRESS and priapism
