Used To Treat

Severe or life-threatening susceptible invasive fungal infections, including selected Candida, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus, Mucorales, and endemic dimorphic fungi. The exact indication, dose, and toxicity depend on whether the product is liposomal, deoxycholate, or another lipid formulation.

Mechanism

Amphotericin's hydrophobic polyene region associates with membrane sterols while its hydrophilic surface lines an aqueous pore. It has greater affinity for fungal ergosterol than mammalian cholesterol, but selectivity is incomplete. Renal injury combines afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, which lowers glomerular blood flow, with direct distal tubular injury, which wastes potassium and magnesium and impairs acid handling. Cytokine release during infusion explains fever, rigors, nausea, hypotension, and tachypnea. Lipid packaging changes the amount of freely available drug reaching host membranes and shifts tissue distribution, explaining why a formulation can preserve antifungal action while reducing, but not eliminating, nephrotoxicity.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to amphotericin or the exact formulation is a major contraindication unless a life-threatening benefit clearly outweighs risk under specialist management.

  • Never substitute deoxycholate, liposomal, or lipid-complex products milligram for milligram. Verify label, dose units, diluent, filter, infusion time, and compatibility.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before each dose, verify formulation, weight-based calculation, recent creatinine/urine output, potassium, magnesium, CBC, fluid status, line, infusion rate, and concomitant nephrotoxins. Follow the product-specific test-dose and premedication policy rather than assuming one universal protocol.

  • During infusion, distinguish cytokine symptoms from anaphylaxis. Stop immediately for severe hypersensitivity; otherwise follow orders for rate adjustment and symptom treatment. Trend cumulative electrolyte replacement needs because tubular wasting can persist.

Interactions

  • Other nephrotoxins such as aminoglycosides, calcineurin inhibitors, cisplatin, foscarnet, or contrast can compound AKI. Diuretics and corticosteroids can worsen potassium loss.

  • Hypokalemia can amplify digoxin toxicity and dysrhythmia risk; marrow-suppressing drugs can compound anemia or cytopenias.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR, urine output, potassium, magnesium, bicarbonate, CBC/hemoglobin, liver tests when indicated, infusion vital signs, organism susceptibility, and clinical/microbiologic response.

  • A normal pre-dose creatinine does not exclude active tubular potassium or magnesium wasting, so electrolytes require their own trend.

Nclex Traps

  • Amphotericin toxicity is formulation-specific, so verify the exact product and actively trend renal function, potassium, magnesium, blood counts, and infusion reactions.

  • Liposomal amphotericin is less nephrotoxic, not kidney safe; nystatin shares the polyene target but cannot replace systemic amphotericin.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: High-alert formulation warning: amphotericin products are not interchangeable by dose. Wrong-product substitution can cause severe underdosing or toxicity. Anaphylaxis, AKI, potassium/magnesium depletion, infusion reactions, and anemia require active prevention and surveillance.

  • Polyene antifungal

Description

Amphotericin B is an IV broad-spectrum polyene antifungal that binds fungal ergosterol, forms ion-permeable membrane channels, and causes lethal electrolyte and solute leakage; it is used when invasive fungal disease is severe, rapidly progressive, resistant, or not adequately covered by a safer narrow agent. Oxidative membrane injury adds to killing of susceptible fungi. The drug also interacts weakly with human cholesterol, which helps explain kidney, infusion, and cellular toxicity. 'Amphotericin B' is not one interchangeable bag: liposomal amphotericin generally reduces renal and infusion toxicity compared with deoxycholate, but each formulation has different distribution and mg/kg dosing. Verify the exact product every time.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion fever, chills/rigors, nausea, vomiting, headache, hypotension or hypertension, tachycardia, dyspnea, phlebitis, and rare anaphylaxis.

  • AKI, distal tubular dysfunction, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, renal tubular acidosis, anemia from reduced erythropoietin, and hepatic or hematologic abnormalities.
