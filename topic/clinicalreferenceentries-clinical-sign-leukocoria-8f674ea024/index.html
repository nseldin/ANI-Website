Definition

Leukocoria is an observed red-reflex sign. It is a clue to white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease. A positive finding means one or both pupils reflect white rather than symmetric red-orange.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Leukocoria means one or both pupils reflect white rather than symmetric red-orange.

Why It Matters

A positive Leukocoria response can signal white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease. An opacity or reflective intraocular mass blocks or replaces normal choroidal red reflection. Leukocoria cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease

Assessment Method

Assess red reflex in a darkened setting or inspect photographs with abnormal white reflex.

Positive Finding

One or both pupils reflect white rather than symmetric red-orange.

Mechanism

An opacity or reflective intraocular mass blocks or replaces normal choroidal red reflection.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Leukocoria response raises concern for white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease because an opacity or reflective intraocular mass blocks or replaces normal choroidal red reflection. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Leukocoria does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Leukocoria to focus evaluation of white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease and the reviewed alternatives: persistent fetal vasculature, retinal detachment, refractive asymmetry. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess red reflex in a darkened setting or inspect photographs with abnormal white reflex. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Leukocoria as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Flash angle and camera artifacts can produce false white pupils. Therefore, Leukocoria cannot confirm or exclude white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • persistent fetal vasculature

  • retinal detachment

  • refractive asymmetry

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from persistent fetal vasculature, retinal detachment, refractive asymmetry, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Leukocoria may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current pediatric ocular emergency sign. Interpret Leukocoria in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Flash angle and camera artifacts can produce false white pupils.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Leukocoria, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Assess red reflex in a darkened setting or inspect photographs with abnormal white reflex. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Arrange urgent ophthalmology evaluation; do not wait for vision complaints in a child.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Assess red reflex in a darkened setting or inspect photographs with abnormal white reflex. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, white pupillary reflex from lens, vitreous, or retinal disease, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
