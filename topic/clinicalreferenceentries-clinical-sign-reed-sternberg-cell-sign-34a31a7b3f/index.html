Definition

Reed-Sternberg cell sign is a histologic laboratory sign. It is a clue to classic Hodgkin lymphoma. A positive finding means large atypical binucleate or multinucleate cells with prominent nucleoli are found in the correct background.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Reed-Sternberg cell sign means large atypical binucleate or multinucleate cells with prominent nucleoli are found in the correct background.

Why It Matters

The Reed-Sternberg cell sign laboratory finding can signal classic Hodgkin lymphoma. Clonal germinal-center B cells acquire abnormal survival programs and recruit a rich inflammatory microenvironment. Reed-Sternberg cell sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • classic Hodgkin lymphoma

Assessment Method

Review an adequately sampled lymph-node biopsy with immunophenotyping.

Positive Finding

Large atypical binucleate or multinucleate cells with prominent nucleoli are found in the correct background.

Mechanism

Clonal germinal-center B cells acquire abnormal survival programs and recruit a rich inflammatory microenvironment.

Clinical Interpretation

The Reed-Sternberg cell sign laboratory finding raises concern for classic Hodgkin lymphoma because clonal germinal-center B cells acquire abnormal survival programs and recruit a rich inflammatory microenvironment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Reed-Sternberg cell sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Reed-Sternberg cell sign to focus evaluation of classic Hodgkin lymphoma and the reviewed alternatives: immunoblasts, metastatic carcinoma, melanoma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review an adequately sampled lymph-node biopsy with immunophenotyping. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Reed-Sternberg cell sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Reed-Sternberg-like cells occur in other conditions and may be sparse. Therefore, Reed-Sternberg cell sign cannot confirm or exclude classic Hodgkin lymphoma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • immunoblasts

  • metastatic carcinoma

  • melanoma

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with immunoblasts, metastatic carcinoma, melanoma.

False Negative Situations

Reed-Sternberg cell sign may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
