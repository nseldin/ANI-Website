Used To Treat

Bacterial conjunctivitis caused by susceptible organisms and specialist-directed corneal infection according to formulation and local microbiology; it does not treat viral, allergic or fungal conjunctivitis.

Description

Moxifloxacin ophthalmic is an antibiotic eye drop that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, trapping these enzymes on cut DNA so the chromosome cannot be resealed or replicated and susceptible bacteria die. High ocular concentrations make it useful for bacterial surface infection, but an antibiotic eye drop cannot identify the cause of every red eye. Pain, photophobia, reduced vision, contact-lens use or corneal opacity should trigger evaluation for keratitis rather than automatic conjunctivitis treatment.

Mechanism

Bacterial type-II topoisomerases cut and reseal DNA to relieve torsion and separate chromosomes. Moxifloxacin stabilizes the enzyme-DNA cleavage complex and prevents religation, producing lethal double-strand damage. Human topoisomerases have much lower affinity, while target mutations, efflux and permeability changes create resistance.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious systemic fluoroquinolone reactions are possible after exposure but systemic levels from eye drops are low. Immediate hypersensitivity can still occur. Prolonged use can overgrow nonsusceptible organisms, including fungi. A contaminated tip can cause bacterial keratitis; severe pain, photophobia or vision loss needs urgent corneal examination.

Adverse Effects

  • Eye irritation/pain, dry eye, tearing, hyperemia, blurred vision, keratitis, dysgeusia, hypersensitivity and superinfection with prolonged use.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to moxifloxacin, other quinolones or formulation components. Clarify suspected viral/fungal disease, corneal ulcer, contact-lens-associated keratitis and prior severe fluoroquinolone reaction.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain culture when severe, recurrent, neonatal, contact-lens-associated or corneal infection requires it. Teach completion of the prescribed course and no sharing or saving for future red eyes.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Topical interactions are limited by low systemic exposure; separate other eye drops. Systemic fluoroquinolones add class exposure. Do not mix the drop with unverified compounded products.

Key Labs

  • Visual acuity and corneal/conjunctival examination

  • Culture and susceptibility for severe, atypical or nonresponding infection

  • Clinical response within the expected interval

Nclex Traps

  • Purulent discharge suggests bacterial disease but does not rule out corneal involvement.

  • Antibiotic drops do not treat viral or allergic conjunctivitis.

  • Contact-lens wear plus pain/photophobia raises concern for microbial keratitis.
