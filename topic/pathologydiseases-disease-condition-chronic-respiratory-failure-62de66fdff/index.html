Definition

Chronic respiratory failure develops when the respiratory system persistently cannot maintain adequate oxygenation, carbon-dioxide removal, or both because of chronic lung, neuromuscular, chest-wall, or control-system disease. The characteristic pattern is stable chronic hypoxemia or hypercapnia with exertional limitation, sleep symptoms, polycythemia, pulmonary hypertension, or compensated bicarbonate elevation. Confirm or stage it with arterial blood gas interpreted with chronic compensation; distinguish it from acute respiratory failure, obesity hypoventilation, sleep apnea, heart failure, anemia, and metabolic alkalosis by baseline gases, symptoms, and cause testing. The priority is to treat the underlying ventilatory or gas-exchange disorder because acute-on-chronic decompensation can complicate Chronic respiratory failure. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, accessory-muscle use, abnormal breath sounds, cough/sputum, chest pain, hypoxemia, cyanosis, fatigue, or confusion.

  • Silent chest, stridor, rising CO2 signs, or exhaustion is more dangerous than a noisy but moving airway.

Diagnostics

  • arterial blood gas interpreted with chronic compensation

  • rest, exertional, and sleep oximetry or carbon-dioxide assessment

  • pulmonary function, imaging, respiratory-muscle, sleep, and cardiac testing to identify the mechanism

  • Distinguish Chronic respiratory failure from acute respiratory failure, obesity hypoventilation, sleep apnea, heart failure, anemia, and metabolic alkalosis by baseline gases, symptoms, and cause testing.

Labs

  • PaO2 or SpO2 may fall with gas-exchange failure

  • PaCO2 may rise with ventilatory failure

  • WBC may rise with infection

  • lactate may rise with severe hypoxia or sepsis

Treatments

  • treat the underlying ventilatory or gas-exchange disorder

  • prescribe oxygen only after qualifying assessment and titrate to the individualized target

  • use nocturnal or continuous ventilatory support, rehabilitation, vaccination, and secretion care when indicated

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for new confusion, inability to stay awake, rapidly increasing oxygen need, worsening acidosis, ineffective cough, severe breathlessness, cyanosis, or failure of home noninvasive support because acute ventilation or airway management may be required.

  • Establish the patient's baseline oxygen, carbon dioxide, mental status, breathing pattern, activity tolerance, and home support because chronic compensation makes trend away from baseline more useful than a single abnormal value.

  • Titrate oxygen to the individualized target and verify delivery equipment because both untreated hypoxemia and excessive unmonitored oxygen in susceptible carbon-dioxide retainers can cause harm.

  • Assess for infection, bronchospasm, heart failure, sedatives, mucus retention, aspiration, or equipment failure because a reversible trigger often explains acute-on-chronic deterioration.

  • Trend blood gases when indicated, bicarbonate, respiratory rate, work of breathing, sleepiness, cough strength, nutrition, and response to noninvasive ventilation because rising carbon dioxide and fatigue may precede arrest.

Complications

  • acute-on-chronic decompensation can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • pulmonary hypertension can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • cor pulmonale can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • arrhythmia can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • cognitive decline can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • oxygen-related worsening hypercapnia in susceptible patients

  • device dependence can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • pressure injury can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

  • infection can complicate Chronic respiratory failure.

Red Flags

  • New somnolence, confusion, or headache with rising carbon dioxide

  • Rapid increase above baseline oxygen requirement

  • Worsening acidosis, respiratory fatigue, or ineffective cough

  • Cyanosis, apnea, or inability to tolerate prescribed ventilatory support

Patient Education

  • Teach the patient and family the personal baseline, oxygen target, equipment checks, airway-clearance plan, and exact signs of acute-on-chronic failure.

  • Never change oxygen or sedating medicines without guidance, avoid smoking around oxygen, and seek emergency care for unusual sleepiness, blue color, or rapidly worsening breathing.

Nclex Traps

  • A chronically high bicarbonate can reflect renal compensation for hypercapnia

  • Do not withhold needed oxygen from a hypoxemic patient; titrate and reassess ventilation
