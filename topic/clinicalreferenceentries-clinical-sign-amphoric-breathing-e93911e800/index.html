Definition

Amphoric breathing is an auscultatory sign. It is a clue to large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax. A positive finding means a hollow metallic blowing sound resembling air across a bottle is heard.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Amphoric breathing means a hollow metallic blowing sound resembling air across a bottle is heard.

Why It Matters

A positive Amphoric breathing response can signal large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax. Airflow resonates within a large cavity that communicates with a bronchus. Amphoric breathing cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax

Assessment Method

Auscultate over the suspected region during breathing.

Positive Finding

A hollow metallic blowing sound resembling air across a bottle is heard.

Mechanism

Airflow resonates within a large cavity that communicates with a bronchus.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Amphoric breathing response raises concern for large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax because airflow resonates within a large cavity that communicates with a bronchus. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Amphoric breathing does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Amphoric breathing to focus evaluation of large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax and the reviewed alternatives: bronchial breathing, metallic wheeze, transmitted upper-airway sound. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate over the suspected region during breathing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Amphoric breathing as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Rare and subjective; imaging determines anatomy. Therefore, Amphoric breathing cannot confirm or exclude large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • bronchial breathing

  • metallic wheeze

  • transmitted upper-airway sound

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from bronchial breathing, metallic wheeze, transmitted upper-airway sound, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Amphoric breathing may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical but descriptive adjunct. Amphoric breathing remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude large smooth-walled pulmonary cavity or open pneumothorax. Apply the reviewed limitation for Amphoric breathing: Rare and subjective; imaging determines anatomy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Amphoric breathing, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate over the suspected region during breathing. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: New amphoric sound with distress warrants imaging for fistula or pneumothorax.
