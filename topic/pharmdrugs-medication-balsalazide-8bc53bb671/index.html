Used To Treat

Mildly to moderately active ulcerative colitis in adults and age-approved pediatric patients; it is an induction/active-disease drug, not a generic treatment for every inflammatory bowel disorder.

Mechanism

The carrier 4-aminobenzoyl-beta-alanine is separated from mesalamine in the colon. Topical mesalamine reduces mucosal prostanoid/leukotriene production, NF-kappa-B-related inflammatory signaling, cytokine activity and oxidative injury. The exact contribution of each pathway is not fully resolved, but efficacy depends more on local mucosal exposure than on systemic drug concentration.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected salicylate/aminosalicylate hypersensitivity or hypersensitivity to balsalazide metabolites/components

  • Clarify renal impairment, nephrotoxic therapy, liver disease, upper-GI obstruction or prior mesalamine intolerance

Nursing Essentials

  • Record stool frequency, blood, urgency, abdominal pain, weight and hydration before treatment so a response can be separated from toxicity.

  • Teach adequate fluid intake, sun protection for photosensitive patients and urgent reporting of chest pain, dyspnea, rash/mucosal lesions or reduced urine output.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic agents increase renal risk.

  • Azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine can increase blood dyscrasia risk.

  • Mesalamine metabolite can falsely elevate urinary normetanephrine by some electrochemical assays.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR and urinalysis before and periodically during therapy

  • CBC and liver tests when clinically indicated

  • Hydration and possible stone evaluation; use an alternative selective normetanephrine assay when needed

Nclex Traps

  • A new flare-like syndrome after initiation can be mesalamine intolerance.

  • Colon-targeted does not mean kidney-safe without monitoring.

  • Balsalazide treats active mild-to-moderate UC; do not confuse it with olsalazine's maintenance-only label.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Assess renal function before treatment and periodically. Stop and evaluate renal decline, mesalamine acute-intolerance syndrome, myocarditis/pericarditis, severe cutaneous reactions, hepatic failure, nephrolithiasis or marked photosensitivity. Acute intolerance can closely mimic a worsening ulcerative-colitis flare.

Description

Balsalazide is an oral colon-targeted prodrug that links mesalamine to an inert carrier through an azo bond. Colonic bacterial azoreductases split that bond and release active 5-aminosalicylic acid onto inflamed mucosa, limiting premature upper-GI release. It treats mild-to-moderate active ulcerative colitis while retaining mesalamine-class kidney, hypersensitivity and intolerance risks.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, respiratory symptoms and arthralgia

  • Acute intolerance with cramping, abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, fever, headache or rash

  • Interstitial nephritis/renal failure, nephrolithiasis, pancreatitis, myocarditis/pericarditis, hepatitis and severe cutaneous reactions
