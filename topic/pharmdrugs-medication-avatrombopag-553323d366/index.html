Class

Oral small-molecule thrombopoietin-receptor agonist for ITP and procedure-timed chronic-liver-disease thrombocytopenia

Used To Treat

Thrombocytopenia in adults with chronic liver disease scheduled for a procedure and persistent or chronic immune thrombocytopenia in eligible adults and children under current labeling. Regimen depends on indication, baseline platelet count, age/weight, and interacting CYP2C9/CYP3A drugs. It is not intended to normalize platelets.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Thrombopoietin-receptor agonists are associated with thrombotic and thromboembolic complications, including portal-vein thrombosis in chronic liver disease. Follow the platelet-based regimen and do not use avatrombopag to normalize counts. A procedure can still bleed even after the platelet response, and thrombosis surveillance remains necessary.

Mechanism

Avatrombopag binds the transmembrane domain of the thrombopoietin receptor MPL at a site distinct from endogenous TPO. It activates JAK2/STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling, stimulating proliferation and differentiation of bone-marrow megakaryocyte progenitors and increasing platelet production. Because new platelets require maturation, the rise is delayed and persists after dosing. Metabolism through CYP2C9 and CYP3A means dual inhibitors or inducers can change exposure and the ITP dose plan. Food improves and stabilizes absorption. The drug does not replace platelets immediately, remove antiplatelet antibodies, or correct portal-hypertension biology.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food. Verify whether the order is the procedure course or chronic ITP regimen and confirm baseline platelet count, procedure date, age/weight, and interacting drugs.

  • Check platelets at label-defined times and assess both bleeding and thrombosis. The count is a decision tool, not the entire clinical outcome.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but serious hypersensitivity requires discontinuation

  • Do not substitute the chronic ITP titration schedule for the chronic-liver-disease procedure course

  • Do not use solely to normalize platelets or without assessing thrombotic risk

Interactions

  • Moderate or strong dual CYP2C9/CYP3A inhibitors can raise exposure; inducers can lower it and change ITP dosing.

  • Other prothrombotic therapies and liver-disease thrombosis risks add harm.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets at baseline and regimen-specific intervals; liver context and smear when indicated

  • Procedure timing, bleeding outcome, portal/venous thrombosis symptoms, and medication interaction review

Nclex Traps

  • Avatrombopag is taken with food; do not import eltrombopag's calcium-separation rule.

  • The liver-disease regimen is a timed preprocedure course, not chronic normalization of the platelet count.

Description

Avatrombopag is an oral small-molecule MPL agonist that stimulates megakaryocyte proliferation and platelet production. Unlike eltrombopag, it is taken with food and does not require separation from dietary calcium or mineral supplements. In chronic liver disease it is a short, procedure-timed course selected by baseline platelets, not an indefinite attempt to correct all thrombocytopenia. In ITP it is titrated to reduce bleeding risk. Portal-vein and other thromboses remain important because liver disease and excess platelet stimulation can converge on an already prothrombotic circulation.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, fatigue, nausea, abdominal discomfort, fever, upper respiratory symptoms, or arthralgia

  • Portal, venous, or arterial thrombosis and thromboembolism

  • Excessive platelet rise or recurrent thrombocytopenia after chronic ITP therapy changes
