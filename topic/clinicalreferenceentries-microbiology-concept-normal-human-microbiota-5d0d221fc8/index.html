Definition

Normal human microbiota are microbial communities commonly present at a body site in people without infection at that site. 'Normal flora' is familiar wording, but microbiota is more accurate because the community contains microorganisms, not plants. Normal presence is site-specific: the colon is densely colonized, while blood and cerebrospinal fluid are normally sterile.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    Resident communities occupy attachment sites, consume nutrients, modify pH and oxygen, produce metabolites, and interact with immune defenses. These effects can create colonization resistance that makes it harder for an invading pathogen to establish itself. Antibiotics, devices, surgery, immune suppression, and barrier injury can disturb that protection or move a resident organism into a vulnerable compartment.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Coagulase-negative staphylococci commonly inhabit skin, many anaerobes inhabit the colon, and lactobacilli often contribute to an acidic vaginal environment. These are patterns rather than guarantees; community composition differs with age, hormones, exposures, health, and recent antimicrobial use.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Nurses and clinicians must distinguish contamination, colonization, and infection. A skin organism in one blood-culture bottle may reflect collection contamination, but repeated recovery with compatible illness or an intravascular device can indicate bloodstream infection. Specimen source, number of positive sets, time to positivity, symptoms, and host risk determine the meaning.

    The term does not mean harmless, permanent, or identical in everyone. It also must not be used to dismiss a result automatically. Clinical interpretation requires the body site, specimen technique, host condition, and evidence of tissue injury or inflammation.
