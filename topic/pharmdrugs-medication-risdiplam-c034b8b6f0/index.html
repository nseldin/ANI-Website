Class

Oral systemic SMN2 pre-mRNA splicing modulator

Used To Treat

Spinal muscular atrophy in pediatric and adult patients using age- and weight-specific once-daily oral solution or tablet administration.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current EVRYSDI labeling has no boxed warning. Animal data support embryo-fetal toxicity and possible impaired male fertility. Risdiplam and its major metabolite inhibit MATE1 and MATE2-K in vitro and may increase exposure to substrates such as metformin; avoid the combination when possible or monitor and adjust the substrate under its labeling.

Mechanism

Risdiplam binds SMN2 pre-mRNA/splice-complex elements and stabilizes recognition of exon 7 splice sites, increasing mature transcripts that retain exon 7 and can make stable full-length SMN protein. It may also alter splicing of other transcripts such as FOXM1 and MADD; these off-target effects are plausible contributors to animal toxicities and explain why 'selective gene correction' would be an inaccurate description.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify age/weight dose, tablet versus pharmacy-reconstituted oral solution, concentration, calibrated syringe, administration through mouth/G-tube/NG-tube, and immediate water flush. Do not administer powder before pharmacy reconstitution.

  • Assess adherence, respiratory infections, fever, diarrhea, rash, pregnancy and contraception, male fertility counseling, all MATE substrates, and motor/bulbar/respiratory function over time.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level. Use pregnancy assessment when applicable, interaction monitoring for MATE substrates, and longitudinal motor, respiratory, nutritional, hepatic/renal and adverse-effect assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Risdiplam is not an antisense oligonucleotide and is not intrathecal; it is an oral small-molecule splicing modifier.

  • SMA improvement does not remove aspiration or respiratory-decompensation risk, especially when motor neuron loss preceded treatment.

Description

Risdiplam is an oral small-molecule RNA-splicing modulator that changes SMN2 processing, increases exon 7 inclusion, and raises full-length survival motor neuron protein in the brain and peripheral tissues of patients with spinal muscular atrophy. Unlike intrathecal nusinersen it is systemic and taken daily; formulation preparation, feeding-tube technique, embryo-fetal risk, potential male-fertility effects, and inhibition of MATE1 and MATE2-K transporters are part of the drug's identity.
