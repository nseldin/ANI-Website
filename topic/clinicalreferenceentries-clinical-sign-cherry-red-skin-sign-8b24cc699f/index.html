Definition

Cherry-red skin sign is an observed color sign. It is a clue to classically severe carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning. A positive finding means unusually bright pink-red coloration is described.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cherry-red skin sign means unusually bright pink-red coloration is described.

Why It Matters

The observed Cherry-red skin sign finding can signal classically severe carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning. Carboxyhemoglobin changes blood optical color, while cyanide-related high venous oxygen can also redden tissues. Cherry-red skin sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • classically severe carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning

Carboxyhemoglobin changes blood optical color, while cyanide-related high venous oxygen can also redden tissues.

Evidence Limitations

The sign is uncommon, lighting dependent, and not a screening tool. Therefore, Cherry-red skin sign cannot confirm or exclude classically severe carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as rosacea, heat exposure, sepsis flushing.

False Negative Situations

Cherry-red skin sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of classically severe carbon monoxide or cyanide poisoning.
