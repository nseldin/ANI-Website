Definition

Sulcus sign is a bedside shoulder-laxity maneuver. It is a clue to inferior glenohumeral laxity. A positive finding means a visible or palpable groove appears below the acromion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Sulcus sign means a visible or palpable groove appears below the acromion.

Why It Matters

A positive Sulcus sign response can signal inferior glenohumeral laxity. Inferior capsular or rotator-interval laxity lets traction translate the humeral head downward, leaving a visible groove between the acromion and deltoid contour. Sulcus sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • inferior glenohumeral laxity

Assessment Method

With the arm relaxed at the side, apply gentle downward traction.

Positive Finding

A visible or palpable groove appears below the acromion.

Mechanism

Inferior capsular or rotator-interval laxity lets traction translate the humeral head downward, leaving a visible groove between the acromion and deltoid contour.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Sulcus sign response raises concern for inferior glenohumeral laxity because inferior capsular or rotator-interval laxity lets traction translate the humeral head downward, leaving a visible groove between the acromion and deltoid contour. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Sulcus sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Sulcus sign to focus evaluation of inferior glenohumeral laxity and the reviewed alternatives: deltoid atrophy, normal hypermobility, axillary nerve injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the arm relaxed at the side, apply gentle downward traction. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Sulcus sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Asymptomatic laxity is common and grade is examiner dependent. Therefore, Sulcus sign cannot confirm or exclude inferior glenohumeral laxity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • deltoid atrophy

  • normal hypermobility

  • axillary nerve injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from deltoid atrophy, normal hypermobility, axillary nerve injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Sulcus sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current laxity sign; can be normal when painless. Interpret Sulcus sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Asymptomatic laxity is common and grade is examiner dependent.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Sulcus sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the arm relaxed at the side, apply gentle downward traction. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not repeatedly traction an acutely injured shoulder.
