Definition

Sclerodactyly is an observed and palpated hand sign. It is a clue to systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease. A positive finding means finger skin is tight, shiny, thickened, and difficult to pinch.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Sclerodactyly means finger skin is tight, shiny, thickened, and difficult to pinch.

Why It Matters

The observed Sclerodactyly finding can signal systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease. Fibroblast activation deposits collagen and contracts dermis around digits. Sclerodactyly cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease

Assessment Method

Inspect and gently assess finger skin mobility.

Positive Finding

Finger skin is tight, shiny, thickened, and difficult to pinch.

Mechanism

Fibroblast activation deposits collagen and contracts dermis around digits.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Sclerodactyly finding raises concern for systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease because fibroblast activation deposits collagen and contracts dermis around digits. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Sclerodactyly does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Sclerodactyly to focus evaluation of systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease and the reviewed alternatives: amyloidosis, burn scar, chronic graft-versus-host disease. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect and gently assess finger skin mobility. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Sclerodactyly as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Edema can precede thickening and other conditions stiffen hands. Therefore, Sclerodactyly cannot confirm or exclude systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • amyloidosis

  • burn scar

  • chronic graft-versus-host disease

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as amyloidosis, burn scar, chronic graft-versus-host disease.

False Negative Situations

Sclerodactyly may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of systemic sclerosis or other fibrosing disease.
