Boxed Warning

High-alert warning focus: ethionamide can cause serious hepatotoxicity, severe GI intolerance, neuropsychiatric toxicity, peripheral neuropathy, and hypothyroidism. It belongs in resistant-TB combination therapy with liver, thyroid, neurologic, mental-status, and adherence monitoring.

Class

Thioamide second-line antitubercular; mycolic-acid synthesis inhibitor prodrug

Used To Treat

Drug-resistant tuberculosis as part of a multidrug regimen when susceptibility and specialist guidance support use.

Description

Ethionamide is a thioamide TB prodrug activated by mycobacterial EthA, then inhibits InhA to disrupt mycolic-acid synthesis in the TB cell wall. The card should make nurses think MDR-TB backup therapy with severe GI intolerance, hepatotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy, hypothyroidism, psychiatric/CNS effects, and pyridoxine support.

Mechanism

Mycobacteria activate ethionamide through a monooxygenase pathway, producing metabolites that form NAD adducts and inhibit InhA, an enoyl-ACP reductase needed for mycolic-acid synthesis. That overlaps conceptually with isoniazid's cell-wall target but has its own intolerance and thyroid/CNS toxicity profile.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food if ordered/tolerated strategy supports it, and monitor nausea/vomiting because intolerance can break adherence.

  • Assess liver symptoms, mood/psychosis, neuropathy, seizure risk, thyroid symptoms, glucose instability, and pyridoxine use.

  • Escalate jaundice, severe vomiting, confusion, hallucinations, suicidal thinking, or numbness/tingling.

Contraindications

  • Severe liver disease or serious prior ethionamide reaction. Clarify pregnancy questions, seizure disorder, psychiatric instability, diabetes, thyroid disease, and heavy alcohol use.

Interactions

  • Alcohol increases toxicity concern. Cycloserine and other neurotoxic TB drugs can compound CNS effects; hepatotoxic TB drugs can compound liver risk.

Key Labs

  • Liver function tests.

  • TSH/free T4 when prolonged therapy or symptoms suggest hypothyroidism.

  • Glucose in diabetes.

  • Neurologic/mental-status assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Ethionamide sounds like isoniazid because both hit mycolic-acid biology, but ethionamide is a second-line thioamide with major GI, thyroid, CNS, and liver toxicity.

  • Pyridoxine teaching is not only for isoniazid; neuropathy risk matters here too.
