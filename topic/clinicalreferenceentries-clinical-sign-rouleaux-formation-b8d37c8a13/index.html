Definition

Rouleaux formation is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion. A positive finding means red cells align in long stacks resembling coins.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rouleaux formation means red cells align in long stacks resembling coins.

Why It Matters

The Rouleaux formation laboratory finding can signal increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion. Immunoglobulins or fibrinogen reduce the zeta potential that normally keeps erythrocytes apart. Rouleaux formation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion

Assessment Method

Review the smear for linear coin-stack aggregates and correlate with plasma-protein studies.

Positive Finding

Red cells align in long stacks resembling coins.

Mechanism

Immunoglobulins or fibrinogen reduce the zeta potential that normally keeps erythrocytes apart.

Clinical Interpretation

The Rouleaux formation laboratory finding raises concern for increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion because immunoglobulins or fibrinogen reduce the zeta potential that normally keeps erythrocytes apart. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Rouleaux formation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Rouleaux formation to focus evaluation of increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion and the reviewed alternatives: cold agglutination, smear artifact, hyperfibrinogenemia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review the smear for linear coin-stack aggregates and correlate with plasma-protein studies. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Rouleaux formation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Thick smear areas and delayed preparation can mimic aggregation. Therefore, Rouleaux formation cannot confirm or exclude increased plasma proteins reducing red-cell repulsion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cold agglutination

  • smear artifact

  • hyperfibrinogenemia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with cold agglutination, smear artifact, hyperfibrinogenemia.

False Negative Situations

Rouleaux formation may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
