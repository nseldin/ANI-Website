Summary

Innate immunity responds quickly to common patterns of infection or damage using barriers, phagocytes, complement, inflammatory mediators, and natural killer cells. Adaptive immunity takes longer on first exposure because rare antigen-specific B and T lymphocytes must be selected and expanded, but it provides precise effector functions and memory. The systems are partners: innate sensing contains danger and supplies antigen presentation and costimulation, while antibodies and T cells sharpen elimination and make future responses faster.

Quick Answer

Skin, mucosa, antimicrobial molecules, neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells, complement, mast cells, and NK cells provide immediate defense. Dendritic cells process antigen and present peptides with appropriate signals to T cells in lymphoid tissue. CD4 helper T cells coordinate; CD8 cytotoxic T cells kill selected infected or abnormal cells; B cells become antibody-secreting plasma cells and memory cells. Antibodies neutralize, opsonize, activate complement, or support cellular killing depending on class and context. Self-tolerance restrains attack on the body. Too little immunity causes recurrent or unusual infection; misdirected or excessive immunity causes allergy, autoimmunity, inflammatory injury, or transplant rejection.

Sections

  • Label

    Structure and cellular organization

    Text

    Barrier epithelia line skin, airways, gut, and genitourinary tracts. Innate cells arise largely from marrow myeloid lineages, including neutrophils, monocytes or macrophages, dendritic cells, eosinophils, basophils, and mast cells; NK cells arise from lymphoid lineage but act rapidly. B cells mature in marrow. T-cell precursors mature and undergo selection in the thymus. Mature cells circulate through blood, lymph, nodes, spleen, and tissues. B-cell follicles and T-cell zones organize adaptive activation. Antibodies circulate or enter secretions. Complement proteins circulate as inactive precursors. This spatial organization places fast sensors at barriers and brings rare specific lymphocytes together with antigen in secondary lymphoid organs.

  • Label

    Causal mechanism: innate recognition

    Text

    Pattern-recognition receptors detect conserved microbial patterns or danger signals from injured cells. Signaling activates cytokines, chemokines, antimicrobial responses, phagocytosis, and inflammation. Vasodilation and permeability recruit proteins and leukocytes; neutrophils arrive rapidly, while macrophages ingest material and coordinate repair. Complement can coat targets, recruit inflammation, and form membrane-attacking complexes. NK cells recognize altered host-cell signals and can induce apoptosis without the same antigen-specific receptor rearrangement used by T cells. These mechanisms are fast because the receptors are preconfigured, but that speed limits specificity. Dendritic cells bridge the systems by carrying antigen and activation context to lymphoid tissue.

  • Label

    Causal mechanism: adaptive specificity and memory

    Text

    Each naive B or T lymphocyte expresses a receptor with one specificity generated before exposure. Antigen plus costimulatory and cytokine signals select a matching clone, which proliferates and differentiates. CD4 T cells coordinate macrophage, B-cell, and other responses; CD8 cells recognize peptide on MHC class I and kill selected host cells. B cells bind antigen and, with appropriate help, become plasma cells that secrete antibody, undergo class switching and affinity maturation, or become memory cells. Because clonal expansion takes time, the primary response is delayed. Memory cells and persistent antibody permit a faster, stronger secondary response, which is the central logic of vaccination.

  • Label

    Regulation, relationships, and tolerance

    Text

    Immune activation needs both recognition and context. Costimulation helps prevent a harmless peptide from triggering full attack. Central tolerance removes or edits many strongly self-reactive developing lymphocytes; peripheral anergy, deletion, inhibitory receptors, and regulatory T cells restrain survivors. Cytokines direct the kind, location, and duration of response. Antibody links back to innate effectors through Fc receptors and complement. Resolution signals stop inflammation and promote repair once danger passes. Pregnancy, age, nutrition, microbiome, stress hormones, medicines, and chronic disease alter responses. Immune regulation is therefore active work, not absence of immunity, and failure can occur through deficiency, mistaken target, or failure to turn off.

  • Label

    Failure consequences and why they happen

    Text

    Neutropenia and impaired phagocyte function weaken early control of bacteria and fungi. Antibody deficiency predisposes to recurrent infections, especially with organisms that require opsonization; T-cell defects broaden risk to viral, fungal, protozoal, and intracellular pathogens. Complement defects create characteristic susceptibility patterns. HIV depletes CD4 cells and disrupts coordination. Allergy directs immune mechanisms against otherwise harmless antigens; anaphylaxis produces systemic mediator release. Autoimmune disease breaks self-tolerance. Cytokine excess and uncontrolled inflammation injure host tissue in severe infection or immune therapy. Immunologic memory can protect, but cross-reactivity or persistent activation can also sustain pathology. Clinical pattern depends on which component is impaired, not simply whether a white count is high or low.

  • Label

    Clinical connections: medications and diseases

    Text

    Vaccines expose antigen in a controlled context to generate memory without the full disease. Passive antibodies provide immediate temporary protection but do not create the recipient's durable memory. Corticosteroids broadly suppress inflammatory gene expression; calcineurin, antimetabolite, biologic, and cytotoxic agents interrupt different immune steps and create different infection and monitoring risks. Antihistamines block selected allergy effects but do not replace epinephrine in anaphylaxis. Antibiotics act on susceptible microbes, not on immune memory, and unnecessary use can disrupt microbiota and select resistance. Cancer immunotherapy releases inhibitory brakes or redirects cells, improving antitumor activity while risking immune-related organ injury. Transplant medicines trade rejection prevention for infection and malignancy risk.

  • Label

    Clinical connections: labs and diagnostics

    Text

    CBC with differential quantifies circulating leukocytes but does not fully measure function or tissue activity. Absolute neutrophil and lymphocyte counts can be more useful than percentages. CRP and ESR reflect inflammation nonspecifically. Cultures, antigen tests, nucleic-acid tests, and serology answer different questions about organism, current material, or immune response. Immunoglobulin levels, complement assays, lymphocyte subsets, flow cytometry, autoantibodies, skin tests, and functional tests investigate selected disorders. Antibody presence may reflect vaccination, past infection, passive transfer, or current response depending on timing and assay. A negative early antibody result does not exclude acute infection; an elevated white count does not prove bacterial disease.

  • Label

    Nursing assessment and actions with rationale

    Text

    Assess fever and hypothermia, infection focus, exposures, vaccine history, wounds and devices, travel, new medicines, immune suppression, allergy history, and organ-specific inflammation. In neutropenia or profound immune suppression, subtle symptoms may be the only warning; obtain ordered cultures promptly without delaying urgent antimicrobials in instability. Use standard and transmission-based precautions correctly, protect line and wound integrity, support vaccination and medication adherence, and teach food, exposure, and fever plans appropriate to the patient's regimen. During biologic infusion or transfusion, distinguish mild reactions from evolving anaphylaxis or cytokine-mediated instability and stop or escalate according to protocol while supporting airway and circulation.

  • Label

    Urgent red flags

    Text

    Airway swelling, wheeze, hypotension, collapse, or rapidly progressive multisystem symptoms after exposure suggest anaphylaxis and require emergency epinephrine-centered care. Fever or hypothermia with altered mentation, hypotension, respiratory distress, mottling, or low urine output suggests sepsis. Fever in severe neutropenia, new rash with mucosal injury, rapidly spreading soft-tissue pain, meningismus, or opportunistic-infection symptoms in a profoundly immunocompromised patient needs urgent evaluation. After immune therapy, new severe diarrhea, hepatitis, pneumonitis, endocrine crisis, myocarditis, or neurologic change may be immune-related toxicity. Do not wait for a dramatic leukocytosis when the patient cannot mount one.

  • Label

    Misconceptions and NCLEX traps

    Text

    Innate does not mean weak, and adaptive does not act alone. Inflammation is a protective process that can also cause injury; it is not synonymous with infection. Antibodies do not directly kill every pathogen and a high titer is not always protection. CD4 cells coordinate more than antibody production; CD8 cells kill selected host cells displaying antigen. Antibiotics do not treat viruses merely because fever is present. Passive immunity is immediate but temporary; active immunity takes time and creates memory. Immunosuppressed patients may lack fever or pus. A vaccine trains adaptive immunity while engaging innate signals; it does not overload a normal immune system with the actual full disease.
