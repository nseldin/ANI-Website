Definition

Collier sign is an observed ocular sign. It is a clue to dorsal midbrain dysfunction. A positive finding means the upper lids are abnormally retracted.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Collier sign means the upper lids are abnormally retracted.

Why It Matters

The observed Collier sign finding can signal dorsal midbrain dysfunction. Damage near the posterior commissure disinhibits levator palpebrae activity. Collier sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dorsal midbrain dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe upper-lid position in primary gaze and attempted upgaze.

Positive Finding

The upper lids are abnormally retracted.

Mechanism

Damage near the posterior commissure disinhibits levator palpebrae activity.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Collier sign finding raises concern for dorsal midbrain dysfunction because damage near the posterior commissure disinhibits levator palpebrae activity. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Collier sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Collier sign to focus evaluation of dorsal midbrain dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: Graves orbitopathy, stimulant effect, facial retraction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe upper-lid position in primary gaze and attempted upgaze. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Collier sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Thyroid eye disease and normal lid anatomy reduce specificity. Therefore, Collier sign cannot confirm or exclude dorsal midbrain dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Graves orbitopathy

  • stimulant effect

  • facial retraction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Graves orbitopathy, stimulant effect, facial retraction.

False Negative Situations

Collier sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of dorsal midbrain dysfunction.

Modern Status

Current localization adjunct. When performed as described, Collier sign can adjust concern for dorsal midbrain dysfunction, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Collier sign: Thyroid eye disease and normal lid anatomy reduce specificity.

Safety Precautions

Observe Collier sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe upper-lid position in primary gaze and attempted upgaze. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Compare with proptosis and thyroid signs before assigning a midbrain cause.
