Definition

Filatov sign is an observed facial-distribution sign. It is a clue to scarlet fever. A positive finding means circumoral pallor contrasts with flushed cheeks.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Filatov sign means circumoral pallor contrasts with flushed cheeks.

Why It Matters

The observed Filatov sign finding can signal scarlet fever. Toxin-mediated vasodilation affects cheeks while perioral vessels remain relatively spared. Filatov sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • scarlet fever

Assessment Method

Inspect the face around the mouth.

Positive Finding

Circumoral pallor contrasts with flushed cheeks.

Mechanism

Toxin-mediated vasodilation affects cheeks while perioral vessels remain relatively spared.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Filatov sign finding raises concern for scarlet fever because toxin-mediated vasodilation affects cheeks while perioral vessels remain relatively spared. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Filatov sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Filatov sign to focus evaluation of scarlet fever and the reviewed alternatives: perioral dermatitis, cyanosis, facial flushing. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the face around the mouth. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Filatov sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Lighting and baseline skin tone reduce reliability. Therefore, Filatov sign cannot confirm or exclude scarlet fever by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • perioral dermatitis

  • cyanosis

  • facial flushing

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as perioral dermatitis, cyanosis, facial flushing.

False Negative Situations

Filatov sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of scarlet fever.

Modern Status

Historical supportive clue. Filatov sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude scarlet fever. Apply the reviewed limitation for Filatov sign: Lighting and baseline skin tone reduce reliability.

Safety Precautions

Observe Filatov sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect the face around the mouth. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not call true perioral cyanosis “pallor”; assess oxygenation when color is blue.
