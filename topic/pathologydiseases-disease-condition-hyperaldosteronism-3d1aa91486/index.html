Definition

Hyperaldosteronism develops when aldosterone activity exceeds physiologic need, causing renal sodium retention, potassium wasting, and hypertension; the excess may be adrenal or driven by renin. The characteristic pattern is hypertension with low potassium, alkalosis, weakness, polyuria, or an adrenal or renovascular clue, interpreted through paired aldosterone and renin. Confirm or stage it with paired aldosterone and renin under standardized conditions; distinguish it from primary aldosteronism from renin-driven secondary aldosteronism by whether renin is suppressed or elevated. The priority is to treat the underlying adrenal, cardiac, hepatic, or renovascular driver because hypertensive cardiovascular and kidney damage can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Urine output change, edema, thirst, dry mucosa, dizziness, flank/suprapubic pain, dysuria, confusion, weakness, weight change, or abnormal heart rhythm can reveal renal, fluid, or electrolyte stress.

  • Escalate anuria, severe potassium abnormality, pulmonary edema, seizure, dysrhythmia, shock, or worsening acidosis.

Diagnostics

  • paired aldosterone and renin under standardized conditions

  • potassium, bicarbonate, kidney function, and medication review

  • cause-directed adrenal imaging, suppression testing, or renovascular evaluation

  • Distinguish Hyperaldosteronism from primary aldosteronism from renin-driven secondary aldosteronism by whether renin is suppressed or elevated.

Labs

  • glucose may be high or low depending on condition

  • sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphate changes can become life-threatening

  • anion gap and ketones matter in DKA

Treatments

  • treat the underlying adrenal, cardiac, hepatic, or renovascular driver

  • use mineralocorticoid-receptor blockade when indicated with potassium and creatinine surveillance

  • correct severe potassium loss cautiously while controlling blood pressure

Nursing Priorities

  • Assess repeated blood pressure, orthostasis, edema or volume status, weakness, cramps, palpitations, thirst, and urine output, and trend potassium, bicarbonate, magnesium, sodium, creatinine, aldosterone, and renin because excess aldosterone retains sodium and wastes potassium, while the renin pattern helps distinguish primary from secondary disease.

  • Verify morning collection conditions, dietary sodium instructions, potassium correction, and the prescriber's plan for interfering antihypertensives before aldosterone-renin testing because low potassium and medication effects can hide or mimic autonomous aldosterone production.

  • Coordinate confirmatory testing and adrenal CT or adrenal-vein sampling only when indicated, and assess for heart, kidney, sleep-apnea, and cerebrovascular injury because identifying lateralized primary disease changes treatment from lifelong blockade to possible cure by adrenalectomy.

  • When a mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonist is used, monitor home and clinic blood pressure, potassium, creatinine or eGFR, renin response, gynecomastia or menstrual effects, and adherence after initiation and each titration because effective blockade lowers cardiovascular risk but can cause hyperkalemia and kidney-function change.

  • Escalate for hypertensive emergency symptoms, severe hypo- or hyperkalemia, dysrhythmia, paralysis, acute kidney decline, or hypotension after treatment because vascular injury and electrolyte-driven electrical instability require immediate correction.

Complications

  • hypertensive cardiovascular and kidney damage

  • hypokalemic paralysis or dysrhythmia

  • hyperkalemia and kidney injury during blockade

Red Flags

  • Severe blood pressure with chest pain, acute dyspnea, neurologic deficit, seizure, or confusion

  • Palpitations, syncope, ECG change, marked weakness, or paralysis with potassium abnormality

  • Rapid creatinine rise, oliguria, or severe hyperkalemia after mineralocorticoid blockade

  • Symptomatic hypotension, dehydration, or inability to take prescribed therapy safely

Patient Education

  • Record blood pressure and take the aldosterone-specific medicine consistently because normal potassium alone does not mean the excess cardiovascular risk is controlled.

  • Do not use potassium supplements, salt substitutes, licorice products, or NSAIDs without review because they can alter blood pressure, potassium, kidney function, or medication response.

Nclex Traps

  • Hyperaldosteronism is a physiology label; renin separates primary from secondary causes

  • Do not start replacement or blockade without trending potassium and kidney function
