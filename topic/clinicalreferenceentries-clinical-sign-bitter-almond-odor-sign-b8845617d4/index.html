Definition

Bitter-almond odor sign is an observed odor clue. It is a clue to possible cyanide exposure. A positive finding means a bitter-almond odor is perceived.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bitter-almond odor sign means a bitter-almond odor is perceived.

Why It Matters

The observed Bitter-almond odor sign finding can signal possible cyanide exposure. Volatile hydrogen cyanide can stimulate olfactory receptors in genetically able observers. Bitter-almond odor sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • possible cyanide exposure

Assessment Method

If an odor is noticed incidentally, document it without deliberate close inhalation.

Positive Finding

A bitter-almond odor is perceived.

Mechanism

Volatile hydrogen cyanide can stimulate olfactory receptors in genetically able observers.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bitter-almond odor sign finding raises concern for possible cyanide exposure because volatile hydrogen cyanide can stimulate olfactory receptors in genetically able observers. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bitter-almond odor sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bitter-almond odor sign to focus evaluation of possible cyanide exposure and the reviewed alternatives: almond product, chemical solvent, subjective error. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: If an odor is noticed incidentally, document it without deliberate close inhalation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bitter-almond odor sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many people cannot genetically detect the odor and smelling exposes staff. Therefore, Bitter-almond odor sign cannot confirm or exclude possible cyanide exposure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • almond product

  • chemical solvent

  • subjective error

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as almond product, chemical solvent, subjective error.

False Negative Situations

Bitter-almond odor sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of possible cyanide exposure.

Historical and unsafe unreliable clue. Bitter-almond odor sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude possible cyanide exposure. Apply the reviewed limitation for Bitter-almond odor sign: Many people cannot genetically detect the odor and smelling exposes staff.
