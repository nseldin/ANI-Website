Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to sargramostim, yeast-derived products, or a formulation component is a contraindication.

  • Do not administer simultaneously with or within the prohibited interval around cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiotherapy in routine oncologic use, and do not use a rising WBC as proof that infection or marrow injury is resolved.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact LEUKINE presentation, reconstitution fluid, final concentration, weight, route, and schedule because formulation and diluent errors can cause toxicity, especially in small patients.

  • Trend the full CBC differential rather than ANC alone because GM-CSF affects a broader myeloid range and can stimulate leukemic myeloid cells.

  • Monitor temperature, lungs, oxygenation, daily weight, edema, intake/output, blood pressure, and rhythm because capillary leak, effusions, respiratory compromise, or supraventricular arrhythmia can resemble infection or fluid resuscitation effects.

  • Assess bone pain and injection reactions while treating fever as possible sepsis until evaluated; growth-factor fever does not safely exclude infection.

Interactions

  • Cytotoxic chemotherapy and radiotherapy can preferentially injure the proliferating progenitors sargramostim stimulates; outside emergency H-ARS use, do not administer sargramostim within 24 hours before or 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiotherapy because separation protects actively dividing marrow precursors.

  • Other colony-stimulating factors and lithium can add to leukocyte stimulation, while diuretics may mask rather than correct capillary leak; combine only through a monitored specialist plan.

Key Labs

  • Serial CBC with differential, ANC, monocytes, eosinophils, platelets, hemoglobin, reticulocytes, and radiation lineage trends.

  • Electrolytes, renal and liver function, albumin, weight and fluid balance, oxygenation, ECG, chest imaging for respiratory symptoms, and cultures or sepsis studies when clinically indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • Sargramostim is GM-CSF and broader than neutrophil-focused G-CSF.

  • Fever during treatment is not automatically a benign drug effect in a neutropenic patient.

  • Fluid leak and effusions explain why weight and lung assessment matter alongside CBC.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Neonates and infants

    Note

    Benzyl-alcohol-containing diluent can cause serious toxicity, including gasping syndrome; use only the label-approved preservative-free preparation and weight-based verification.

  • Population

    Patients with cardiac, pulmonary, or renal disease

    Note

    Reduced ability to tolerate fluid shifts, effusions, and tachyarrhythmia raises the consequence of capillary leak, so weight, lungs, rhythm, and fluid balance require closer surveillance.

  • Population

    Patients with myeloid malignancy, pregnancy, lactation, or advanced age

    Note

    Malignant-cell stimulation, limited reproductive data, and lower organ reserve complicate risk, but credible H-ARS may still justify treatment under hematology and emergency guidance.

Used To Treat

Product-specific myeloid recovery after selected leukemia, progenitor-cell mobilization, transplantation, graft failure, and acute exposure to myelosuppressive radiation. Sargramostim stimulates a broader myeloid network than G-CSF; it is not an antibiotic, platelet-specific treatment, thyroid blocker, or decorporation agent.

Mechanism

Sargramostim binds the GM-CSF receptor alpha subunit, recruits the shared beta common chain, and activates JAK2/STAT5, Ras/MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling. These pathways increase myeloid-progenitor survival, proliferation, differentiation, and mature-cell activation across more lineages than G-CSF. Cytokine-driven endothelial and inflammatory effects can increase permeability, explaining edema and pleural or pericardial effusions. The drug can amplify survivors but cannot recreate a marrow compartment eradicated by radiation, so response depends on dose and residual stem-cell reserve.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): sargramostim can cause severe hypersensitivity, capillary leak, pleural or pericardial effusion, respiratory compromise, and supraventricular arrhythmia. New edema, hypoxemia, or rapid weight gain is urgent.

Description

Sargramostim is yeast-derived recombinant GM-CSF that promotes survival, proliferation, differentiation, and function across granulocyte, monocyte/macrophage, eosinophil, and related progenitor populations. In H-ARS it amplifies surviving marrow after radiation injury, but recovery is not limited to ANC and can be accompanied by fever, fluid retention, effusions, capillary leak, or supraventricular dysrhythmia. Broader myeloid stimulation is the reason CBC differential, fluid balance, lungs, and rhythm are monitored together.

Adverse Effects

  • Fever, chills, bone pain, myalgia, headache, injection or infusion reaction, leukocytosis, eosinophilia, nausea, and diarrhea can occur.

  • Capillary leak, edema, pleural or pericardial effusion, respiratory failure, severe hypersensitivity, supraventricular arrhythmia, and stimulation of malignant myeloid cells are serious risks.

Administration Timing

  • For credible H-ARS, begin the current label's weight-based subcutaneous regimen as soon as possible after suspected or confirmed myelosuppressive radiation exposure rather than waiting for the nadir.

  • Obtain serial CBC with differential and platelets and continue the product's regimen through the specified recovery endpoint, adjusting only under the incident hematology protocol.

  • Outside H-ARS, preserve separation from cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiotherapy because stimulating progenitors during cytotoxic exposure can worsen marrow injury.

Escalation Recurrence

  • New hypoxemia, rapid weight gain, edema, hypotension, effusion, tachyarrhythmia, anaphylaxis, or marked leukocytosis requires interruption and urgent evaluation.

  • Absent lineage recovery or recurrent cytopenia requires reassessment of radiation dose, residual stem cells, infection, consumption, malignant marrow involvement, and need for advanced hematology support.

Evidence Limitations

  • The H-ARS indication was established under the FDA Animal Rule; human efficacy cannot be tested deliberately and is inferred from animal survival plus human pharmacology and safety.

  • Sargramostim is not equivalent to filgrastim or pegfilgrastim: lineage breadth, formulation, dosing, adverse effects, and labels differ.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled for product-specific hematologic indications and to increase survival after acute myelosuppressive radiation exposure; H-ARS efficacy was established under the Animal Rule.
