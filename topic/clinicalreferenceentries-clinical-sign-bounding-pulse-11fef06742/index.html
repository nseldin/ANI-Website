Definition

Bounding pulse means an arterial pulse feels stronger and expands more forcefully than expected. It can be a normal temporary response or a clue to a hyperdynamic or rapid-runoff circulation.

Plain Language

The pulse feels unusually strong or forceful under the fingers.

Why It Matters

A bounding pulse can accompany fever, anemia, pregnancy, thyroid excess, early vasodilatory shock, or aortic regurgitation. It does not guarantee good organ perfusion, so blood pressure, pulse pressure, rhythm, temperature, mental status, urine output, and accompanying symptoms determine urgency.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

With the patient resting, palpate matched peripheral pulses bilaterally using consistent light pressure, assess rate, rhythm, amplitude, contour, and symmetry, palpate one carotid at a time when central contour is needed, and correlate with blood pressure, pulse pressure, temperature, perfusion, murmur, medication, fever, anemia, thyroid, and sepsis context.

Positive Finding

The artery expands more forcefully and with greater amplitude than expected and may have a brisk upstroke or be difficult to compress.

Mechanism

A large or rapidly ejected stroke volume, reduced peripheral vascular resistance, rapid diastolic runoff, or a combination produces a high-amplitude pressure wave.

Evidence Limitations

Pulse grading and force are examiner dependent; anxiety, recent activity, temperature, vessel stiffness, and palpation pressure alter the impression, and a strong radial pulse does not establish adequate organ perfusion. Therefore, Bounding pulse cannot confirm or exclude hyperdynamic circulation from exercise, anxiety, fever, anemia, pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, or early vasodilation, and rapid arterial runoff from aortic regurgitation, patent ductus arteriosus, or arteriovenous fistula by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal strong pulse, Corrigan pulse, transmitted pulsation, arterial aneurysm, wide pulse-pressure state.

False Negative Situations

Bounding pulse may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of hyperdynamic circulation from exercise, anxiety, fever, anemia, pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, or early vasodilation, and rapid arterial runoff from aortic regurgitation, patent ductus arteriosus, or arteriovenous fistula.
