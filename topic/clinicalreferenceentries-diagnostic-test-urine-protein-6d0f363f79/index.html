Summary

Urine protein is a high-yield Renal/Urologic diagnostic reference. Detects proteinuria in kidney disease and pregnancy hypertensive disorders.

Quick Answer

Urine protein: Detects proteinuria in kidney disease and pregnancy hypertensive disorders. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative / trace

    Meaning

    No meaningful proteinuria is detected, though concentrated urine and timing can affect dipstick readings.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    Proteinuria suggests kidney disease, preeclampsia, fever/exercise effect, infection, or orthostatic proteinuria depending context.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Quantify persistent protein with protein/creatinine ratio or timed urine as ordered and assess edema, BP, creatinine, and pregnancy status.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Detects proteinuria in kidney disease and pregnancy hypertensive disorders.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
