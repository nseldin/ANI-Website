Used To Treat

Systemic treatment of dermatophyte infections of hair, skin or nails when topical treatment is inadequate, including selected tinea capitis; it has no effect on bacteria, Candida or other non-dermatophyte fungi.

Mechanism

Griseofulvin binds fungal tubulin, interferes with spindle microtubule assembly and arrests mitosis. Keratin affinity carries drug into newly formed tissue, which becomes less susceptible to Trichophyton, Microsporum and Epidermophyton invasion. It is not a general antifungal. Hepatic CYP induction accelerates metabolism of other drugs, while absorption of poorly soluble microsize product improves with a fatty meal. Cure requires organism control plus replacement of infected keratin.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy

  • Porphyria

  • Hepatocellular failure

  • Known griseofulvin hypersensitivity and careful review of reproductive plans/drug interactions

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm a dermatophyte with KOH, fungal culture or biopsy before therapy and identify particle-size formulation.

  • Give with food/fat as directed, teach photoprotection and continue until infected keratin is replaced and the prescribed course is complete.

Interactions

  • CYP induction lowers warfarin, hormonal contraceptive and possibly other substrate exposure.

  • Alcohol can cause a disulfiram-like flushing/tachycardia reaction.

  • Barbiturates can reduce griseofulvin absorption/effect; monitor interacting anticonvulsants and immunosuppressants.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test before treatment

  • CBC, liver and renal function during prolonged therapy

  • KOH/culture or nail/hair diagnostic confirmation and clinical regrowth

Nclex Traps

  • Griseofulvin is fungistatic microtubule inhibition, not ergosterol blockade.

  • Microsize and ultramicrosize doses are not automatically equivalent.

  • Backup nonhormonal contraception is needed because enzyme induction can reduce contraceptive reliability.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in pregnancy because conjoined-twin and fetal-harm reports exist, and in porphyria or hepatocellular failure. It reduces hormonal-contraceptive and warfarin effects through enzyme induction. Severe skin reactions, hepatotoxicity, leukopenia and photosensitivity can occur; alcohol may provoke tachycardia/flushing.

Description

Griseofulvin is an oral fungistatic dermatophyte antifungal derived from Penicillium that binds fungal microtubular proteins and disrupts the mitotic spindle. It also deposits in keratin precursor cells, so newly produced hair, skin and nail resist dermatophyte invasion while infected keratin grows out. This explains both its usefulness in tinea capitis and its slow clinical timeline. Microsize and ultramicrosize formulations absorb differently and cannot be exchanged milligram-for-milligram without checking the product.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, fatigue, dizziness, nausea, vomiting and diarrhea

  • Photosensitivity, rash, urticaria, SJS/TEN or lupus-like reaction

  • Hepatotoxicity, leukopenia, granulocytopenia and proteinuria

  • Candida overgrowth during prolonged therapy
