Definition

Nixon sign is a percussion maneuver. It is a clue to splenomegaly. A positive finding means an expanded area of dullness suggests an enlarged spleen.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Nixon sign means an expanded area of dullness suggests an enlarged spleen.

Why It Matters

A positive Nixon sign response can signal splenomegaly. Gravity brings the spleen forward while adjacent gas-filled stomach and colon remain more resonant. Nixon sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • splenomegaly

Assessment Method

With the patient in right lateral decubitus, percuss obliquely across the left lower chest.

Positive Finding

An expanded area of dullness suggests an enlarged spleen.

Mechanism

Gravity brings the spleen forward while adjacent gas-filled stomach and colon remain more resonant.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Nixon sign response raises concern for splenomegaly because gravity brings the spleen forward while adjacent gas-filled stomach and colon remain more resonant. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nixon sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Nixon sign to focus evaluation of splenomegaly and the reviewed alternatives: renal mass, pleural disease, fecal loading. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient in right lateral decubitus, percuss obliquely across the left lower chest. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Nixon sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus and bowel contents limit accuracy. Therefore, Nixon sign cannot confirm or exclude splenomegaly by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • renal mass

  • pleural disease

  • fecal loading

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from renal mass, pleural disease, fecal loading, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Nixon sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct. Nixon sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude splenomegaly. Apply the reviewed limitation for Nixon sign: Body habitus and bowel contents limit accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Nixon sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the patient in right lateral decubitus, percuss obliquely across the left lower chest. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use percussion to clear a patient for contact activity when splenomegaly is possible.
