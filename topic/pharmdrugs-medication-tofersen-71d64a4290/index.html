Class

Intrathecal SOD1-mRNA-degrading antisense oligonucleotide

Used To Treat

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis in adults with a confirmed mutation in the superoxide dismutase 1 gene; approval is accelerated based on plasma neurofilament light reduction.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current QALSODY labeling has no boxed warning. Serious neurologic inflammatory complications include myelitis, radiculitis, papilledema with increased intracranial pressure, and aseptic meningitis. New back or radicular pain, weakness or sensory change, headache, visual symptoms, fever, neck stiffness, photophobia, altered consciousness or bladder dysfunction requires urgent evaluation.

Mechanism

The oligonucleotide forms a DNA-RNA hybrid with SOD1 transcript in CNS cells. RNase H1 cleaves the RNA strand, lowering both mutant and wild-type SOD1 protein available to misfold, aggregate and exert toxic gain-of-function effects. The treatment does not correct the underlying DNA mutation or restore motor neurons already destroyed.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm pathogenic SOD1 mutation, loading/maintenance schedule, prior dose, lumbar-puncture safety, anticoagulants, infection, spinal anatomy, baseline neurologic/visual findings and CSF removal/administration procedure.

  • After dosing monitor puncture complications and delayed inflammatory symptoms; continue respiratory, bulbar, nutritional, mobility, communication and goals-of-care support independent of biomarker response.

Key Labs

  • Genetic confirmation, CSF and neuroimaging evaluation when inflammatory complications are suspected, plasma neurofilament light and CSF SOD1 only in specialist context, plus longitudinal ALSFRS-R, respiratory and functional measures.

Nclex Traps

  • A lower neurofilament light level is a surrogate endpoint and does not by itself prove that an individual patient's strength will improve.

  • Tofersen is only for SOD1-ALS; most ALS is not caused by an SOD1 mutation.

Description

Tofersen is an intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide that binds SOD1 mRNA, recruits RNase H1, and reduces synthesis of superoxide dismutase 1 protein in mutation-confirmed SOD1-associated ALS. It is a genotype-specific treatment, not a drug for every ALS case, and its U.S. approval relies on neurofilament light reduction as a surrogate reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit while confirmatory evidence continues.
