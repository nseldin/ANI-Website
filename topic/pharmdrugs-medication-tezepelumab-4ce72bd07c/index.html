Class

Subcutaneous thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP)-blocking monoclonal antibody acting upstream in airway inflammation

Used To Treat

Add-on maintenance treatment of severe asthma in adults and adolescents age twelve and older and inadequately controlled chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps in the same age group. It is not treatment for acute bronchospasm, status asthmaticus, or an acute nasal obstruction emergency.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hypersensitivity can occur within hours or be delayed for days; postmarketing anaphylaxis has been reported. Tezepelumab is not acute treatment. Do not abruptly reduce corticosteroids. Treat pre-existing helminth infection and interrupt therapy if infection persists despite treatment. Avoid live attenuated vaccines because concomitant use has not been evaluated and TSLP participates in immune responses.

Mechanism

Tezepelumab-ekko is a human IgG2-lambda antibody that binds soluble human TSLP with high affinity and prevents TSLP from binding the TSLP-receptor/IL-7-receptor-alpha heterodimer. TSLP produced mainly by epithelial cells activates dendritic cells, ILC2 cells, mast cells, basophils, eosinophils, and lymphocytes, promoting type 2 cytokines and broader airway inflammation. Blocking this upstream signal reduces eosinophils, FeNO, IL-5, IL-13, and serum IgE, but the complete clinical mechanism is not definitively established. Unlike omalizumab it does not bind IgE; unlike mepolizumab it does not bind IL-5; unlike benralizumab it does not deplete IL-5-receptor-bearing cells through ADCC. This target position explains broader biomarker eligibility rather than universal response.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify 210 mg subcutaneous every four weeks, product form, storage, injection technique, and whether self-administration has been approved and taught.

  • Track exacerbations, steroid courses, symptoms, spirometry, eosinophils and FeNO where available, injection reactions, helminth risk, and delayed hypersensitivity.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to tezepelumab-ekko or an excipient

  • Not for acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus; avoid live attenuated vaccines and treat relevant helminth infection

Interactions

  • Avoid live attenuated vaccines during treatment according to labeling.

  • Do not abruptly reduce corticosteroids or combine asthma biologics without a specialist plan.

Key Labs

  • Exacerbation frequency, oral-steroid burden, spirometry, eosinophils, FeNO, IgE context, and symptom control

  • Helminth history, vaccination review, hypersensitivity, and injection-site assessment

Nclex Traps

  • TSLP is an upstream epithelial cytokine; tezepelumab does not directly block beta2, IgE, IL-5, or IL-5R.

  • Broader biomarker eligibility does not mean the injection treats acute bronchospasm or every asthma phenotype equally.

Description

Tezepelumab is a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody that blocks thymic stromal lymphopoietin, an epithelial 'alarmin' released near the top of the airway inflammatory cascade after allergens, viruses, pollutants, or tissue injury. By preventing TSLP from engaging its heterodimeric receptor, it reduces downstream activation across dendritic cells, ILC2 cells, mast cells, eosinophils, and other pathways. Blood eosinophils, FeNO, IL-5, IL-13, and IgE can all fall. Because the target is upstream, severe-asthma use is not restricted to the same allergic-IgE or eosinophil thresholds as some other biologics, although phenotype and biomarkers still help predict response and exclude alternative causes. It prevents future events and never substitutes for a rapid reliever.

Adverse Effects

  • Pharyngitis, arthralgia, back pain, injection-site reaction, upper-respiratory infection, epistaxis, or influenza depending on indication

  • Anaphylaxis or delayed hypersensitivity, rash, allergic conjunctivitis, helminth-related concern, or antibody development
