Used To Treat

Label-specific Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, rheumatoid arthritis with methotrexate, ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, and plaque psoriasis. It is an induction-and-maintenance IV biologic, not a rescue infusion for undifferentiated joint pain or infection.

Mechanism

Infliximab binds monomeric and trimeric soluble TNF-alpha and cell-surface TNF with high affinity, preventing TNFR1/TNFR2 signaling through NF-kappaB, MAPK, adhesion, chemokine, and metalloproteinase pathways. Its intact IgG1 Fc can recruit complement and Fc-receptor effector functions against cells expressing transmembrane TNF. TNF neutralization decreases inflammatory-cell migration and tissue injury but weakens macrophage activation and granuloma integrity. Murine-derived antigen-binding sequences increase immunogenic potential; antidrug antibodies accelerate clearance, shorten response, and increase infusion reactions. Scheduled induction and maintenance reduce gaps that favor immunogenicity, while methotrexate can suppress antibody formation in rheumatoid arthritis. The IV route produces high immediate systemic exposure, explaining the need for monitored infusion assessment.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: serious infections and malignancy. TB, sepsis, invasive fungal disease, and other opportunistic infections can be fatal. Test for latent TB, treat before therapy when indicated, and stop infliximab for serious infection or sepsis. Consider empiric antifungal therapy in severe systemic illness with endemic exposure while diagnostic work proceeds. Lymphoma and other malignancies are reported; hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma has occurred mainly in adolescent and young adult patients with Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis receiving TNF blockers with azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine.

Contraindications

  • Infliximab doses above 5 mg/kg are contraindicated in moderate or severe heart failure; prior severe hypersensitivity to infliximab, murine proteins, or excipients also precludes use.

  • Do not infuse through active serious infection. Clarify TB, hepatitis B, endemic fungi, malignancy, demyelination, liver disease, vaccine status, and prior infusion reaction.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify weight-based dose, indication, induction versus maintenance week, infection screen, premedication plan when used, IV access, and reaction equipment. Monitor during and after infusion per protocol.

  • Stop or slow infusion and assess fever/chills, pruritus, urticaria, chest pain, dyspnea, hypotension, or hypertension; delayed arthralgia, rash, fever, or edema can reflect immune-complex disease.

Interactions

  • Avoid combination with another biologic, anakinra, abatacept, or a JAK inhibitor; additive immunosuppression raises serious infection.

  • Live vaccines and therapeutic infectious agents are avoided. Immunogenicity rises with treatment gaps and may fall with concomitant methotrexate or other immunomodulation, at the cost of added toxicity.

Key Labs

  • TB and hepatitis B assessment, CBC, liver tests, infection evaluation, disease activity, and weight for each dose

  • Vital signs and cardiopulmonary status around infusion; antidrug antibody/trough testing when clinically indicated for loss of response

Nclex Traps

  • Infliximab is IV and chimeric; adalimumab is subcutaneous and fully human. Sharing a TNF target does not erase infusion or immunogenicity differences.

  • A delayed reaction days after re-exposure can be immune-complex mediated even when the monitored infusion itself seemed uneventful.

Description

Infliximab is an intravenous chimeric IgG1 antibody with murine variable regions that bind TNF-alpha and human constant regions that provide Fc function. It neutralizes soluble and transmembrane TNF, reducing destructive inflammation in joints, bowel, skin, and spine. The chimeric structure and intermittent IV exposure make antidrug antibodies and infusion reactions particularly important; concomitant methotrexate can reduce immunogenicity in rheumatoid regimens. Acute reactions can occur during or soon after infusion, while delayed serum-sickness-like reactions may appear days later. TNF blockade also disrupts granuloma maintenance, creating serious TB and endemic-fungal risk. Doses above 5 mg/kg are contraindicated in moderate or severe heart failure because clinical worsening and mortality were observed.

Adverse Effects

  • Acute infusion reaction, delayed serum-sickness-like reaction, infection, headache, abdominal symptoms, rash, and antidrug-antibody loss of response

  • TB/HBV reactivation, invasive fungal infection, lymphoma, hepatotoxicity, cytopenia, demyelination, lupus-like syndrome, severe skin reaction, arrhythmia/ischemia during infusion, and heart-failure worsening
