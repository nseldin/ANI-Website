Class

Topical carbonic-anhydrase-II inhibitor ophthalmic suspension aqueous suppressant

Used To Treat

Elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious sulfonamide hypersensitivity is possible after topical administration. Avoid or use only under specialist direction in severe renal impairment or hyperchloremic acidosis. Corneal edema can occur in patients with low endothelial reserve. Shake the suspension or the delivered dose may be inconsistent.

Mechanism

Inhibiting carbonic anhydrase II in ciliary processes reduces bicarbonate formation and the linked sodium/fluid transport that creates aqueous humor. Lower production reduces intraocular pressure. The insoluble suspension formulation keeps drug deliverable to the eye but requires resuspension before each dose for consistent concentration.

Nursing Essentials

  • Shake the suspension well before every dose. Assess renal function, acid-base risk, corneal history and sulfonamide hypersensitivity; warn about short-lived blurred vision after instillation.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to brinzolamide or formulation components. Clarify severe renal impairment, hyperchloremic acidosis, sulfonamide reaction history, corneal endothelial disease and concurrent systemic CA inhibitors.

Interactions

  • Systemic carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors add metabolic acidosis/electrolyte risk. High-dose salicylates may amplify systemic toxicity. Separate other eye drops by at least 5 minutes.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic nerve and visual fields

  • Corneal examination

  • Renal function, bicarbonate and electrolytes when systemic risk exists

Nclex Traps

  • Brinzolamide is a suspension and must be shaken.

  • Topical administration does not eliminate renal or acid-base considerations.

  • The bitter taste is a clue to systemic drainage, not an oral dosing error.

Description

Brinzolamide is a topical carbonic-anhydrase-II inhibitor suspension that reduces ciliary-body aqueous secretion. Its pathway is similar to dorzolamide, but the suspension must be shaken and often causes transient blurred vision. Systemic absorption, red-cell binding and renal elimination mean severe kidney dysfunction, sulfonamide reactions and acid-base effects still matter even though it is an eye drop.

Adverse Effects

  • Blurred vision, bitter/sour taste, ocular discomfort, blepharitis/conjunctivitis, dry eye, headache, corneal edema and rare systemic sulfonamide or acid-base effects.
