Contraindications

  • Age under 2 years, hypersensitivity and product-specific conflicts with monoamine oxidase inhibitors. Clarify severe cardiovascular/vascular-insufficiency disease, depression and concurrent CNS depressants.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check age, alertness, blood pressure, pulse and fall risk. Teach caregivers that unusual sleepiness, limpness, slowed breathing or poor feeding after exposure requires urgent assessment.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • CNS depressants increase sedation. Antihypertensives and cardiac glycosides can add hypotension/bradycardia. MAO inhibitors and tricyclic antidepressants can alter adrenergic response; verify product guidance before combining.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic nerve and visual fields

  • Blood pressure, pulse, alertness and respiratory status in vulnerable patients

  • Ocular allergy/tachyphylaxis assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Brimonidine is contraindicated below age 2, not simply dose-reduced.

  • A sleepy infant after accidental eye-drop exposure may have alpha-2 toxicity.

  • Long-term red/itchy eyes may be drug allergy, not worsening glaucoma.

Used To Treat

Lowering intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension; some lower-concentration products are also marketed for ocular redness with different use limits.

Mechanism

Presynaptic alpha-2 receptor activation couples to Gi proteins, lowers cAMP and reduces norepinephrine release and ciliary epithelial fluid secretion; downstream outflow effects add pressure reduction. Drug entering systemic circulation can reduce sympathetic tone in brainstem and vasculature, explaining fatigue, hypotension and dangerous infant CNS/respiratory depression.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in neonates and infants under 2 years because CNS depression, apnea, bradycardia and hypotension can be severe. Use caution with depression, cerebral/coronary insufficiency, Raynaud phenomenon, orthostatic hypotension and severe cardiovascular disease. Allergic follicular conjunctivitis and tachyphylaxis commonly limit long-term use.

Description

Brimonidine is a relatively alpha-2-selective eye drop that lowers pressure by reducing aqueous-humor production and modestly increasing uveoscleral outflow. Systemic absorption can suppress the CNS and lower blood pressure. Neonates and infants younger than 2 years can develop apnea, bradycardia, hypotension, hypothermia and profound somnolence, making that age group a clear contraindication.

Adverse Effects

  • Allergic conjunctivitis, hyperemia, itching, dry mouth, fatigue/somnolence, headache, blurred vision, hypotension, bradycardia and reduced effectiveness over time.
