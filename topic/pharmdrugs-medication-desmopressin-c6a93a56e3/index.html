Used To Treat

Product- and route-specific treatment of central diabetes insipidus, selected mild hemophilia A with factor VIII activity above the labeled threshold and no inhibitor, selected mild to moderate type 1 von Willebrand disease, and certain nocturia/enuresis indications. Formulations are not dose-for-dose interchangeable.

Mechanism

Desmopressin binds Gs-coupled V2 receptors. In collecting-duct principal cells, adenylyl cyclase raises cAMP, protein kinase A phosphorylates trafficking proteins, and aquaporin-2 channels move to the apical membrane; water reabsorption rises while urine volume falls. In vascular endothelial cells, V2-linked signaling triggers exocytosis of Weibel-Palade bodies containing VWF and factor VIII-associated stores. Plasma VWF and VIII rise within minutes, but benefit depends on the patient having releasable functional protein. Type 3 VWD lacks useful stores, many qualitative variants are unsuitable, and type 2B release can worsen platelet aggregation and thrombocytopenia. Repeated doses at short intervals deplete stores while continuing water retention, so hemostatic benefit falls as hyponatremia risk accumulates.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Desmopressin can cause life-threatening hyponatremia. Ensure sodium is normal before starting or resuming; restrict fluid according to route and indication; monitor sodium within the label-defined interval and more often in high-risk patients. Interrupt or discontinue for hyponatremia. Loop diuretics, glucocorticoids, polydipsia, SIADH, substantial renal impairment, and acute fluid/electrolyte illness are major labeled hazards.

Contraindications

  • Hyponatremia or history of hyponatremia, polydipsia, SIADH, or labeled fluid/electrolyte illness

  • Creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min, heart failure, uncontrolled hypertension, or concomitant loop diuretic/systemic or inhaled glucocorticoid use under current injection labeling

  • Not effective for nephrogenic diabetes insipidus or severe/unresponsive VWD; avoid type 2B hemostatic use without specialist direction

Nursing Essentials

  • Match indication, formulation, route, concentration, and dose. Intranasal, oral, sublingual, IV, and SC products are not simple microgram-for-microgram substitutions.

  • Track sodium, intake/output, weight, urine concentration, neurologic status, and fluid restriction. For bleeding use, document prior DDAVP challenge and factor/VWF response.

Interactions

  • SSRIs, tricyclics, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, opioids, NSAIDs, thiazides, and other hyponatremia-promoting drugs increase water-intoxication risk.

  • Loop diuretics and systemic or inhaled glucocorticoids are contraindicated with specified desmopressin products because severe hyponatremia risk rises.

Key Labs

  • Serum sodium, osmolality, urine output, urine osmolality/specific gravity, renal function, and weight

  • For hemostasis: VWF activity, factor VIII activity, CBC/platelets, bleed response, and subtype/prior challenge

Nclex Traps

  • Desmopressin is antidiuretic and hemostatic through the same V2 receptor family but different tissues.

  • Improved urine output or bleeding does not cancel sodium monitoring; closely repeated doses can retain water after VWF stores are depleted.

Description

Desmopressin is a selective vasopressin V2-receptor agonist that activates renal V2 signaling, promotes collecting-duct aquaporin-2 insertion to concentrate urine, and releases endothelial von Willebrand factor and factor VIII for selected mild bleeding disorders. These renal and endothelial effects are its two clinically important identities. The same water-retaining mechanism can dilute sodium enough to cause seizure, coma, or death. Repeated hemostatic doses can lose effect because endothelial stores become depleted, which explains tachyphylaxis.

Adverse Effects

  • Water intoxication and hyponatremia with headache, nausea, confusion, seizure, coma, or death

  • Flushing, headache, nausea, abdominal discomfort, blood-pressure change, or tachycardia

  • Thrombosis or worsened thrombocytopenia in susceptible VWD phenotypes
