Definition

Traube gastric splash sign is an auscultatory movement sign. It is a clue to gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying. A positive finding means a splash persists several hours after intake.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Traube gastric splash sign means a splash persists several hours after intake.

Why It Matters

A positive Traube gastric splash sign response can signal gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying. Retained gastric air and fluid move because emptying is impaired. Traube gastric splash sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying

Assessment Method

After an appropriate fasting interval, gently rock the upper abdomen while auscultating.

Positive Finding

A splash persists several hours after intake.

Mechanism

Retained gastric air and fluid move because emptying is impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Traube gastric splash sign response raises concern for gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying because retained gastric air and fluid move because emptying is impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Traube gastric splash sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Traube gastric splash sign to focus evaluation of gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying and the reviewed alternatives: large hiatal hernia, bowel splash, normal postprandial stomach. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: After an appropriate fasting interval, gently rock the upper abdomen while auscultating. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Traube gastric splash sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Recent drinking and normal gastric contents cause false positives. Therefore, Traube gastric splash sign cannot confirm or exclude gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • large hiatal hernia

  • bowel splash

  • normal postprandial stomach

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from large hiatal hernia, bowel splash, normal postprandial stomach, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Traube gastric splash sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; imaging or endoscopy determines cause. Traube gastric splash sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude gastric outlet obstruction or delayed gastric emptying. Apply the reviewed limitation for Traube gastric splash sign: Recent drinking and normal gastric contents cause false positives.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Traube gastric splash sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: After an appropriate fasting interval, gently rock the upper abdomen while auscultating. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not vigorously shake a painful or postoperative abdomen.
