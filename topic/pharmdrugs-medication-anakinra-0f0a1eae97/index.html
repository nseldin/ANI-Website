Class

Recombinant human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist that blocks IL-1 alpha and IL-1 beta signaling

Used To Treat

Adults with moderate to severe active rheumatoid arthritis after inadequate response to one or more DMARDs, neonatal-onset multisystem inflammatory disease within CAPS, and deficiency of the IL-1 receptor antagonist. It is daily subcutaneous therapy and not interchangeable with all IL-1-targeted agents or approved for routine pediatric juvenile rheumatoid arthritis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious infections and neutropenia can occur. Do not start during active infection, interrupt for serious infection in rheumatoid treatment, monitor neutrophils before and during therapy, and avoid live vaccines. Do not combine with TNF blockers because serious infection and neutropenia increase. Hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, requires immediate treatment. Current labeling also warns about amyloidosis monitoring in long-standing autoinflammatory disease.

Mechanism

IL-1 alpha or beta binds IL-1 receptor type I, then recruits IL-1 receptor accessory protein. The intracellular TIR domains assemble MyD88, IRAK kinases, and TRAF6, activating NF-kappaB and MAPK transcription of cytokines, chemokines, cyclooxygenase-2, adhesion molecules, and fever pathways. Anakinra competitively binds IL-1R1 but does not recruit IL-1RAcP, so the complex is silent. In DIRA, inherited absence of endogenous IL-1Ra leaves IL-1 signaling unopposed; replacement directly corrects that missing brake. The protein is cleared substantially by the kidney, so exposure rises in severe renal impairment. A short half-life explains daily administration and why missed doses can rapidly permit disease return.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach daily refrigerated injection, rotation, expected early local reactions, and when a spreading or systemic reaction is not routine.

  • Obtain ANC before treatment, monthly for three months, then quarterly for a year under rheumatoid labeling; monitor kidney function and disease-specific amyloid/organ findings.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to E. coli-derived proteins, anakinra, or excipients; active serious infection requires delay or interruption according to indication.

  • Clarify neutropenia, recurrent infection, TB risk, renal impairment, live vaccines, concurrent biologic therapy, and latex sensitivity in device components.

Interactions

  • Do not combine with etanercept or another TNF blocker and generally avoid other biologic DMARD combinations because serious infection rises.

  • Avoid live vaccines. Renal impairment increases exposure even without a CYP interaction because anakinra is a protein cleared substantially by the kidney.

Key Labs

  • CBC with ANC, renal function, infection/TB context, liver tests when clinically indicated, and disease activity

  • Injection-site and hypersensitivity assessment; urine protein and organ function in chronic autoinflammatory disease where amyloidosis is a concern

Nclex Traps

  • Anakinra blocks the IL-1 receptor and therefore blocks signaling from both IL-1 alpha and IL-1 beta; it does not bind only one ligand.

  • Its short half-life and renal clearance explain daily dosing and reduced frequency in severe renal impairment.

Description

Anakinra is a recombinant IL-1 receptor antagonist that competitively occupies IL-1R1 without activating it, blocking inflammatory signaling from both IL-1 alpha and IL-1 beta. It is a nonglycosylated version of the natural human antagonist and prevents either cytokine from recruiting the accessory protein that starts signaling. IL-1 drives fever, endothelial activation, pain sensitization, neutrophil recruitment, cartilage damage, and autoinflammatory syndromes. Because anakinra replaces a short-lived endogenous brake rather than depleting a cell, it is given daily and its effect wanes relatively quickly after stopping. Renal clearance is important, so severe kidney impairment calls for less frequent dosing. Combining it with a TNF inhibitor markedly increases serious infection and neutropenia without enough added benefit.

Adverse Effects

  • Very common injection-site erythema, pain, bruising or swelling; headache, nausea, diarrhea, and upper-respiratory infection

  • Serious infection, neutropenia, anaphylaxis/angioedema, liver-enzyme elevation, and uncontrolled autoinflammation with amyloid organ injury
