Class

IV fourth-generation cephalosporin plus ESBL-focused beta-lactamase inhibitor

Used To Treat

Adults with complicated urinary tract infection, including pyelonephritis, caused by designated susceptible Escherichia coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Proteus mirabilis, or Enterobacter cloacae complex under the U.S. label.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cefepime neurotoxicity can occur, especially with renal impairment and unadjusted exposure, and can progress from confusion or myoclonus to seizure or nonconvulsive status epilepticus. Enmetazobactam does not protect against encephalopathy or unsupported carbapenemases.

Mechanism

Enmetazobactam covalently inhibits susceptible serine beta-lactamases. Protected cefepime enters through porins, acylates PBPs, and stops transpeptidation, with fT>MIC driving killing. Renal clearance controls both components. The label uses longer infusion in augmented renal clearance because rapid elimination shortens exposure, while renal impairment requires lower dose or longer interval to prevent accumulation. This two-direction rule explains why current eGFR is checked at least daily during change: kidney recovery can cause failure, and kidney decline can cause neurotoxicity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check serum creatinine and eGFR at least daily when renal function changes, then verify the matching dose and infusion duration because both underexposure and neurotoxicity are preventable exposure errors.

  • Perform serial attention, speech, tremor, myoclonus, and consciousness assessment in high-risk patients because subtle cefepime toxicity can precede convulsion.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to cefepime, enmetazobactam, cephalosporins, penicillins, or other beta-lactams is a labeled contraindication.

  • Do not extrapolate the cUTI label to unsupported infection sites or resistance enzymes without susceptibility and specialist rationale.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides and potent diuretics can add renal risk or require compatibility planning; use separate administration unless validated.

  • Copper-reduction urine glucose tests can give false-positive results with cephalosporins, so glucose oxidase methods are preferred when the distinction matters.

Key Labs

  • Urine/blood culture and MIC, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, AST/ALT/bilirubin, coagulation when risk exists, mental status, and EEG for suspected nonconvulsive status.

  • A reported ESBL should lead to the actual combination MIC, not an assumption that every ESBL is enmetazobactam susceptible.

Nclex Traps

  • Enmetazobactam is an ESBL shield; cefepime is the PBP-binding antibiotic.

  • Both augmented and impaired renal function require attention, but for opposite exposure reasons.

    Note

Description

Cefepime/enmetazobactam, branded Exblifep, combines a fourth-generation cephalosporin with a penicillanic-acid-sulfone inhibitor optimized against selected class A ESBLs such as CTX-M. Enmetazobactam protects cefepime from ESBL hydrolysis; cefepime remains the bactericidal component that binds PBPs and is relatively stable against AmpC. The combination is U.S.-labeled for adult cUTI/pyelonephritis caused by designated susceptible organisms. It is not a universal CRE, KPC, OXA-48-like, or MBL regimen. Cefepime's concentration-related encephalopathy, myoclonus, and seizure risk remains central because the inhibitor does not change CNS pharmacology.

Display Name

Cefepime Enmetazobactam

Adverse Effects

  • Increased transaminases or bilirubin, headache, phlebitis/infusion-site reaction, diarrhea, nausea, rash, and positive direct Coombs test.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, CDI, prolonged prothrombin time, cytopenias, encephalopathy, aphasia, myoclonus, nonconvulsive status, or seizure.

Class Pathway

  • Enmetazobactam inhibits selected ESBLs

  • Cefepime traverses porins and binds PBPs

  • Renal-function-specific infusion preserves exposure
