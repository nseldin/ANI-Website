Class

Long-acting aminosteroid nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker with vagolytic effects

Used To Treat

Longer skeletal-muscle paralysis for surgery or mechanical ventilation under general anesthesia/continuous sedation, analgesia, ventilation and quantitative neuromuscular monitoring.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Accidental administration can be fatal because respiratory paralysis occurs without unconsciousness. Use only with immediate ventilation/intubation, continuous sedation and analgesia, and quantitative train-of-four monitoring. Marked tachycardia, hypertension, dysrhythmia and prolonged paralysis are higher-yield concerns than with several newer alternatives.

Mechanism

Pancuronium occupies nicotinic acetylcholine receptor sites at the motor end plate without activating them, preventing depolarization and skeletal-muscle contraction. It also blocks cardiac M2-mediated vagal influence and can reduce norepinephrine reuptake, producing tachycardia. Hepatic metabolism contributes, but unchanged renal excretion is important, so accumulation extends blockade when renal clearance falls.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document sedation/analgesia independently from paralysis and monitor heart rate, rhythm and blood pressure closely because vagolysis can be clinically important.

  • Use quantitative train-of-four monitoring and expect prolonged recovery in renal dysfunction, hypothermia or interacting-drug exposure. Plan reversal and extubation from actual recovery, not elapsed time.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to pancuronium or bromide. Clarify severe tachyarrhythmia/ischemia, renal failure, neuromuscular disease, electrolyte disturbance and concurrent medicines that potentiate blockade.

Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics, aminoglycosides, magnesium, lithium and several antiarrhythmics potentiate blockade. Renal impairment promotes accumulation. Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors can reverse appropriate residual block with antimuscarinic protection; sugammadex is not routinely used as a dependable pancuronium reversal strategy.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative train-of-four ratio, continuous ECG/blood pressure and ventilation

  • Renal function, potassium, magnesium, calcium, acid-base status and temperature

  • Post-paralytic respiratory strength and airway-protection assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Pancuronium-associated tachycardia comes from vagolytic/catecholamine effects, not inadequate paralysis.

  • Renal dysfunction can greatly prolong a drug already considered long acting.

  • No movement does not prove unconsciousness.

Description

Pancuronium is a long-acting nondepolarizing paralytic that competitively blocks Nm nicotinic receptors. Its defining difference is cardiac muscarinic/vagal blockade and catecholamine effects that commonly increase heart rate, blood pressure and cardiac output. Significant renal elimination can make paralysis prolonged in kidney dysfunction. Like every paralytic, it has no effect on consciousness or pain.

Adverse Effects

  • Prolonged apnea/paralysis, residual weakness, tachycardia, hypertension, increased myocardial oxygen demand, dysrhythmia, salivation, anaphylaxis and rare bronchospasm.
