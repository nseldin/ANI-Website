Summary

Free T3 is a high-yield Endocrine diagnostic reference. Triiodothyronine level, often relevant in hyperthyroid evaluation.

Quick Answer

Free T3: Triiodothyronine level, often relevant in hyperthyroid evaluation. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Normal

    Meaning

    T3 is not elevated at that draw; this does not replace TSH/free T4 in most screening.

  • Label

    High

    Meaning

    Elevated T3 can support hyperthyroidism, including T3 toxicosis when T4 is not high.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    T3 is most helpful in selected hyperthyroid evaluation, not as the first general thyroid screen.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Triiodothyronine level, often relevant in hyperthyroid evaluation.

    • Before / preparation

    • Check timing, fasting status, current endocrine medications, steroid use, pregnancy status when relevant, and whether dynamic testing requires serial specimens.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect the result to symptoms, glucose safety, thyroid/adrenal crisis risk, and medication teaching.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Severe hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, thyroid storm/myxedema cues, adrenal crisis, altered mental status, dehydration, or unstable vital signs.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Endocrine tests often require correct timing. Wrong timing can make the result misleading.
