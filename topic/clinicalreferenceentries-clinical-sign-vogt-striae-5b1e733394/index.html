Definition

Vogt striae is an observed corneal sign. It is a clue to keratoconus or corneal ectasia. A positive finding means fine vertical stromal lines may disappear with pressure.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Vogt striae means fine vertical stromal lines may disappear with pressure.

Why It Matters

The observed Vogt striae finding can signal keratoconus or corneal ectasia. Mechanical stress in a thinned ectatic cornea creates compression folds in posterior stroma and Descemet membrane. Vogt striae cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • keratoconus or corneal ectasia

Assessment Method

Examine deep cornea by slit lamp and apply very gentle globe pressure only in specialist examination.

Positive Finding

Fine vertical stromal lines may disappear with pressure.

Mechanism

Mechanical stress in a thinned ectatic cornea creates compression folds in posterior stroma and Descemet membrane.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Vogt striae finding raises concern for keratoconus or corneal ectasia because mechanical stress in a thinned ectatic cornea creates compression folds in posterior stroma and Descemet membrane. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Vogt striae does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Vogt striae to focus evaluation of keratoconus or corneal ectasia and the reviewed alternatives: postoperative edema, acute hydrops, contact-lens warpage. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine deep cornea by slit lamp and apply very gentle globe pressure only in specialist examination. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Vogt striae as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Lines may be absent and other corneal stress creates folds. Therefore, Vogt striae cannot confirm or exclude keratoconus or corneal ectasia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • postoperative edema

  • acute hydrops

  • contact-lens warpage

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as postoperative edema, acute hydrops, contact-lens warpage.

False Negative Situations

Vogt striae may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of keratoconus or corneal ectasia.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not press a painful, postoperative, or structurally compromised eye.
