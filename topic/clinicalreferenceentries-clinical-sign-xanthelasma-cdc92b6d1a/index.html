Definition

Xanthelasma is an observed eyelid sign. It is a clue to local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia. A positive finding means soft yellow plaques are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Xanthelasma means soft yellow plaques are present.

Why It Matters

The observed Xanthelasma finding can signal local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia. Dermal macrophages accumulate cholesterol within superficial eyelid tissue. Xanthelasma cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia

Assessment Method

Inspect the medial upper and lower eyelids.

Positive Finding

Soft yellow plaques are present.

Mechanism

Dermal macrophages accumulate cholesterol within superficial eyelid tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Xanthelasma finding raises concern for local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia because dermal macrophages accumulate cholesterol within superficial eyelid tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Xanthelasma does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Xanthelasma to focus evaluation of local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia and the reviewed alternatives: syringoma, sebaceous hyperplasia, skin tumor. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the medial upper and lower eyelids. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Xanthelasma as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many patients have normal lipids and appearance is not diagnostic. Therefore, Xanthelasma cannot confirm or exclude local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • syringoma

  • sebaceous hyperplasia

  • skin tumor

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as syringoma, sebaceous hyperplasia, skin tumor.

False Negative Situations

Xanthelasma may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of local lipid deposition sometimes associated with dyslipidemia.
