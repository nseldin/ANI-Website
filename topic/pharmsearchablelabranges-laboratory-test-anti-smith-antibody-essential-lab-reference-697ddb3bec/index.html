Range

Anti-Smith is usually reported as negative or positive, and some laboratories also provide an assay-specific numeric or semiquantitative value. Use the performing laboratory's antigen method and reference limit; platforms differ, so there is no universal numeric cutoff that establishes systemic lupus erythematosus.

Why

A positive Anti-Smith (anti-Sm) result is associated with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and can support lupus evaluation after a positive ANA, but anti-Sm is not present in everyone with SLE. Assess rash or photosensitivity, oral ulcers, inflammatory joint symptoms, chest pain or dyspnea, neurologic symptoms, fever or infection risk, blood pressure, edema, and urine changes. Evaluation can include ANA, anti-dsDNA, complements, CBC, kidney function, urinalysis, urine protein quantification, and rheumatology review; kidney biopsy may be needed when findings suggest lupus nephritis. Escalate urgently for chest pain, severe pain, seizure, acute neurologic change, or rapid deterioration, and promptly report proteinuria, hematuria, rising creatinine, hypertension, or edema. A positive Anti-Smith result does not diagnose SLE by itself, and a negative result does not rule out SLE; the 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria use anti-Sm only as one immunologic criterion after the ANA entry requirement.

High Causes

  • Systemic lupus erythematosus when the clinical and laboratory evaluation fits

  • An SLE-specific-antibody criterion within the 2019 EULAR/ACR classification framework after the ANA entry requirement

  • Assay or laboratory variation affecting a borderline or discordant result

Low Causes

  • Anti-Smith not detected; this does not exclude systemic lupus erythematosus because anti-Sm is not present in everyone with SLE.

Critical Concerns

  • Proteinuria, hematuria, rising creatinine, hypertension, edema, or active urinary sediment in a lupus evaluation needs prompt clinician follow-up for possible lupus nephritis.

  • Chest pain, severe pain, seizure, acute neurologic change, rapid deterioration, or fever during immunosuppressive therapy requires urgent evaluation independent of Anti-Smith status.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess skin, oral, joint, cardiopulmonary, neurologic, hematologic, infection, blood-pressure, edema, and urinary findings rather than focusing on the antibody alone.

  • Review ANA, anti-dsDNA, complements, CBC, creatinine, urinalysis, and urine protein quantification, and coordinate rheumatology or kidney evaluation when indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • A positive Anti-Smith result supports a compatible lupus evaluation but does not diagnose SLE by itself.

  • A negative Anti-Smith result does not rule out SLE.

  • Do not confuse anti-Smith with anti-smooth-muscle antibody, which is used in a different autoimmune liver evaluation.
