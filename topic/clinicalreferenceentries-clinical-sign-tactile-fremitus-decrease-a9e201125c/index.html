Definition

Tactile fremitus decrease is a palpation sign. It is a clue to air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction. A positive finding means vibration is reduced or absent over one region.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tactile fremitus decrease means vibration is reduced or absent over one region.

Why It Matters

A positive Tactile fremitus decrease response can signal air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction. Pleural air or fluid blocks transmission, while an obstructed bronchus prevents vibration reaching distal lung. Tactile fremitus decrease cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction

Assessment Method

Compare vocal vibrations over symmetric lung fields.

Positive Finding

Vibration is reduced or absent over one region.

Mechanism

Pleural air or fluid blocks transmission, while an obstructed bronchus prevents vibration reaching distal lung.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Tactile fremitus decrease response raises concern for air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction because pleural air or fluid blocks transmission, while an obstructed bronchus prevents vibration reaching distal lung. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tactile fremitus decrease does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tactile fremitus decrease to focus evaluation of air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction and the reviewed alternatives: thick chest wall, poor effort, mainstem obstruction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compare vocal vibrations over symmetric lung fields. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tactile fremitus decrease as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity and weak voice cause diffuse reduction. Therefore, Tactile fremitus decrease cannot confirm or exclude air or fluid separating lung from chest wall or airway obstruction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • thick chest wall

  • poor effort

  • mainstem obstruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from thick chest wall, poor effort, mainstem obstruction, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Tactile fremitus decrease may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Apply the reviewed limitation for Tactile fremitus decrease: Obesity and weak voice cause diffuse reduction.

Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Combine with percussion and breath sounds to distinguish air from fluid.
