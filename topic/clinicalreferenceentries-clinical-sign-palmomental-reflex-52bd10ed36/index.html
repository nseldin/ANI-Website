Definition

Palmomental reflex is a bedside primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. A positive finding means the ipsilateral mentalis muscle contracts and the chin wrinkles.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Palmomental reflex means the ipsilateral mentalis muscle contracts and the chin wrinkles.

Why It Matters

A positive Palmomental reflex response can signal frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. Loss of cortical inhibition permits a primitive hand-to-chin reflex linkage. Palmomental reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction

Assessment Method

Stroke the thenar eminence with a blunt stimulus.

Positive Finding

The ipsilateral mentalis muscle contracts and the chin wrinkles.

Mechanism

Loss of cortical inhibition permits a primitive hand-to-chin reflex linkage.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Palmomental reflex response raises concern for frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction because loss of cortical inhibition permits a primitive hand-to-chin reflex linkage. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Palmomental reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Palmomental reflex to focus evaluation of frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: normal aging, anxiety, facial synkinesis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Stroke the thenar eminence with a blunt stimulus. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Palmomental reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is common in healthy older adults and lacks standalone diagnostic value. Therefore, Palmomental reflex cannot confirm or exclude frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal aging

  • anxiety

  • facial synkinesis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal aging, anxiety, facial synkinesis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Palmomental reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct with low specificity. Palmomental reflex remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude frontal release or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. Apply the reviewed limitation for Palmomental reflex: It is common in healthy older adults and lacks standalone diagnostic value.

Before assessing Palmomental reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Stroke the thenar eminence with a blunt stimulus. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid overcalling a subtle chin twitch or using the finding to diagnose dementia.
