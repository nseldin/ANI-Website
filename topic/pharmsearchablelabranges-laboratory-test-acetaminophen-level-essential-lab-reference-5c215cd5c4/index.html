Name

Acetaminophen level

Range

An acetaminophen concentration has no single therapeutic or toxic reference range. For an acute ingestion lasting less than 24 hours with a reliable time, use a serum or plasma concentration drawn 4-24 hours after the ingestion began with the revised Rumack-Matthew nomogram; a value drawn before 4 hours cannot risk-stratify the patient. The nomogram is not used for repeated supratherapeutic ingestion spanning more than 24 hours, an unreliable or unknown time, or as the sole guide when extended-release formulation or a coingestant may delay absorption. Units, assay conventions, and protocols vary, so use the laboratory report and poison-center guidance.

Why

An acetaminophen level helps estimate hepatotoxicity risk after suspected exposure and raises concern for acute liver injury when it plots on or above the appropriate treatment line or remains detectable with evolving liver abnormalities. Patients may look well early. Assess the exact product and formulation, amount, time and duration, repeated doses, coingestants, body weight, vomiting or right-upper-quadrant pain, mental status, alcohol or liver history, and self-harm risk. Cause-directed evaluation includes serial acetaminophen concentrations when indicated plus AST/ALT, PT/INR, bilirubin, glucose, electrolytes, creatinine/BUN, and acid-base or lactate testing in severe poisoning. Notify Poison Help or toxicology immediately; administer ordered acetylcysteine without avoidable delay when the reviewed pathway indicates, and do not stop it based on the clock or one level alone. EXTRIP reserves hemodialysis for selected severe presentations with major clinical abnormalities and very high concentrations; the reported dose or concentration alone is generally not a dialysis indication when acetylcysteine is being given. Escalate immediately for altered consciousness, seizure, respiratory distress, shock, hypoglycemia, severe acidosis, or rapid deterioration. One acetaminophen level does not diagnose liver injury or exclude poisoning if it was drawn too early or interpreted outside appropriate nomogram conditions.

High Causes

  • A single acute acetaminophen overdose, including an intentional ingestion

  • Unintentional duplicate exposure from more than one acetaminophen-containing prescription or over-the-counter product

  • Repeated supratherapeutic dosing for pain or fever

  • Delayed absorption from an extended-release product or selected opioid or anticholinergic coingestants

  • Reduced clearance or evolving hepatic injury in a severe presentation

Low Causes

  • A sample drawn before 4 hours can be falsely reassuring and cannot be plotted for acute-ingestion risk

  • A late or falling level can coexist with established hepatic injury, so pair it with the timeline, AST/ALT, PT/INR, and clinical condition

  • An undetectable value does not reconstruct an unreliable exposure history or exclude a repeated-ingestion pathway

Critical Concerns

  • Do not wait for symptoms before contacting Poison Help or the treating clinician; early acetaminophen poisoning can be clinically quiet while the treatment window is time-sensitive.

  • Altered consciousness, severe metabolic or lactic acidosis, shock, hypoglycemia, rising aminotransferases or PT/INR, acute kidney injury, or hepatic encephalopathy signals severe poisoning and requires urgent toxicology-level care.

  • EXTRIP reserves hemodialysis for selected extreme poisonings with severe clinical features; a reported dose or concentration alone generally does not determine dialysis when acetylcysteine is being administered.

Nursing Considerations

  • Verify the exact collection time against when ingestion began and document whether the time is reliable, the formulation is immediate- or extended-release, and exposure was acute or repeated.

  • Trend the concentration and hepatic, coagulation, renal, glucose, electrolyte, and acid-base findings according to the poison-center or toxicology plan; report a detectable late level or worsening laboratory trend promptly.

  • Administer acetylcysteine exactly as ordered and monitor for vomiting or infusion-related hypersensitivity; never independently delay, stop, or extend therapy from one number.

Nclex Traps

  • A pre-4-hour acetaminophen level cannot be used on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram and may falsely reassure the team.

  • The revised nomogram is for a timed acute ingestion, not repeated supratherapeutic use or an unknown timeline.

  • Normal early appearance does not mean low risk; acetylcysteine is most protective when indicated treatment is started promptly.
