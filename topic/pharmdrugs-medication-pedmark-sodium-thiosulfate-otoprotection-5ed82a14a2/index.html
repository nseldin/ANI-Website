Used To Treat

Reduction of cisplatin-associated ototoxicity in pediatric patients aged one month and older with localized, nonmetastatic solid tumors. The labeled formulation is given after cisplatin on a strict schedule; it is not interchangeable with other sodium-thiosulfate products and is not established for metastatic disease or adult otoprotection.

Description

PEDMARK is a formulation-specific sodium thiosulfate product that chemically reacts with and neutralizes reactive platinum species after cisplatin has had time to exert antitumor activity. The delay is the central design feature: too early may protect tumor cells, while too late may miss cochlear platinum injury. It reduces but does not eliminate hearing loss, so baseline and follow-up audiology remain essential. Electrolyte disturbances, hypersensitivity, nausea, and vomiting require laboratory review and monitored infusion.

Mechanism

Thiosulfate acts as a sulfur-containing nucleophile and reducing agent that reacts with electrophilic platinum species, forming less-reactive complexes and increasing detoxification. In the cochlea, this limits platinum-driven oxidative and cellular injury to hair cells and supporting structures. Administering PEDMARK six hours after cisplatin separates otoprotection from peak tumor exposure; this timing explains both the benefit and the concern that earlier systemic neutralization could reduce cisplatin efficacy. The drug cannot regenerate hair cells already lost, so prevention precedes measurable hearing decline.

Administration Timing

  • Verify age, localized nonmetastatic disease, cisplatin schedule, infusion completion time, and PEDMARK formulation before each dose; other sodium-thiosulfate products are not substitutable.

  • Give the label-directed dose six hours after completion of each cisplatin infusion and respect the label's minimum separation from the next cisplatin dose because both early and crowded dosing can undermine the intended treatment separation.

  • Review electrolytes and prior reactions before infusion, use prescribed antiemetic and hydration support, and continue scheduled audiology because a tolerated infusion does not prove hearing protection.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document the exact cisplatin stop time and independently verify the PEDMARK start time because the six-hour delay protects the treatment logic, not merely workflow convenience.

  • Confirm the vial and formulation rather than substituting generic sodium thiosulfate because concentration, impurity control, and labeling are product specific.

  • Obtain age-appropriate baseline and serial hearing assessment because early high-frequency loss can affect language development before a child or caregiver notices it.

  • Monitor airway, skin, blood pressure, nausea, vomiting, hydration, and infusion tolerance because hypersensitivity and fluid-electrolyte consequences can require interruption.

Key Labs

  • Serum sodium, potassium, phosphorus, bicarbonate, calcium, magnesium, creatinine, and fluid balance before each course as directed; correct clinically important abnormalities before administration.

  • Baseline and serial audiometry or developmentally appropriate auditory testing, plus CBC and oncology response assessments so hearing benefit is interpreted alongside cancer control.

Adverse Effects

  • Vomiting, nausea, decreased hemoglobin, hypernatremia, hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, and other infusion- or electrolyte-related effects can occur.

  • Serious hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis, is possible; thiosulfate's sodium load can worsen fluid or sodium imbalance in susceptible patients.

Contraindications

  • History of severe hypersensitivity to sodium thiosulfate or formulation components is a contraindication.

  • Do not generalize the indication to metastatic tumors, adults, infants younger than one month, or a different thiosulfate formulation; efficacy and tumor-protection risk are context specific.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Airway symptoms, hypotension, generalized urticaria, severe vomiting, or major electrolyte change requires stopping the infusion and urgent treatment.

  • New hearing loss, tinnitus, speech regression, or abnormal audiology despite therapy requires audiology and oncology escalation because prevention is incomplete and later rehabilitation may be time sensitive.

Interactions

  • Cisplatin timing is the defining interaction: systemic platinum neutralization too early could reduce antitumor exposure, so never move PEDMARK earlier for convenience.

  • Other sodium-containing infusions, nephrotoxic therapy, or medicines that worsen potassium or phosphate loss can amplify electrolyte and volume risk; reconcile the full treatment-day regimen before dosing.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children with metastatic disease

    Note

    The labeled indication excludes metastatic disease because survival concerns have been raised in broader trial populations; do not infer safety or efficacy beyond localized nonmetastatic tumors.

  • Population

    Infants and children with developing speech and language

    Note

    Even partial high-frequency hearing loss can disrupt language acquisition, so objective serial audiology matters more than waiting for a verbal complaint.

  • Population

    Patients with renal, cardiac, or electrolyte disease

    Note

    Sodium and solute handling may be less tolerant, while cisplatin itself injures kidneys; closer fluid and electrolyte assessment is required before each delayed dose.

Evidence Limitations

  • PEDMARK evidence and approval apply to its specific formulation, pediatric age range, timing, and localized nonmetastatic solid-tumor population.

  • Otoprotection is not complete, and long-term hearing and oncologic outcomes still require follow-up; this card must not promise preserved hearing.

Nclex Traps

  • PEDMARK is not generic cyanide-treatment sodium thiosulfate and products are not substitutable.

  • The six-hour delay is intended to preserve cisplatin's antitumor action while protecting the cochlea.

  • Continue audiology even when PEDMARK was given correctly.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled to reduce cisplatin-associated ototoxicity in pediatric patients one month and older with localized, nonmetastatic solid tumors; the label states that PEDMARK is not substitutable with other sodium-thiosulfate products.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): starting PEDMARK at the wrong interval can undermine the designed separation from cisplatin. Verify the six-hour timing independently and treat anaphylaxis or major electrolyte disturbance immediately.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
