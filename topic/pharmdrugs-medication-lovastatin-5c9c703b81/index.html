Used To Treat

LDL-C and coronary-risk reduction in label-defined adults and selected adolescents with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. It provides low- or moderate-intensity therapy depending on dose and formulation.

Mechanism

Hydrolysis opens lovastatin's lactone ring to create the active beta-hydroxy acid that resembles the HMG-CoA substrate. Competitive reductase inhibition lowers hepatocyte cholesterol and activates LDL-receptor transcription. Immediate-release oral bioavailability is low because of first-pass extraction, and food increases absorption, explaining the evening-meal rule. The extended-release matrix changes the absorption curve and therefore uses a bedtime fasting schedule. CYP3A4 metabolism and OATP-mediated hepatic uptake control exposure; inhibition leaves more active drug in plasma and muscle. Short activity makes timing more consequential than for atorvastatin or rosuvastatin.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Strong CYP3A inhibitors and gemfibrozil can cause dangerous lovastatin accumulation and are contraindicated or avoided under current labeling. Formulation and food instructions are not interchangeable; verify whether the product is immediate or extended release.

Contraindications

  • Active liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis and serious hypersensitivity are major class barriers; use in stable chronic liver disease is individualized rather than automatically prohibited.

  • Most pregnant patients discontinue because therapy is preventive and fetal cholesterol biology matters. Current labeling permits rare individualized high-risk decisions; breastfeeding is generally not recommended during treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline lipid panel, exact product and dose, expected intensity, liver and thyroid context, kidney function when relevant, pregnancy plans, muscle history, alcohol, and all interacting medicines or supplements.

  • Teach urgent review for severe weakness, dark urine, oliguria, jaundice, or systemic illness. Mild symptoms still deserve assessment, but one episode does not prove permanent intolerance to every statin.

  • Immediate release: give with the evening meal. Extended release: give at bedtime without food and swallow whole. Confirm formulation every time the schedule changes.

  • Review new macrolide, azole, antiviral, transplant, and cardiovascular drugs before the next dose rather than waiting for muscle symptoms.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors, cyclosporine, gemfibrozil, danazol, and large grapefruit exposure can be contraindicated or require avoidance. Amiodarone, verapamil, diltiazem, and other named drugs impose dose limits.

  • Other fibrates and lipid-dose niacin add myopathy. Warfarin response can change, so monitor INR after initiation or dose change.

Key Labs

  • Repeat lipids after initiation or dose change to verify the expected percentage LDL reduction. Obtain baseline hepatic assessment; repeat liver tests for symptoms or clinical concern rather than routine asymptomatic monthly screening.

  • Use CK, creatinine, potassium, phosphate, urinalysis, and urine output for significant muscle symptoms or dark urine. Check TSH or other reversible contributors when the presentation suggests them.

Nclex Traps

  • Statins lower LDL mainly by increasing hepatic LDL-receptor clearance after intracellular cholesterol falls; they do not simply dissolve plaque or block intestinal fat.

  • Intensity is defined by expected percentage LDL reduction, not by comparing milligram numbers across drugs.

  • Food increases immediate-release lovastatin absorption; this is unlike many medicines where food is avoided. Altoprev uses a different bedtime fasting rule.

  • Lovastatin is a lactone prodrug and CYP3A substrate; it is not milligram equivalent to pravastatin or rosuvastatin.

Description

Lovastatin, historically branded Mevacor and available as extended-release Altoprev, is an inactive fungal-derived lactone prodrug converted to an active HMG-CoA-reductase inhibitor. Immediate-release lovastatin is taken with the evening meal because food substantially improves absorption and the short active window then overlaps nocturnal cholesterol synthesis. Extended-release lovastatin has a different release profile and is taken at bedtime without food; the formulations are not casually interchangeable. Lovastatin is mainly metabolized by CYP3A4, so strong inhibitors, grapefruit, cyclosporine, gemfibrozil, and several cardiovascular drugs can sharply raise myopathy risk. It cannot provide standard high-intensity therapy. A patient who needs a larger LDL reduction should generally switch or add a complementary agent rather than exceed product limits.

Adverse Effects

  • Muscle symptoms can range from aches with normal CK to myopathy and rare rhabdomyolysis with hyperkalemia and myoglobin-mediated acute kidney injury. Risk rises with greater exposure, interacting drugs, CKD, hypothyroidism, frailty, and acute illness.

  • Mild aminotransferase and glucose or A1c increases can occur. Serious liver failure is rare, and the small diabetes signal usually does not outweigh prevention benefit when the indication is strong.
