Definition

Apley compression test is a prone knee maneuver. It is a clue to meniscal pathology. A positive finding means joint-line pain or clicking occurs with compression.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Apley compression test means joint-line pain or clicking occurs with compression.

Why It Matters

A positive Apley compression test response can signal meniscal pathology. Compression and rotation load the meniscus between articular surfaces. Apley compression test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • meniscal pathology

Assessment Method

With the patient prone and knee flexed, apply downward axial pressure through the heel while rotating the tibia.

Positive Finding

Joint-line pain or clicking occurs with compression.

Mechanism

Compression and rotation load the meniscus between articular surfaces.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Apley compression test response raises concern for meniscal pathology because compression and rotation load the meniscus between articular surfaces. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Apley compression test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Apley compression test to focus evaluation of meniscal pathology and the reviewed alternatives: collateral sprain, patellofemoral pain, fracture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient prone and knee flexed, apply downward axial pressure through the heel while rotating the tibia. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Apley compression test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Knee pain from many structures can be reproduced. Therefore, Apley compression test cannot confirm or exclude meniscal pathology by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • collateral sprain

  • patellofemoral pain

  • fracture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from collateral sprain, patellofemoral pain, fracture, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Apley compression test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with limited standalone accuracy. When performed as described, Apley compression test can adjust concern for meniscal pathology, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Apley compression test: Knee pain from many structures can be reproduced.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Apley compression test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the patient prone and knee flexed, apply downward axial pressure through the heel while rotating the tibia. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid axial grinding in acute fracture, severe swelling, or postoperative knee.
