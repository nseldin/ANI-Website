Definition

Spastic catch is an observed tone sign. It is a clue to upper motor neuron syndrome. A positive finding means a velocity-dependent catch or sudden resistance appears.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Spastic catch means a velocity-dependent catch or sudden resistance appears.

Why It Matters

The observed Spastic catch finding can signal upper motor neuron syndrome. Hyperexcitable stretch reflexes activate disproportionately during rapid muscle lengthening. Spastic catch cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • upper motor neuron syndrome

Assessment Method

Move a relaxed limb passively at increasing velocities.

Positive Finding

A velocity-dependent catch or sudden resistance appears.

Mechanism

Hyperexcitable stretch reflexes activate disproportionately during rapid muscle lengthening.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Spastic catch finding raises concern for upper motor neuron syndrome because hyperexcitable stretch reflexes activate disproportionately during rapid muscle lengthening. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Spastic catch does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Spastic catch to focus evaluation of upper motor neuron syndrome and the reviewed alternatives: rigidity, dystonia, fixed contracture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Move a relaxed limb passively at increasing velocities. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Spastic catch as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Contracture and pain create nonvelocity-dependent resistance. Therefore, Spastic catch cannot confirm or exclude upper motor neuron syndrome by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • rigidity

  • dystonia

  • fixed contracture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as rigidity, dystonia, fixed contracture.

False Negative Situations

Spastic catch may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of upper motor neuron syndrome.
