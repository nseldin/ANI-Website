Class

Topical aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist

Used To Treat

Once-daily plaque psoriasis in adults and atopic dermatitis in adults and children age 2 years and older.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Folliculitis and contact dermatitis are prominent adverse effects and can mimic worsening disease or infection. Apply only to skin, not eyes, mouth or vagina. Confirm the diagnosis and reassess pustules, painful nodules, spreading erythema or treatment failure rather than simply continuing an assumed flare.

Mechanism

Ligand binding releases AhR from its cytosolic chaperone complex and permits nuclear translocation. AhR heterodimerizes with aryl hydrocarbon receptor nuclear translocator and binds response elements in DNA. Resulting transcription can increase barrier proteins and antioxidant programs while reducing inflammatory cytokine networks relevant to psoriasis and AD. Unlike topical corticosteroids, tapinarof does not activate the glucocorticoid receptor and does not produce steroid skin atrophy by that mechanism.

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a thin layer once daily and wash hands unless hands are treated; no steroid taper is required for tapinarof itself.

  • Differentiate sterile folliculitis from bacterial infection and track itch, BSA and lesion morphology.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindications

  • Avoid known serious hypersensitivity and mucosal/ocular exposure

  • Clarify infected-appearing follicular eruptions and incorrect diagnosis

Interactions

  • No major label-defined drug interactions

  • Other irritating topicals can increase dermatitis

  • Do not assume the absence of CYP warnings permits mixing products on the same site without stability data

Key Labs

  • No routine laboratory monitoring

  • Serial BSA, lesion/itch severity and folliculitis/contact-dermatitis assessment

  • Culture only when infection is clinically suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Tapinarof is an AhR agonist, not a steroid, calcineurin inhibitor or JAK inhibitor.

  • Folliculitis is a characteristic adverse effect and should be assessed rather than mislabeled automatically as disease progression.

  • Its AD approval includes age 2 and older; psoriasis labeling remains adult.

Description

Tapinarof is a once-daily, nonsteroidal topical drug that activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor in keratinocytes and immune cells to change barrier, antioxidant and inflammatory gene programs in plaque psoriasis and atopic dermatitis. Ligand-bound AhR moves from its cytosolic chaperone complex into the nucleus, partners with ARNT, and binds DNA response elements. The exact chain from receptor activation to clinical clearing is not completely established, so the card separates known receptor pharmacology from plausible downstream biology.

Adverse Effects

  • Folliculitis, contact dermatitis, pruritus and application-site irritation

  • Headache and nasopharyngitis in psoriasis trials

  • Upper/lower respiratory infection, vomiting, asthma/wheezing and ear infection reported in AD trials
