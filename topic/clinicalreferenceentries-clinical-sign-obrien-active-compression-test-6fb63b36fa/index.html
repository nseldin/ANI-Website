Definition

O'Brien active-compression test is a bedside shoulder-loading maneuver. It is a clue to superior labral or acromioclavicular pain. A positive finding means deep pain or clicking improves palm-up; top-of-shoulder pain suggests AC joint.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive O'Brien active-compression test means deep pain or clicking improves palm-up; top-of-shoulder pain suggests AC joint.

Why It Matters

A positive O'Brien active-compression test response can signal superior labral or acromioclavicular pain. The positions alter load through the biceps-labral complex and acromioclavicular joint. O'Brien active-compression test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • superior labral or acromioclavicular pain

Assessment Method

Resist downward force with the arm flexed and adducted first thumb-down then palm-up.

Positive Finding

Deep pain or clicking improves palm-up; top-of-shoulder pain suggests AC joint.

Mechanism

The positions alter load through the biceps-labral complex and acromioclavicular joint.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive O'Brien active-compression test response raises concern for superior labral or acromioclavicular pain because the positions alter load through the biceps-labral complex and acromioclavicular joint. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; O'Brien active-compression test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use O'Brien active-compression test to focus evaluation of superior labral or acromioclavicular pain and the reviewed alternatives: biceps tendinopathy, impingement, arthritis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Resist downward force with the arm flexed and adducted first thumb-down then palm-up. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating O'Brien active-compression test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain location and change are subjective and accuracy is variable. Therefore, O'Brien active-compression test cannot confirm or exclude superior labral or acromioclavicular pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • biceps tendinopathy

  • impingement

  • arthritis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from biceps tendinopathy, impingement, arthritis, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

O'Brien active-compression test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with limited specificity. When performed as described, O'Brien active-compression test can adjust concern for superior labral or acromioclavicular pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for O'Brien active-compression test: Pain location and change are subjective and accuracy is variable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing O'Brien active-compression test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Resist downward force with the arm flexed and adducted first thumb-down then palm-up. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not treat one painful position as proof of a labral tear.
