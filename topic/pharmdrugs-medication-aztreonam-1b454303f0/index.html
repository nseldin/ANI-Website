Used To Treat

Susceptible aerobic gram-negative infections, including selected Pseudomonas disease, when the regimen accounts for absent gram-positive and anaerobic activity. Inhaled aztreonam lysine is a distinct product for selected cystic-fibrosis airway infection.

Mechanism

Aztreonam acylates PBP3, a transpeptidase needed for septal peptidoglycan cross-linking. Susceptible cells elongate into filaments, fail division, and lyse. Gram-positive organisms and anaerobes lack a clinically useful combination of access and target affinity. The monobactam ring resists metallo-beta-lactamases, but co-produced ESBL, AmpC, KPC, or other serine beta-lactamases may hydrolyze it; this is why specialist aztreonam-avibactam or ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam strategies can matter for selected resistance patterns. Renal elimination makes dose adjustment and dialysis timing essential.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Aztreonam has no current U.S. boxed warning. Serious hypersensitivity, C. difficile colitis, cytopenias, liver injury, and renal accumulation can occur. Its narrow spectrum is the most important safety concept: untreated gram-positive or anaerobic infection can progress even while gram-negative coverage is excellent. Inhaled Cayston and injectable Azactam use different salts, routes, devices, and indications and are not interchangeable.

Contraindications

  • Known serious aztreonam hypersensitivity; clarify true ceftazidime allergy because side-chain similarity creates a special cross-reactivity concern.

  • Do not use injectable aztreonam alone when empiric gram-positive or anaerobic coverage is required, and do not substitute injectable arginine product for inhaled lysine product.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before giving, identify infection site and expected organism, culture plan, renal function, allergy phenotype including ceftazidime, and companion coverage.

  • For inhaled therapy, give bronchodilator in the ordered sequence, use the specified nebulizer, and do not mix other drugs in the device unless product guidance permits.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides may provide in vitro synergy against selected gram-negative organisms but add kidney and ear toxicity; use only through a deliberate regimen.

  • Nephrotoxic drugs or acute kidney injury increase accumulation risk; dialysis modality and timing determine supplemental dosing.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, renal function, CBC and liver tests during prolonged therapy, stool evaluation for significant diarrhea, and respiratory assessment with inhaled use.

  • Trend the actual infection and hemodynamics; a falling gram-negative count does not compensate for missing gram-positive or anaerobic coverage.

Nclex Traps

  • Aztreonam covers aerobic gram-negative bacteria only; it is not a universal penicillin-allergy replacement.

  • Remember the ceftazidime side-chain exception and the noninterchangeability of inhaled aztreonam lysine and injectable aztreonam.

Description

Aztreonam is a single-ring monobactam beta-lactam that enters susceptible gram-negative bacteria through porins, binds PBP3, and blocks septal cell-wall synthesis, killing susceptible aerobic gram-negative organisms while leaving gram-positive and anaerobic organisms uncovered. It cannot replace a broad beta-lactam by itself when those organisms matter. Immunologic cross-reactivity with most penicillins and cephalosporins is uncommon because the core and side chains differ. The important exception is ceftazidime, which shares a similar side chain; a true ceftazidime allergy needs specific caution.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, rash, injection-site pain or phlebitis, transient liver-enzyme elevation, and blood-count changes; inhaled product can cause cough, wheeze, fever, or nasal symptoms.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, neutropenia or thrombocytopenia, hepatitis, bronchospasm with inhaled use, encephalopathy, and seizures with accumulation.
