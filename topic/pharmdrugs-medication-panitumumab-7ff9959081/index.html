Class

Fully human IgG2 kappa monoclonal antibody against extracellular EGFR

Used To Treat

Defined RAS-wild-type metastatic colorectal-cancer settings as monotherapy or with FOLFOX, and with sotorasib for selected previously treated KRAS G12C-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer. A complete RAS result and the exact regimen matter because most constitutively active RAS tumors bypass isolated EGFR blockade.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: dermatologic toxicity. Skin reactions occur in most patients and can become severe, complicated by infection, abscess, sepsis, or tissue necrosis. Monitor from the first dose through recovery, use proactive skin care and prescribed treatment, withhold or reduce for severe toxicity, and permanently discontinue life-threatening reactions according to current labeling.

Mechanism

Panitumumab binds EGFR with high affinity, prevents EGF and TGF-alpha from activating the receptor, and promotes receptor internalization. Reduced phosphorylation suppresses RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT signaling only when the downstream pathway remains EGFR-dependent. Constitutively active RAS bypasses that control point; in KRAS G12C disease, sotorasib can inhibit mutant KRAS while panitumumab blocks compensatory EGFR feedback. Normal EGFR blockade disrupts follicular keratinocytes and EGF-dependent TRPM6 magnesium transport, linking the mechanism directly to acneiform rash, paronychia, and hypomagnesemia.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify an FDA-approved complete RAS result and distinguish standard RAS-wild-type use from the labeled KRAS G12C plus sotorasib regimen.

  • Start skin protection early: moisturizer, sun protection, gentle cleansing, and prescribed topical or oral anti-inflammatory/antibiotic therapy are meant to prevent barrier breakdown, infection, dose interruption, and avoidable suffering rather than merely improve appearance.

  • Check magnesium and calcium before therapy, periodically during treatment, and for up to 8 weeks after the last dose; monitor potassium as clinically indicated because magnesium depletion makes potassium repletion difficult and increases dysrhythmia risk.

Contraindications

  • Do not use panitumumab as routine therapy for RAS-mutant or unknown-RAS metastatic colorectal cancer; lack of pathway dependence can produce no benefit and worse outcomes in inappropriate combinations.

  • The KRAS G12C plus sotorasib regimen is a specific molecular exception, not permission to treat all RAS-mutant disease.

  • Permanently discontinue confirmed ILD/pneumonitis or a life-threatening dermatologic or hypersensitivity reaction according to labeling.

Interactions

  • Panitumumab plus bevacizumab-containing chemotherapy increased toxicity and mortality and should not be improvised as a broader biologic combination.

  • Oxaliplatin-containing treatment in RAS-mutant disease is harmful or ineffective because EGFR blockade does not suppress the active downstream driver.

  • Magnesium-wasting drugs, nephrotoxins, severe diarrhea, and QT-prolonging therapies can compound electrolyte and rhythm risk.

Key Labs

  • Complete RAS status and KRAS G12C result when applicable

  • Magnesium, calcium, potassium, creatinine, and hydration status before, during, and up to 8 weeks after therapy

  • CBC and liver tests according to regimen

  • Oxygenation and chest imaging for new respiratory symptoms

  • Ophthalmic evaluation for eye pain, photophobia, or vision change

Nclex Traps

  • Fully human does not mean reaction-free or toxicity-free; panitumumab can still cause infusion reactions, severe rash, electrolyte loss, and ILD.

  • The boxed rash is not ordinary acne. It reflects EGFR-dependent follicular injury and can become infected or dose-limiting.

  • Sotorasib plus panitumumab for KRAS G12C is pathway dual blockade; it does not overturn the general rule that upstream EGFR blockade alone fails in active RAS disease.

Population Risks

  • Patients with baseline skin disease, poor wound healing, malnutrition, dehydration, renal vulnerability, or low magnesium can deteriorate faster.

  • Pulmonary disease or prior pneumonitis complicates recognition of ILD.

  • Pregnancy exposure can harm a fetus; use product-specific contraception and follow the labeled interval after the last dose.

Description

Panitumumab is an IV fully human IgG2 antibody that occupies extracellular EGFR, prevents ligand-triggered receptor phosphorylation, and drives receptor internalization so colorectal tumor cells lose upstream growth and survival signaling. It is not a generic antibody for every colorectal cancer: ordinary RAS-mutant disease continues signaling below the blocked receptor, while the labeled sotorasib combination addresses the specific KRAS G12C escape pathway. Severe follicular skin toxicity is boxed, and renal magnesium wasting can continue after the final dose because normal epithelial and distal-nephron EGFR remain inhibited while antibody persists.

Adverse Effects

  • Acneiform dermatitis, xerosis, pruritus, fissures, paronychia, alopecia or abnormal eyelash growth, and secondary infection

  • Hypomagnesemia with possible hypokalemia or hypocalcemia

  • Diarrhea, dehydration, fatigue, abdominal symptoms, stomatitis, and poor intake

  • Infusion reaction, ocular keratitis, and photosensitivity

  • Interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis and embryo-fetal harm
