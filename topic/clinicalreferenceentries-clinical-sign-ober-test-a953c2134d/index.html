Definition

Ober test is a bedside soft-tissue length maneuver. It is a clue to iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness. A positive finding means the thigh remains abducted and does not fall toward the table.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ober test means the thigh remains abducted and does not fall toward the table.

Why It Matters

A positive Ober test response can signal iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness. Short lateral fascial structures limit passive hip adduction. Ober test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness

Assessment Method

In side-lying, stabilize the pelvis, extend and abduct the upper hip, then allow controlled adduction.

Positive Finding

The thigh remains abducted and does not fall toward the table.

Mechanism

Short lateral fascial structures limit passive hip adduction.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ober test response raises concern for iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness because short lateral fascial structures limit passive hip adduction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ober test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ober test to focus evaluation of iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness and the reviewed alternatives: hip arthritis, gluteal contracture, fear. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: In side-lying, stabilize the pelvis, extend and abduct the upper hip, then allow controlled adduction. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ober test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pelvic rotation and examiner support strongly affect result. Therefore, Ober test cannot confirm or exclude iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip arthritis

  • gluteal contracture

  • fear

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hip arthritis, gluteal contracture, fear, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ober test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current flexibility adjunct; not a direct pain diagnosis. When performed as described, Ober test can adjust concern for iliotibial band and tensor fasciae latae tightness, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Ober test: Pelvic rotation and examiner support strongly affect result.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ober test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: In side-lying, stabilize the pelvis, extend and abduct the upper hip, then allow controlled adduction. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not let the leg drop or extend a painful lumbar spine.
