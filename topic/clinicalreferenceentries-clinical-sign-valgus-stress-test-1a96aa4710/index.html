Definition

Valgus stress test is a bedside knee-stability maneuver. It is a clue to medial collateral ligament injury. A positive finding means medial joint opening or pain exceeds the opposite knee.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Valgus stress test means medial joint opening or pain exceeds the opposite knee.

Why It Matters

A positive Valgus stress test response can signal medial collateral ligament injury. The MCL resists valgus angulation; laxity in extension suggests broader capsular injury. Valgus stress test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • medial collateral ligament injury

Assessment Method

Apply controlled valgus force at about 30 degrees and near extension while stabilizing the thigh.

Positive Finding

Medial joint opening or pain exceeds the opposite knee.

Mechanism

The MCL resists valgus angulation; laxity in extension suggests broader capsular injury.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Valgus stress test response raises concern for medial collateral ligament injury because the MCL resists valgus angulation; laxity in extension suggests broader capsular injury. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Valgus stress test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Valgus stress test to focus evaluation of medial collateral ligament injury and the reviewed alternatives: medial compartment arthritis, fracture, physeal injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply controlled valgus force at about 30 degrees and near extension while stabilizing the thigh. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Valgus stress test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain alone is less specific than asymmetric gapping. Therefore, Valgus stress test cannot confirm or exclude medial collateral ligament injury by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • medial compartment arthritis

  • fracture

  • physeal injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from medial compartment arthritis, fracture, physeal injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Valgus stress test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Valgus stress test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply controlled valgus force at about 30 degrees and near extension while stabilizing the thigh. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not apply force before excluding fracture or knee dislocation.
