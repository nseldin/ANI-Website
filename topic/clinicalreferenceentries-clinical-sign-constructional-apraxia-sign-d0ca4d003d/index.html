Definition

Constructional apraxia sign is a bedside visuospatial sign. It is a clue to parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. A positive finding means spatial relationships are distorted despite adequate vision and basic motor power.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Constructional apraxia sign means spatial relationships are distorted despite adequate vision and basic motor power.

Why It Matters

The observed Constructional apraxia sign finding can signal parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction. Parietal visuospatial integration cannot translate a perceived model into an organized motor construction. Constructional apraxia sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to copy a simple intersecting figure or clock appropriate to ability.

Positive Finding

Spatial relationships are distorted despite adequate vision and basic motor power.

Mechanism

Parietal visuospatial integration cannot translate a perceived model into an organized motor construction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Constructional apraxia sign finding raises concern for parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction because parietal visuospatial integration cannot translate a perceived model into an organized motor construction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Constructional apraxia sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Constructional apraxia sign to focus evaluation of parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: visual impairment, motor weakness, low literacy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to copy a simple intersecting figure or clock appropriate to ability. Evidence Limitations

Education, tremor, poor vision, and hand weakness confound performance. Therefore, Constructional apraxia sign cannot confirm or exclude parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • visual impairment

  • low literacy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as visual impairment, motor weakness, low literacy.

False Negative Situations

Constructional apraxia sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of parietal or diffuse cerebral dysfunction.

Apply the reviewed limitation for Constructional apraxia sign: Education, tremor, poor vision, and hand weakness confound performance.
