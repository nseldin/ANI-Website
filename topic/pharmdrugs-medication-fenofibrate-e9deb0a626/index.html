Contraindications

  • Severe renal impairment including dialysis, active liver disease, primary biliary cirrhosis, preexisting gallbladder disease, serious hypersensitivity, and breastfeeding are major label contraindications.

  • Formulation-specific doses and food rules differ. Do not substitute one fenofibrate product solely by matching the milligram number.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check fasting triglycerides when appropriate, renal and liver function, gallbladder history, thyroid and diabetes control, alcohol, pregnancy or lactation, and exact statin before initiation.

  • Teach severe muscle symptoms, dark urine, oliguria, jaundice, rash, and RUQ pain as prompt-review signs. Reassess renal function early and lipids after an adequate trial.

Interactions

  • Statins add muscle risk, although fenofibrate is generally safer than gemfibrozil as a partner. Colchicine and other myotoxic drugs add risk.

  • Warfarin effect can increase; reduce and monitor anticoagulant according to INR response. Cyclosporine and nephrotoxic drugs can worsen renal function.

Key Labs

  • Triglycerides, complete lipid panel, non-HDL-C, creatinine/eGFR before and after initiation, ALT/AST/bilirubin, INR with warfarin, and symptom-triggered CK, potassium, creatinine, and urinalysis.

  • If LDL rises while triglycerides fall, assess non-HDL-C or ApoB and the clinical goal rather than stopping from one isolated value.

Nclex Traps

  • Fenofibrate is preferred over gemfibrozil with a statin because the pharmacokinetic interaction is smaller, not because muscle toxicity is impossible.

  • A creatinine increase may reverse after stopping, but it still requires renal dose review and cannot be ignored.

Used To Treat

Adjunct to diet for severe hypertriglyceridemia and selected primary hypercholesterolemia or mixed dyslipidemia after secondary causes are addressed. The strongest practical role is often lowering triglyceride burden when pancreatitis risk is a concern.

Mechanism

Fenofibrate esterases produce fenofibric acid, which binds PPAR-alpha. The receptor heterodimerizes with RXR and changes transcription: lipoprotein lipase and fatty-acid beta-oxidation rise, ApoC-III falls, hepatic VLDL substrate and secretion decrease, and ApoA-I/A-II expression can increase. Triglyceride-rich particles are hydrolyzed and remnants clear more efficiently. Conversion of large VLDL to LDL can transiently raise LDL-C in severe hypertriglyceridemia even while non-HDL burden improves. Fenofibric acid is glucuronidated and primarily renally eliminated, explaining accumulation in CKD. PPAR-alpha changes biliary cholesterol saturation, explaining gallstone risk. The creatinine rise is often reversible after discontinuation, but it still narrows dosing and requires trend interpretation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Fenofibrate can cause severe liver injury, myopathy or rhabdomyolysis, gallstones, and renal-function changes. Obtain renal and hepatic assessment and use a statin combination only for a defined indication with monitoring.

Class Pathway

  • Hydrolysis to fenofibric acid

  • Nuclear PPAR-alpha activation

  • More LPL and fatty-acid oxidation with less ApoC-III and VLDL

Description

Fenofibrate is a prodrug rapidly hydrolyzed to fenofibric acid, which activates PPAR-alpha and increases clearance of triglyceride-rich chylomicrons and VLDL. It lowers triglycerides more than LDL and is generally preferred over gemfibrozil when a fibrate must be combined with a statin because it causes less inhibition of statin uptake and glucuronidation. Preferred does not mean harmless: myopathy remains possible, especially with CKD, diabetes, hypothyroidism, older age, or high statin exposure. Fenofibrate can raise serum creatinine, promote cholesterol gallstones, and cause liver injury. Formulations differ in bioavailability, food requirements, and strength and are not automatically milligram interchangeable. Dose reduction is required in mild-to-moderate renal impairment, and severe renal dysfunction is a major contraindication.

Adverse Effects

  • Abdominal pain, nausea, headache, aminotransferase elevation, reversible creatinine rise, rash, and back pain can occur.

  • Myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, hepatitis, gallstones, cholecystitis, pancreatitis, severe cutaneous reactions, cytopenias, venous thrombosis, and hypersensitivity are serious concerns.
