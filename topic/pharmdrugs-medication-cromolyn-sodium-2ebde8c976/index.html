Class

Mast-cell stabilizer with route-specific preventive uses

Description

Cromolyn is a preventive mast-cell stabilizer. It reduces release of histamine and other inflammatory mediators when taken consistently, but it does not relax bronchial smooth muscle, reverse anaphylaxis, or rapidly relieve symptoms that are already severe. Oral, inhaled, nasal, and ophthalmic products act mainly at the surface where they are delivered because systemic absorption is low. The route therefore determines the indication and technique.

Mechanism

Cromolyn stabilizes sensitized mast cells and reduces stimulus-triggered degranulation and mediator release. Lower histamine, leukotriene, and related signaling reduces later allergic inflammation. Because it prevents mediator release rather than blocking receptors after release, benefit develops with regular prophylactic use and is weak as rescue therapy. Low systemic absorption limits systemic toxicity but also means an oral concentrate cannot substitute for an inhaled or ophthalmic formulation.

Used To Treat

Route-specific uses include oral symptom control in mastocytosis, inhaled prevention of bronchial asthma symptoms in selected patients, ophthalmic treatment of allergic conjunctivitis, and nasal prevention or treatment of allergic rhinitis. Product availability and approved indications differ by formulation.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. Cromolyn must not replace epinephrine for anaphylaxis or a rapid bronchodilator for acute bronchospasm because mast-cell stabilization is preventive and too slow for immediate airway rescue.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to cromolyn or the formulation.

  • Do not administer oral concentrate by inhalation or injection, and do not substitute ophthalmic, nasal, inhaled, and oral products for one another.

Adverse Effects

  • Inhaled: cough, throat irritation, wheeze, or paradoxical bronchospasm; nasal: sneezing or irritation; ophthalmic: transient burning or stinging; oral: nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea, headache, or rash.

  • Rare severe hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis.

Interactions

  • Clinically important systemic metabolic interactions are uncommon because absorption is low, but device, route, and other inhaled or ophthalmic treatments must be sequenced according to the care plan.

  • Do not reduce corticosteroids or other controller therapy abruptly when cromolyn begins; improvement does not prevent withdrawal or disease rebound.

Nursing Essentials

  • Match the product to the route and indication before teaching technique. Oral ampules are labeled for oral use, not nebulization.

  • Teach regular scheduled use and track symptom frequency, rescue-medication use, peak flow when applicable, eye or nasal findings, and mastocytosis symptoms.

  • Stop inhalation and assess immediately for paradoxical bronchospasm; acute breathing difficulty still requires the rescue plan.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level

  • Pulmonary symptoms and peak-flow trend for inhaled therapy

  • Renal and hepatic context during prolonged oral therapy because excretion uses both routes

Administration

  • Use each formulation exactly as labeled. Oral concentrate is diluted and taken at regular intervals before meals and bedtime; inhaled, nasal, and ophthalmic products require route-specific device technique.

  • Benefit can take days to weeks. Continue rescue medication access and do not use extra cromolyn as an emergency substitute.

Patient Education

  • Cromolyn works best before triggers and with regular use; feeling no immediate effect does not mean it is inactive.

  • Seek emergency help for severe wheeze, throat swelling, faintness, or generalized allergic symptoms and use epinephrine when prescribed.

Nclex Traps

  • Cromolyn prevents mediator release; it does not replace epinephrine for anaphylaxis or a rescue bronchodilator for acute bronchospasm.

  • Oral, inhaled, nasal, and ophthalmic formulations are not interchangeable even though they contain the same active ingredient.

Routes

  • Oral concentrate, inhalation solution, metered nasal spray, and ophthalmic solution; each formulation has a separate indication and technique.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Anaphylaxis or other severe hypersensitivity.

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm after inhalation, which requires immediate discontinuation and rescue assessment.

Toxicity Management

  • Systemic overdose is uncommon because absorption is low. Stop the product, assess airway and route-specific injury, and provide supportive care; severe bronchospasm or anaphylaxis requires the emergency rescue plan rather than additional cromolyn.

Special Populations

  • Age eligibility is formulation-specific; verify the current oral, inhaled, nasal, or ophthalmic label rather than transferring one product's pediatric instructions to another.

  • Pregnancy and lactation data are limited despite low systemic absorption, so use requires indication-specific benefit-risk review.

  • Use additional caution with prolonged oral therapy in substantial renal or hepatic impairment because absorbed drug is cleared through both routes; older adults also require device and adherence assessment.
