Nursing Essentials

  • Assess emetogenic therapy timing, hydration, constipation, headache, ECG/QT risk, electrolytes if high-risk, and correct formulation timing.

  • For patches, teach placement/timing and skin reaction monitoring.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious hypersensitivity, significant QT-risk context, or severe electrolyte abnormality before administration.

Interactions

  • QT-prolonging drugs and serotonergic medicines can increase monitoring needs.

Key Labs

  • Potassium/magnesium and ECG if QT-risk context exists.

Nclex Traps

  • Granisetron is a setron/5-HT3 blocker; it prevents serotonin-triggered emesis but does not treat the vestibular cholinergic pathway of motion sickness well.

Description

Granisetron is a selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonist antiemetic: it blocks serotonin 5-HT3 receptors on vagal afferent nerves in the gut and in central emesis pathways, preventing serotonin released during chemotherapy or tissue stress from activating the vomiting reflex. It is used for prevention of chemotherapy-, radiation-, or postoperative nausea/vomiting. Some formulations support longer coverage such as transdermal chemotherapy-related prevention.
