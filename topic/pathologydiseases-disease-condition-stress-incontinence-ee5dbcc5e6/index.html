Definition

Stress incontinence develops when urethral support or sphincter closure fails when abdominal pressure rises, allowing small-volume leakage without detrusor urgency. The characteristic pattern is urine loss with coughing, laughing, lifting, or exercise, usually with a low postvoid residual and no preceding urgency. Confirm or stage it with history, bladder diary, pelvic examination, cough stress test, and urinalysis; distinguish it from urge, overflow, fistula, and functional incontinence by trigger pattern, urgency, residual volume, and examination. The priority is to teach correctly performed pelvic-floor muscle training because skin irritation can complicate Stress incontinence. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Urine output change, edema, thirst, dry mucosa, dizziness, flank/suprapubic pain, dysuria, confusion, weakness, weight change, or abnormal heart rhythm can reveal renal, fluid, or electrolyte stress.

  • Escalate anuria, severe potassium abnormality, pulmonary edema, seizure, dysrhythmia, shock, or worsening acidosis.

Diagnostics

  • history, bladder diary, pelvic examination, cough stress test, and urinalysis

  • postvoid residual when emptying dysfunction is possible

  • urodynamic testing only when diagnosis is uncertain or complex treatment planning requires it

  • Distinguish Stress incontinence from urge, overflow, fistula, and functional incontinence by trigger pattern, urgency, residual volume, and examination.

Labs

  • creatinine and BUN rise when filtration worsens

  • potassium may rise with kidney failure

  • urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hr in adults is concerning

  • WBC/nitrites/leukocyte esterase support UTI

Treatments

  • teach correctly performed pelvic-floor muscle training

  • address weight, constipation, chronic cough, and activity triggers

  • consider pessary or surgical support when conservative care is insufficient

Nursing Priorities

  • Use a bladder diary to link leakage with cough, laugh, lift, exercise, volume, urgency, childbirth, pelvic surgery, constipation, and medicines because pressure-triggered leakage must be distinguished from urgency, retention, fistula, and functional causes.

  • Assess skin, pelvic-floor contraction, cough, mobility, urinalysis, and postvoid residual when indicated because infection, pelvic support loss, incomplete emptying, and incorrect muscle recruitment can undermine treatment.

  • Teach correctly isolated pelvic-floor contractions with full relaxation and refer for pelvic-floor physical therapy or biofeedback because supervised technique strengthens urethral support while straining the abdomen can worsen leakage.

  • Address chronic cough, smoking, constipation, weight, lifting technique, and timed access to a toilet and monitor pessary or device fit and vaginal tissue because reducing repeated abdominal pressure and friction prevents leaks and skin injury.

  • Escalate for gross hematuria, painful inability to void, fever with urinary symptoms, a new pelvic mass or prolapse with obstruction, continuous leakage after surgery, or new saddle anesthesia because cancer, retention, infection, fistula, or neurologic disease may be present.

Complications

  • skin irritation can complicate Stress incontinence.

  • activity restriction can complicate Stress incontinence.

  • sexual distress can complicate Stress incontinence.

  • falls from rushing can complicate Stress incontinence.

  • urinary retention or voiding dysfunction after a procedure

  • persistent mixed incontinence can complicate Stress incontinence.

Red Flags

  • Gross hematuria, clots, unexplained pelvic pain, or a new pelvic mass

  • Painful inability to urinate, severe distention, or rapidly rising postvoid residual

  • Fever, flank pain, hypotension, or recurrent complicated urinary infection

  • Continuous leakage after pelvic surgery, saddle anesthesia, or new leg weakness

Patient Education

  • Teach that pelvic-floor exercises are practiced with an empty bladder rather than by repeatedly stopping urine flow because interrupted voiding can promote incomplete emptying.

  • Explain that pads protect clothing but do not treat weakened support, so skin care should accompany a structured pelvic-floor and contributing-factor plan.

Nclex Traps

  • Leakage with exertion is a closure problem, not an overactive-bladder contraction

  • Do not prescribe urgency medicines without confirming the symptom pattern
