Class

Oral mutant-selective EGFR tyrosine-kinase inhibitor used with amivantamab

Used To Treat

With amivantamab as first-line therapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with an FDA-approved-test-confirmed EGFR exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R substitution. It is not labeled as stand-alone first-line therapy in the United States.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-alert risks include serious or fatal venous thromboembolism with amivantamab, ILD/pneumonitis, severe dermatologic toxicity, hepatotoxicity, ocular toxicity, and embryo-fetal harm. Use prophylactic anticoagulation for the first 4 months of combination therapy unless contraindicated; vitamin K antagonists are not recommended for this purpose. Withhold for suspected ILD and permanently discontinue confirmed disease.

Mechanism

Lazertinib inhibits intracellular EGFR kinase activity and is more potent against sensitizing exon 19 deletion and L858R mutant EGFR than wild-type receptor, reducing receptor phosphorylation and downstream MAPK and PI3K-AKT growth signaling. Amivantamab adds extracellular EGFR/MET blockade, receptor degradation, and Fc-mediated immune clearance, which can suppress parallel or escape signaling. The precise biological mechanism behind the combination's excess VTE is not fully established; prophylaxis is justified by the frequency and seriousness of observed DVT and PE rather than by a single proven molecular cause.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the EGFR result, amivantamab partner, prophylactic anticoagulant, bleeding risk, and medication interactions before the first dose. Lazertinib is taken once daily with or without food and, on shared treatment days, before amivantamab.

  • Use the current preventive skin regimen rather than waiting for severe injury: prescribed doxycycline or minocycline during the early treatment period, moisturizer, scalp care, hand/foot antiseptic care, and sun protection reduce barrier damage, infection, pain, and treatment interruption.

  • Assess unilateral swelling, limb pain, chest pain, sudden dyspnea, hemoptysis, syncope, tachycardia, and oxygenation at every contact. Prophylaxis lowers risk but does not make those symptoms routine or benign.

Contraindications

  • Do not use as if it were a general oral EGFR drug; verify exon 19 deletion or L858R and the amivantamab combination indication.

  • Confirmed ILD/pneumonitis requires permanent discontinuation; active unexplained respiratory symptoms require immediate withholding and evaluation.

  • If anticoagulation is contraindicated, the prescriber must explicitly reassess combination VTE risk rather than silently omit prophylaxis.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers because they can lower lazertinib exposure and jeopardize tumor control; rifampin is a high-yield example.

  • Lazertinib can increase exposure of sensitive CYP3A4 or BCRP substrates, so review narrow-therapeutic-index medicines and follow current label recommendations.

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, and NSAIDs increase bleeding risk during VTE prophylaxis; this requires surveillance and individualized selection, not automatic cancellation of prophylaxis.

Key Labs

  • FDA-approved EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R result

  • CBC, ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, creatinine, electrolytes, and hydration status

  • Focused VTE and bleeding assessment during anticoagulant prophylaxis

  • Pulse oximetry and chest imaging for respiratory symptoms

  • Ophthalmic evaluation for eye pain, photophobia, or vision change

Nclex Traps

  • Lazertinib is the oral intracellular partner; amivantamab is the EGFR/MET antibody partner.

  • Four months describes the labeled prophylaxis interval, not the maximum treatment duration and not a day after which VTE becomes impossible.

  • Rash prevention is mechanism-based supportive care: protecting EGFR-dependent skin barriers helps prevent infection and preserves dose intensity.

Population Risks

  • Patients with prior VTE, immobility, cancer-associated thrombosis, obesity, central venous access, or inherited thrombophilia may have higher clot risk; active bleeding, severe thrombocytopenia, or high fall risk complicates prophylaxis.

  • Prior ILD or reduced pulmonary reserve makes respiratory toxicity more dangerous.

  • Pregnancy exposure can harm a fetus; follow current contraception and post-treatment guidance for both lazertinib and amivantamab.

Description

Lazertinib is a once-daily oral mutant-selective EGFR kinase inhibitor used with amivantamab for EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R metastatic NSCLC. It blocks the receptor from inside the cell while amivantamab blocks EGFR/MET from the outside and recruits immune effectors, creating complementary pathway suppression. The combination also creates a major early VTE hazard, so labeled anticoagulant prophylaxis is used for the first 4 months; that time point is a prevention protocol based on observed risk, not a guarantee that clot risk disappears on day 121.

Adverse Effects

  • Venous thromboembolism including DVT and PE, especially early in amivantamab combination therapy

  • Acneiform rash, paronychia, xerosis, pruritus, fissures, scalp inflammation, and secondary infection

  • ILD/pneumonitis, cough, dyspnea, fever, or hypoxia

  • Hepatotoxicity and laboratory liver abnormalities

  • Stomatitis, diarrhea, edema, fatigue, musculoskeletal symptoms, and ocular surface toxicity
