Used To Treat

Selected susceptible aerobic gram-negative infections, including Pseudomonas, especially when another beta-lactam cannot be used and the regimen separately addresses any needed gram-positive or anaerobic coverage.

Description

Aztreonam is a beta-lactam, but its single-ring monobactam structure gives it a narrow biological lane. It penetrates aerobic gram-negative bacteria and binds PBP3, disrupting septum formation and cell division. It has essentially no useful activity against gram-positive organisms or anaerobes because target access and affinity do not support those groups. That narrowness can be valuable when a patient has a serious penicillin allergy, because immunologic cross-reactivity with most other beta-lactams is uncommon. The important exception is ceftazidime: aztreonam and ceftazidime share a similar side chain, so a true ceftazidime allergy requires specific caution rather than blanket reassurance.

Mechanism

Aztreonam crosses gram-negative outer-membrane porins and acylates PBP3, a transpeptidase required for septal peptidoglycan synthesis. Cells elongate into filaments and lyse as division fails. Gram-positive bacteria lack the same outer-membrane entry context and have different clinically relevant PBP affinity, while obligate anaerobes are intrinsically outside its reliable spectrum. Many metallo-beta-lactamases hydrolyze other beta-lactams but not aztreonam's ring, although co-produced serine beta-lactamases can still inactivate it; this explains selected specialist combination strategies without making aztreonam universally resistant to beta-lactamases.

Boxed Warning

Aztreonam has no current U.S. boxed warning. Confirm that the infection is caused by a susceptible aerobic gram-negative organism or that companion drugs cover the missing gram-positive and anaerobic lanes. Serious hypersensitivity can still occur. Review a ceftazidime allergy carefully because the shared side chain creates more cross-reactivity concern than a remote nonspecific penicillin rash.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, rash, injection-site discomfort or phlebitis, transient liver-enzyme elevation, and blood-count changes.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, cytopenia, hepatitis, seizures with excessive exposure, and clinical failure from missing gram-positive or anaerobic coverage.

Contraindications

  • Known serious aztreonam hypersensitivity; clarify ceftazidime reactions because side-chain similarity changes the allergy assessment.

  • Do not use as solo therapy when the syndrome requires empiric gram-positive or anaerobic activity unless culture data justify that narrow plan.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the suspected organism, site, culture plan, renal function, allergy phenotype, and companion coverage before the first dose.

  • Reassess when cultures return. Narrow gram-negative coverage is a strength only when it matches the infection; otherwise it is an untreated gap.

Interactions

  • Concomitant nephrotoxic drugs increase the consequence of renal decline and accumulation even though aztreonam is not a classic nephrotoxin.

  • In vitro synergy with aminoglycosides may occur against selected gram-negative organisms, but combination use adds kidney and ear toxicity and must be intentional.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, renal function, CBC and liver tests during prolonged therapy, temperature, WBC trend, and stool assessment for significant diarrhea.

  • Monitor the actual infected site and hemodynamics; a falling fever does not prove every required organism is covered.

Nclex Traps

  • Aztreonam is not a broad substitute for every beta-lactam allergy. It covers susceptible aerobic gram-negative bacteria, not gram-positive organisms or anaerobes.

  • Ceftazidime is the allergy exception to remember because of side-chain similarity, not because all cephalosporins cross-react equally.
