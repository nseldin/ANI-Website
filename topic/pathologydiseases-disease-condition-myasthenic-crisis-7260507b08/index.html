Definition

Myasthenic crisis develops when autoimmune neuromuscular-junction failure becomes severe enough to compromise ventilation or airway protection. The characteristic pattern is rapidly worsening bulbar, neck-flexor, or respiratory weakness with dysphagia, weak cough, pooling secretions, and declining vital capacity. Confirm or stage it with serial forced vital capacity, negative inspiratory force, cough, bulbar function, and blood gas; distinguish it from cholinergic crisis, Guillain-Barre syndrome, botulism, brainstem stroke, and sedative effect by reflexes, pupils, secretions, exposure, and pattern. The priority is to secure the airway before gas values become catastrophic because aspiration can complicate Myasthenic crisis. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Altered level of consciousness, focal weakness, speech change, pupil change, seizure activity, sensory loss, severe headache, gait change, or new swallowing trouble.

  • Sudden change from baseline is a red flag even when vital signs are not dramatic.

Diagnostics

  • serial forced vital capacity, negative inspiratory force, cough, bulbar function, and blood gas

  • infection, medication, aspiration, thyroid, and surgical trigger assessment

  • electrophysiology and antibody history for diagnosis but not as a reason to delay respiratory support

  • Distinguish Myasthenic crisis from cholinergic crisis, Guillain-Barre syndrome, botulism, brainstem stroke, and sedative effect by reflexes, pupils, secretions, exposure, and pattern.

Labs

  • glucose must be checked with altered mental status

  • sodium abnormalities can worsen seizures/confusion

  • infection markers may rise with meningitis/encephalitis

Treatments

  • secure the airway before gas values become catastrophic

  • give plasma exchange or intravenous immunoglobulin

  • treat triggers and avoid medicines that impair neuromuscular transmission

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for inability to count or speak, weak cough, pooling secretions, paradoxical breathing, declining respiratory measurements, hypercapnia, or altered consciousness because urgent intensive care, ventilation, intravenous immunoglobulin, or plasma exchange may be needed.

  • Assess speech length, cough strength, secretion handling, neck flexion, respiratory pattern, forced vital capacity, and negative inspiratory force serially because oxygen saturation may remain normal until neuromuscular ventilation is critically weak.

  • Elevate the head, keep suction and bag-mask support ready, and minimize exhausting activity because bulbar weakness and fatigue increase aspiration and respiratory-arrest risk.

  • Prepare noninvasive support only for a cooperative patient who can protect the airway and prepare early intubation for worsening bulbar or respiratory weakness because delayed airway control can turn a controlled procedure into an arrest.

  • Review infection, surgery, missed therapy, magnesium, sedatives, neuromuscular blockers, and other exacerbating medicines because removing the trigger is essential to recovery.

Complications

  • aspiration can complicate Myasthenic crisis.

  • pneumonia can complicate Myasthenic crisis.

  • hypercapnic arrest can complicate Myasthenic crisis.

  • dysrhythmia can complicate Myasthenic crisis.

  • treatment thrombosis or infection

Red Flags

  • Inability to handle secretions or protect the airway

  • Paradoxical breathing, orthopnea, or rapidly shallow respirations

  • Serial decline in forced vital capacity or inspiratory force

  • Rising carbon dioxide, fatigue, or altered consciousness

Patient Education

  • Explain that myasthenic crisis is breathing-muscle weakness, so a normal early oxygen reading does not rule out danger and worsening speech or cough needs urgent care.

  • Keep an updated medication and caution list, wear medical identification, and seek emergency help for new breathlessness, weak cough, choking, or rapidly worsening swallowing.

Nclex Traps

  • Pulse oximetry can remain normal until late ventilatory failure

  • Increasing pyridostigmine is not the acute rescue for a ventilatory crisis
