Summary

McBurney point tenderness is focal right-lower-quadrant tenderness near the common surface location of the appendix; it supports appendicitis/peritoneal irritation but is not diagnostic alone.

Quick Answer

A **positive McBurney sign** means maximal tenderness near McBurney point in the right lower quadrant (RLQ). It raises concern for appendicitis when paired with migration of pain, anorexia, nausea/vomiting, fever, guarding, rebound, WBC/CRP trend, pregnancy context, and imaging plan.

Sections

    • What to notice

    • Pain is localized near the point roughly between the umbilicus and right anterior superior iliac spine, near the usual base of the appendix.

    • What it means

    • Localized RLQ tenderness suggests the appendix or nearby peritoneum is inflamed. It is supportive evidence, not a stand-alone diagnosis.

    • Nursing action

    • Assess pain migration, fever, GI symptoms, guarding/rebound, pregnancy status, NPO status if ordered, IV access/labs/imaging plan, and escalation for worsening pain, rigidity, hypotension, or sepsis cues.

    • NCLEX trap

    • Appendix location varies. Absence of McBurney tenderness does not rule out appendicitis, especially retrocecal appendix, pregnancy, older adults, or atypical presentations.
