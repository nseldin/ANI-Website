Used To Treat

Ovulation induction, controlled ovarian stimulation for assisted reproduction, and selected male hypogonadotropic infertility protocols. Follitropin alfa supplies recombinant FSH, menotropins supplies FSH and LH bioactivity, and chorionic gonadotropin supplies a long-acting LH-receptor signal.

Description

Gonadotropins are fertility hormones used in sequence: FSH-containing drugs stimulate granulosa cells to recruit and grow follicles, LH activity supports theca-cell androgen production and luteinization, and hCG activates LH/CG receptors to trigger final oocyte maturation and ovulation. They are not interchangeable fertility injections because follicular growth, steroid support, and ovulatory triggering are different biological jobs with different timing. hCG resembles LH but persists longer, which makes it useful as a trigger after follicles reach the intended stage and also explains why it can convert a high ovarian response into clinically important OHSS. Excessively stimulated ovaries may enlarge and release VEGF, making capillaries leaky and shifting fluid into the abdomen or chest while the bloodstream becomes concentrated.

Mechanism

FSH binds a Gs-coupled FSH receptor on granulosa cells, raising cAMP and protein kinase A signaling. This increases aromatase, estradiol production, inhibin, granulosa proliferation, and follicle survival. Menotropins adds LH activity; LH receptor signaling in theca cells raises steroidogenic acute regulatory protein and androgen synthesis, which provides substrate for granulosa-cell aromatase. When an adequate follicle is present, hCG binds the LH/CG receptor and generates a prolonged cAMP signal that resumes oocyte meiosis, breaks down the follicular wall, triggers ovulation, and luteinizes remaining cells. Multiple recruited follicles create multiple oocytes, while excessive luteinization and VEGF production increase vascular permeability, thrombosis risk, ascites, and respiratory compromise.

Boxed Warning

These products require clinicians experienced in infertility and access to ultrasound and hormonal monitoring. Serious risks include OHSS, pulmonary or vascular complications, ovarian torsion, ovarian rupture with hemoperitoneum, ectopic pregnancy, and multifetal gestation. Hold the hCG trigger and contact the fertility team when monitoring shows an excessive ovarian response because hCG intensifies luteinization, VEGF release, and vascular leak. Rapid weight gain, increasing abdominal distention or pain, vomiting, reduced urine output, dyspnea, chest pain, unilateral leg swelling, syncope, or severe pelvic pain requires urgent assessment for OHSS, thrombosis, torsion, rupture, or ectopic pregnancy. OHSS can worsen after injections stop because endogenous hCG from pregnancy continues stimulating luteinized ovaries. Units are based on biological activity and are not casually interchangeable among products or with insulin-style volume markings.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, injection-site reaction, pelvic or abdominal pain, bloating, breast tenderness, and ovarian enlargement

  • OHSS with ascites or pleural effusion, hemoconcentration, renal dysfunction, thromboembolism, torsion, rupture, ectopic pregnancy, and multifetal gestation

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, primary ovarian failure or high FSH indicating absent ovarian response, uncontrolled endocrine disease, sex-hormone-dependent tumors, unexplained bleeding, or ovarian enlargement not explained by PCOS.

  • Do not administer a trigger until the monitoring team confirms the follicular response and safety plan; a missed or extra dose can alter ovulation timing and risk.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify product, International Unit dose, diluent, route, timing, and the exact cycle day or trigger clock. Have the patient repeat the plan back.

  • Teach that OHSS can begin or worsen after the trigger and after a positive pregnancy test; symptoms do not become harmless because injections stopped.

Interactions

  • Other gonadotropins have additive ovarian effects and must be used only within the prescribed stimulation protocol.

  • hCG can interfere with pregnancy testing for a limited interval and can magnify a high response produced by preceding FSH/LH stimulation.

Key Labs

  • Serial transvaginal ultrasound follicle count and size, estradiol trend, baseline pregnancy and endocrine testing, and semen parameters for male treatment

  • Weight, abdominal girth, urine output, hematocrit, electrolytes, creatinine, liver tests, oxygenation, ultrasound for ascites/ovarian size, and thrombosis evaluation when symptomatic

Nclex Traps

  • Follitropin alfa is recombinant FSH, not andexanet alfa and not the hCG ovulation trigger.

  • OHSS is a vascular-permeability syndrome, so a patient can have third-spaced fluid, ascites, and edema while the effective circulating volume and renal perfusion fall.
