Class Example Names

  • Azacitidine

  • Decitabine

Used To Treat

Myelodysplastic syndromes, acute myeloid leukemia, and selected related myeloid neoplasms under formulation- and regimen-specific criteria.

Description

DNA hypomethylating agents are cytidine analogs that enter nucleic acids and covalently trap DNA methyltransferases, lowering abnormal DNA methylation so silenced differentiation and tumor-suppressor programs can re-emerge. At higher incorporation they are directly cytotoxic. Clinical improvement often requires repeated cycles, while early marrow suppression may worsen counts before a response becomes visible.

Mechanism

After intracellular phosphorylation, decitabine incorporates into DNA; azacitidine incorporates mainly into RNA but a metabolized fraction also enters DNA. DNMT binding to incorporated analog forms covalent complexes, depletes active methyltransferase, produces passive hypomethylation during replication, alters gene expression, and can trigger differentiation or apoptosis.

Boxed Warning

High-yield class safety context: no shared boxed warning applies. Severe neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, infection, bleeding, embryo-fetal toxicity, and tumor lysis require surveillance. Oral and injectable azacitidine are not interchangeable and a substitution error can be fatal or ineffective.

Nursing Essentials

  • Set expectations that response may require multiple cycles; do not interpret every early cytopenic nadir as immediate treatment failure.

  • Verify exact molecule, formulation, route, schedule, antiemetic plan, CBC recovery criteria, infection/bleeding precautions, and renal/hepatic context.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential/platelets before and during each cycle, creatinine/electrolytes, liver tests, tumor-lysis labs when indicated, and marrow/response studies per protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Hypomethylating does not mean harmless: DNMT trapping and nucleic-acid incorporation also kill dividing marrow cells.

  • Onureg oral azacitidine and Vidaza/injectable azacitidine have different exposure, indication, dose, and schedule and must never be substituted.
