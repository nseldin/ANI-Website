Definition

Bandl ring is an observed and palpated labor sign. It is a clue to obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction. A positive finding means a visible or palpable ring rises between a thick upper and thinned lower uterine segment.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bandl ring means a visible or palpable ring rises between a thick upper and thinned lower uterine segment.

Why It Matters

The observed Bandl ring finding can signal obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction. Continued upper-segment contraction against obstruction retracts muscle while the lower segment overstretches. Bandl ring cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate the abdomen during labor for an abnormal transverse ridge.

Positive Finding

A visible or palpable ring rises between a thick upper and thinned lower uterine segment.

Mechanism

Continued upper-segment contraction against obstruction retracts muscle while the lower segment overstretches.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bandl ring finding raises concern for obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction because continued upper-segment contraction against obstruction retracts muscle while the lower segment overstretches. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bandl ring does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bandl ring to focus evaluation of obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction and the reviewed alternatives: constriction ring, bladder distention, fetal part. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect and palpate the abdomen during labor for an abnormal transverse ridge. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bandl ring as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is late and often absent; do not wait for it to diagnose obstruction. Therefore, Bandl ring cannot confirm or exclude obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • constriction ring

  • bladder distention

  • fetal part

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as constriction ring, bladder distention, fetal part.

False Negative Situations

Bandl ring may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of obstructed labor with pathologic uterine retraction.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Escalate immediately and stop unsafe labor stimulation when rupture is possible.
