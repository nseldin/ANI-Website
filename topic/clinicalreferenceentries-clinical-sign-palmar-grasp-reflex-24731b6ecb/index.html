Definition

Palmar grasp reflex is an infant primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to normal early corticospinal developmental pattern. A positive finding means the fingers flex and grasp automatically.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Palmar grasp reflex means the fingers flex and grasp automatically.

Why It Matters

A positive Palmar grasp reflex response can signal normal early corticospinal developmental pattern. Palmar tactile input activates a primitive spinal-brainstem grasp circuit before cortical inhibition matures. Palmar grasp reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • normal early corticospinal developmental pattern

Assessment Method

Place a finger across the infant's palm.

Positive Finding

The fingers flex and grasp automatically.

Mechanism

Palmar tactile input activates a primitive spinal-brainstem grasp circuit before cortical inhibition matures.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Palmar grasp reflex response raises concern for normal early corticospinal developmental pattern because palmar tactile input activates a primitive spinal-brainstem grasp circuit before cortical inhibition matures. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Palmar grasp reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Palmar grasp reflex to focus evaluation of normal early corticospinal developmental pattern and the reviewed alternatives: voluntary grasp, contracture, weakness. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place a finger across the infant's palm. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Palmar grasp reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Strength varies with arousal and prematurity. Therefore, Palmar grasp reflex cannot confirm or exclude normal early corticospinal developmental pattern by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • voluntary grasp

  • contracture

  • weakness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from voluntary grasp, contracture, weakness, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Palmar grasp reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Before assessing Palmar grasp reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place a finger across the infant's palm. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use clean fingers and never lift an infant by the grasp.
