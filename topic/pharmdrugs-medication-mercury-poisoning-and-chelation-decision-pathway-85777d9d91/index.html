Used To Treat

Elemental-mercury vapor inhalation, corrosive inorganic-mercury ingestion, or organic-mercury neurotoxicity using species-appropriate testing, environmental control, supportive care, and selected chelation.

Description

Mercury poisoning is an enzyme-disrupting heavy-metal toxicity syndrome in which elemental, inorganic, and organic forms enter through different routes and bind different tissues, so testing and chelation must be species-specific. Elemental mercury vaporizes at room temperature, enters through the lungs and blood-brain barrier, and can cause pneumonitis, pulmonary edema, tremor, irritability, memory change, gingivostomatitis, and kidney injury; inorganic salts damage the gastrointestinal tract and kidneys, while methylmercury enters nervous tissue and the developing fetus. Swallowed elemental mercury is usually absorbed poorly through an intact gastrointestinal tract, although aspiration, prolonged retention, or abnormal anatomy changes risk. Blood and urine answer different questions: urine is more useful for elemental and inorganic body burden, while blood better reflects recent organic exposure; seafood consumption can raise organic-mercury blood levels. Chelation is not automatic for every detectable result. First identify species, route, symptoms, and ongoing source, then involve toxicology and public health. Never vacuum or sweep an elemental spill because breaking droplets apart increases vaporization and spreads contamination.

Mechanism

Mercury binds sulfhydryl and selenol groups, disrupting enzymes, antioxidant defenses, membranes, mitochondria, and microtubules. Elemental vapor crosses alveoli and the blood-brain barrier, where oxidation traps ionic mercury in tissue; inorganic salts concentrate in kidney and directly corrode mucosa; methylmercury forms complexes that cross the placenta and enter the brain. These kinetic differences explain why the same laboratory specimen cannot assess every form. Removing people from a contaminated building and professional remediation stops repeated inhalation, often the largest controllable dose. Succimer or DMPS can bind selected ionic mercury for urinary excretion, but benefit depends on species, timing, organ function, and symptoms. Dimercaprol is generally avoided in organic-mercury poisoning because it can increase brain distribution; chelation cannot reverse established developmental injury and carries its own hepatic, hematologic, and renal risks.

Administration Timing

  • Call 911 for breathing difficulty, severe cough, confusion, seizure, corrosive ingestion, shock, or a high-level heated-vapor exposure. Leave the contaminated area, prevent re-entry, remove contaminated clothing, irrigate eyes or skin, and call Poison Help.

  • For a spill, isolate the area, ventilate to the outside when authorities advise, and contact local health or environmental agencies for mercury-vapor assessment and professional cleanup. Never use a household vacuum, broom, washer, dryer, or drain because these spread and volatilize mercury.

  • Obtain species- and route-appropriate blood or urine testing and organ studies. Use succimer, DMPS, or another chelator only under medical-toxicology guidance after weighing symptoms, concentration, source control, kidney function, pregnancy, and mercury form; provide respiratory, renal, neurologic, and corrosive-injury support in parallel.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask whether mercury was elemental liquid, heated vapor, an inorganic salt, a skin-lightening or traditional product, occupational material, dental source, contaminated seafood, or unknown; document spill amount, vacuuming or heating, room, duration, residents, children, and pregnancy.

  • Trend respiratory status, oxygen need, chest findings, mental status, tremor, behavior, memory, coordination, sensation, oral inflammation, blood pressure, urine output, skin, and GI injury because different mercury forms injure the lungs, nervous system, kidneys, mucosa, and skin on different timelines. Assess all household or workplace contacts when a shared vapor source exists because the same contaminated air can continue dosing everyone who enters the space.

  • Coordinate specimens with a laboratory or poison center that understands mercury species and units. Verify chelator, measured weight, CBC, liver and kidney monitoring, and follow-up; document source remediation before declaring treatment successful.

Key Labs

  • Use whole-blood mercury primarily for recent organic or substantial acute exposure and 24-hour or corrected urine mercury for elemental or inorganic exposure because mercury species distribute and clear through different compartments. Obtain CBC, urinalysis, protein, creatinine, electrolytes, liver studies, and chest imaging or blood gas for vapor lung injury because these tests detect kidney, hematologic, hepatic, or pulmonary consequences that a mercury concentration alone cannot measure.

  • Hair analysis may help reconstruct methylmercury exposure in specialized public-health settings but is vulnerable to contamination and is not a stand-alone chelation trigger. A total mercury value without species, specimen, units, timing, and seafood history is incomplete.

Adverse Effects

  • Elemental vapor can cause chemical pneumonitis, pulmonary edema, erethism, tremor, cognitive and motor injury, gingivostomatitis, acrodynia, hypertension, kidney injury, and death. Inorganic salts add corrosive GI shock; organic mercury causes sensory, visual, auditory, coordination, and fetal neurodevelopmental injury.

  • Chelators can cause rash, GI symptoms, neutropenia, liver-enzyme elevation, mineral loss, or redistribution. Cleanup errors can convert a small localized spill into prolonged whole-building exposure.

Contraindications

  • Never vacuum, sweep, heat, wash, or pour elemental mercury down a drain. Do not allow untrained family or staff to clean a substantial spill or contaminated porous material.

  • Do not chelate from a nonspecific detectable level alone, and do not use dimercaprol reflexively for organic mercury because redistribution toward the brain may worsen neurologic burden. Artist, industrial, and home remedies are not medical chelators.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Respiratory distress, pulmonary edema, corrosive shock, severe neurologic findings, kidney failure, high ongoing vapor levels, exposed children or pregnancy, or inability to remediate the source requires hospital, toxicology, nephrology or pulmonology, and environmental-public-health escalation.

Interactions

  • Seafood predominantly contributes organic methylmercury and changes blood interpretation; an indoor elemental spill primarily drives vapor inhalation and urine burden. Dental amalgam and occupational sources require context rather than assumption.

  • Other nephrotoxins compound inorganic-mercury kidney injury. Sulfur-containing chelators can also bind essential metals and interact with organ dysfunction, so monitoring is part of treatment.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    Floor-level vapor, hand-to-mouth behavior, developing brain, and nonspecific irritability or regression make household spills especially dangerous and easily missed.

  • Population

    Pregnancy and fetus

    Note

    Methylmercury crosses the placenta and elemental vapor also threatens development; source removal and specialist testing must occur promptly.

  • Population

    Workers

    Note

    Mining, recycling, lamp, laboratory, chlor-alkali, gold extraction, and cultural-product exposures require occupational hygiene and take-home contamination control.

Evidence Limitations

  • No single concentration predicts symptoms across mercury species, and chelation outcome evidence is limited. CDC and NIOSH strongly support exposure control; poison-center, environmental-health, and medical-toxicology expertise is needed for testing and chelation thresholds.

Nclex Traps

  • Elemental mercury vapor is dangerous to inhale even though swallowed elemental mercury is often poorly absorbed.

  • A household vacuum increases mercury vapor and spreads droplets through the building.

  • Blood and urine mercury tests answer different questions, so species and route must be identified first.

Regulatory Status

No universal mercury antidote or single chelation threshold exists. Succimer and other chelators have product- or access-specific status; mercury chelation is off-label or specialist directed, and environmental remediation is indispensable.

Boxed Warning

MERCURY WARNING: call 911 for breathing difficulty, severe neurologic symptoms, corrosive ingestion, or heated-vapor exposure. Leave the source and call Poison Help; never vacuum or sweep elemental mercury. Identify mercury species and route before choosing blood versus urine testing or toxicology-guided chelation.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning identifies the emergency, explains the mechanism of harm, and separates immediate stabilization from specialist antidote or elimination decisions.
