Class

Oral targeted synthetic DMARD with preferential JAK1 inhibition at pharmacologic exposure

Used To Treat

Label-defined patients with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, atopic dermatitis, ulcerative colitis, Crohn disease, ankylosing spondylitis, nonradiographic axial spondyloarthritis, and selected other immune indications after inadequate response or intolerance to specified prior therapy. Extended-release tablets and oral solution have age-, indication-, and dose-specific roles and are not interchangeable by simple volume conversion.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Serious infections including TB and opportunistic infection; increased mortality; malignancy including lymphoma and lung cancer; major adverse cardiovascular events; and thrombosis including DVT, PE, and arterial thrombosis can occur. Risks are especially important in older patients, current or former smokers, and patients with cardiovascular, cancer, or clot risk. Use after inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF blockers where labeling requires it, interrupt for serious infection, and discontinue for thrombosis, MI, or stroke. Screening does not eliminate risk, so symptom surveillance continues throughout treatment.

Mechanism

Cytokine binding activates receptor-associated JAK pairs, which phosphorylate STAT proteins that dimerize and regulate immune genes. Upadacitinib reversibly occupies JAK ATP-binding sites and at therapeutic exposure preferentially suppresses JAK1-dependent STAT phosphorylation. This reduces interferon antiviral/inflammatory signals, IL-6-family acute-phase and synovial programs, and common-gamma-chain cytokine effects when JAK1 pairs with JAK3. It does not neutralize one extracellular cytokine, so one tablet influences several pathways. CYP3A4 is the major metabolic route; strong inhibitors increase exposure and strong inducers reduce it. The extended-release tablet must be swallowed whole because crushing defeats its release design, while the oral solution has its own pediatric dosing rules.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain TB and hepatitis assessment, CBC, liver and renal tests, lipids, pregnancy context, and vaccines before treatment; repeat CBC/liver monitoring and lipids at about 12 weeks according to labeling and indication.

  • Verify formulation, induction versus maintenance dose, and indication. Teach urgent reporting of infection, shingles, chest pain, dyspnea, unilateral swelling, focal neurologic change, severe abdominal pain, or sudden visual symptoms.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to upadacitinib; active serious infection requires avoidance or interruption, and severe hepatic impairment is not recommended.

  • Clarify smoking, age, ASCVD/VTE/cancer risk, TB/hepatitis, zoster, diverticulitis, retinal symptoms, cytopenias, renal/hepatic status, pregnancy, vaccines, and CYP3A4 medicines.

Interactions

  • Avoid biologic DMARDs, other JAK inhibitors, and potent immunosuppressants; avoid live vaccines.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can require indication-specific dose limitation; strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampin can reduce effectiveness and should generally be avoided.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and hemoglobin, AST/ALT, creatinine/eGFR, lipids, TB/hepatitis assessment, pregnancy testing when relevant

  • Infection/zoster surveillance, cardiovascular and VTE assessment, skin/cancer review, abdominal and visual symptoms, and objective disease response

Nclex Traps

  • JAK1-preferential is not JAK1-only and does not remove the boxed class risks.

  • The same brand uses different induction and maintenance doses across diseases; never infer a bowel-disease regimen from rheumatoid arthritis or eczema dosing.

Description

Upadacitinib is an oral targeted synthetic DMARD and Janus kinase inhibitor that preferentially inhibits JAK1-dependent STAT signaling, reducing inflammatory cytokine programs across joints, skin, spine, and bowel. JAK1 transmits signals from IL-6-family cytokines, type I and II interferons, and receptors paired with JAK3 or TYK2, so preferential JAK1 inhibition can reduce synovitis, enthesitis, axial inflammation, eczema, and bowel inflammation with less direct JAK2 inhibition than a nonselective drug. Preferential does not mean exclusive: exposure, tissue, and receptor pairing determine the real effect, and the medicine still carries the JAK-class risks of infection, malignancy, MACE, death, and thrombosis. The many indications also create a dosing hazard because induction, maintenance, age, and renal/hepatic restrictions vary substantially.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, acne, nausea, fever, headache, herpes zoster/simplex, folliculitis, cough, abdominal pain, elevated CPK, lipids, and liver enzymes

  • Serious/opportunistic infection, TB, malignancy, MACE, thrombosis, GI perforation, cytopenias, hepatotoxicity, retinal detachment in at-risk settings, and severe hypersensitivity
