Used To Treat

LDL-C reduction in adults and age-approved heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, improved glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, and common off-label bile-acid diarrhea treatment.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with triglycerides above 500 mg/dL, prior hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis or a history of bowel obstruction. Triglycerides above 300 mg/dL can rise substantially. Severe constipation, abdominal pain, dysphagia or vomiting can signal obstruction. Interacting oral drugs commonly need to be taken at least 4 hours before.

Mechanism

The insoluble polymer interrupts enterohepatic bile-acid recycling. Hepatic cholesterol 7-alpha-hydroxylase and LDL-receptor activity increase, clearing LDL from plasma. The mechanism of A1c reduction is not established; proposed bile-acid receptor and nutrient-signaling effects should not be stated as proven. The drug remains in the GI tract and is excreted in stool.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check fasting triglycerides before starting and periodically; do not focus on LDL alone.

  • Take tablets with a meal and liquid; use suspension when swallowing large tablets is unsafe and teach exact separation from other drugs.

Contraindications

  • Triglycerides above 500 mg/dL

  • History of hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis

  • History of bowel obstruction

  • Clarify severe constipation, gastroparesis, major GI surgery and swallowing difficulty

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine, phenytoin, thyroid hormone, warfarin, oral contraceptives, olmesartan and sulfonylureas can have reduced exposure; commonly give at least 4 hours before.

  • Monitor INR when warfarin is started/stopped with colesevelam.

  • Metformin extended-release exposure can increase; monitor glycemic control.

Key Labs

  • Fasting lipid panel with triglycerides

  • A1c/glucose when used in type 2 diabetes

  • INR, TSH/free T4 or drug-specific monitoring when interacting therapy is present

  • Fat-soluble vitamin/coagulation assessment when deficiency risk exists

Nclex Traps

  • A drug can lower LDL and still be contraindicated because triglycerides are dangerously high.

  • Colesevelam improves type 2 diabetes control but does not treat type 1 diabetes or DKA.

  • Oral contraceptives can be bound; administration timing matters.

Description

Colesevelam is a high-capacity, nonabsorbed polymer that binds bile acids in the intestinal lumen. It lowers LDL by forcing hepatic cholesterol conversion and LDL-receptor upregulation, and it modestly improves glycemic control through an incompletely defined bile-acid/metabolic mechanism. It can increase metformin extended-release exposure while reducing absorption of many other oral drugs, and it can raise triglycerides or obstruct the gut.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, dyspepsia, nausea, abdominal pain and headache

  • Hypertriglyceridemia and acute pancreatitis

  • Dysphagia, esophageal obstruction or bowel obstruction

  • Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency and reduced drug absorption
