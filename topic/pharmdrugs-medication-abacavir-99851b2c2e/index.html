Used To Treat

Treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral medications. It is not a broad antiviral for influenza, herpes, hepatitis, or COVID-19.

Mechanism

Abacavir is phosphorylated intracellularly to carbovir triphosphate. Carbovir triphosphate competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate and causes HIV viral DNA chain termination after reverse transcriptase incorporation; hypersensitivity can be life-threatening and is strongly linked to HLA-B*5701.

Boxed Warning

Important warning: serious or life-threatening hypersensitivity can occur. Screen for HLA-B*5701 before use when possible, never restart after a suspected abacavir hypersensitivity reaction, and watch for lactic acidosis/severe hepatomegaly with steatosis.

Contraindications

  • Previous abacavir hypersensitivity reaction

  • HLA-B*5701 positive status

  • Moderate or severe hepatic impairment

  • Serious hypersensitivity to any product component

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm HLA-B*5701 status and allergy history before starting when possible.

  • Teach the hypersensitivity cluster: fever, rash, GI symptoms, malaise/fatigue/aches, and respiratory symptoms. Two or more groups is a stop-and-call-now pattern.

  • Never rechallenge after suspected hypersensitivity because recurrence can be rapid and fatal.

  • Monitor liver function and symptoms of lactic acidosis such as profound fatigue, abdominal pain, dyspnea, tachycardia, or unexplained weakness.

  • Emphasize adherence and combination antiretroviral therapy. Abacavir does not cure HIV.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

Interactions

  • Alcohol can increase abacavir exposure.

  • Methadone levels may decrease in some clients; monitor for withdrawal symptoms if clinically relevant.

  • Screen abacavir for additive sedation, bleeding, QT prolongation, potassium changes, blood-pressure effects, renal/hepatic clearance issues, and duplicate therapy when relevant.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • HLA-B*5701

  • HIV viral load

  • CD4 count

  • LFTs

  • lactate if symptomatic

  • Liver function for azoles, renal function/electrolytes for amphotericin B, infusion reactions, drug interactions, and pregnancy risk.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not restart abacavir after hypersensitivity symptoms resolve.

  • Abacavir is an HIV NRTI, not a general antiviral for influenza/herpes/hepatitis.

  • A rash alone is not the only clue; GI, respiratory, fever, and malaise symptoms matter.

  • Azole antifungals have many CYP interactions. Amphotericin B is associated with kidney injury and electrolyte loss.

Description

Abacavir is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) antiretroviral: it is phosphorylated intracellularly to carbovir triphosphate. It is used for HIV-1 treatment as part of combination antiretroviral therapy when HLA-B*5701 hypersensitivity risk and regimen fit are addressed.
