Class

Topical dicarboxylic-acid anti-inflammatory and keratinization normalizer

Used To Treat

Inflammatory papules and pustules of mild-to-moderate rosacea with 15% gel/foam; 20% cream is used for mild-to-moderate inflammatory acne by product labeling.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Stop for severe or persistent irritation or hypersensitivity. Hypopigmentation can occur and should be monitored in darker skin. Avoid eyes and mucosa. Worsening asthma has been reported; new wheeze or respiratory decline needs assessment rather than attribution to ordinary skin irritation.

Mechanism

Azelaic acid enters epidermal cells and microbes, where it can inhibit oxidoreductive enzymes, reduce neutrophil-generated reactive oxygen species, and normalize keratinization. It also inhibits tyrosinase, explaining both use in hyperpigmentation contexts and the warning to monitor hypopigmentation, especially in darker skin. Because several pathways are plausible and formulation data differ, a single receptor-level mechanism should not be falsely claimed for rosacea.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify formulation and target: 15% gel treats inflammatory rosacea lesions, while acne labeling differs by product.

  • Use a mild cleanser, allow skin to dry, apply a thin layer, and avoid alcohol cleansers, astringents, abrasives and peels.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication for 15% gel; avoid known serious hypersensitivity

  • Avoid ocular, oral or intravaginal exposure

  • Clarify severe asthma worsening, persistent pigment change or marked barrier disruption

Interactions

  • Other topical irritants, peels, abrasive cleansers and alcohol/astringents compound burning.

  • No major CYP interaction is expected from limited topical absorption.

  • Cosmetics may be applied after the gel dries when the label permits.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum monitoring

  • Serial papule/pustule count, erythema context, pigment and irritation

  • Respiratory assessment if asthma worsens

Nclex Traps

  • The mechanism for rosacea is not fully established; do not invent one receptor target.

  • Papulopustular rosacea improvement does not prove isolated erythema will respond.

  • A dicarboxylic acid can alter pigment through tyrosinase inhibition, so color change deserves monitoring.

Description

Azelaic acid is a naturally occurring nine-carbon dicarboxylic-acid anti-inflammatory that reduces reactive-oxygen inflammation, inhibits abnormal keratinocyte behavior, and suppresses C. acnes and Staphylococcus epidermidis in papulopustular rosacea and acne. The exact mechanism in rosacea is not established. Its first clinical distinction is lesion-specific: evidence supports inflammatory papules and pustules, not proven treatment of isolated background erythema.

Adverse Effects

  • Application-site burning, stinging, pain, pruritus, dryness and erythema

  • Hypopigmentation or other color change

  • Contact dermatitis, swelling or urticaria

  • Reported asthma exacerbation
