Definition

Weight gain means measured body weight has increased from a verified baseline. The change may represent fat or lean tissue, pregnancy, retained stool, or accumulated body fluid.

Plain Language

The person's measured weight is higher than before, but the scale alone cannot show what caused the increase.

Why It Matters

Gradual gain may reflect intended nutrition, energy balance, medication, hypothyroidism, or glucocorticoid excess. Rapid gain with swelling, dyspnea, orthopnea, abdominal distention, or low urine output can signal fluid overload from heart, kidney, or liver disease and needs prompt assessment.

Associated Conditions

  • intended tissue gain or increased energy intake, reduced activity, medication effect, hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid excess, pregnancy, fluid retention from cardiac, renal, or hepatic disease, or less commonly another endocrine or hypothalamic disorder

Assessment Method

Verify serial weights under comparable conditions and calculate amount and time course; ask about intent, intake, activity, pregnancy possibility, medications, sleep, endocrine symptoms, edema, abdominal distention, dyspnea, urine output, and functional change.

Positive Finding

Body weight rises from a verified baseline; rapid day-to-day gain with swelling is more consistent with fluid accumulation than new adipose tissue.

Mechanism

Weight increases when stored fat or lean tissue increases, pregnancy adds maternal and fetal tissue, or sodium and water retention expands body fluid; hormonal and medication effects can alter each pathway.

Evidence Limitations

Scale, clothing, time of day, constipation, hydration, edema, pregnancy, and recent fluid or diuretic treatment affect weight; BMI does not reveal tissue distribution or fluid status. Therefore, Weight gain cannot confirm or exclude intended tissue gain or increased energy intake, reduced activity, medication effect, hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid excess, pregnancy, fluid retention from cardiac, renal, or hepatic disease, or less commonly another endocrine or hypothalamic disorder by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • intended nutritional gain

  • pregnancy

  • edema

  • scale or documentation error

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as intended nutritional gain, pregnancy, edema, scale or documentation error.

False Negative Situations

Weight gain may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of intended tissue gain or increased energy intake, reduced activity, medication effect, hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid excess, pregnancy, fluid retention from cardiac, renal, or hepatic disease, or less commonly another endocrine or hypothalamic disorder.

Modern Status

Current measured finding, not a diagnosis or moral judgment; the time course and whether the change is tissue, pregnancy, or retained fluid determine clinical meaning.
