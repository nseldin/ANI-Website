Definition

Bacteroides fragilis is a clinically important bacterium associated with intra-abdominal abscess, secondary peritonitis, pelvic infection, and bloodstream infection. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Bacteroides fragilis is a Gram-negative bacillus with a obligate anaerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

  • Content

    A colonic commensal whose polysaccharide capsule promotes abscess formation after bowel-barrier disruption; beta-lactamase and oxygen tolerance aid persistence. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Bacteroides fragilis is associated with intra-abdominal abscess, secondary peritonitis, pelvic infection, and bloodstream infection. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
