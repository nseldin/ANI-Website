Definition

Lelli lever sign is a bedside ACL maneuver. It is a clue to anterior cruciate ligament rupture. A positive finding means the heel fails to rise from the table.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lelli lever sign means the heel fails to rise from the table.

Why It Matters

A positive Lelli lever sign response can signal anterior cruciate ligament rupture. An intact ACL transmits femoral pressure into anterior tibial rotation that lifts the heel. Lelli lever sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • anterior cruciate ligament rupture

Assessment Method

Place a fist beneath the proximal calf and press down on the distal thigh.

Positive Finding

The heel fails to rise from the table.

Mechanism

An intact ACL transmits femoral pressure into anterior tibial rotation that lifts the heel.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Lelli lever sign response raises concern for anterior cruciate ligament rupture because an intact ACL transmits femoral pressure into anterior tibial rotation that lifts the heel. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lelli lever sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lelli lever sign to focus evaluation of anterior cruciate ligament rupture and the reviewed alternatives: PCL injury, fracture, pain inhibition. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place a fist beneath the proximal calf and press down on the distal thigh. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lelli lever sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Table padding, limb size, and associated injury affect the response. Therefore, Lelli lever sign cannot confirm or exclude anterior cruciate ligament rupture by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • PCL injury

  • fracture

  • pain inhibition

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from PCL injury, fracture, pain inhibition, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Lelli lever sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with evolving evidence. When performed as described, Lelli lever sign can adjust concern for anterior cruciate ligament rupture, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Lelli lever sign: Table padding, limb size, and associated injury affect the response.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Lelli lever sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place a fist beneath the proximal calf and press down on the distal thigh. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use gentle pressure and never lever across a suspected fracture.
