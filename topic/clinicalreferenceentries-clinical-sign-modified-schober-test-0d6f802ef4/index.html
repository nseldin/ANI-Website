Definition

Modified Schober test is a bedside spinal-mobility maneuver. It is a clue to reduced lumbar flexion. A positive finding means the marked span expands less than the method's reference expectation.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Modified Schober test means the marked span expands less than the method's reference expectation.

Why It Matters

A positive Modified Schober test response can signal reduced lumbar flexion. Restricted lumbar movement limits separation of skin landmarks. Modified Schober test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • reduced lumbar flexion

Assessment Method

Mark points above and below the lumbosacral junction using the validated modified method and remeasure in flexion.

Positive Finding

The marked span expands less than the method's reference expectation.

Mechanism

Restricted lumbar movement limits separation of skin landmarks.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Modified Schober test response raises concern for reduced lumbar flexion because restricted lumbar movement limits separation of skin landmarks. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Modified Schober test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Modified Schober test to focus evaluation of reduced lumbar flexion and the reviewed alternatives: mechanical pain, poor effort, hip stiffness. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Mark points above and below the lumbosacral junction using the validated modified method and remeasure in flexion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Modified Schober test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Skin stretch is an indirect proxy and does not isolate inflammation. Therefore, Modified Schober test cannot confirm or exclude reduced lumbar flexion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • mechanical pain

  • poor effort

  • hip stiffness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from mechanical pain, poor effort, hip stiffness, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Modified Schober test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current preferred variant; still technique dependent. Interpret Modified Schober test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Skin stretch is an indirect proxy and does not isolate inflammation.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Modified Schober test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Mark points above and below the lumbosacral junction using the validated modified method and remeasure in flexion. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Document the exact method and measured change rather than only “positive”.
