Class

Deoxycytidine-analog DNA methyltransferase inhibitor and hypomethylating agent

Used To Treat

Adults with myelodysplastic syndromes under labeled IV schedules; oral decitabine/cedazuridine is a separate combination product designed to reproduce IV exposure and must be selected deliberately.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Severe myelosuppression is expected and can be fatal through infection or bleeding; obtain CBC/platelets before each cycle and as needed, delay or reduce according to recovery, and prevent fetal exposure.

Mechanism

After phosphorylation, decitabine is incorporated into replicating DNA. DNA methyltransferase attempts to methylate the analog and becomes covalently trapped, depleting active DNMT and lowering methylation with subsequent cell divisions. At higher incorporation, DNA-DNMT adducts are directly cytotoxic; nondividing cells are relatively insensitive.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend fever, infection, mucosal or other bleeding, fatigue/dyspnea, transfusion needs, and count recovery across cycles; response may require repeated treatment.

  • Do not confuse IV decitabine with oral decitabine/cedazuridine; formulation and schedule determine exposure.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential/platelets before each cycle and at nadir/recovery, CMP/renal/liver context, pregnancy testing when applicable, and marrow/response studies per protocol.

Nclex Traps

  • Decitabine enters DNA directly, whereas azacitidine also has major RNA effects.

  • A fever during profound neutropenia is an emergency even when cytopenia is an expected pharmacologic effect.

Description

Decitabine is a deoxycytidine analog that incorporates directly into DNA and covalently traps DNA methyltransferase, producing hypomethylation, re-expression of differentiation-control genes, and apoptosis in abnormal myeloid cells. Unlike azacitidine it does not primarily enter RNA; profound neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, infection, bleeding, and fetal toxicity dominate monitoring.
