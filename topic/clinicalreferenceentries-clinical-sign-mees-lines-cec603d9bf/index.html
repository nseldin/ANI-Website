Definition

Mees lines is an observed nail sign. It is a clue to temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure. A positive finding means nonblanching white transverse bands span the nail.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mees lines means nonblanching white transverse bands span the nail.

Why It Matters

The observed Mees lines finding can signal temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure. Temporary disruption of nail-plate keratinization creates opaque bands incorporated into the plate. Mees lines cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure

Assessment Method

Inspect multiple nail plates for transverse white bands that move distally with growth.

Positive Finding

Nonblanching white transverse bands span the nail.

Mechanism

Temporary disruption of nail-plate keratinization creates opaque bands incorporated into the plate.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mees lines finding raises concern for temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure because temporary disruption of nail-plate keratinization creates opaque bands incorporated into the plate. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mees lines does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Mees lines to focus evaluation of temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure and the reviewed alternatives: Muehrcke lines, Beau lines, trauma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect multiple nail plates for transverse white bands that move distally with growth. Evidence Limitations

They appear weeks later and many illnesses or chemotherapy cause them. Therefore, Mees lines cannot confirm or exclude temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Muehrcke lines, Beau lines, trauma.

False Negative Situations

Mees lines may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of temporary nail-matrix injury from systemic illness or toxic exposure.
