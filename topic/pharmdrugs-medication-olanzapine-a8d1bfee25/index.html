Used To Treat

Schizophrenia and bipolar I manic/mixed episodes and maintenance; with fluoxetine for bipolar depression or treatment-resistant depression. Short-acting IM treats selected acute agitation, while long-acting pamoate has separate post-injection monitoring requirements.

Mechanism

D2 antagonism reduces mesolimbic psychosis; 5-HT2A antagonism modulates dopamine release and lowers EPS relative to equally sustained pure D2 blockade. H1 and 5-HT2C antagonism promotes appetite, sedation and weight gain; muscarinic blockade causes dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention and cognitive effects; alpha-1 blockade causes orthostasis. Olanzapine is metabolized substantially by CYP1A2 and glucuronidation. Tobacco smoke polycyclic hydrocarbons induce CYP1A2 and lower exposure, so abrupt smoking cessation during hospitalization can raise olanzapine levels even though nicotine replacement itself does not reproduce the induction.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Olanzapine is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Severe hyperglycemia, ketoacidosis/hyperosmolar crisis, dyslipidemia and major weight gain require active monitoring. Do not give short-acting IM olanzapine close to parenteral benzodiazepines because excessive sedation and cardiorespiratory depression can occur.

Contraindications

  • Product hypersensitivity

  • Assess severe metabolic disease, ileus/retention, glaucoma, liver and fall risk

  • Follow formulation-specific restrictions for IM and long-acting pamoate products

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure weight early and frequently; do not wait for visibly dramatic obesity before checking glucose and lipids.

  • Document smoking status and changes because smoke induction, not nicotine, changes CYP1A2 exposure.

Interactions

  • Fluvoxamine and other CYP1A2 inhibitors raise exposure; carbamazepine and smoking lower it.

  • Parenteral benzodiazepines plus IM olanzapine can cause dangerous cardiorespiratory depression.

  • Alcohol, sedatives, anticholinergics and antihypertensives add impairment, ileus and hypotension.

Key Labs

  • Weight/BMI/waist and blood pressure

  • Fasting glucose/A1c and fasting lipids

  • Liver tests, CBC and AIMS/EPS when indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Normal baseline glucose does not remove the need for repeat metabolic monitoring.

  • Nicotine patches do not maintain the CYP1A2 induction caused by cigarette smoke.

  • Short-acting IM olanzapine and long-acting pamoate have different purposes and monitoring.

Description

Olanzapine is a highly effective second-generation antipsychotic whose broad D2, 5-HT2A/2C, H1, muscarinic and alpha-1 antagonism produces strong antipsychotic/antimanic effects but one of the greatest weight, glucose and lipid burdens in the class. H1 and 5-HT2C blockade increase appetite and impair metabolic regulation; insulin resistance and extreme hyperglycemia can occur beyond what weight change alone predicts. Oral, short-acting IM and long-acting pamoate formulations have different safety rules and must not be treated as interchangeable injections.

Adverse Effects

  • Major weight gain, hyperglycemia/diabetes and dyslipidemia

  • Somnolence, increased appetite and orthostasis

  • Anticholinergic constipation/retention and cognitive impairment

  • EPS, tardive dyskinesia, NMS, liver-enzyme elevation and neutropenia
