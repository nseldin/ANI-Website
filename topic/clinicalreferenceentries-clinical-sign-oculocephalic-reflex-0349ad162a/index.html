Definition

Oculocephalic reflex is a brainstem reflex maneuver. It is a clue to pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma. A positive finding means eyes move conjugately opposite the head turn when the reflex is intact; fixed movement with the head is abnormal.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Oculocephalic reflex means eyes move conjugately opposite the head turn when the reflex is intact; fixed movement with the head is abnormal.

Why It Matters

A positive Oculocephalic reflex response can signal pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma. Vestibular input drives compensatory eye movement through the medial longitudinal fasciculus and ocular motor nuclei. Oculocephalic reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma

Assessment Method

After cervical injury is excluded, briskly turn the head side to side while holding the eyelids open.

Positive Finding

Eyes move conjugately opposite the head turn when the reflex is intact; fixed movement with the head is abnormal.

Mechanism

Vestibular input drives compensatory eye movement through the medial longitudinal fasciculus and ocular motor nuclei.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Oculocephalic reflex response raises concern for pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma because vestibular input drives compensatory eye movement through the medial longitudinal fasciculus and ocular motor nuclei. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Oculocephalic reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Oculocephalic reflex to focus evaluation of pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma and the reviewed alternatives: deep sedation, cranial nerve palsy, ear injury. Evidence Limitations

Sedatives, ocular motor palsy, and vestibular injury alter the response. Therefore, Oculocephalic reflex cannot confirm or exclude pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • deep sedation

  • cranial nerve palsy

  • ear injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from deep sedation, cranial nerve palsy, ear injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Oculocephalic reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current coma examination adjunct with major contraindication. When performed as described, Oculocephalic reflex can adjust concern for pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Oculocephalic reflex: Sedatives, ocular motor palsy, and vestibular injury alter the response.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Oculocephalic reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: After cervical injury is excluded, briskly turn the head side to side while holding the eyelids open. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never perform until cervical-spine instability has been excluded.
