Definition

MTP squeeze test is a bedside joint-compression maneuver. It is a clue to metatarsophalangeal synovitis. A positive finding means pain is reproduced across the forefoot.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive MTP squeeze test means pain is reproduced across the forefoot.

Why It Matters

A positive MTP squeeze test response can signal metatarsophalangeal synovitis. Side-to-side compression narrows multiple metatarsophalangeal joint spaces and raises pressure in inflamed synovial capsules, reproducing forefoot pain. MTP squeeze test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • metatarsophalangeal synovitis

Assessment Method

Compress the forefoot across the MTP joints.

Positive Finding

Pain is reproduced across the forefoot.

Mechanism

Side-to-side compression narrows multiple metatarsophalangeal joint spaces and raises pressure in inflamed synovial capsules, reproducing forefoot pain.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive MTP squeeze test response raises concern for metatarsophalangeal synovitis because side-to-side compression narrows multiple metatarsophalangeal joint spaces and raises pressure in inflamed synovial capsules, reproducing forefoot pain. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; MTP squeeze test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use MTP squeeze test to focus evaluation of metatarsophalangeal synovitis and the reviewed alternatives: stress fracture, gout, plantar-plate injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compress the forefoot across the MTP joints. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating MTP squeeze test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Footwear injury and mechanical pain cause false positives. Therefore, MTP squeeze test cannot confirm or exclude metatarsophalangeal synovitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • stress fracture

  • gout

  • plantar-plate injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from stress fracture, gout, plantar-plate injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

MTP squeeze test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; nonspecific. When performed as described, MTP squeeze test can adjust concern for metatarsophalangeal synovitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for MTP squeeze test: Footwear injury and mechanical pain cause false positives.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing MTP squeeze test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Compress the forefoot across the MTP joints. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not squeeze an acutely red, injured, ulcerated, or ischemic foot.
