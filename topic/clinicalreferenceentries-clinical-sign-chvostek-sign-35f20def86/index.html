Definition

Chvostek sign is a bedside neuromuscular maneuver. It is a clue to neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia. A positive finding means ipsilateral facial muscles twitch.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chvostek sign means ipsilateral facial muscles twitch.

Why It Matters

A positive Chvostek sign response can signal neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia. Low extracellular calcium lowers the threshold for peripheral nerve depolarization. Chvostek sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia

Assessment Method

Tap the facial nerve just anterior to the ear while the face is relaxed.

Positive Finding

Ipsilateral facial muscles twitch.

Mechanism

Low extracellular calcium lowers the threshold for peripheral nerve depolarization.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Chvostek sign response raises concern for neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia because low extracellular calcium lowers the threshold for peripheral nerve depolarization. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chvostek sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chvostek sign to focus evaluation of neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia and the reviewed alternatives: normal brisk response, facial nerve irritation, anxiety hyperventilation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Tap the facial nerve just anterior to the ear while the face is relaxed. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chvostek sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Specificity is poor and absence does not exclude dangerous hypocalcemia. Therefore, Chvostek sign cannot confirm or exclude neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal brisk response

  • facial nerve irritation

  • anxiety hyperventilation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal brisk response, facial nerve irritation, anxiety hyperventilation, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Chvostek sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct; commonly positive in healthy people. When performed as described, Chvostek sign can adjust concern for neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Chvostek sign: Specificity is poor and absence does not exclude dangerous hypocalcemia.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Chvostek sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Tap the facial nerve just anterior to the ear while the face is relaxed. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use gentle tapping and confirm symptoms, ECG, ionized calcium, and magnesium.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Chvostek sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Ipsilateral facial muscles twitch. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Tap the facial nerve just anterior to the ear while the face is relaxed. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, neuromuscular irritability from hypocalcemia, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
