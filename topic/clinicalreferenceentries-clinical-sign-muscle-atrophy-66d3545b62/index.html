Definition

Muscle atrophy is a decrease in muscle volume or bulk caused by shrinkage or loss of muscle tissue. It may result from disuse, loss of nerve supply, primary muscle disease, malnutrition, systemic illness, aging, or medication effects.

Plain Language

Muscle atrophy means a muscle has become visibly or measurably smaller because muscle tissue has been lost.

Why It Matters

Muscle atrophy can explain weakness, reduced mobility, poor balance, falls, and loss of independence. Its distribution and associated strength, reflex, sensation, and fasciculation findings help distinguish disuse from nerve, spinal-cord, muscle, or systemic causes; rapid progression with swallowing or breathing weakness requires prompt evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • loss of muscle tissue from disuse, denervation, primary muscle disease, malnutrition, systemic illness, aging, or medication effects

Assessment Method

Inspect exposed paired muscle groups from several angles, palpate bulk, compare circumference at the same landmarks when trending, and assess strength, tone, reflexes, sensation, fasciculations, nutrition, mobility, medication exposure, and time course.

Positive Finding

One muscle or muscle group has visibly or measurably reduced bulk compared with baseline, the opposite side, or expected anatomy.

Mechanism

Muscle fibers shrink or are lost when mechanical loading, motor-nerve input, nutrition, anabolic signaling, or muscle integrity is inadequate.

Evidence Limitations

Normal asymmetry, body habitus, edema, fat replacement, measurement landmarks, and examiner angle affect apparent bulk; preserved size does not exclude weakness and enlarged-looking muscle can be pseudohypertrophy. Therefore, Muscle atrophy cannot confirm or exclude loss of muscle tissue from disuse, denervation, primary muscle disease, malnutrition, systemic illness, aging, or medication effects by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as constitutional asymmetry, edema, pseudohypertrophy, poor measurement technique.

False Negative Situations

Muscle atrophy may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of loss of muscle tissue from disuse, denervation, primary muscle disease, malnutrition, systemic illness, aging, or medication effects.

Current localization and functional clue, not a diagnosis; distribution, speed of change, weakness, reflexes, sensation, and systemic findings help distinguish disuse, neurogenic, myopathic, and catabolic causes.
