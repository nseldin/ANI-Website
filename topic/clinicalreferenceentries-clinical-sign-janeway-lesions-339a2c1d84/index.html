Definition

Janeway lesions is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to infective endocarditis with septic microembolization. A positive finding means small painless erythematous or hemorrhagic macules or nodules are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Janeway lesions means small painless erythematous or hemorrhagic macules or nodules are present.

Why It Matters

The observed Janeway lesions finding can signal infective endocarditis with septic microembolization. Septic microemboli seed dermal vessels and produce microabscess-like lesions. Janeway lesions cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • infective endocarditis with septic microembolization

Assessment Method

Inspect palms and soles.

Positive Finding

Small painless erythematous or hemorrhagic macules or nodules are present.

Mechanism

Septic microemboli seed dermal vessels and produce microabscess-like lesions.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Janeway lesions finding raises concern for infective endocarditis with septic microembolization because septic microemboli seed dermal vessels and produce microabscess-like lesions. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Janeway lesions does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Janeway lesions to focus evaluation of infective endocarditis with septic microembolization and the reviewed alternatives: Osler nodes, meningococcemia, palmar trauma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect palms and soles. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Janeway lesions as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Appearance overlaps with other purpura and lesions may be absent. Therefore, Janeway lesions cannot confirm or exclude infective endocarditis with septic microembolization by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Osler nodes

  • meningococcemia

  • palmar trauma

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Osler nodes, meningococcemia, palmar trauma.

False Negative Situations

Janeway lesions may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of infective endocarditis with septic microembolization.
