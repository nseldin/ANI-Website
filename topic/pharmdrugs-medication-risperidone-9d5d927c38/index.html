Used To Treat

Schizophrenia, acute bipolar I manic/mixed episodes as monotherapy or with lithium/valproate, and irritability associated with autistic disorder; age, dose and formulation indications differ.

Mechanism

Mesolimbic D2 antagonism reduces positive symptoms; 5-HT2A antagonism can increase dopamine release in striatal pathways and initially buffers EPS, but that protection is incomplete at higher D2 occupancy. Tuberoinfundibular D2 blockade removes dopamine's inhibition of lactotrophs, explaining amenorrhea, galactorrhea, gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction and long-term bone concerns. Alpha-1 antagonism produces orthostasis; H1 antagonism contributes sedation and weight gain. CYP2D6 forms paliperidone, while renal elimination of the active moiety explains lower starting doses in severe renal/hepatic impairment and important fluoxetine/paroxetine interactions.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Risperidone is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Monitor cerebrovascular events in vulnerable older adults, EPS, prolactin effects, orthostasis and metabolic change. Long-acting products have distinct loading, oral-overlap and injection schedules and are not interchangeable.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to risperidone or paliperidone

  • Clarify the lower starting dose and titration plan for severe renal or hepatic impairment before giving.

  • Do not select a long-acting formulation without product-specific initiation and established tolerability

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask directly about menstrual change, galactorrhea, sexual function, breast symptoms and growth/puberty because patients may not volunteer prolactin effects.

  • Monitor orthostatic vitals during titration and perform serial AIMS/EPS plus metabolic surveillance.

Interactions

  • Fluoxetine/paroxetine and other CYP2D6 inhibitors raise risperidone; carbamazepine and other inducers lower active moiety.

  • Levodopa/dopamine agonists are opposed; antihypertensives add hypotension.

  • Other QT, sedative or prolactin-raising drugs add toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Weight/BMI, blood pressure, glucose/A1c and lipids

  • Prolactin when symptoms or long-term risk warrant

  • Renal function, AIMS/EPS and ECG/electrolytes based on risk

Nclex Traps

  • Atypical does not mean low prolactin or no EPS.

  • Paliperidone is risperidone's active metabolite, so hypersensitivity crosses the pair.

  • Different risperidone LAIs use different loading/oral-overlap rules.

Description

Risperidone is a second-generation atypical antipsychotic that blocks dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors to reduce psychosis and mania and to treat autism-associated irritability. Compared with many SGAs it has a strong, dose-related tendency to raise prolactin and produce EPS because D2 occupancy becomes substantial as dose rises. CYP2D6 converts it to active 9-hydroxyrisperidone (paliperidone); together they form the active moiety. Renal function therefore matters because active drug/metabolite clearance falls even when liver metabolism is intact.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyperprolactinemia and sexual/reproductive dysfunction

  • Dose-related EPS, akathisia and tardive dyskinesia

  • Weight gain, glucose/lipid changes and sedation

  • Orthostatic hypotension, QT effects, NMS and cerebrovascular events
