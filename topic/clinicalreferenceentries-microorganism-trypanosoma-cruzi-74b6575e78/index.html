Definition

Trypanosoma cruzi is a clinically important protozoan parasite associated with acute Chagas disease, Chagas cardiomyopathy, and Chagas gastrointestinal disease. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Reduce triatomine exposure, screen blood and organ donations and at-risk pregnancies under current policy, and use food hygiene in endemic settings. The reviewed route description is vector borne and congenital bloodborne transmission. Timing differs for vector, oral, transfusion, transplant, and congenital routes, and chronic organ disease may appear decades later.     Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Vector-associated contamination or other transmission routes seed bloodstream infection; intracellular amastigotes persist in tissue and can later injure myocardium and enteric nerves. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Trypanosoma cruzi is associated with acute Chagas disease, Chagas cardiomyopathy, and Chagas gastrointestinal disease.
