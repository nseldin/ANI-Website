Definition

Abdominojugular reflux is a bedside venous-pressure maneuver. It is a clue to limited right-heart preload reserve. A positive finding means jVP rises and remains elevated during pressure rather than briefly adapting.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Abdominojugular reflux means jVP rises and remains elevated during pressure rather than briefly adapting.

Why It Matters

A positive Abdominojugular reflux response can signal limited right-heart preload reserve. Increased venous return cannot be accommodated by a failing right ventricle, so right-atrial pressure stays high. Abdominojugular reflux cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • limited right-heart preload reserve

Assessment Method

Apply firm sustained pressure over the mid-abdomen while observing jugular venous pressure.

Positive Finding

JVP rises and remains elevated during pressure rather than briefly adapting.

Mechanism

Increased venous return cannot be accommodated by a failing right ventricle, so right-atrial pressure stays high.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Abdominojugular reflux response raises concern for limited right-heart preload reserve because increased venous return cannot be accommodated by a failing right ventricle, so right-atrial pressure stays high. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Abdominojugular reflux does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Abdominojugular reflux to focus evaluation of limited right-heart preload reserve and the reviewed alternatives: abdominal guarding, tricuspid regurgitation, superior vena cava obstruction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply firm sustained pressure over the mid-abdomen while observing jugular venous pressure. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Abdominojugular reflux as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity, poor JVP visualization, Valsalva, and brief pressure reduce accuracy. Therefore, Abdominojugular reflux cannot confirm or exclude limited right-heart preload reserve by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • abdominal guarding

  • tricuspid regurgitation

  • superior vena cava obstruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from abdominal guarding, tricuspid regurgitation, superior vena cava obstruction, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Abdominojugular reflux may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct when performed correctly. When performed as described, Abdominojugular reflux can adjust concern for limited right-heart preload reserve, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Abdominojugular reflux: Obesity, poor JVP visualization, Valsalva, and brief pressure reduce accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Abdominojugular reflux, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply firm sustained pressure over the mid-abdomen while observing jugular venous pressure. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Stop if painful and do not press over recent surgery, aneurysm, pregnancy, or tender organs.
