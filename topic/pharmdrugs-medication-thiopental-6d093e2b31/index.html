Class

Ultrashort IV thiobarbiturate GABA-A induction anesthetic

Used To Treat

Historical and selected special-access induction of general anesthesia and specialist reduction of cerebral metabolic demand or refractory seizures; U.S. routine availability is limited, so local formulary status must be verified.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Treat as a potent general anesthetic requiring continuous airway, ventilation and circulatory monitoring with immediate resuscitation capability. Avoid in acute intermittent or other acute porphyrias. Severe extravasation can cause tissue necrosis and accidental intra-arterial injection can cause vasospasm, thrombosis and limb ischemia. Verify current product source and institutional protocol because routine U.S. availability is limited.

Mechanism

Thiopental prolongs GABA-A chloride-channel opening and suppresses excitatory neuronal signaling. Rapid brain entry explains induction speed; redistribution explains early awakening; slow hepatic metabolism and fat storage explain accumulation. Reduced cerebral neuronal activity lowers oxygen consumption and cerebral blood flow. Like other barbiturates, it induces ALA synthase and can precipitate acute porphyria.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify venous patency before injection and stop immediately for severe pain, blanching, absent pulse or suspected extravasation/intra-arterial delivery. Maintain full anesthesia monitoring and separate analgesia.

  • Trend cumulative dose and recovery rather than assuming every bolus will remain ultrashort. In neurocritical use, monitor hemodynamics, EEG goals, infection/nutrition issues and prolonged accumulation.

Contraindications

  • Acute porphyria, barbiturate hypersensitivity, inability to maintain an airway, and situations where severe cardiovascular collapse cannot be tolerated. Clarify shock, severe asthma, major hepatic disease and product availability before use.

Interactions

  • Opioids, benzodiazepines, volatile anesthetics, antihypertensives and other CNS depressants amplify apnea and hypotension. Hepatic enzyme induction can alter other medicines with ongoing exposure.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG, blood pressure, oxygenation and ventilation

  • Neurologic/EEG and intracranial-pressure goals when used in neurocritical care

  • Acid-base, perfusion, renal/hepatic context and limb neurovascular checks after injection injury

Nclex Traps

  • Thiopental does not provide analgesia.

  • Ultrashort after one bolus does not mean short after repeated dosing.

  • Severe injection-site pain or blanching can signal limb-threatening intra-arterial exposure.

Description

Thiopental is a highly lipid-soluble thiobarbiturate that produces unconsciousness within one circulation time. A single dose wears off mainly by redistribution from brain to muscle and fat, but repeated doses saturate peripheral compartments and prolong recovery. It lowers cerebral metabolic rate, blood flow and intracranial pressure, yet can cause profound apnea, hypotension and tissue injury if extravasated or injected intra-arterially. It provides no analgesia.

Adverse Effects

  • Apnea, respiratory depression, laryngospasm/bronchospasm, hypotension, myocardial depression, arrhythmia, prolonged sedation after repeated doses, injection pain, extravasation necrosis, intra-arterial ischemia, nausea and emergence effects.
