Contraindications

  • Bowel obstruction and serious hypersensitivity

  • Use caution with dysphagia, severe motility disorder, severe constipation and major GI surgery

  • Do not take away from food and expect phosphate control

Nursing Essentials

  • Administer with meals and titrate to meal phosphate load and serum phosphorus; assess actual adherence with eating.

  • Ask about dysphagia, severe constipation, abdominal pain and GI bleeding before simply increasing pill burden.

Interactions

  • Separately dose ciprofloxacin, mycophenolate and any oral drug whose reduced bioavailability is clinically important.

  • Can reduce fat-soluble vitamins and folate; monitor/supplement according to dialysis plan.

  • Levothyroxine and narrow-index therapies require spacing/response monitoring.

Key Labs

  • Serum phosphorus, calcium and intact PTH

  • Bicarbonate and chloride

  • Fat-soluble vitamin/folate status and drug levels when clinically relevant

Nclex Traps

  • Sevelamer works in the gut and must be taken with food.

  • It avoids calcium load but is not free of serious GI toxicity.

  • Renvela carbonate and Renagel hydrochloride share the polymer but differ in acid-base effect.

Used To Treat

Control of serum phosphorus in patients with CKD on dialysis under current labeling; selected practice contexts use sevelamer more broadly under specialist guidance.

Mechanism

Cross-linked polyallylamine groups become protonated in the intestinal lumen and capture phosphate through ionic and hydrogen bonding across a broad pH range. Because action requires simultaneous dietary phosphate, doses belong with meals. Sevelamer carbonate contributes carbonate buffering, whereas the hydrochloride salt can worsen acid load; both share the active polymer and GI-binding liabilities.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in bowel obstruction. Serious dysphagia, esophageal tablet retention, bleeding ulcers, colitis, crystal-associated ulceration/necrosis, obstruction and perforation have required hospitalization or surgery. Consider powder/suspension for swallowing disorders and review severe constipation or prior major GI surgery.

Description

Sevelamer is a nonabsorbed polymer whose protonated amines bind dietary phosphate in the GI tract so the complex leaves in stool. It adds neither calcium nor absorbable metal, which avoids calcium loading, and it can bind bile acids and lower LDL; however, the same bulky polymer and nonspecific binding can cause dysphagia, obstruction, ulceration or perforation and reduce absorption of medications and vitamins.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, flatulence and constipation

  • Dysphagia, fecal impaction, ileus, obstruction and GI ulceration/perforation

  • Reduced absorption of selected drugs and fat-soluble vitamins

  • Metabolic-acid-base changes, especially with hydrochloride formulation
