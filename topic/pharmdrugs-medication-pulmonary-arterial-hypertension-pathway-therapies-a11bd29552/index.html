Used To Treat

WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension using risk-guided single or combination therapy that lowers pulmonary vascular resistance, unloads the right ventricle, slows clinical worsening, or modifies vascular remodeling. These drugs are not interchangeable with treatment for every WHO pulmonary-hypertension group because left-heart, lung, thromboembolic, and mixed causes require cause-specific management.

Description

PAH therapy targets four connected abnormalities rather than one generic high blood pressure. Endothelin-receptor antagonists reduce a vasoconstrictor and growth signal; PDE5 inhibitors or the soluble-guanylate-cyclase stimulator riociguat strengthen nitric-oxide/cGMP relaxation; prostacyclin analogs or the IP-receptor agonist selexipag raise cAMP and oppose vasoconstriction, platelet activation, and remodeling; sotatercept traps selected activin-family ligands to rebalance proliferative signaling. Parallel pathways explain why combination therapy can add benefit and why drugs sharing an indication can have completely different emergencies and monitoring.

Mechanism

Endothelin-1 activates ETA and smooth-muscle ETB receptors through Gq-phospholipase-C-IP3-calcium signaling, causing constriction and proliferation. Endothelial nitric oxide activates soluble guanylate cyclase, raises cGMP, and activates protein kinase G; sildenafil and tadalafil slow cGMP breakdown, while riociguat directly stimulates sGC and increases its nitric-oxide sensitivity. Prostacyclin IP receptors couple to Gs, adenylyl cyclase, cAMP, and protein kinase A, lowering smooth-muscle contractility and platelet activation. Sotatercept acts farther upstream on remodeling by trapping selected ActRIIA ligands and reducing excessive Smad2/3 signaling relative to protective BMPRII-Smad1/5/8 signaling. None of these mechanisms instantly reverses fixed right-ventricular injury, so response is followed over time rather than inferred from one dose.

Boxed Warning

Warnings are pathway and product specific. Endothelin antagonists carry embryo-fetal toxicity boxed warnings, and bosentan also has boxed hepatotoxicity with monthly aminotransferases. PDE5 inhibitors must not be combined with nitrates, and riociguat must not be combined with PDE5 inhibitors because excessive cGMP can cause profound hypotension. Continuous epoprostenol and infused treprostinil must not be interrupted because loss of short-acting vasodilator support can cause rebound PAH and death. Sotatercept can raise hemoglobin, lower platelets, and cause serious bleeding, so it requires scheduled blood counts rather than blood-pressure monitoring alone.

Adverse Effects

  • Vasodilator pathways commonly cause headache, flushing, hypotension, dizziness, jaw pain, nausea, and edema, but route adds line infection, infusion-site pain, cough, bronchospasm, or device failure.

  • Endothelin antagonists can cause edema, anemia, liver injury, and reproductive toxicity. Sotatercept can cause epistaxis, telangiectasia, erythrocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and serious bleeding.

Contraindications

  • Confirm WHO group and hemodynamic cause before applying a PAH regimen. A drug proven in WHO Group 1 PAH should not be reflexively used for pulmonary hypertension caused by left-heart disease or hypoxic lung disease.

  • Product-specific contraindications include pregnancy for endothelin antagonists, nitrates with PDE5 inhibitors, PDE5 inhibitors or nitrates with riociguat, strong CYP2C8 inhibition with selexipag, and severe left-ventricular systolic failure or pulmonary edema with epoprostenol.

Interactions

  • Stacking vasodilators can improve parallel pathway control but also magnify hypotension, syncope, and perfusion risk, so combination therapy is deliberate and titrated rather than improvised.

  • Anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs add bleeding risk to prostacyclin-pathway therapy and sotatercept. CYP interactions differ markedly among bosentan, macitentan, treprostinil, and selexipag.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before treatment, verify WHO group, functional class, right-heart status, oxygenation, blood pressure, pregnancy potential, anemia or bleeding risk, liver and renal function, current PAH pathways, and the exact route and device.

  • Trend symptoms, syncope, edema, weight, oxygenation, six-minute-walk or functional capacity, BNP/NT-proBNP, echocardiographic and hemodynamic measures, and clinical worsening. A normal systemic pressure does not prove the right ventricle is safe.

  • Treat a prostacyclin pump failure as an emergency, pulmonary edema after a vasodilator as possible pulmonary veno-occlusive disease, and worsening edema as a differential problem rather than automatically labeling it medication fluid retention.

Key Labs

  • Product-specific pregnancy testing, hemoglobin/hematocrit, platelets, liver aminotransferases and bilirubin, renal function and electrolytes, BNP or NT-proBNP, oxygenation, ECG, echocardiography, six-minute-walk distance, and right-heart-catheterization variables when indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • PAH is WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension; the terms are not interchangeable with every cause of elevated pulmonary pressure.

  • Sildenafil inhibits PDE5, whereas riociguat stimulates sGC. Combining them can dangerously over-amplify cGMP.

  • Sotatercept modifies remodeling over time; it is not an extra rescue vasodilator for sudden dyspnea or hypotension.

  • Never interrupt continuous epoprostenol while troubleshooting the pump or line.

Population Risks

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Endothelin antagonists and riociguat have major embryo-fetal restrictions, and sotatercept may cause fetal harm. Each product has a specific contraception, pregnancy-testing, post-treatment, and lactation plan.

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric use

    Note

    Pediatric evidence and labeling are product specific; bosentan has selected use from age 3 years, while many other pathway drugs retain adult indications or specialist-only pediatric use.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adults

    Note

    Older adults may have mixed pulmonary-hypertension mechanisms, lower systemic pressure, renal or hepatic impairment, anemia, falls, and polypharmacy, so confirm diagnosis and titrate by tolerability and perfusion.
