Class

Topical vitamin D3 analog; nuclear vitamin-D-receptor agonist

Used To Treat

Plaque psoriasis by formulation, commonly as a steroid-sparing agent or in a fixed combination with a topical corticosteroid.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Stop and assess hypercalcemia symptoms such as weakness, nausea, constipation, polyuria, confusion or dysrhythmia. Avoid use in known hypercalcemia or vitamin-D toxicity and do not exceed product-specific weekly dose/BSA limits. Conventional ointment is not for face, eyes or mucosa because irritation is common.

Mechanism

Calcipotriene binds VDR, which heterodimerizes with retinoid X receptor and binds vitamin-D response elements. Gene changes reduce keratinocyte proliferation, promote terminal differentiation and modulate T-cell/dendritic inflammatory signals. Systemically absorbed drug is rapidly converted to less active metabolites, yet large doses can still increase intestinal/bone/kidney calcium effects. Combining a corticosteroid attacks inflammation quickly while calcipotriene supports steroid-sparing normalization.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify formulation, site, BSA and weekly gram limit; apply to plaques rather than normal surrounding skin.

  • Teach that it is a vitamin-D-receptor drug, not a dietary supplement, and reconcile calcium/vitamin D products.

Contraindications

  • Hypercalcemia or evidence of vitamin-D toxicity

  • Known serious hypersensitivity

  • Avoid excessive weekly dose, unapproved facial/mucosal use and clarify severe renal/calcium disorders

Interactions

  • Vitamin D analogs and high-dose calcium can add hypercalcemia risk.

  • Salicylic acid can chemically inactivate calcipotriene in unvalidated mixtures.

  • Use only studied fixed combinations with topical steroids rather than mixing products arbitrarily.

Key Labs

  • Serum calcium and urine calcium if high exposure, renal disease or symptoms warrant

  • Renal function and vitamin D/calcium intake in vulnerable patients

  • Serial plaque thickness, scale, BSA and irritation

Nclex Traps

  • Calcipotriene is a VDR agonist, not a topical steroid.

  • Topical therapy can still produce systemic hypercalcemia when dose/BSA is excessive.

  • Do not compound it casually with salicylic acid because acidity can inactivate the vitamin-D analog.

Description

Calcipotriene is a synthetic vitamin D3 analog that treats plaque psoriasis by activating nuclear vitamin D receptors in keratinocytes. VDR-RXR transcription slows excessive epidermal proliferation and promotes more normal differentiation, directly opposing the rapid, immature scale production of a psoriatic plaque. It is not a corticosteroid and does not cause steroid skin atrophy, but excessive surface area or dose can increase systemic calcium absorption and cause hypercalcemia or hypercalciuria.

Adverse Effects

  • Burning, itching, irritation, dryness, peeling and erythema

  • Contact dermatitis, photosensitivity or facial irritation

  • Hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria with excessive exposure

  • Worsening psoriasis or folliculitis
