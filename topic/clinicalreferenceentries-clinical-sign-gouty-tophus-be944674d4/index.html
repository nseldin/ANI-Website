Definition

Gouty tophus is an observed and palpated deposit sign. It is a clue to chronic monosodium urate deposition. A positive finding means firm irregular nodules may show white chalky material.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gouty tophus means firm irregular nodules may show white chalky material.

Why It Matters

The observed Gouty tophus finding can signal chronic monosodium urate deposition. Persistent urate supersaturation deposits crystals that organize into granulomatous masses. Gouty tophus cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic monosodium urate deposition

Assessment Method

Inspect ears, fingers, elbows, and toes for firm nodules.

Positive Finding

Firm irregular nodules may show white chalky material.

Mechanism

Persistent urate supersaturation deposits crystals that organize into granulomatous masses.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Gouty tophus finding raises concern for chronic monosodium urate deposition because persistent urate supersaturation deposits crystals that organize into granulomatous masses. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gouty tophus does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Gouty tophus to focus evaluation of chronic monosodium urate deposition and the reviewed alternatives: xanthoma, calcinosis cutis, olecranon bursitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect ears, fingers, elbows, and toes for firm nodules. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Gouty tophus as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Tophi do not indicate the current serum urate level and infection can coexist. Therefore, Gouty tophus cannot confirm or exclude chronic monosodium urate deposition by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • xanthoma

  • calcinosis cutis

  • olecranon bursitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as xanthoma, calcinosis cutis, olecranon bursitis.

False Negative Situations

Gouty tophus may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic monosodium urate deposition.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not incise a suspected tophus without excluding infection and planning wound care.
