Sections

  • Content

    Airborne Precautions are transmission-based measures added to Standard Precautions for infectious agents that remain infectious while suspended in air and can be inhaled beyond immediate face-to-face droplet range.

    Id

    scope

    Title

    Scope

  • Content

    Use them for suspected or confirmed conditions listed in current infection-control guidance, including infectious pulmonary or laryngeal tuberculosis. The clinical syndrome can justify immediate precautions before laboratory confirmation because waiting may expose others.

    Id

    indications

    Title

    Indications

  • Content

    An airborne infection-isolation room controls airflow and exhaust, while a fit-tested respirator reduces inhalation of infectious particles. Source control on the patient during necessary transport reduces particles released into shared air. These layers interrupt different points in the same transmission pathway.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Promptly identify the risk, place the patient in an appropriate airborne infection-isolation room when available, keep the door managed under facility policy, require indicated fit-tested respiratory protection before entry, limit transport, apply patient source control during transport, and coordinate diagnostic and public-health steps without delaying emergency stabilization.

    Id

    procedure

    Title

    Procedure

  • Content

    A surgical mask on staff is not a substitute for a required respirator, and a negative early test does not always remove clinical suspicion. Room engineering, respirator programs, pathogen-specific duration, and contingency plans vary by facility and must follow current policy.

    Id

    limitations

    Title

    Limitations

  • Content

    Never leave an unstable patient unattended while arranging isolation. Confirm respirator fit and medical clearance, protect communication and monitoring, explain the reason for precautions, and reassess discontinuation only against disease-specific criteria.
