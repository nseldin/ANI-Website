Class

Class III antiarrhythmic; potassium-channel blocker

Used To Treat

Selected atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter rhythm-control situations.

Boxed Warning

Drug-specific boxed warning: increased risk of death, stroke, and heart failure in clients with decompensated heart failure or permanent atrial fibrillation.

Mechanism

Dronedarone works by blocking cardiac potassium currents so repolarization and the effective refractory period last longer. That can interrupt reentry rhythms, but it also lengthens QT and can provoke torsades when bradycardia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, renal accumulation, or other QT-prolonging drugs are present.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor rhythm, heart failure symptoms, liver injury symptoms, electrolytes, QTc, and interactions.

  • Question use if the client has worsening heart failure or permanent atrial fibrillation.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors, other QT-prolonging drugs, and some rate-slowing medications can create serious risk.

  • Can interact with digoxin and anticoagulant plans; verify orders and monitoring.

Key Labs

  • ECG/QTc

Nclex Traps

  • Dronedarone is not a safer amiodarone substitute for every client; heart failure and permanent atrial fibrillation are danger contexts.
