Definition

Target lesion sign is an observed lesion-morphology sign. It is a clue to erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions. A positive finding means a lesion has a dusky center, paler edematous ring, and outer erythematous ring.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Target lesion sign means a lesion has a dusky center, paler edematous ring, and outer erythematous ring.

Why It Matters

The observed Target lesion sign finding can signal erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions. Spatially varying epidermal injury and inflammation create concentric zones. Target lesion sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions

Assessment Method

Inspect individual lesions for concentric zones.

Positive Finding

A lesion has a dusky center, paler edematous ring, and outer erythematous ring.

Mechanism

Spatially varying epidermal injury and inflammation create concentric zones.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Target lesion sign finding raises concern for erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions because spatially varying epidermal injury and inflammation create concentric zones. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Target lesion sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Target lesion sign to focus evaluation of erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions and the reviewed alternatives: Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Lyme erythema migrans, vasculitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect individual lesions for concentric zones. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Target lesion sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Two-zone atypical lesions and transient wheals are often mislabeled. Therefore, Target lesion sign cannot confirm or exclude erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Stevens-Johnson syndrome

  • Lyme erythema migrans

  • vasculitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Lyme erythema migrans, vasculitis.

False Negative Situations

Target lesion sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of erythema multiforme and other targetoid eruptions.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Assess mucosa, pain, blistering, medications, and systemic illness rather than relying on the “bull's-eye” word.
