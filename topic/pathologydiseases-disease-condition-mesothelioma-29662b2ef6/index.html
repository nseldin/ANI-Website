Definition

Mesothelioma develops when malignant mesothelial cells, most often after remote asbestos exposure, diffusely encase pleural surfaces and restrict lung expansion. The characteristic pattern is progressive unilateral chest pain, dyspnea, recurrent pleural effusion, weight loss, and pleural thickening decades after exposure. Confirm or stage it with contrast chest CT to define pleural disease; distinguish it from metastatic adenocarcinoma, benign asbestos pleural disease, empyema, and other pleural tumors by tissue morphology and immunophenotype. The priority is to use multidisciplinary systemic therapy according to histology and stage because progressive restrictive respiratory failure can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, accessory-muscle use, abnormal breath sounds, cough/sputum, chest pain, hypoxemia, cyanosis, fatigue, or confusion.

  • Silent chest, stridor, rising CO2 signs, or exhaustion is more dangerous than a noisy but moving airway.

Diagnostics

  • contrast chest CT to define pleural disease

  • thoracoscopic or image-guided tissue biopsy with immunohistochemistry because fluid cytology alone is often insufficient

  • staging with nodal and distant-disease assessment

  • Distinguish Mesothelioma from metastatic adenocarcinoma, benign asbestos pleural disease, empyema, and other pleural tumors by tissue morphology and immunophenotype.

Labs

  • PaO2 or SpO2 may fall with gas-exchange failure

  • PaCO2 may rise with ventilatory failure

  • WBC may rise with infection

  • lactate may rise with severe hypoxia or sepsis

Treatments

  • use multidisciplinary systemic therapy according to histology and stage

  • consider surgery only for carefully selected resectable disease in expert centers

  • drain symptomatic effusions and provide early pain, respiratory, and palliative support

Nursing Priorities

  • Assess dyspnea, pleuritic or chest-wall pain, cough, fatigue, weight loss, performance status, occupational asbestos exposure, abdominal symptoms, and goals because pleural and peritoneal disease create different respiratory, nutrition, and symptom burdens.

  • Trend oxygen saturation, respiratory rate, breath sounds, chest expansion, pain, weight, edema, abdominal girth, blood counts, kidney and liver function, and imaging response because effusion, trapped lung, progression, and treatment toxicity can change quickly.

  • Position for ventilation, provide prescribed oxygen and multimodal analgesia, pace activity, and coordinate pulmonary rehabilitation or nutrition support because breathlessness and invasive chest-wall pain otherwise accelerate deconditioning and poor intake.

  • Manage pleural drain or postoperative site with sterile technique and measure output, air leak, respiratory response, fever, and subcutaneous emphysema because drainage may relieve dyspnea but can cause infection, obstruction, bleeding, or pneumothorax.

  • Escalate immediately for acute respiratory distress, rapidly increasing oxygen need, unilateral absent breath sounds, hypotension, hemoptysis, fever with drain-site infection, unilateral leg swelling, or immune-therapy organ symptoms because tension physiology, sepsis, pulmonary embolism, or treatment toxicity may be present.

Complications

  • progressive restrictive respiratory failure

  • trapped lung and recurrent effusion

  • chest-wall invasion can complicate Mesothelioma.

  • thrombosis can complicate Mesothelioma.

  • treatment toxicity can complicate Mesothelioma.

Red Flags

  • Acute respiratory distress, rapidly rising oxygen need, or unilateral absent breath sounds

  • Hypotension, syncope, massive hemoptysis, or rapidly increasing bloody drain output

  • Fever, purulent drain output, spreading chest-wall erythema, or sepsis findings

  • Unilateral leg swelling, pleuritic pain, sudden dyspnea, or new immunotherapy toxicity

Patient Education

  • Teach the patient to report a meaningful change in breathlessness, drain output, pain, fever, or leg swelling early because complications can progress before the next oncology visit.

  • Explain that palliative symptom care can be provided alongside cancer-directed treatment because controlling pain, breathlessness, sleep, and nutrition supports function throughout therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • A remote asbestos history matters because latency is often measured in decades

  • Do not diagnose mesothelioma from pleural fluid alone when tissue can be obtained
