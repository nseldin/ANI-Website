Class

Short-term ophthalmic alpha-2 adrenergic agonist aqueous suppressant

Used To Treat

Prevention/control of acute intraocular-pressure elevation around anterior-segment laser procedures and short-term adjunctive pressure lowering; allergy and tachyphylaxis limit chronic use.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Use is generally short term because tachyphylaxis and allergic conjunctivitis are common. Monitor cardiovascular status and CNS depression, and verify contraindications/interactions with monoamine oxidase inhibitors or tricyclic antidepressants under the exact label.

Mechanism

Predominant alpha-2 activation reduces ciliary-body norepinephrine/cAMP signaling and aqueous secretion; some alpha-1 activity and outflow changes contribute. Receptor desensitization with repeated exposure explains loss of effect. Systemic absorption can still reduce sympathetic tone and cause dry mouth, fatigue, bradycardia or blood-pressure change.

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure IOP before and after the laser/procedure at the ordered interval. For ongoing use, reassess both allergy and waning efficacy rather than escalating indefinitely.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to apraclonidine/clonidine components and label-specific use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors. Clarify severe cardiovascular disease, vascular insufficiency and depression.

Interactions

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclics, antihypertensives, cardiac glycosides and CNS depressants can alter blood pressure, heart rate or sedation. Other alpha agonists add systemic effects.

Key Labs

  • Pre/post-procedure intraocular pressure

  • Pulse, blood pressure, alertness and ocular allergy

  • Treatment duration and evidence of tachyphylaxis

Nclex Traps

  • Apraclonidine is mainly a short-term/laser-related pressure drug, not the same long-term role as brimonidine.

  • A fading IOP response can be tachyphylaxis.

  • Red itchy lids may be drug allergy.

Description

Apraclonidine is a clonidine-related ophthalmic alpha agonist designed for local pressure lowering with less blood-brain penetration than clonidine. It rapidly reduces aqueous production and may improve outflow, making it useful around laser procedures or as a short bridge. Chronic benefit fades through tachyphylaxis, and allergic blepharoconjunctivitis is common enough that it is not a routine long-term glaucoma anchor.

Adverse Effects

  • Allergic blepharoconjunctivitis, hyperemia, eyelid retraction, dry mouth/nose, fatigue, headache, altered taste, bradycardia, hypotension or hypertension and loss of pressure response over time.
