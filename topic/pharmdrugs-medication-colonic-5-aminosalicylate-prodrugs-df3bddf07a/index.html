Class Example Names

  • Balsalazide

  • Olsalazine

Used To Treat

Ulcerative-colitis induction or maintenance according to the individual product; balsalazide treats mildly to moderately active disease, whereas olsalazine is labeled for maintenance of remission in adults intolerant of sulfasalazine.

Description

Balsalazide and olsalazine are colon-targeted 5-aminosalicylate prodrugs. Their azo bonds resist upper-GI digestion and are cleaved by colonic bacterial azoreductases, releasing mesalamine directly where ulcerative-colitis inflammation is concentrated. They share mesalamine renal, hypersensitivity, myocarditis/pericarditis, severe skin-reaction, intolerance-syndrome, and nephrolithiasis risks, but they are not indication-equivalent: one is an active-disease drug and the other is a remission-maintenance drug.

Mechanism

Released mesalamine acts mainly on colonic mucosa rather than through a high systemic concentration. It reduces inflammatory eicosanoid and cytokine signaling, interferes with cyclooxygenase/lipoxygenase and NF-kappa-B-related pathways, and scavenges reactive species. The azo carrier changes delivery, not the active anti-inflammatory moiety. Olsalazine can itself promote intestinal water/electrolyte secretion, explaining its prominent diarrhea signal.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Assess renal function before treatment and periodically. New cramping, abdominal pain, bloody diarrhea, fever, headache, or rash can be mesalamine acute-intolerance syndrome rather than ordinary colitis progression. Chest pain or dyspnea can signal myocarditis/pericarditis; rash with mucosal injury can signal a severe cutaneous reaction.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, headache and rash

  • Interstitial nephritis or renal decline, nephrolithiasis, pancreatitis, hepatitis/hepatic failure, myocarditis/pericarditis and severe cutaneous reactions

  • Mesalamine acute-intolerance syndrome that can mimic an ulcerative-colitis flare

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to salicylates, aminosalicylates, the carrier molecule, or formulation components

  • Clarify significant renal disease, severe hepatic impairment, obstructive upper-GI disease, active severe skin reaction, or prior mesalamine intolerance

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify whether the goal is induction of active disease or maintenance of remission; do not substitute cards solely because both release mesalamine.

  • Trend stool frequency, blood, urgency, abdominal pain, hydration, weight, inflammatory activity and extraintestinal symptoms while separately screening for drug intolerance.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic drugs can increase renal risk.

  • Azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine can add bone-marrow toxicity.

  • Mesalamine metabolites can cause a falsely elevated urinary normetanephrine result with some assays.

Key Labs

  • Serum creatinine/eGFR and urinalysis before and during treatment

  • CBC and liver tests when clinically indicated

  • Disease activity, hydration and stool pattern; use a selective normetanephrine assay if that test is needed

Nclex Traps

  • A worsening bloody-diarrhea syndrome after starting therapy can be drug intolerance, not automatic treatment failure from uncontrolled disease.

  • Olsalazine is labeled for maintenance, while balsalazide is labeled for active mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis.

  • Colon-targeted delivery does not eliminate kidney or hypersensitivity toxicity.
