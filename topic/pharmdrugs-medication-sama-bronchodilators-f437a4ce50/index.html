Description

Short-acting muscarinic antagonist bronchodilators, such as ipratropium, rapidly block airway M3 signaling to reduce vagal bronchoconstriction for acute symptom relief or exacerbation support. They are bronchodilator partners, not inhaled steroids, and they require inhaler/nebulizer technique plus anticholinergic safety screening.

Used To Treat

COPD bronchospasm, acute exacerbation bronchodilator combinations, and selected asthma exacerbation protocols when ordered.

Mechanism

Short-acting inhaled muscarinic blockade relaxes bronchial smooth muscle for several hours by preventing acetylcholine from constricting airways.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess anticholinergic burden, urinary retention/BPH, glaucoma cautions, dry mouth, and nebulizer mask eye exposure.

  • Differentiate it from beta agonists: SAMA blocks acetylcholine; SABA stimulates beta-2 receptors.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not call ipratropium a steroid or beta agonist. It is an antimuscarinic bronchodilator.
