Used To Treat

Escalation when maximally tolerated statin therapy does not produce the needed LDL reduction, when statin exposure is limited by intolerance or interactions, or when familial hypercholesterolemia requires multiple pathways.

Description

Nonstatin therapy is chosen by the missing mechanism, needed LDL reduction, outcome evidence, route, cost, adherence pattern, and patient risk. Ezetimibe is an oral NPC1L1 inhibitor that usually adds modest LDL lowering and has cardiovascular outcomes evidence with statin therapy. Bempedoic acid is an oral ATP-citrate-lyase inhibitor activated mainly in liver, reducing LDL with less direct skeletal-muscle activation but adding hyperuricemia, gout, tendon, and statin-dose interactions. Evolocumab and alirocumab are injected antibodies that neutralize circulating PCSK9, preserve LDL-receptor recycling, produce large LDL reductions, and have outcomes evidence. Inclisiran is clinician-administered siRNA that reduces hepatic PCSK9 synthesis with twice-yearly maintenance dosing; durable LDL lowering is established while hard-outcomes evidence is still being completed. Bile-acid sequestrants are nonabsorbed but constipating, interaction prone, and able to raise triglycerides. Lomitapide and evinacumab are specialist HoFH therapies with LDL-receptor-independent activity, so they can lower LDL despite severely limited receptor function, but that benefit carries substantial monitoring and access burdens.

Mechanism

Combining independent pathways creates additive receptor pressure: statins and bempedoic acid reduce hepatic synthesis; ezetimibe reduces incoming cholesterol; each lowers hepatocyte cholesterol and promotes LDL-receptor expression. PCSK9 blockade prevents destruction of those receptors. This synergy explains why low doses of complementary drugs may achieve more LDL lowering than pushing a poorly tolerated statin alone. In receptor-negative HoFH, receptor-dependent strategies have limited effect; lomitapide blocks microsomal triglyceride transfer protein and therefore VLDL assembly, while evinacumab blocks ANGPTL3 and accelerates lipoprotein lipase or endothelial lipase pathways without requiring LDL receptors. The benefit-risk question is not which drug is newest, but which pathway addresses the patient's residual particle burden with proven clinical value and feasible adherence.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Warnings are agent specific. Lomitapide carries a hepatotoxicity boxed warning and REMS; bempedoic acid can cause gout and tendon rupture; injectable therapies can cause hypersensitivity; bile-acid sequestrants can obstruct the GI tract and worsen severe hypertriglyceridemia.

Adverse Effects

  • Oral agents add GI, hepatic, uric-acid, tendon, absorption, or interaction burden depending on target. Injectables add local reaction, access, cold-chain, and rare hypersensitivity concerns.

  • Inadequate LDL response can reflect missed doses, injection technique, access gaps, receptor biology, secondary disease, or wrong diagnosis rather than simple drug failure.

Contraindications

  • Do not add a drug without confirming the target and expected incremental reduction. A patient 5 percent above goal and a receptor-negative HoFH patient require different strategies.

  • Avoid bile-acid sequestrants in severe hypertriglyceridemia; use lomitapide only for diagnosed HoFH under REMS; review pregnancy and lactation for every systemic agent.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify why the nonstatin is being added, expected percentage reduction, route and schedule, authorization and access, adverse-effect plan, and follow-up lipid date.

  • Teach that a twice-yearly maintenance injection does not mean the disease is cured. Missed clinician visits can create long gaps, and lifestyle plus other therapies continue.

Interactions

  • Ezetimibe must be separated from bile-acid sequestrants; bempedoic acid limits simvastatin and pravastatin doses; bile-acid sequestrants bind many oral drugs; lomitapide has major CYP3A interactions.

  • PCSK9 antibodies and inclisiran have few classic CYP interactions, but that does not remove injection, allergy, pregnancy, access, or adherence considerations.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel at a drug-appropriate response interval; uric acid and symptoms for bempedoic acid; liver testing for lomitapide and selected combinations; triglycerides before bile-acid sequestrants; pregnancy and specialist testing when relevant.

  • Measure response against the expected mechanism. A large LDL reduction after PCSK9 blockade and a more modest reduction after ezetimibe are both successful when they match the treatment plan.

Nclex Traps

  • Inclisiran and PCSK9 antibodies target the same pathway at different molecular levels: siRNA reduces production inside hepatocytes; antibodies neutralize protein outside cells.

  • Nonstatin does not mean nonserious. Lomitapide has one of the most intensive liver-safety programs in lipid pharmacology.

    Note

    Older adults often have CKD, frailty, polypharmacy, and interacting cardiovascular drugs. Start from the indication and review muscle, liver, kidney, bleeding, and interaction risk rather than withholding useful prevention solely because of age.

    Note

    Pregnancy and lactation decisions are drug specific. Most lipid-lowering therapy can be paused during pregnancy, but severe familial hypercholesterolemia or established atherosclerotic disease requires specialist review rather than an automatic rule copied from an old label.
