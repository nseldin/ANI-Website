Definition

Neisseria gonorrhoeae is a clinically important bacterium associated with urethritis and cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, epididymitis, disseminated gonococcal infection, and neonatal conjunctivitis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    identity-classification

    Title

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Neisseria gonorrhoeae is a Gram-negative coccus with a capnophile oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and facultative intracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Screening, barrier protection, partner evaluation and treatment, perinatal eye prevention, and guideline-based follow-up reduce complications and reinfection. The reviewed route description is contact transmission. Symptom onset varies by anatomic site and many infections remain asymptomatic. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Pili and Opa-mediated adherence, antigenic variation, mucosal inflammation, and survival around neutrophils support reinfection, ascending disease, and dissemination. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Neisseria gonorrhoeae is associated with urethritis and cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, epididymitis, disseminated gonococcal infection, and neonatal conjunctivitis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    NAAT from the appropriate anatomic site is central; culture and susceptibility testing are important for suspected treatment failure or resistance surveillance. A positive result can represent infection, colonization, past exposure, residual material, or contamination depending on the method and source; a negative result can reflect timing, specimen quality, prior therapy, or an untested target.
