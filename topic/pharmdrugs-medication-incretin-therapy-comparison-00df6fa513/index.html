Used To Treat

Type 2 diabetes and product-specific cardiovascular, kidney, chronic weight-management, or obstructive-sleep-apnea indications. DPP-4 inhibitors primarily lower glucose; GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists generally produce stronger A1c and weight effects.

Description

Incretins are gut hormones released after nutrients arrive. GLP-1 and GIP tell beta cells that glucose is incoming, so insulin secretion rises in a glucose-dependent way. DPP-4 inhibitors prevent rapid enzymatic destruction of the body's own GLP-1/GIP and are oral, weight-neutral, and modest. Semaglutide, liraglutide, and dulaglutide directly stimulate GLP-1 receptors at pharmacologic levels; tirzepatide stimulates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. Direct agonists also slow early gastric emptying and engage hypothalamic satiety circuits, explaining greater weight loss, nausea, and peri-procedure aspiration concerns.

Mechanism

GLP-1 receptor activation is Gs-coupled: adenylate cyclase raises cAMP, activating PKA/Epac pathways that amplify glucose-triggered insulin granule exocytosis. It suppresses inappropriate glucagon, slows gastric emptying most strongly early in therapy, and increases satiety. GIP receptor activation also raises beta-cell cAMP and may shape adipose/central responses; tirzepatide's combined signaling produces potent glycemic and weight effects. DPP-4 normally cleaves endogenous GLP-1 and GIP within minutes, so sitagliptin or linagliptin prolong physiologic signals without directly slowing the stomach as strongly. Because insulin amplification requires glucose, monotherapy hypoglycemia risk is low, but insulin or secretagogue combinations can still cause it.

Boxed Warning

GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist labels carry rodent thyroid C-cell tumor boxed warnings and MTC/MEN 2 contraindications. DPP-4 inhibitors do not carry that boxed warning, but pancreatitis, severe joint pain, bullous pemphigoid, hypersensitivity, and heart-failure vigilance remain important.

Adverse Effects

  • Direct agonists: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea/constipation, delayed gastric emptying, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, dehydration-related AKI, and aspiration concern

  • DPP-4 inhibitors: nasopharyngitis, severe arthralgia, pancreatitis, bullous pemphigoid, hypersensitivity, and class heart-failure warning context

  • Hypoglycemia rises when either pathway is combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea

Contraindications

  • MTC/MEN 2 for direct agonists with boxed warning; severe gastroparesis is a major limitation.

  • Do not combine a DPP-4 inhibitor with a GLP-1 receptor agonist merely to duplicate the same incretin axis.

  • Verify exact molecule, formulation, indication, titration, renal plan, pregnancy washout, and procedure instructions.

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain that gradual titration is intended to let gastric and central pathways adapt; racing the dose increases GI intolerance and dehydration without teaching the body faster.

  • Ask about severe persistent abdominal pain, vomiting, inability to hydrate, gallbladder symptoms, visual change, planned anesthesia, and neck symptoms.

Interactions

  • Delayed gastric emptying can alter absorption of oral drugs; tirzepatide has specific backup-contraception teaching after initiation and dose escalation.

  • Insulin and sulfonylureas add hypoglycemia and often require reassessment.

  • Sitagliptin requires renal adjustment; linagliptin is largely nonrenal, a clinically important class distinction.

Key Labs

  • A1c, glucose pattern, weight, hydration, and renal function

  • Pancreatitis/gallbladder evaluation when symptomatic and retinopathy follow-up when rapid glucose improvement is relevant

  • Medication/formulation, dose-escalation date, pregnancy plans, and procedure/anesthesia history

Nclex Traps

  • Tirzepatide is not simply a stronger GLP-1 drug; it is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist.

  • DPP-4 inhibitors do not usually produce the same weight loss or gastric slowing as direct agonists.

  • A thyroid boxed warning describes a rodent signal with unknown human relevance, not proof that every neck symptom is cancer or that routine ultrasound prevents risk.
