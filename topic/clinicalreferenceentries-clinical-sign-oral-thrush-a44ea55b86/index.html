Definition

Oral thrush is a visible form of oropharyngeal candidiasis in which Candida yeast overgrows in the mouth or throat and usually forms creamy white plaques that can be gently removed from the mucosa.

Plain Language

Oral thrush is a yeast infection that usually causes soft white patches inside the mouth.

Why It Matters

Thrush can cause pain, taste change, and poor intake, and new or recurrent disease may reveal antibiotic or steroid effects, diabetes, dry mouth, denture problems, or immune suppression. Painful or difficult swallowing can indicate spread into the esophagus and needs prompt assessment.

Assessment Method

Inspect the tongue, inner cheeks, palate, gums, mouth corners, and visible oropharynx in good light; document whether plaques remove gently, the underlying mucosa, pain, burning, taste change, oral intake, dysphagia or odynophagia, dentures, recent antibiotics, inhaled corticosteroid technique, dry mouth, diabetes, and immune status.

Positive Finding

Creamy white plaque-like lesions are present on oral mucosa and can usually be gently wiped or scraped away, revealing an erythematous and sometimes tender surface.

Mechanism

Normally controlled Candida organisms overgrow when local microbiome, saliva, mucosal barrier, or immune defenses are disrupted, producing superficial yeast and hyphal growth with mucosal inflammation.

Evidence Limitations

Milk residue may wipe away without an inflamed base, oral hairy leukoplakia and chronic hyperplastic lesions do not remove easily, Candida carriage alone does not prove disease, and oral appearance does not exclude esophageal involvement.
