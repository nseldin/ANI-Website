Class

Low-blood-gas-solubility nonpungent volatile fluorinated general anesthetic

Used To Treat

Inhaled induction and maintenance of general anesthesia in adults and children under trained anesthesia care; its relatively nonpungent odor supports mask induction.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in known or suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility. Rising end-tidal CO2, tachycardia, rigidity, acidosis, hyperkalemia or temperature requires immediate trigger discontinuation, 100% oxygen and dantrolene. Follow product fresh-gas-flow limits to minimize Compound A renal risk, monitor QT in susceptible patients, and replace desiccated absorbent because exothermic degradation can overheat the circuit.

Mechanism

Sevoflurane changes activity across GABA-A, glycine, potassium, nicotinic and other neural targets, suppressing cortical integration and spinal movement. Low blood solubility limits uptake into blood, speeding equilibration and recovery. Metabolism by CYP2E1 releases inorganic fluoride, while base-containing CO2 absorbents degrade it to fluoromethyl compounds including Compound A; exposure rises with low flows, higher concentration, heat and certain absorbents.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor inspired/end-tidal sevoflurane, end-tidal CO2, oxygenation, ventilation, ECG, blood pressure and temperature continuously. Verify vaporizer, absorber and fresh-gas flow.

  • For unexpected tachycardia or rising CO2, check ventilation and circuit while treating malignant hyperthermia as a time-critical diagnosis. Do not wait for late fever.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility, hypersensitivity to halogenated anesthetics, or prior unexplained significant hepatic dysfunction after a halogenated agent. Clarify severe long-QT risk and renal exposure conditions.

Interactions

  • Succinylcholine increases malignant-hyperthermia risk in susceptible patients. Opioids, nitrous oxide and sedatives reduce MAC and compound respiratory depression. Sevoflurane potentiates nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers. QT-prolonging drugs add repolarization risk.

Key Labs

  • Continuous end-tidal CO2, anesthetic concentration, ECG, blood pressure, oxygenation and temperature

  • Potassium, ABG/VBG, lactate, creatine kinase, urine myoglobin and renal/coagulation studies if malignant hyperthermia is suspected

  • Renal function when prolonged low-flow exposure raises Compound A concern

Nclex Traps

  • Sevoflurane is suitable for smooth inhaled induction because it is relatively nonpungent; that is not true of desflurane.

  • Compound A risk is about absorber chemistry and exposure conditions, not an automatic kidney injury in every patient.

  • Rising CO2 is an earlier malignant-hyperthermia clue than high temperature.

Description

Sevoflurane is a nonpungent volatile general anesthetic that suppresses cortical and spinal neural networks to produce unconsciousness and immobility; low blood-gas solubility lets inspired concentration change brain level and anesthetic depth quickly. Its airway profile makes it useful for inhaled pediatric induction. Dose-dependent vasodilation and respiratory depression lower blood pressure and ventilation. It can trigger malignant hyperthermia and reacts with carbon-dioxide absorbents to form Compound A, making fresh-gas-flow and absorber management clinically relevant.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, respiratory depression/apnea, cough/laryngospasm, nausea/vomiting, emergence agitation, bradycardia or arrhythmia, QT prolongation, malignant hyperthermia, hepatic reaction and potential renal injury related to degradation exposure.
