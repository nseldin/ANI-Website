Class

Oral thrombopoietin-receptor agonist used as a fixed preprocedure course in chronic liver disease

Used To Treat

Thrombocytopenia in adults with chronic liver disease who are scheduled for a procedure. Current labeling uses 3 mg once daily for 7 days, beginning 8 to 14 days before the procedure; the procedure occurs 2 to 8 days after the last dose. It is not a chronic ITP treatment and not intended to normalize platelets.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Thrombopoietin-receptor agonists can cause thrombotic and thromboembolic complications. Portal-vein thrombosis has occurred in chronic liver disease. Use the fixed regimen only for the indicated scheduled procedure, do not extend treatment to normalize platelets, and assess new abdominal pain, ascites change, limb swelling, chest pain, or neurologic deficit urgently.

Mechanism

Lusutrombopag binds the transmembrane domain of human thrombopoietin receptor MPL and activates signaling without competing with endogenous TPO. JAK2/STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt pathways promote megakaryocyte progenitor proliferation and differentiation, leading to platelet release over several days. The fixed seven-day course ends before the procedure because pharmacodynamic platelet production continues after the last tablet. The drug does not transfuse platelets, reduce splenic sequestration, correct portal hypertension, or eliminate procedural bleeding. Raising production in a circulation already prone to portal thrombosis explains why platelet and thrombosis monitoring are both required.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the procedure date before the first dose: start 8 to 14 days before, give 3 mg daily for 7 days, and schedule the procedure 2 to 8 days after the last dose.

  • Obtain platelets before treatment and close to the procedure per protocol; assess thrombosis and bleeding rather than assuming a higher count guarantees safety.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but serious hypersensitivity requires discontinuation

  • Not for chronic ITP or routine long-term thrombocytopenia correction

  • Do not extend beyond 7 days or repeat casually because the scheduled course is built around delayed platelet production

Interactions

  • No major cation-separation rule like eltrombopag, but all prothrombotic therapies and liver-disease risk factors add concern.

  • Other platelet-raising agents can overshoot the intended preprocedure response.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets before treatment and before procedure, plus liver disease context

  • Portal/venous thrombosis symptoms, procedure bleeding, and exact dose/procedure calendar

Nclex Traps

  • Lusutrombopag is a seven-day preprocedure drug for chronic liver disease, not a chronic ITP medicine.

  • The patient can have thrombocytopenia and still be at risk for portal thrombosis.

Description

Lusutrombopag is an oral small-molecule thrombopoietin-receptor agonist that activates MPL and stimulates megakaryocyte maturation and platelet production before a planned procedure in an adult with chronic liver disease. It is taken for seven days, then stopped while newly produced platelets continue to mature; the procedure is placed in the label-defined post-dose window. This timing reflects marrow biology rather than immediate drug action. The main safety tension is that chronic liver disease can cause thrombocytopenia and still remain prothrombotic, so portal-vein thrombosis is possible even when the platelet count began low.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache and mild GI or systemic symptoms

  • Portal-vein, mesenteric, venous, arterial, or other thromboembolic events

  • Excessive platelet response or a procedure occurring outside the intended response window
