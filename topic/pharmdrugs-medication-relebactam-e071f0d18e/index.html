Class

Diazabicyclooctane inhibitor of selected class A and class C serine beta-lactamases

Used To Treat

Used in Recarbrio with imipenem/cilastatin for labeled susceptible complicated urinary, intra-abdominal, hospital-acquired, and ventilator-associated infections according to current indication and microbiology.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Relebactam does not remove imipenem's seizure risk or carbapenem-valproate interaction, and it does not inhibit MBL or OXA-48-like enzymes.

Mechanism

Relebactam covalently but reversibly carbamylates the active-site serine of susceptible enzymes. Imipenem then survives to bind multiple PBPs. Relebactam can restore activity in selected KPC-producing Enterobacterales and Pseudomonas with AmpC plus permeability-related resistance, but it cannot create activity when an MBL, OXA-48-like enzyme, severe porin/efflux combination, or altered target dominates. All components require renal adjustment, while imipenem preserves its seizure and valproate interaction concerns.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify all three components and the complete Recarbrio dose because confusing cilastatin and relebactam erases the distinction between a human enzyme inhibitor and a bacterial enzyme inhibitor.

  • Trend renal function, neurologic status, cultures, and response because dose and resistance mechanism can change during critical illness.

Contraindications

  • Do not treat relebactam as interchangeable with avibactam because avibactam has activity against some OXA-48-like enzymes that relebactam lacks.

  • Avoid carbapenem-valproate coadministration when possible because relebactam does not preserve valproate exposure.

Interactions

  • Imipenem/cilastatin/relebactam can markedly lower valproate through the imipenem carbapenem component.

  • Ganciclovir and other proconvulsant exposures have been associated with seizure concern alongside imipenem/cilastatin.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, KPC/AmpC or carbapenemase data, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, mental status, and valproate concentration when relevant.

  • A positive MBL or OXA-48-like result should trigger a different mechanism discussion rather than automatic Recarbrio selection.

Nclex Traps

  • Cilastatin protects imipenem from a human kidney enzyme; relebactam protects it from selected bacterial enzymes.

  • Relebactam covers KPC/AmpC, not NDM/VIM/IMP or OXA-48-like enzymes.

Display Name

Relebactam

Description

Relebactam is a non-beta-lactam diazabicyclooctane inhibitor that protects imipenem from many class A enzymes including KPC and class C AmpC enzymes. It has no direct antibacterial activity. It does not reliably inhibit class B metallo-beta-lactamases or class D OXA-48-like carbapenemases. Recarbrio therefore contains three mechanistically distinct components: imipenem kills by binding PBPs, cilastatin blocks human renal dehydropeptidase I (DHP-I) so imipenem is not destroyed in the kidney, and relebactam blocks selected bacterial beta-lactamases.

Class Pathway

  • Inhibit KPC and AmpC-like enzymes

  • Protect imipenem

  • Cilastatin separately blocks human renal dehydropeptidase

Adverse Effects

  • Combination effects include nausea, diarrhea, liver-test elevation, phlebitis, allergy, CDI, cytopenias, encephalopathy, myoclonus, and seizures.

  • Clinical failure can reflect an unsupported enzyme, underexposure, source-control failure, or emergent permeability and target resistance.
