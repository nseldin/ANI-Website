Used To Treat

Invasive aspergillosis and invasive mucormycosis with current age-, weight-, route-, and formulation-specific labeling. IV and capsule regimens use a loading phase followed by daily maintenance.

Description

Isavuconazonium sulfate, branded Cresemba, is a water-soluble prodrug rapidly converted to isavuconazole, a triazole that inhibits fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP51), depletes ergosterol, and covers invasive Aspergillus and Mucorales. That Mucorales activity makes it fundamentally different from fluconazole and from voriconazole. It is available orally and IV without the cyclodextrin vehicle used by some other IV azoles. Unlike most clinically discussed azoles, it dose-dependently shortens QT and is contraindicated in familial short-QT syndrome. It is a sensitive CYP3A substrate and moderate CYP3A inhibitor, so 'fewer interactions' does not mean interaction free.

Mechanism

Blood esterases cleave the water-soluble prodrug into isavuconazole and an inactive cleavage product. Prodrug solubility allows both oral and cyclodextrin-free IV delivery. Active drug binds CYP51 heme iron and inhibits lanosterol demethylation, which produces ergosterol depletion and abnormal sterol accumulation. The active drug has a large distribution volume and long terminal half-life, so unassisted accumulation would be slow; the loading phase therefore raises exposure rapidly before once-daily maintenance. Isavuconazole shortens QT through altered cardiac repolarization. Familial short-QT syndrome already increases malignant-arrhythmia risk, which explains the contraindication even though the exact incremental clinical risk is incompletely defined. Strong CYP3A inhibition increases isavuconazole exposure and toxicity risk, while strong induction decreases exposure and creates breakthrough-infection risk. Routine TDM is not required in every patient, but an unusual interaction, absorption problem, toxicity, or treatment failure creates a specific reason to measure exposure with specialist interpretation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in familial short-QT syndrome and with strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers that can cause toxic or ineffective exposure. Serious hepatic and infusion/hypersensitivity reactions can occur.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, elevated liver tests, hypokalemia, cough, peripheral edema, and infusion reactions.

  • Severe hepatic injury, anaphylaxis/hypersensitivity, serious skin reaction, embryo-fetal harm, and interaction-mediated toxicity or breakthrough infection.

Contraindications

  • Familial short-QT syndrome; coadministration with strong CYP3A inhibitors or strong inducers under current labeling; serious hypersensitivity.

  • Do not call the prodrug and active moiety interchangeable dose names: the label expresses strengths in both isavuconazonium sulfate and isavuconazole equivalents.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify six-dose loading phase over 48 hours before daily maintenance unless the protocol documents otherwise. Confirm exact capsule count or IV vial equivalence, infusion filter, liver tests, pregnancy context, and CYP3A medication plan.

  • Monitor during infusion for hypotension, dyspnea, chills, dizziness, or paresthesia. Review QT history; this drug shortens rather than prolongs QT.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors increase isavuconazole; strong inducers reduce it and are contraindicated. Isavuconazole can raise tacrolimus, sirolimus, cyclosporine, and other CYP3A substrate exposure.

  • Ritonavir exposure and effect are dose dependent; use the current label or interaction service rather than a class shortcut.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT/alkaline phosphatase/bilirubin, potassium/magnesium, ECG in a short-QT or complex arrhythmia context, immunosuppressant levels, and cultures/imaging for response.

  • Routine levels are not universally required, but a correctly timed level may help with unusual failure, toxicity, adherence, or interaction.

Nclex Traps

  • Isavuconazonium is the prodrug; isavuconazole is active.

  • It shortens QT and covers Mucorales, two distinctions from many other triazoles.
