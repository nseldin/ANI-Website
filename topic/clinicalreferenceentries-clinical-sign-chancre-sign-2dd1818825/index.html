Definition

Chancre sign is an observed and palpated ulcer sign. It is a clue to primary syphilis. A positive finding means a classically solitary clean-based indurated painless ulcer with regional nodes is present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chancre sign means a classically solitary clean-based indurated painless ulcer with regional nodes is present.

Why It Matters

The observed Chancre sign finding can signal primary syphilis. Treponemal multiplication at the inoculation site causes endarteritis and tissue ulceration. Chancre sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • primary syphilis

Assessment Method

Inspect any genital, oral, or anal ulcer with consent and appropriate precautions.

Positive Finding

A classically solitary clean-based indurated painless ulcer with regional nodes is present.

Mechanism

Treponemal multiplication at the inoculation site causes endarteritis and tissue ulceration.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Chancre sign finding raises concern for primary syphilis because treponemal multiplication at the inoculation site causes endarteritis and tissue ulceration. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chancre sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Chancres can be painful, multiple, or hidden, and clinical morphology is not sufficiently accurate. Therefore, Chancre sign cannot confirm or exclude primary syphilis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as traumatic ulcer, LGV, malignancy.

False Negative Situations

Chancre sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of primary syphilis.
