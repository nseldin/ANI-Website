Definition

Blue-dot sign is an observed scrotal sign. It is a clue to torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis. A positive finding means a small tender blue or dark spot is visible beneath the skin.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Blue-dot sign means a small tender blue or dark spot is visible beneath the skin.

Why It Matters

The observed Blue-dot sign finding can signal torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis. An infarcted twisted appendage becomes congested and visible through thin scrotal tissue. Blue-dot sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis

Assessment Method

Inspect the upper testis through scrotal skin.

Positive Finding

A small tender blue or dark spot is visible beneath the skin.

Mechanism

An infarcted twisted appendage becomes congested and visible through thin scrotal tissue.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Blue-dot sign finding raises concern for torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis because an infarcted twisted appendage becomes congested and visible through thin scrotal tissue. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Blue-dot sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Blue-dot sign to focus evaluation of torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis and the reviewed alternatives: hematoma, varicocele thrombosis, skin lesion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the upper testis through scrotal skin. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Blue-dot sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The sign is absent in most cases and bruising can mimic it. Therefore, Blue-dot sign cannot confirm or exclude torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hematoma

  • varicocele thrombosis

  • skin lesion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hematoma, varicocele thrombosis, skin lesion.

False Negative Situations

Blue-dot sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of torsion of the appendix testis or appendix epididymis.
