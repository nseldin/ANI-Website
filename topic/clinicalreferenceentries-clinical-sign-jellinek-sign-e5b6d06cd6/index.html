Definition

Jellinek sign is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to thyroid eye disease, historically described. A positive finding means brownish hyperpigmentation is present on the upper lids.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Jellinek sign means brownish hyperpigmentation is present on the upper lids.

Why It Matters

The observed Jellinek sign finding can signal thyroid eye disease, historically described. Proposed local pigment and vascular changes accompany chronic thyroid disease, but the mechanism is uncertain. Jellinek sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid eye disease, historically described

Assessment Method

Inspect upper eyelid skin color.

Positive Finding

Brownish hyperpigmentation is present on the upper lids.

Mechanism

Proposed local pigment and vascular changes accompany chronic thyroid disease, but the mechanism is uncertain.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Jellinek sign finding raises concern for thyroid eye disease, historically described because proposed local pigment and vascular changes accompany chronic thyroid disease, but the mechanism is uncertain. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Jellinek sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Jellinek sign to focus evaluation of thyroid eye disease, historically described and the reviewed alternatives: melasma, postinflammatory pigment, cosmetics. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect upper eyelid skin color. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Jellinek sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The association is weak and pigmentation varies normally. Therefore, Jellinek sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease, historically described by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • melasma

  • postinflammatory pigment

  • cosmetics

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as melasma, postinflammatory pigment, cosmetics.

False Negative Situations

Jellinek sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thyroid eye disease, historically described.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable. Jellinek sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude thyroid eye disease, historically described. Apply the reviewed limitation for Jellinek sign: The association is weak and pigmentation varies normally.

Safety Precautions

Observe Jellinek sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect upper eyelid skin color. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not interpret eyelid color as a thyroid-function test.
