Used To Treat

Treatment of CMV retinitis in immunocompromised adults and prevention of CMV disease in selected transplant recipients at risk. Site, severity, immune status, absorption, marrow reserve, kidney function, and resistance determine whether IV ganciclovir or oral valganciclovir is used.

Mechanism

CMV UL97 phosphorylates ganciclovir to the monophosphate, after which cellular kinases form the diphosphate and active triphosphate. Ganciclovir triphosphate competes with deoxyguanosine triphosphate for UL54 DNA polymerase and becomes incorporated into viral DNA, slowing elongation and eventually terminating synthesis. The triphosphate persists inside infected cells, supporting antiviral activity after plasma concentrations fall. UL97 mutations reduce initial activation and commonly cause ganciclovir resistance; UL54 polymerase mutations can create higher-level resistance and cross-resistance to cidofovir or sometimes foscarnet. The parent drug is cleared by glomerular filtration and active secretion, so declining renal function raises triphosphate exposure in host tissues and makes cytopenia more likely.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: severe leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, bone-marrow failure, and aplastic anemia can occur. Animal and mechanistic data support impaired fertility, fetal toxicity, mutagenicity, and carcinogenic potential. Handle as a hazardous drug, avoid pregnancy, use label-specified contraception, and monitor CBC closely. A falling absolute neutrophil count matters because CMV treatment may continue while antibacterial and antifungal defense deteriorates.

Contraindications

  • Clinically significant hypersensitivity to ganciclovir, valganciclovir, or a component.

  • Do not give an unadjusted dose during renal impairment or ignore severe cytopenia; balance disease threat with dose modification, growth-factor support, or an alternative antiviral.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use hazardous-drug handling precautions and verify CBC, creatinine, weight, dose, and infusion preparation. Do not crush or expose staff to powder or solution unnecessarily.

  • For CMV retinitis, trend serial dilated retinal examinations because lesion activity and location, not blood viral load alone, determine immediate vision risk.

Interactions

  • Other marrow suppressants, including zidovudine, mycophenolate, chemotherapy, or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, can make cytopenia dose-limiting.

  • Nephrotoxins increase accumulation; imipenem-cilastatin with ganciclovir has been associated with seizures and is generally avoided unless benefit outweighs risk.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential and platelets, creatinine/eGFR, liver tests, pregnancy assessment, and disease-specific CMV monitoring

  • Retinal examination, visual symptoms, fever/infection, bleeding, neurologic status, hydration, adherence, immune recovery, and resistance testing after confirmed failure

Nclex Traps

  • Ganciclovir is activated by CMV UL97, not HSV thymidine kinase as its defining CMV step.

  • A blood CMV load does not replace direct retinal examination in CMV retinitis.

Description

Ganciclovir is a CMV-active acyclic guanosine analogue that requires viral UL97 phosphorylation and then forms a triphosphate that inhibits UL54 DNA polymerase, slowing or terminating cytomegalovirus DNA elongation. The same nucleoside-antimetabolite exposure injures rapidly dividing human marrow precursors, which explains neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. Intravenous administration gives reliable induction exposure when disease is severe, sight-threatening, or oral absorption is uncertain. The drug does not eradicate latent CMV, so relapse risk depends on immune recovery and maintenance strategy. Renal elimination links kidney injury directly to worsening marrow and neurologic toxicity.

Adverse Effects

  • Neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, fever, infection, nausea, diarrhea, headache, and infusion reactions

  • Bone-marrow failure, renal accumulation, seizures or confusion, liver injury, retinal detachment related to CMV disease, fertility impairment, and fetal toxicity
