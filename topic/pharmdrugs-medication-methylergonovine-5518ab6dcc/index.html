Contraindications

  • Hypertension, preeclampsia/eclampsia or toxemia, pregnancy before the appropriate post-delivery point, serious ergot hypersensitivity, and situations where vasoconstriction is unacceptable.

  • Use great caution with coronary disease or risk factors, peripheral vascular disease, sepsis, hepatic or renal impairment, and any concurrent strong CYP3A inhibitor.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check and document blood pressure immediately before each dose. Hold and escalate for hypertension, severe headache, neurologic symptoms, chest pain, dyspnea, or limb ischemia rather than treating the number as expected postpartum discomfort.

  • Confirm placental delivery, uterine tone, route, dose, and storage location. Reassess bleeding and tone after administration while the team continues to evaluate all causes of hemorrhage.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors, including selected macrolides, azoles, protease inhibitors, and grapefruit products, can increase ergot exposure and ischemic vasospasm risk.

  • Other vasoconstrictors, triptans, stimulants, nicotine, and some beta blockers can compound hypertension or peripheral/coronary ischemia.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure and clinical perfusion are primary; obtain CBC, platelets, fibrinogen and coagulation studies for hemorrhage, plus renal/liver testing when organ impairment matters

  • Uterine tone, quantified blood loss, headache or focal deficit, chest pain/ECG/troponin when ischemia is suspected, respiratory status, and limb perfusion

Nclex Traps

  • Methylergonovine is paired with hypertension risk because its vasoconstriction is part of the pharmacology, not an unrelated adverse effect.

  • A strong uterine contraction can reduce atony bleeding, but continued bleeding from a firm uterus demands another diagnosis rather than repeated ergot dosing.

Used To Treat

Prevention or control of postpartum uterine atony, hemorrhage, and subinvolution after delivery of the placenta; selected administration after delivery of the anterior shoulder occurs only under full obstetric supervision.

Mechanism

Methylergonovine is a semisynthetic ergoline that acts as a partial agonist or antagonist across serotonin 5-HT receptors and stimulates alpha-adrenergic and uterine smooth-muscle pathways. Receptor activation increases phospholipase-C signaling, inositol trisphosphate, intracellular calcium, calmodulin, and myosin-light-chain phosphorylation. In postpartum myometrium this produces rapid, forceful, sustained contraction and shortens the relaxed interval, compressing uterine vessels. In arterial smooth muscle, serotonergic and alpha-adrenergic signaling produces vasoconstriction. Coronary vasospasm can therefore reduce myocardial oxygen supply even in a patient without fixed coronary obstruction, while generalized vasoconstriction can sharply raise blood pressure and cerebral-vessel stress. CYP3A-mediated metabolism helps explain dangerous exposure increases with strong inhibitors.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Methylergonovine is contraindicated in hypertension, toxemia/preeclampsia, pregnancy before delivery, and hypersensitivity. Routine IV administration is avoided because abrupt exposure can cause sudden severe hypertension, stroke, seizure, coronary vasospasm, myocardial infarction, or death. If IV use is judged lifesaving, current labeling requires slow administration over at least 60 seconds with careful blood-pressure monitoring. Store it separately from neonatal vitamin K and hepatitis B products because accidental newborn injection has caused respiratory depression, seizures, cyanosis, and oliguria.

Description

Methylergonovine is an ergot-derived uterotonic that produces a strong, sustained uterine contraction rather than the rhythmic contraction pattern typical of oxytocin. By compressing placental-bed vessels it can rapidly reduce atony-related postpartum bleeding and help an enlarged postpartum uterus involute. The same receptor promiscuity that tightens the uterus also constricts systemic and coronary arteries, so hypertension, preeclampsia, vascular disease, chest pain, and route are central safety issues. It is not given during pregnancy before delivery as a routine labor drug, and it cannot repair genital-tract trauma or correct retained tissue, inversion, rupture, or coagulopathy.

Adverse Effects

  • Uterine cramping, nausea, vomiting, headache, sweating, dizziness, tinnitus, and transient blood-pressure elevation

  • Hypertensive crisis, stroke, seizure, coronary vasospasm, myocardial ischemia or infarction, arrhythmia, peripheral ischemia, pulmonary edema, or severe medication error in a newborn
