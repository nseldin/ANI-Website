Definition

Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon is an observed congenital synkinesis sign. It is a clue to aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation. A positive finding means the upper eyelid elevates with jaw movement.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon means the upper eyelid elevates with jaw movement.

Why It Matters

The observed Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon finding can signal aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation. Miswired trigeminal motor activity coactivates the levator palpebrae through the oculomotor pathway. Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation

Assessment Method

Observe a ptotic eyelid while the patient opens the mouth or moves the jaw side to side.

Positive Finding

The upper eyelid elevates with jaw movement.

Mechanism

Miswired trigeminal motor activity coactivates the levator palpebrae through the oculomotor pathway.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon finding raises concern for aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation because miswired trigeminal motor activity coactivates the levator palpebrae through the oculomotor pathway. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Small winks may be missed and the name must not be confused with Marcus Gunn pupil. Therefore, Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon cannot confirm or exclude aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as facial synkinesis, voluntary eyebrow elevation, mechanical ptosis.

False Negative Situations

Gunn jaw-winking phenomenon may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of aberrant trigeminal-to-oculomotor innervation.
