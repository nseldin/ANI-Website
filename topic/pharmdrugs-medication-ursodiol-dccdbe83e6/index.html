Used To Treat

Primary biliary cholangitis with tablet formulations; selected capsule labeling also covers dissolution/prevention of certain radiolucent noncalcified cholesterol gallstones in carefully selected patients.

Mechanism

After absorption, hepatic conjugation with glycine or taurine and secretion into bile, ursodiol enters enterohepatic circulation. Repeated dosing enriches bile with hydrophilic UDCA, dilutes toxic hydrophobic species, reduces bile-acid-induced apoptosis and inflammatory injury, and promotes transporter-mediated choleresis. In cholesterol stones it reduces biliary cholesterol saturation over months, allowing gradual dissolution only when stone composition and gallbladder function are suitable.

Contraindications

  • Complete biliary obstruction or a nonfunctioning gallbladder for dissolution therapy

  • Clarify calcified/pigment stones, acute cholecystitis/cholangitis, pancreatitis and inability to undergo needed definitive care

  • Product hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify formulation and indication because PBC and gallstone labeling use different weight-based regimens.

  • Teach adherence with food as labeled and explain that biochemical or stone response requires months of therapy and scheduled monitoring.

Interactions

  • Cholestyramine, colestipol and other bile-acid sequestrants reduce absorption; separate administration.

  • Aluminum-based antacids can bind bile acids and reduce absorption.

  • Drugs that increase biliary cholesterol can oppose gallstone dissolution.

Key Labs

  • Alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, AST/ALT, albumin and INR

  • Clinical decompensation assessment and platelet/portal-hypertension context

  • Gallbladder imaging and stone characteristics when used for dissolution

Nclex Traps

  • Ursodiol changes bile composition; it does not mechanically remove an obstructing stone.

  • PBC improvement is monitored with both biochemical response and clinical liver status.

  • Bile-acid resins can bind away ursodiol and make an apparently adherent regimen ineffective.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Monitor liver tests and clinical progression; worsening bilirubin, INR, albumin, ascites, encephalopathy or variceal bleeding requires reassessment even if alkaline phosphatase improved. Do not use the concept of 'improves bile flow' to bypass complete biliary obstruction. Gallstone dissolution is slow, selective and recurrence-prone.

Description

Ursodiol is a naturally occurring hydrophilic bile acid that enriches the biliary pool, displaces membrane-injuring hydrophobic bile acids and promotes protective hepatobiliary secretion. In PBC it is disease-modifying first-line therapy; it is not a rapid pain treatment and cannot relieve a mechanically obstructed duct.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, nausea, pruritus, rash and weight gain

  • Rare hypersensitivity or worsening clinical liver status from disease progression

  • Incomplete gallstone dissolution or recurrence despite prolonged therapy
