Used To Treat

Formulation- and protocol-specific bleeding driven by fibrinolysis: IV CYKLOKAPRON is labeled for short-term prevention/reduction of bleeding around dental extraction in hemophilia; oral LYSTEDA is labeled for cyclic heavy menstrual bleeding. Trauma, postpartum and surgical uses follow separate evidence-based protocols.

Mechanism

Fibrin exposes lysine residues that recruit plasminogen and accelerate its activation to plasmin. Tranexamic acid occupies those lysine-binding sites, reducing plasminogen attachment and plasmin-mediated fibrin cleavage. At higher concentrations it also directly inhibits plasmin. Because unchanged drug is cleared in urine and can antagonize inhibitory glycine receptors in the CNS at high exposure, renal accumulation and excessive dosing increase seizure risk.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING for CYKLOKAPRON injection - risk of fatal medication error from wrong route. It is IV only. Accidental intrathecal or epidural injection has caused seizures, cardiac arrhythmias, permanent neurologic injury and death. Physically separate it from neuraxial medications and trace every line. Additional warnings include active intravascular clotting, subarachnoid hemorrhage, thrombosis with prothrombotic products, seizures, visual disturbance, hypersensitivity and hypotension if infused faster than 1 mL/min.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact product, indication, time from bleeding onset, IV versus oral route, renal dose, thrombotic history, color-vision symptoms and concurrent hormonal or prothrombotic drugs.

  • For IV use, read back 'tranexamic acid, intravenous only,' inspect the connection from bag/syringe to vein, keep away from the neuraxial field and infuse at the labeled rate.

Key Labs

  • CBC/hemoglobin, renal function, fibrinogen/coagulation and viscoelastic testing when clinically indicated, bleeding trend, thrombosis/neurologic assessment and ophthalmic evaluation for persistent visual symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • The boxed warning is now a specific wrong-route neuraxial warning, not vague advice to confirm the label.

  • TXA stabilizes clot by limiting fibrinolysis; it does not replace fibrinogen or reverse heparin/warfarin.

Description

Tranexamic acid is an antifibrinolytic lysine analog that preserves an existing fibrin clot by preventing plasminogen and plasmin from docking to fibrin. It does not create fibrin, replace a missing factor or reverse an anticoagulant. Benefit depends on early, indication-specific dosing; toxicity depends on renal exposure, route, thrombosis risk and seizure threshold. Why does the IV dental label say short term? Its 2-to-8-day course is indication-specific: it spans the high-fibrinolysis period around tooth extraction in hemophilia and was not written as a universal duration for trauma, postpartum hemorrhage, surgery, or heavy menstrual bleeding. Extending therapy without reassessing the bleeding source can preserve pathologic clot as well as hemostatic clot, while renal accumulation raises exposure and seizure risk. Therefore each formulation and indication needs its own evidence-based dose, start time, stop point, renal adjustment, and thrombosis review rather than an automatic refill.
