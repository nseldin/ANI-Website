Definition

Patellar tap sign is a bedside effusion maneuver. It is a clue to moderate or large knee effusion. A positive finding means the patella taps the femoral trochlea and rebounds.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Patellar tap sign means the patella taps the femoral trochlea and rebounds.

Why It Matters

A positive Patellar tap sign response can signal moderate or large knee effusion. Sufficient intra-articular fluid floats the patella above the femur. Patellar tap sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • moderate or large knee effusion

Assessment Method

Milk suprapatellar fluid downward and press the patella toward the femur.

Positive Finding

The patella taps the femoral trochlea and rebounds.

Mechanism

Sufficient intra-articular fluid floats the patella above the femur.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Patellar tap sign response raises concern for moderate or large knee effusion because sufficient intra-articular fluid floats the patella above the femur. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Patellar tap sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Patellar tap sign to focus evaluation of moderate or large knee effusion and the reviewed alternatives: prepatellar bursitis, fat pad swelling, normal laxity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Milk suprapatellar fluid downward and press the patella toward the femur. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Patellar tap sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Small effusions and thick soft tissue reduce sensitivity. Therefore, Patellar tap sign cannot confirm or exclude moderate or large knee effusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • prepatellar bursitis

  • fat pad swelling

  • normal laxity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from prepatellar bursitis, fat pad swelling, normal laxity, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Patellar tap sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct mainly for larger effusions. When performed as described, Patellar tap sign can adjust concern for moderate or large knee effusion, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Patellar tap sign: Small effusions and thick soft tissue reduce sensitivity.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Patellar tap sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Milk suprapatellar fluid downward and press the patella toward the femur. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use gentle pressure and avoid repeated tapping in an acutely painful knee.
