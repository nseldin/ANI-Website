Mechanism

Diphenhydramine blocks H1 receptors and has significant antimuscarinic activity. CNS penetration produces sedation and impairment; muscarinic blockade explains dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation, tachycardia, and confusion risk.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for diphenhydramine in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

Diphenhydramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine that crosses the blood-brain barrier and also blocks muscarinic receptors. That makes it useful for allergy/itching and motion symptoms, but also makes sedation, delirium, falls, urinary retention, constipation, and anticholinergic toxicity central nursing concerns.
