Definition

Negativism is an observed behavioral-motor sign. It is a clue to catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness. A positive finding means the patient resists or does the opposite without an apparent motive.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Negativism means the patient resists or does the opposite without an apparent motive.

Why It Matters

The observed Negativism finding can signal catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness. Disordered motor-volitional circuits produce automatic opposition rather than goal-directed refusal. Negativism cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness

Assessment Method

Give simple neutral instructions and observe response without coercion.

Positive Finding

The patient resists or does the opposite without an apparent motive.

Mechanism

Disordered motor-volitional circuits produce automatic opposition rather than goal-directed refusal.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Negativism finding raises concern for catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness because disordered motor-volitional circuits produce automatic opposition rather than goal-directed refusal. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Negativism does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Negativism to focus evaluation of catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness and the reviewed alternatives: autism, trauma response, oppositional behavior. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Give simple neutral instructions and observe response without coercion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Negativism as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fear, language barriers, pain, and informed refusal must not be mislabeled. Therefore, Negativism cannot confirm or exclude catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • autism

  • trauma response

  • oppositional behavior

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as autism, trauma response, oppositional behavior.

False Negative Situations

Negativism may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonia or severe neuropsychiatric illness.
