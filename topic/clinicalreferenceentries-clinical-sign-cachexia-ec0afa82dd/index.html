Definition

Cachexia is an illness-related metabolic wasting syndrome in which skeletal muscle is progressively lost, with or without loss of fat. Ordinary nutrition alone often cannot fully reverse it.

Plain Language

A serious chronic illness drives ongoing muscle and sometimes fat loss, causing weakness and loss of function even when food is offered.

Why It Matters

Cachexia can reduce mobility, independence, wound healing, treatment tolerance, and survival. Weight alone may miss it because obesity or edema can hide muscle loss, so nurses should trend strength, intake, swallowing, symptoms, function, weight, and muscle stores and coordinate early nutrition, rehabilitation, symptom, and goal-concordant care.

Associated Conditions

  • advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake

Assessment Method

Trend weight, percentage change, muscle and fat stores, grip and proximal strength, gait, activities, intake, appetite, taste, swallowing, nausea, stool, pain, mood, inflammation, edema, medications, treatment tolerance, goals, and caregiver observations using respectful nutrition and functional assessment.

Positive Finding

There is ongoing involuntary loss of skeletal muscle, with or without fat loss, usually accompanied by weight loss, weakness, fatigue, reduced function, poor appetite, or early satiety in the setting of chronic disease.

Mechanism

Inflammatory and disease signals increase protein and fat breakdown, alter hormones and metabolism, reduce appetite and activity, and prevent ordinary intake alone from fully reversing tissue loss.

Clinical Interpretation

The Cachexia pattern raises concern for advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake because inflammatory and disease signals increase protein and fat breakdown, alter hormones and metabolism, reduce appetite and activity, and prevent ordinary intake alone from fully reversing tissue loss. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cachexia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cachexia to focus evaluation of advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake and the reviewed alternatives: simple starvation, sarcopenia, dehydration, major depression. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Trend weight, percentage change, muscle and fat stores, grip and proximal strength, gait, activities, intake, appetite, taste, swallowing, nausea, stool, pain, mood, inflammation, edema, medications, treatment tolerance, goals, and caregiver observations using respectful nutrition and functional assessment. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cachexia as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Weight alone can miss muscle loss, edema can hide falling tissue mass, and criteria vary by disease and guideline; appearance cannot distinguish cachexia from starvation, sarcopenia, depression, or another cause of wasting. Therefore, Cachexia cannot confirm or exclude advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • simple starvation

  • sarcopenia

  • dehydration

  • major depression

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by simple starvation, sarcopenia, dehydration, major depression without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Cachexia may be absent even when advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Modern Status

Current clinical syndrome with condition-specific diagnostic and staging criteria; it is more than simple thinness or starvation and may be partly hidden by obesity or edema. Interpret Cachexia in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Weight alone can miss muscle loss, edema can hide falling tissue mass, and criteria vary by disease and guideline; appearance cannot distinguish cachexia from starvation, sarcopenia, depression, or another cause of wasting.

Safety Precautions

Observe Cachexia with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Trend weight, percentage change, muscle and fat stores, grip and proximal strength, gait, activities, intake, appetite, taste, swallowing, nausea, stool, pain, mood, inflammation, edema, medications, treatment tolerance, goals, and caregiver observations using respectful nutrition and functional assessment. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not blame, shame, or promise that calories alone will reverse the syndrome; promptly address dysphagia, dehydration, uncontrolled symptoms, falls, pressure-injury risk, severe weakness, inability to perform basic activities, or treatment toxicity with coordinated medical, nursing, nutrition, rehabilitation, and goal-concordant care.

Nursing Relevance

For Cachexia, document which defined components are present or absent, their timing and trend, and whether the positive pattern is met: There is ongoing involuntary loss of skeletal muscle, with or without fat loss, usually accompanied by weight loss, weakness, fatigue, reduced function, poor appetite, or early satiety in the setting of chronic disease. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Trend weight, percentage change, muscle and fat stores, grip and proximal strength, gait, activities, intake, appetite, taste, swallowing, nausea, stool, pain, mood, inflammation, edema, medications, treatment tolerance, goals, and caregiver observations using respectful nutrition and functional assessment. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, advanced cancer, chronic heart failure, chronic lung or kidney disease, chronic infection, inflammatory disease, or another severe sustained illness with abnormal metabolism and reduced intake, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
