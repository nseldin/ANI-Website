Definition

Fluctuation sign for ascites is a bedside fluid-wave maneuver. It is a clue to large-volume ascites. A positive finding means a transmitted impulse is felt across the abdomen.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Fluctuation sign for ascites means a transmitted impulse is felt across the abdomen.

Why It Matters

A positive Fluctuation sign for ascites response can signal large-volume ascites. Free intraperitoneal fluid carries the pressure wave after the abdominal-wall wave is damped. Fluctuation sign for ascites cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large-volume ascites

Assessment Method

Have an assistant or patient press the ulnar hand at midline, then tap one flank and feel the opposite flank.

Positive Finding

A transmitted impulse is felt across the abdomen.

Mechanism

Free intraperitoneal fluid carries the pressure wave after the abdominal-wall wave is damped.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Fluctuation sign for ascites response raises concern for large-volume ascites because free intraperitoneal fluid carries the pressure wave after the abdominal-wall wave is damped. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Fluctuation sign for ascites does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Fluctuation sign for ascites to focus evaluation of large-volume ascites and the reviewed alternatives: adipose wall wave, large ovarian cyst, distended bladder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Have an assistant or patient press the ulnar hand at midline, then tap one flank and feel the opposite flank. Evidence Limitations

It is insensitive for small fluid and obesity can transmit false waves. Therefore, Fluctuation sign for ascites cannot confirm or exclude large-volume ascites by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • adipose wall wave

  • large ovarian cyst

  • distended bladder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from adipose wall wave, large ovarian cyst, distended bladder, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Fluctuation sign for ascites may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct mainly for large ascites. When performed as described, Fluctuation sign for ascites can adjust concern for large-volume ascites, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Fluctuation sign for ascites: It is insensitive for small fluid and obesity can transmit false waves.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Fluctuation sign for ascites, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Have an assistant or patient press the ulnar hand at midline, then tap one flank and feel the opposite flank. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use painful force or rely on it instead of ultrasound.
