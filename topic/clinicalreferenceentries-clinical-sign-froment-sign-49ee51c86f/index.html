Definition

Froment sign is a bedside hand-motor sign. It is a clue to ulnar nerve weakness. A positive finding means the thumb interphalangeal joint flexes to maintain grip.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Froment sign means the thumb interphalangeal joint flexes to maintain grip.

Why It Matters

The observed Froment sign finding can signal ulnar nerve weakness. Weak adductor pollicis is compensated by median-innervated flexor pollicis longus. Froment sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ulnar nerve weakness

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to hold paper between thumb and index while the examiner pulls.

Positive Finding

The thumb interphalangeal joint flexes to maintain grip.

Mechanism

Weak adductor pollicis is compensated by median-innervated flexor pollicis longus.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Froment sign finding raises concern for ulnar nerve weakness because weak adductor pollicis is compensated by median-innervated flexor pollicis longus. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Froment sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Froment sign to focus evaluation of ulnar nerve weakness and the reviewed alternatives: thumb arthritis, anterior interosseous compensation, weak grip. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to hold paper between thumb and index while the examiner pulls. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Froment sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain and poor instruction can cause compensation. Therefore, Froment sign cannot confirm or exclude ulnar nerve weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • thumb arthritis

  • anterior interosseous compensation

  • weak grip

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as thumb arthritis, anterior interosseous compensation, weak grip.

False Negative Situations

Froment sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of ulnar nerve weakness.
