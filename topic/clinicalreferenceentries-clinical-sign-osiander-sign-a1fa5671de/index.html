Definition

Osiander sign is a palpated vascular pulsation sign. It is a clue to increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy. A positive finding means increased vaginal-artery pulsation is felt.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Osiander sign means increased vaginal-artery pulsation is felt.

Why It Matters

The observed Osiander sign finding can signal increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy. Pregnancy increases uterine and vaginal blood flow, amplifying arterial pulsation. Osiander sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy

Assessment Method

During an otherwise indicated pelvic examination, gently note pulsation in lateral fornices.

Positive Finding

Increased vaginal-artery pulsation is felt.

Mechanism

Pregnancy increases uterine and vaginal blood flow, amplifying arterial pulsation.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Osiander sign finding raises concern for increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy because pregnancy increases uterine and vaginal blood flow, amplifying arterial pulsation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Osiander sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Osiander sign to focus evaluation of increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy and the reviewed alternatives: pelvic inflammation, fibroid, normal arterial pulse. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During an otherwise indicated pelvic examination, gently note pulsation in lateral fornices. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Osiander sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Subjective pulsation is nonspecific and potentially misleading. Therefore, Osiander sign cannot confirm or exclude increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pelvic inflammation

  • fibroid

  • normal arterial pulse

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pelvic inflammation, fibroid, normal arterial pulse.

False Negative Situations

Osiander sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy.

Modern Status

Historical presumptive sign; obsolete for diagnosis. Osiander sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude increased pelvic blood flow in early pregnancy. Apply the reviewed limitation for Osiander sign: Subjective pulsation is nonspecific and potentially misleading.

Safety Precautions

Observe Osiander sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: During an otherwise indicated pelvic examination, gently note pulsation in lateral fornices. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not seek forcefully or use it to diagnose pregnancy.
