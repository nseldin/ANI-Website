Definition

V sign of dermatomyositis is an observed rash-distribution sign. It is a clue to dermatomyositis. A positive finding means photosensitive erythema forms a V over exposed chest skin.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive V sign of dermatomyositis means photosensitive erythema forms a V over exposed chest skin.

Why It Matters

The observed V sign of dermatomyositis finding can signal dermatomyositis. Ultraviolet light enhances immune injury in the neckline exposure pattern. V sign of dermatomyositis cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dermatomyositis

Assessment Method

Inspect the anterior neck and upper chest.

Positive Finding

Photosensitive erythema forms a V over exposed chest skin.

Mechanism

Ultraviolet light enhances immune injury in the neckline exposure pattern.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed V sign of dermatomyositis finding raises concern for dermatomyositis because ultraviolet light enhances immune injury in the neckline exposure pattern. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; V sign of dermatomyositis does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use V sign of dermatomyositis to focus evaluation of dermatomyositis and the reviewed alternatives: contact dermatitis, polymorphous light eruption, sunburn. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the anterior neck and upper chest. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating V sign of dermatomyositis as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Clothing, fragrance, and sun exposure can create identical distribution. Therefore, V sign of dermatomyositis cannot confirm or exclude dermatomyositis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • contact dermatitis

  • polymorphous light eruption

  • sunburn

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as contact dermatitis, polymorphous light eruption, sunburn.

False Negative Situations

V sign of dermatomyositis may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of dermatomyositis.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not diagnose dermatomyositis without assessing muscle and systemic involvement.
