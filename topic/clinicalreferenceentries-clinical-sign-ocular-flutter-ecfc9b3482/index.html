Definition

Ocular flutter is an observed ocular motor sign. It is a clue to brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction. A positive finding means bursts of horizontal back-to-back saccades occur without pauses.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ocular flutter means bursts of horizontal back-to-back saccades occur without pauses.

Why It Matters

The observed Ocular flutter finding can signal brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction. Failure of omnipause-neuron inhibition permits repetitive horizontal saccades. Ocular flutter cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction

Assessment Method

Observe fixation and eye movements.

Positive Finding

Bursts of horizontal back-to-back saccades occur without pauses.

Mechanism

Failure of omnipause-neuron inhibition permits repetitive horizontal saccades.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Ocular flutter finding raises concern for brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction because failure of omnipause-neuron inhibition permits repetitive horizontal saccades. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ocular flutter does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ocular flutter to focus evaluation of brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: opsoclonus, nystagmus, square-wave jerks. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe fixation and eye movements. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ocular flutter as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Small-amplitude flutter may require eye-movement recording. Therefore, Ocular flutter cannot confirm or exclude brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • opsoclonus

  • nystagmus

  • square-wave jerks

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as opsoclonus, nystagmus, square-wave jerks.

False Negative Situations

Ocular flutter may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of brainstem-cerebellar saccadic network dysfunction.
