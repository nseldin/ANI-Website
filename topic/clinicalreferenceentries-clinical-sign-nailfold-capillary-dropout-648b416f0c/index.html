Definition

Nailfold capillary dropout is an observed microvascular sign. It is a clue to systemic sclerosis-spectrum microangiopathy. A positive finding means enlarged loops, hemorrhage, disorganization, and avascular areas are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Nailfold capillary dropout means enlarged loops, hemorrhage, disorganization, and avascular areas are present.

Why It Matters

The observed Nailfold capillary dropout finding can signal systemic sclerosis-spectrum microangiopathy. Endothelial injury causes capillary dilation, leakage, loss, and abnormal repair. Nailfold capillary dropout cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • systemic sclerosis-spectrum microangiopathy

Assessment Method

Examine periungual capillaries with dermoscopy or capillaroscopy.

Positive Finding

Enlarged loops, hemorrhage, disorganization, and avascular areas are present.

Mechanism

Endothelial injury causes capillary dilation, leakage, loss, and abnormal repair.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Nailfold capillary dropout finding raises concern for systemic sclerosis-spectrum microangiopathy because endothelial injury causes capillary dilation, leakage, loss, and abnormal repair. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nailfold capillary dropout does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Technique, trauma, smoking, and cold alter appearance. Therefore, Nailfold capillary dropout cannot confirm or exclude systemic sclerosis-spectrum microangiopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Negative Situations
