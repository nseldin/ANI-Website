Used To Treat

Acute bacterial skin and skin-structure infections caused by susceptible gram-positive organisms, including MRSA, using single- or two-dose IV regimens.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for dalbavancin in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Dalbavancin binds the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus of peptidoglycan precursors and inhibits transglycosylation and transpeptidation, preventing cell-wall polymerization and cross-linking. Its lipid side chain strengthens membrane association and antibacterial potency.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain cultures when indicated, confirm gram-positive skin-infection fit and source control, infuse at the ordered rate, and assess glycopeptide hypersensitivity.

  • Monitor infusion reaction, rash, diarrhea/C. difficile, liver tests, renal context, and whether the client can return if the infection worsens after a long-acting dose.

Key Labs

  • Cultures, CBC, liver tests, renal function, temperature/WBC, and wound/skin response.

Nclex Traps

  • A long half-life is convenient but unforgiving: dalbavancin is not a broad empiric substitute when drainage, gram-negative coverage, or close reassessment is needed.

Description

Dalbavancin is a very long-acting IV lipoglycopeptide antibiotic that binds D-Ala-D-Ala precursors and blocks gram-positive peptidoglycan polymerization and cross-linking in susceptible skin infections. Its lipophilic anchor and prolonged half-life allow one or two infusions, making correct diagnosis, cultures, allergy review, and follow-up especially important because the exposure cannot be quickly removed.
