Definition

Gegenhalten is an observed tone sign. It is a clue to frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia. A positive finding means resistance increases in proportion to the examiner's force and occurs in both directions.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gegenhalten means resistance increases in proportion to the examiner's force and occurs in both directions.

Why It Matters

The observed Gegenhalten finding can signal frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia. Frontal disinhibition produces involuntary opposition to imposed movement. Gegenhalten cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia

Assessment Method

Move a relaxed limb passively at different speeds while asking the patient not to help.

Positive Finding

Resistance increases in proportion to the examiner's force and occurs in both directions.

Mechanism

Frontal disinhibition produces involuntary opposition to imposed movement.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Gegenhalten finding raises concern for frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia because frontal disinhibition produces involuntary opposition to imposed movement. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gegenhalten does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Gegenhalten to focus evaluation of frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia and the reviewed alternatives: spasticity, Parkinson rigidity, guarding. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Move a relaxed limb passively at different speeds while asking the patient not to help. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Gegenhalten as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, fear, and poor instruction can create voluntary resistance. Therefore, Gegenhalten cannot confirm or exclude frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • spasticity

  • Parkinson rigidity

  • guarding

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as spasticity, Parkinson rigidity, guarding.

False Negative Situations

Gegenhalten may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of frontal lobe dysfunction or advanced dementia.
