Definition

Echopraxia is an observed imitation sign. It is a clue to catatonia or frontal disinhibition. A positive finding means actions are repeatedly imitated without instruction.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Echopraxia means actions are repeatedly imitated without instruction.

Why It Matters

The observed Echopraxia finding can signal catatonia or frontal disinhibition. Impaired frontal inhibition releases stimulus-bound motor imitation. Echopraxia cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonia or frontal disinhibition

Assessment Method

Observe whether the patient involuntarily copies examiner movements.

Positive Finding

Actions are repeatedly imitated without instruction.

Mechanism

Impaired frontal inhibition releases stimulus-bound motor imitation.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Echopraxia finding raises concern for catatonia or frontal disinhibition because impaired frontal inhibition releases stimulus-bound motor imitation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Echopraxia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Echopraxia to focus evaluation of catatonia or frontal disinhibition and the reviewed alternatives: tic disorder, utilization behavior, developmental mirroring. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe whether the patient involuntarily copies examiner movements. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Echopraxia as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Deliberate imitation and rapport behaviors can mimic it. Therefore, Echopraxia cannot confirm or exclude catatonia or frontal disinhibition by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tic disorder

  • utilization behavior

  • developmental mirroring

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tic disorder, utilization behavior, developmental mirroring.

False Negative Situations

Echopraxia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonia or frontal disinhibition.

Safety Precautions

Observe Echopraxia with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe whether the patient involuntarily copies examiner movements. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Use neutral movements and avoid humiliating demonstrations.

Record the exact Echopraxia observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Actions are repeatedly imitated without instruction. Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
