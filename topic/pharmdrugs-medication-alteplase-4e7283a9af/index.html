Used To Treat

Selected acute ischemic stroke, acute myocardial infarction, acute massive pulmonary embolism with hemodynamic instability, and catheter occlusion using a distinct low-dose Cathflo product protocol.

Mechanism

Fibrin binding changes alteplase conformation and accelerates conversion of clot-bound plasminogen to plasmin. Plasmin cleaves cross-linked fibrin and circulating fibrinogen, loosening thrombus structure. Plasma alteplase clears quickly, largely through the liver, but downstream fibrinolysis and impaired hemostasis persist. Reperfusion rescues viable ischemic tissue only while meaningful penumbra remains; later, infarcted microvasculature is fragile and more prone to hemorrhagic transformation. Plasmin also activates kallikrein-bradykinin pathways, helping explain orolingual angioedema, especially with ACE inhibitors.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe and fatal internal or intracranial hemorrhage is the defining hazard. Use only through an indication-specific protocol with rapid imaging and expert capability to manage bleeding. Activase stroke, AMI, and PE regimens differ, and Cathflo catheter clearance is not systemic thrombolysis. Minimize invasive procedures, control blood pressure, and stop the infusion for new severe headache, vomiting, acute hypertension, neurologic decline, airway swelling, or major bleeding.

Contraindications

  • For acute ischemic stroke: current intracranial or subarachnoid hemorrhage, active internal bleeding, recent intracranial/intraspinal surgery or serious head trauma, selected intracranial lesions, bleeding diathesis, and severe uncontrolled hypertension are label contraindications.

  • AMI and PE exclusions overlap but are not identical; confirm the exact indication and current protocol rather than reusing a stroke checklist.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document exact weight, last-known-well or symptom timing, neurologic or hemodynamic baseline, glucose, imaging, pressure, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, recent procedures, bleeding history, and puncture sites before infusion.

  • Use frequent neurologic, airway, blood-pressure, and bleeding assessments during and after therapy; avoid unnecessary punctures and follow post-treatment antithrombotic and imaging timing exactly.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, other thrombolytics, and drugs that impair hemostasis increase bleeding risk; timing rules are indication specific.

  • ACE inhibitors increase concern for bradykinin-mediated orolingual angioedema, so lip or tongue swelling is an airway emergency.

Key Labs

  • Glucose, CBC and platelets, PT/INR, aPTT, renal function, pregnancy testing when relevant, type and screen when indicated, and protocol-specific brain or vascular imaging.

  • Trend neurologic status, blood pressure, airway, puncture sites, urine/stool/emesis, hemoglobin, and follow-up imaging rather than relying on the infusion ending safely.

Nclex Traps

  • Alteplase converts plasminogen to plasmin; it is not an antiplatelet, an anticoagulant, or a mechanical thrombectomy device.

  • Do not reflexively give aspirin immediately after stroke alteplase. Follow protocol after hemorrhage reassessment and post-treatment imaging.

Description

Alteplase is recombinant human tissue-type plasminogen activator, a thrombolytic that dissolves fibrin-rich clot rather than preventing the next platelet from aggregating. It binds fibrin and converts nearby plasminogen to plasmin, which cuts the clot's structural mesh and can restore perfusion. The same process weakens hemostatic plugs anywhere in the body, so benefit depends on the diagnosis, time window, imaging, blood pressure, recent surgery or trauma, anticoagulant status, and bleeding risk. A stroke-like deficit must not be assumed ischemic: brain imaging must exclude hemorrhage because alteplase would amplify a bleeding stroke.

Adverse Effects

  • Bleeding or oozing at puncture sites, nausea, hypotension, fever, and reperfusion dysrhythmias in myocardial infarction.

  • Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, retroperitoneal or GI bleeding, hemopericardium, cholesterol embolization, orolingual angioedema, anaphylactoid reaction, and death.
