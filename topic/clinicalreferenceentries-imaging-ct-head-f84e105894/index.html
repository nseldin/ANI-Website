Summary

CT head (computed tomography head scan) is a high-yield Neurology diagnostic reference. Rapid head imaging for bleed, stroke triage, trauma, mass effect, and acute neurologic change.

Quick Answer

CT head (computed tomography head scan): Rapid head imaging for bleed, stroke triage, trauma, mass effect, and acute neurologic change. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    No acute finding

    Meaning

    No acute bleed, mass effect, large infarct, or fracture is seen on the scan; early ischemic stroke may still be invisible.

  • Label

    Positive / abnormal

    Meaning

    Hemorrhage, mass effect, fracture, hydrocephalus, edema, or evolving infarct changes the urgency and treatment pathway.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    New neuro deficit is still treated as urgent even if early imaging is negative.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Rapid head imaging for bleed, stroke triage, trauma, mass effect, and acute neurologic change.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess baseline neurologic status, seizure precautions when needed, bleeding/anticoagulant risk for invasive procedures, and MRI metal/implant restrictions.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend neuro checks, level of consciousness, pupils, motor/sensory findings, headache, seizure activity, and procedure-site complications.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Acute neuro deficit, seizure, decreased level of consciousness, sudden severe headache, signs of increased ICP, or CSF leak/meningitis symptoms.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • A small neurologic change can be the whole emergency.
