Contraindications

  • Active cardiac, pulmonary, renal, or hepatic disease and serious hypersensitivity; asthma or significant reactive-airway disease makes bronchospasm risk clinically decisive.

  • Do not substitute repeated carboprost for hemorrhage source control when tone becomes firm or blood loss continues despite adequate uterine contraction.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check asthma and pulmonary history before the emergency, ideally during hemorrhage-risk planning. During use, monitor oxygen saturation, work of breathing, lung sounds, blood pressure, temperature, uterine tone, and quantified loss.

  • Prepare antiemetic/antidiarrheal support as ordered but never let expected GI effects distract from shock, sepsis, or severe prostaglandin toxicity.

Interactions

  • Other oxytocic agents can produce additive uterine activity; combination use belongs inside a monitored hemorrhage protocol.

  • Bronchoactive or vasoactive medicines and underlying pulmonary/cardiovascular disease can change the severity of systemic prostaglandin effects.

Key Labs

  • Hemorrhage CBC, platelets, fibrinogen, coagulation studies and type/cross; renal, liver, blood gas/lactate or infection studies as the clinical picture requires

  • Uterine tone, quantified blood loss, oxygenation and lung exam, temperature trajectory, blood pressure, perfusion, urine output, and response after each 250-mcg dose

Nclex Traps

  • Carboprost and asthma are a dangerous pairing because FP/prostanoid signaling can constrict bronchial smooth muscle.

  • Diarrhea and fever can be medication effects, but neither explains persistent hemodynamic instability or worsening hypoxemia without further assessment.

Used To Treat

Refractory postpartum hemorrhage caused by uterine atony that has not responded to conventional management, and selected termination of pregnancy under its product-specific obstetric indication and specialist protocol.

Mechanism

Carboprost binds prostanoid FP receptors on myometrial cells. FP receptors couple mainly to Gq/11, activating phospholipase C, generating inositol trisphosphate and diacylglycerol, releasing intracellular calcium, and activating calcium-calmodulin-dependent myosin-light-chain kinase. The uterus develops strong coordinated or sustained contractions that compress its vascular bed. The drug can also activate prostanoid signaling in bronchial, vascular, and gastrointestinal smooth muscle. Bronchial calcium signaling narrows airways; intestinal smooth-muscle and secretory effects produce cramping, diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting; hypothalamic prostaglandin effects can produce fever. Because it acts downstream at smooth muscle, it cannot correct hemorrhage caused primarily by laceration, retained placenta, uterine inversion, rupture, or coagulation failure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Carboprost should be administered only by medically trained personnel in a hospital able to provide immediate intensive and surgical care. Active cardiac, pulmonary, renal, or hepatic disease is a labeled contraindication, and asthma or prior bronchospasm requires particularly cautious avoidance or specialist judgment because severe bronchoconstriction and oxygenation failure can occur. Fever is common enough to confuse the picture, but persistent fever, hypoxemia, hypotension, altered mental status, or disproportionate pain still requires evaluation for infection, embolic disease, ongoing shock, or another obstetric emergency.

Description

Carboprost is a long-acting prostaglandin F2-alpha analogue that activates FP receptors, raises myometrial calcium, and forcefully contracts the gravid uterus to compress placental-bed vessels. In postpartum hemorrhage it is used when uterine atony persists despite massage, oxytocin, and other appropriate measures. The 15-methyl substitution slows metabolic inactivation compared with endogenous PGF2-alpha, extending effect. Prostaglandin receptors also exist in bronchi and gastrointestinal smooth muscle, which is why bronchospasm, diarrhea, vomiting, fever, and hypertension can accompany the desired uterine action. Asthma is therefore a mechanism-linked danger, not a trivia-list contraindication.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramping, fever, chills, flushing, headache, and transient blood-pressure change

  • Severe bronchospasm, hypoxemia, pulmonary edema, hypertension or hypotension, uterine rupture, anaphylaxis, persistent fever, or cardiovascular collapse
