Contraindications

  • Clarify severe hypertension, ventricular tachycardia, severe peripheral/mesenteric ischemia, or profound bradycardia/low-output state before infusion unless emergency-directed.

  • Do not treat oral/nasal decongestant use as equivalent to IV vasopressor use.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor MAP, heart rate, rhythm, peripheral perfusion, chest pain, urine output, and IV site.

  • Expect possible reflex bradycardia; worsening perfusion despite higher BP is a danger sign.

  • Use central access when possible for vasopressor infusion and manage extravasation immediately per protocol.

Interactions

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, other vasopressors, ergot alkaloids, and oxytocic drugs can increase hypertension or ischemia risk.

  • Beta blockers may worsen reflex bradycardia or alter response.

Key Labs

  • Continuous blood pressure and ECG monitoring.

  • Urine output and perfusion markers.

  • Lactate/acid-base status in shock.

Nclex Traps

  • Phenylephrine is mostly alpha-1. It can raise the number on the monitor while cardiac output or tissue perfusion worsens.

  • Do not confuse IV phenylephrine with oral cold-product phenylephrine.

Boxed Warning

High-alert safety focus: severe hypertension, reflex bradycardia, reduced cardiac output, tissue ischemia, and extravasation injury.

Class

Alpha-1 adrenergic agonist; vasopressor and vasoconstrictor

Used To Treat

Selected hypotension or vasodilatory shock states, especially anesthesia/procedural hypotension or when tachydysrhythmia limits beta-agonist vasopressors. Non-IV products have separate nasal, ophthalmic, or hemorrhoid indications.

Mechanism

Selectively stimulates alpha-1 receptors, causing arterial and venous vasoconstriction. This raises blood pressure but can trigger reflex bradycardia and may reduce stroke volume/cardiac output in vulnerable clients.

Description

Phenylephrine is an alpha-1 adrenergic agonist and vasopressor and vasoconstrictor: it selectively stimulates alpha-1 receptors, causing arterial and venous vasoconstriction. It is used for selected hypotension or vasodilatory shock states, especially anesthesia/procedural hypotension or when tachydysrhythmia limits beta-agonist vasopressors. Non-IV products have separate nasal, ophthalmic, or hemorrhoid indications.
