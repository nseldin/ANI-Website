Description

Parenteral direct thrombin inhibitors are non-heparin anticoagulants that bind thrombin itself, blocking both fibrin formation and thrombin-driven platelet activation. Bivalirudin is prominent during PCI; argatroban is a core option for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.

Used To Treat

Anticoagulation during selected percutaneous coronary intervention and treatment or prevention of thrombosis in heparin-induced thrombocytopenia when a non-heparin agent is required.

Mechanism

These drugs inhibit thrombin directly rather than requiring antithrombin. Bivalirudin binds thrombin's active site and exosite and has partial renal clearance; argatroban reversibly occupies the active site and is hepatically cleared, so kidney-versus-liver dysfunction changes agent selection and dosing.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor bleeding, access sites, hemoglobin, platelets, aPTT or ACT by protocol, renal/hepatic function, infusion accuracy, and transition plans; argatroban also raises INR and complicates warfarin overlap interpretation.

Key Labs

  • aPTT or ACT by indication, CBC/hemoglobin, platelets, renal function for bivalirudin, liver function for argatroban, and INR during argatroban-warfarin transition.

Nclex Traps

  • Direct thrombin inhibitors are not heparin and do not use a vitamin K-style reversal pathway; match bivalirudin versus argatroban to procedure, HIT, and organ clearance.
