Used To Treat

FDA-labeled prevention of NSAID-induced gastric ulcers in high-risk patients; protocol-based reproductive uses include cervical ripening, labor induction, medication abortion with mifepristone, management of pregnancy loss, and postpartum hemorrhage depending on dose, route, gestational age, uterine scar, and guideline.

Mechanism

Misoprostol is rapidly de-esterified to misoprostol acid. In gastric parietal cells, EP3 receptor activation couples to Gi, lowers adenylyl cyclase and cAMP, and reduces histamine-stimulated proton-pump acid secretion. In surface epithelial cells, prostaglandin signaling increases mucus and bicarbonate and supports mucosal perfusion. In cervix, EP-receptor signaling increases matrix metalloproteinases, collagen disorganization, glycosaminoglycans, and tissue hydration, producing softening and dilation. In myometrium, receptor subtype balance and gestational receptor expression increase intracellular calcium and contractile coupling. Pregnancy advances and uterine scarring reduce the margin for excessive contraction, so a dose that is appropriate for one gestational indication cannot be copied into another.

Boxed Warning

U.S. labeling warns that misoprostol administered during pregnancy can cause abortion, premature birth, congenital injury after ongoing exposure, or uterine rupture. Rupture risk increases with advancing gestational age and prior uterine surgery, including cesarean delivery. It must not be used for NSAID-ulcer prevention in a pregnant patient. Reproductive use requires the exact evidence-based protocol, informed counseling, and access to emergency care. Severe or prolonged bleeding, syncope, persistent fever, respiratory or hemodynamic compromise, or pain that does not follow the expected trajectory requires urgent evaluation rather than reassurance that cramping is normal.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy when the indication is NSAID-ulcer prevention, prostaglandin hypersensitivity, and any obstetric situation in which the selected protocol, route, or uterine contraction is unsafe.

  • Prior uterine surgery, gestational age, placenta and presentation, ectopic risk, hemorrhage risk, and access to urgent care must be evaluated before reproductive use.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm indication out loud before administration because the same drug has opposite desired outcomes across GI and obstetric care. Verify microgram dose, route, gestational context, and protocol.

  • Teach expected timing and effects plus explicit emergency thresholds. Persistent severe pain after medication abortion can signal ectopic pregnancy or infection even when bleeding seems limited.

Interactions

  • Other uterotonics and prostaglandins can add tachysystole; sequence and waiting intervals are protocol-specific.

  • Magnesium-containing antacids can worsen misoprostol-associated diarrhea; NSAID coadministration is expected for the labeled ulcer-prevention purpose.

Key Labs

  • For GI use, monitor clinical ulcer risk and bleeding; for reproductive use, assess pregnancy location/age, hemoglobin and Rh status when indicated, and follow-up completion

  • Contractions and fetal status during viable pregnancy, bleeding quantity, pain, temperature, vital signs, uterine tone postpartum, hydration, and signs of infection or rupture

Nclex Traps

  • Misoprostol protects the stomach by replacing prostaglandin effects lost to NSAID inhibition; its diarrhea and cramping are extensions of prostaglandin physiology.

  • A previous cesarean matters because powerful contractions stress scarred myometrium, and rupture risk rises with gestational age and cumulative uterotonic exposure.

Description

Misoprostol is a prostaglandin E1 (PGE1) analogue whose active metabolite activates EP receptors: it lowers parietal-cell acid output and strengthens mucus, bicarbonate, and mucosal blood-flow defenses in the stomach while remodeling the cervix and increasing uterine contraction in reproductive tissue. It therefore replaces prostaglandin activity suppressed by NSAIDs for ulcer prevention but deliberately uses the same signaling family for cervical ripening, pregnancy evacuation, labor, or postpartum uterine contraction. That dual identity explains why one tablet can protect gastric mucosa in one context yet cause abortion, labor, hemorrhage treatment, or uterine rupture in another. Dose, route, gestational age, prior uterine surgery, and intended protocol are therefore part of the drug identity rather than optional details.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, headache, dizziness, fever, chills, shivering, and uterine cramping

  • Uterine tachysystole, fetal heart-rate compromise, uterine rupture, hemorrhage, incomplete abortion, serious infection, amniotic-fluid embolism syndrome, or fetal malformation after failed pregnancy exposure
