Class

Oral AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3 kinase inhibitor

Description

Capivasertib blocks AKT, a central kinase in the PI3K-AKT-PTEN growth and survival pathway. Tumors with activating PIK3CA or AKT1 alterations or loss of PTEN can depend on this signaling. Blocking AKT can restore control of growth signals when endocrine or androgen-pathway therapy alone is insufficient, but AKT also regulates normal glucose handling, intestinal cells, and skin. On-target pathway inhibition therefore explains the characteristic hyperglycemia, diarrhea, and rash.

Mechanism

Capivasertib inhibits all three AKT isoforms and reduces phosphorylation of downstream proteins that promote cell survival, metabolism, and proliferation. In a susceptible tumor, this weakens signaling driven by PIK3CA or AKT1 activation or PTEN loss. In normal tissue, reduced AKT signaling decreases insulin-mediated glucose uptake and increases hepatic glucose output, producing hyperglycemia; effects on rapidly renewing gut and skin tissue contribute to diarrhea and cutaneous reactions.

Used To Treat

In combination with fulvestrant for selected HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer with PIK3CA, AKT1, or PTEN alteration after endocrine progression. The June 2026 label also includes capivasertib with abiraterone and prednisone for biomarker-selected PTEN-deficient metastatic androgen-pathway-modulation-naive or -sensitive prostate cancer.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. Severe hyperglycemia including ketoacidosis, severe diarrhea with dehydration, serious cutaneous reactions, embryo-fetal toxicity, and major CYP3A interactions are the leading hazards.

Contraindications

  • Severe hypersensitivity to capivasertib or a component.

  • Do not begin without the indication-specific biomarker and companion regimen. Stabilize uncontrolled glucose and assess severe diarrhea or active serious skin disease before treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, cutaneous reactions, nausea, fatigue, vomiting, stomatitis, decreased appetite, headache, and hyperglycemia.

  • Severe hyperglycemia or ketoacidosis, dehydration and kidney injury from diarrhea, erythema multiforme or other severe skin reaction, infection complications, and embryo-fetal harm.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong CYP3A inhibitors when possible; if unavoidable, use the label-defined reduction. Moderate CYP3A inhibitors also require dose reduction.

  • Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A inducers because reduced exposure can cause treatment failure. Avoid grapefruit products because they can increase CYP3A-mediated exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check fasting glucose and HbA1c before therapy, optimize diabetes, and monitor glucose frequently early in treatment and during illness because ketoacidosis can occur even when diabetes was not previously diagnosed.

  • Assess diarrhea immediately, begin ordered antidiarrheal and hydration measures, and hold or reduce treatment by severity before dehydration becomes kidney injury.

  • Inspect skin and mucosa and escalate spreading rash, blistering, fever, facial edema, or target lesions because severe cutaneous reactions require prompt interruption and evaluation.

Key Labs

  • Fasting glucose and HbA1c

  • Electrolytes, creatinine, hydration status during diarrhea

  • Liver tests and CBC as directed by the regimen

  • Pregnancy test and disease-directed imaging

Administration

  • Take twice daily about 12 hours apart for 4 consecutive days followed by 3 days off, with or without food. Swallow tablets whole.

  • If a dose is more than 4 hours late or vomiting occurs after a dose, do not take an extra dose; resume at the next scheduled time.

Patient Education

  • Report thirst, frequent urination, rapid breathing, confusion, persistent diarrhea, inability to drink, or any blistering or painful rash immediately.

  • Use effective contraception, avoid grapefruit, and keep glucose and oncology monitoring even when symptoms are mild.

Nclex Traps

  • Capivasertib can cause severe hyperglycemia and ketoacidosis even without preexisting diabetes; polyuria, thirst, nausea, rapid breathing, or confusion needs urgent metabolic assessment.

  • Do not improvise missed-dose replacement or convert its intermittent schedule into daily dosing.

Routes

  • Oral tablet administered on the labeled intermittent schedule with the required companion anticancer regimen.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Severe hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state.

  • Severe diarrhea with dehydration or acute kidney injury.

  • Severe cutaneous adverse reaction, including erythema multiforme or drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms, and embryo-fetal toxicity.

Toxicity Management

  • No specific antidote exists. Hold capivasertib, obtain urgent glucose, ketone, electrolyte, renal, hydration, and skin assessment, and provide protocol-directed metabolic, fluid, dermatologic, or emergency treatment.

Special Populations

  • Capivasertib can cause fetal harm; use label-directed contraception and avoid breastfeeding during and after treatment for the specified interval.

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established. Older adults may have greater dehydration, metabolic, renal, and skin vulnerability.

  • Evidence is limited in severe renal or hepatic impairment; follow current labeling and avoid unreviewed dose extrapolation.
