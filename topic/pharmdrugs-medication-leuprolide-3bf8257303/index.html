Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity to GnRH or GnRH analogues, and product-specific restrictions; undiagnosed vaginal bleeding needs evaluation before gynecologic suppression.

  • Assess spinal metastasis, obstruction, bone density, mood, seizure, diabetes and cardiovascular/QT risk before long-term treatment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify exact depot name, dose, route and interval. Record the next due date and do not substitute a one-, three-, four- or six-month product by volume.

  • Teach flare timing and emergency symptoms. For endometriosis, explain add-back norethindrone as protection against bone and vasomotor toxicity rather than evidence the agonist stopped working.

Interactions

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs and electrolyte disturbances can compound androgen-deprivation arrhythmia risk.

  • Other bone-depleting drugs, glucocorticoids, smoking and alcohol increase skeletal harm; antidiabetic therapy may need adjustment as glucose rises.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test when relevant, testosterone or estradiol, PSA or disease-specific marker, glucose/A1c, lipids, electrolytes, bone-density assessment, and growth/bone age in pediatric use

  • Flare symptoms, neurologic and urinary status, bleeding/pain response, hot flashes, mood, sexual effects, injection site, fracture risk, and depot adherence

Nclex Traps

  • Continuous GnRH agonism suppresses LH/FSH only after an initial stimulatory flare; an agonist is not always a permanently activating drug.

  • Leuprolide depot does not provide reliable contraception during the flare, and fertility can recover after suppression ends.

Used To Treat

Product-specific treatment of advanced prostate cancer, endometriosis, uterine fibroids before surgery with iron therapy, and central precocious puberty; it is also used in specialist fertility protocols for pituitary suppression or as a trigger in selected antagonist cycles.

Mechanism

Leuprolide binds pituitary GnRH receptors, which normally respond to hypothalamic pulses through Gq/11, phospholipase C, inositol trisphosphate, calcium, and LH/FSH exocytosis. Initial continuous occupancy increases gonadotropins and gonadal steroids. Persistent exposure removes the normal recovery interval, uncouples receptor signaling, promotes receptor internalization, and reduces receptor expression. This functional receptor downregulation makes LH and FSH fall, decreasing Leydig-cell testosterone and ovarian estradiol/progesterone. Hormone-dependent tissue slows, but reduced sex-steroid signaling also increases osteoclast activity, changes thermoregulation and sexual function, and alters glucose, lipids, and cardiovascular physiology. In a short fertility trigger, the initial endogenous LH/FSH surge is intentionally used before desensitization develops.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Leuprolide products have no shared current U.S. boxed warning, but tumor flare during the first weeks can worsen bone pain, hematuria, bladder or ureteral obstruction, neuropathy, or spinal-cord compression. High-risk cancer patients require a flare-management plan and urgent evaluation for weakness, numbness, urinary retention, or severe new pain. Long-term use can reduce bone density, worsen glucose and cardiovascular risk, prolong QT in susceptible androgen-deprivation settings, cause severe mood symptoms or seizures, and rarely precipitate pituitary apoplexy. Pregnancy must be excluded for reproductive-age suppression use; depot formulations are not contraceptives.

  • Reproductive endocrine medication

  • GnRH agonist

Description

Leuprolide is a long-acting GnRH-receptor agonist that initially activates pituitary LH/FSH release, then desensitizes and downregulates the continuously stimulated receptor to suppress testosterone or estradiol. This flare-then-suppression sequence explains both benefit and early hazard. Prostate-cancer bone pain or obstruction and endometriosis bleeding or pain may transiently worsen before sex steroids fall. Sustained hypoestrogenism or androgen deprivation causes hot flashes, sexual dysfunction, mood and metabolic changes, and bone loss. Depot products differ in dose, release system, interval, route, and indication and cannot be exchanged because both contain leuprolide.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, sweating, headache, mood change, decreased libido, vaginal dryness or bleeding, erectile dysfunction, injection-site reaction, and initial symptom flare

  • Spinal-cord compression or urinary obstruction from tumor flare, bone loss/fracture, hyperglycemia, cardiovascular event, QT-related arrhythmia, severe depression, seizure, or pituitary apoplexy
