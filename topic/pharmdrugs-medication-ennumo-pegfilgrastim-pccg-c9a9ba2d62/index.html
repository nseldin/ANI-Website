Used To Treat

Prevention of febrile neutropenia in labeled myelosuppressive-chemotherapy settings and increased survival after myelosuppressive radiation exposure in adults of any weight and pediatric patients weighing at least 45 kg. The H-ARS indication is marrow rescue, not treatment of radioactive material inside the body.

Description

Ennumo (pegfilgrastim-pccg) is a long-acting, pegylated G-CSF biosimilar that binds the G-CSF receptor and expands neutrophil precursors to support recovery after chemotherapy or marrow-damaging radiation. FDA approved on May 7, 2026 and biosimilar to Neulasta, it still has product-specific presentation and H-ARS dosing boundaries, so an order cannot be inferred from the word pegfilgrastim alone. In H-ARS it is started as soon as possible after a suspected or confirmed myelosuppressive dose greater than 2 Gy because marrow progenitors are injured before the blood-count nadir becomes visible. It supports neutrophil recovery but does not remove radionuclides, replace antimicrobial care, or repair gastrointestinal, skin, neurovascular, or pulmonary radiation injury.

Mechanism

Pegfilgrastim-pccg binds the G-CSF receptor on neutrophil precursors and drives proliferation, differentiation, and release of functional neutrophils. Covalent attachment of polyethylene glycol reduces renal clearance, so exposure persists longer than filgrastim and permits an H-ARS course based on two separated doses rather than daily dosing. Earlier stimulation matters because surviving progenitors need time to expand before infection risk peaks. A rising ANC shows a marrow response, but it does not kill microbes or prove that sepsis has resolved.

Administration Timing

  • For credible H-ARS, begin as soon as possible after suspected or confirmed exposure above 2 Gy; do not wait for neutropenia to become profound because the biologic injury precedes the CBC nadir.

  • For adults of any weight and pediatric patients weighing at least 45 kg, the H-ARS label uses two 6 mg subcutaneous doses one week apart.

  • Do not administer ENNUMO for H-ARS to a pediatric patient below 45 kg: no ENNUMO presentation permits accurate weight-based dosing below that boundary. Select a different label-supported countermeasure through the radiation-emergency protocol rather than improvising a partial syringe dose.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify ENNUMO, the -pccg suffix, indication, weight, presentation, dose, and incident schedule because biosimilar products and delivery systems are not automatically interchangeable.

  • Trend CBC with differential and temperature through nadir and recovery because ANC improvement can coexist with invasive infection.

  • Assess left-upper-quadrant or shoulder pain, hypoxemia, edema, hypotension, urine changes, fever, and focal vascular pain because splenic rupture, ARDS, capillary leak, glomerulonephritis, and aortitis require urgent evaluation.

Key Labs

  • Obtain a baseline and serial CBC with differential and platelets because trends reveal lineage injury, the nadir, and marrow recovery. Exposure estimates and biodosimetry guide the incident response, but treatment should not be delayed when they are unavailable because cellular injury precedes the visible blood-count nadir.

  • Creatinine, urinalysis, oxygenation, fluid balance, cultures, and organ-specific studies when symptoms suggest G-CSF toxicity, infection, or multi-organ radiation injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Bone and extremity pain, leukocytosis, and injection-site symptoms can occur because marrow production is accelerated.

  • Rare serious effects include splenic rupture, ARDS, anaphylaxis, sickle-cell crisis, glomerulonephritis, thrombocytopenia, capillary-leak syndrome, and aortitis.

Contraindications

  • A history of serious allergic reaction to pegfilgrastim, filgrastim, or product components is a labeled contraindication.

  • Do not substitute this card for radiation triage, decorporation, antimicrobial therapy, transfusion support, or management of nonhematopoietic radiation syndromes.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Dyspnea, new infiltrates, hypoxemia, left-upper-quadrant pain, shock, facial swelling, diffuse rash, oliguria, or painful large-vessel inflammation requires immediate clinical escalation.

  • Persistent fever, falling counts, bleeding, or worsening organ dysfunction despite G-CSF requires hematology, infectious-disease, and radiation-emergency reassessment because H-ARS is not a neutrophil-only illness.

Interactions

  • Cytotoxic chemotherapy can injure the proliferating progenitors the drug stimulates; keep oncology timing rules separate from the emergency H-ARS regimen.

  • Other colony-stimulating factors and lithium may add to leukocyte stimulation, while transfusions and antimicrobials complement rather than replace marrow support.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Pediatric patients below 45 kg

    Note

    The H-ARS indication and available ENNUMO presentations do not permit dosing in this group. Do not calculate or improvise a smaller ENNUMO dose; use an alternative label-supported countermeasure through the incident protocol.

  • Population

    Sickle-cell disorders

    Note

    Rapid myeloid stimulation can precipitate a severe or fatal sickle-cell crisis, so pain, hypoxemia, and hemolytic findings require urgent evaluation.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, older age, or limited marrow reserve

    Note

    Human H-ARS effectiveness data are necessarily limited; the credible lethality of exposure and individual organ reserve guide emergency use.

Evidence Limitations

  • H-ARS effectiveness was established under the FDA Animal Rule because deliberate lethal-radiation efficacy trials in humans are unethical.

  • Biosimilarity does not make every pegfilgrastim presentation or label operationally interchangeable; verify the exact product.

Nclex Traps

  • Ennumo is pegfilgrastim-pccg, not generic pegfilgrastim without a suffix.

  • H-ARS dosing starts before the ANC nadir; waiting for severe neutropenia loses time.

  • G-CSF improves neutrophil production but neither removes radionuclides nor treats sepsis directly.

Regulatory Status

FDA approved May 7, 2026 for labeled oncology use and to increase survival after myelosuppressive radiation exposure; the H-ARS indication is supported under the Animal Rule.

Boxed Warning

PRESENTATION GAP: no ENNUMO presentation permits H-ARS weight-based dosing in a pediatric patient below 45 kg. Do not improvise a partial dose because inaccurate delivery can underdose a lethal exposure or cause a dosing error. Confirm product and eligibility, and monitor for serious splenic, pulmonary, allergic, renal, vascular, and capillary-leak complications because these can deteriorate rapidly or resemble the underlying radiation illness.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The warning states the product-specific contraindication, medication-error hazard, or emergency-response limit together with the action needed to reduce harm.
