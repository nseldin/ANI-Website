Definition

Cherry-red spot of the macula is an observed funduscopic sign. It is a clue to retinal whitening around preserved foveal color. A positive finding means a bright red fovea contrasts with pale surrounding retina.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cherry-red spot of the macula means a bright red fovea contrasts with pale surrounding retina.

Why It Matters

The observed Cherry-red spot of the macula finding can signal retinal whitening around preserved foveal color. Opaque ischemic or storage-laden inner retina surrounds the thin fovea, where underlying choroidal color remains visible. Cherry-red spot of the macula cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • retinal whitening around preserved foveal color

Assessment Method

Examine the macula through dilated fundus examination or retinal imaging.

Positive Finding

A bright red fovea contrasts with pale surrounding retina.

Mechanism

Opaque ischemic or storage-laden inner retina surrounds the thin fovea, where underlying choroidal color remains visible.
