Definition

Melena is an observed gastrointestinal bleeding sign. It is a clue to digested blood in stool, usually from upper gastrointestinal bleeding but sometimes from a slower small-bowel or right-colon source. A positive finding means the stool is black, tarry, sticky, and characteristically strong smelling rather than simply dark.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Melena means the stool is black, tarry, sticky, and characteristically strong smelling rather than simply dark.

Why It Matters

A positive Melena result can signal digested blood in stool, usually from upper gastrointestinal bleeding but sometimes from a slower small-bowel or right-colon source. Blood exposed to gastric acid, digestive enzymes, and intestinal bacteria is broken down during transit, turning the stool black and tar-like. Melena cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Evidence Limitations

Iron, bismuth, some foods, and swallowed blood can darken stool; rapid upper bleeding may instead appear maroon or red. Therefore, Melena cannot confirm or exclude digested blood in stool, usually from upper gastrointestinal bleeding but sometimes from a slower small-bowel or right-colon source by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • iron or bismuth discoloration

  • right-colon bleeding

  • swallowed blood

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as iron or bismuth discoloration, right-colon bleeding, swallowed blood.

False Negative Situations

Melena may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Melena, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask about stool appearance and timing, inspect a sample when available, assess vital signs and perfusion, and test for blood when clinically indicated. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Treat melena with dizziness, syncope, tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, chest pain, or ongoing bleeding as an emergency; quantify trends and do not document every dark stool as confirmed melena without description.
