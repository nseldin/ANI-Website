Definition

Petechiae is an observed skin and mucosal bleeding sign. It is a clue to tiny hemorrhages from capillaries or small vessels caused by platelet disorders, vascular injury, infection, trauma, or coagulation abnormalities. A positive finding means multiple pinpoint flat red, purple, or brown spots remain visible when pressed because blood is outside the vessels.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Petechiae means multiple pinpoint flat red, purple, or brown spots remain visible when pressed because blood is outside the vessels.

Why It Matters

The observed Petechiae finding can signal tiny hemorrhages from capillaries or small vessels caused by platelet disorders, vascular injury, infection, trauma, or coagulation abnormalities. Red blood cells leak through injured small vessels or escape when platelet plugs and hemostasis cannot contain microscopic bleeding, leaving tiny nonblanching spots in tissue. Petechiae cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

  • tiny hemorrhages from capillaries or small vessels caused by platelet disorders, vascular injury, infection, trauma, or coagulation abnormalities

Assessment Method

Inspect skin and mucosa in good light, note distribution and progression, and apply gentle pressure or a transparent surface to assess blanching without repeatedly traumatizing the area.

Evidence Limitations

Appearance varies with skin tone, age, lighting, and lesion stage; a visual pattern does not identify the cause or measure platelet function. Therefore, Petechiae cannot confirm or exclude tiny hemorrhages from capillaries or small vessels caused by platelet disorders, vascular injury, infection, trauma, or coagulation abnormalities by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as insect bites, folliculitis, cherry angiomas, pressure marks.

False Negative Situations

Petechiae may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of tiny hemorrhages from capillaries or small vessels caused by platelet disorders, vascular injury, infection, trauma, or coagulation abnormalities.

Modern Status

Current morphology that can be benign after pressure or trauma but may signal thrombocytopenia, sepsis, vasculitis, or another serious bleeding disorder when widespread or accompanied by illness. Observe Petechiae with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect skin and mucosa in good light, note distribution and progression, and apply gentle pressure or a transparent surface to assess blanching without repeatedly traumatizing the area. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Fever, toxicity, rapid spread, mucosal bleeding, hypotension, severe headache, or neurologic change with petechiae requires urgent evaluation; do not dismiss a nonblanching rash as a simple allergy.
