Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric ages, reduction of heart-failure hospitalization in adults with NYHA class II-IV heart failure, and reduction of cardiovascular mortality in clinically stable adults with left-ventricular failure or dysfunction after myocardial infarction according to labeling.

Mechanism

Valsartan competitively and selectively blocks AT1 receptors without requiring metabolic activation. This interrupts angiotensin-II-driven Gq and intracellular-calcium vasoconstriction, adrenal aldosterone release, renal sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, hypertrophy, fibrosis, and adverse ventricular remodeling. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers glomerular capillary pressure, which can reduce albumin leakage but can lower GFR during poor renal inflow. Reduced aldosterone decreases collecting-duct potassium secretion. Valsartan does not inhibit ACE or directly accumulate bradykinin, explaining less cough than an ACE inhibitor. In post-MI and heart failure care, lower afterload and remodeling signaling reduce future events rather than acting as an immediate inotrope or decongestant.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Valsartan carries fetal toxicity whether given alone or inside sacubitril/valsartan. Ingredient recognition matters during medication reconciliation.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes.

  • A separate ARB should not be added to sacubitril/valsartan because the combination already contains valsartan.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • In heart failure or post-MI use, trend pressure, perfusion, congestion, potassium, creatinine, urine output, and outcome trajectory rather than judging success from pressure alone.

  • Expand brand names: Entresto supplies valsartan; Diovan is valsartan; Exforge and other combinations may also contain it.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Interactions

  • Sacubitril/valsartan or other valsartan combinations create hidden duplicate exposure; ACE inhibitor, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and potassium interactions require review.

  • MRAs can be appropriate in HFrEF but add potassium risk, so evidence-based combination therapy still needs laboratory surveillance.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, urine output, weight/congestion, and post-MI or heart-failure perfusion.

  • No routine valsartan level is used; clinical and laboratory tolerance guide titration.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Valsartan is an ARB component of Entresto. Do not add another ARB because the brand name hides the duplicate ingredient.

  • It reduces heart-failure hospitalization and post-MI mortality in labeled populations through remodeling and hemodynamic effects, not by directly increasing contractility.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Valsartan is an active, nonprodrug angiotensin-II receptor blocker that selectively prevents AT1 signaling. It relaxes arterioles, reduces aldosterone-mediated sodium retention, lowers intraglomerular pressure, and reduces maladaptive myocardial remodeling without directly inhibiting bradykinin breakdown. Its identity is strongly cardiovascular: besides hypertension, current labeling includes heart failure and clinically stable left-ventricular failure or dysfunction after MI. Valsartan is also the ARB component of sacubitril/valsartan, so a medication list containing Entresto already contains valsartan. Adding standalone valsartan would duplicate AT1 blockade. In heart failure or after MI, a modest pressure fall may be expected, but worsening mentation, urine output, renal function, potassium, or shock means the patient is not tolerating the hemodynamic effect.

Adverse Effects

  • Heart-failure patients may develop dizziness, hypotension, renal-function change, or hyperkalemia, especially with diuretics or mineralocorticoid antagonists.

  • Post-MI hypotension, oliguria, progressive azotemia, or severe potassium elevation requires prompt reassessment of perfusion and interacting therapy.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
