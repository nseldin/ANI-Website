Class

Iron-based phosphate binder and absorbable ferric iron replacement product

Used To Treat

Control of serum phosphorus in adults with CKD on dialysis; treatment of iron-deficiency anemia in adults with CKD not on dialysis. Dose and monitoring differ by indication.

Description

Ferric citrate is both a GI phosphate binder and an absorbable iron source used to treat iron deficiency anemia in adults with nondialysis CKD. Ferric iron binds dietary phosphate into poorly absorbed complexes for fecal elimination, while a portion of iron is absorbed and can replenish stores. That dual action is useful but can cause iron overload, especially when IV or other oral iron continues. The card must therefore be read as two indications with different endpoints, not as a generic renal supplement.

Mechanism

In the intestinal lumen, ferric iron exchanges citrate/water ligands for phosphate and forms insoluble ferric-phosphate complexes. Unbound ferric iron can be reduced/transported into enterocytes and incorporated into transferrin and hemoglobin. Binding lowers serum phosphorus; absorption raises ferritin, transferrin saturation and hemoglobin. The same chemistry chelates selected oral drugs.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in iron-overload syndromes. Assess ferritin and transferrin saturation before and during therapy; reduce other iron sources when stores rise. Accidental iron overdose is a leading poisoning hazard in children, so store securely. Dark stool is expected but can obscure GI bleeding.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, constipation, nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain

  • Dark stool and possible tooth/oral discoloration if tablets are crushed or chewed

  • Iron overload with high ferritin/transferrin saturation

  • Potential infection/oxidative concerns when iron stores are excessive

Contraindications

  • Hemochromatosis or other iron-overload syndromes

  • Do not use as an unmonitored additive to IV/oral iron

  • Clarify active GI disease and serious hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • For phosphate control, give with meals and titrate phosphorus; for anemia, titrate hemoglobin/iron indices. The endpoint identifies the indication.

  • Swallow tablets whole and secure them from children.

Interactions

  • Separate doxycycline and ciprofloxacin according to labeling because iron chelation reduces absorption.

  • Concomitant IV/oral iron increases overload risk.

  • Review other narrow-index oral drugs for binding and monitor response.

Key Labs

  • Serum phosphorus, calcium and PTH

  • Ferritin, transferrin saturation, hemoglobin and CBC

  • Liver function and infection context when iron overload is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Ferric citrate is not only a phosphate binder; absorbed iron changes anemia and overload risk.

  • Dark stool can be expected but does not rule out bleeding.

  • The dialysis phosphate indication and nondialysis anemia indication use different goals.
