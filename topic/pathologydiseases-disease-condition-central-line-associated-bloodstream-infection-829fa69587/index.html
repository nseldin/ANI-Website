Definition

Central line-associated bloodstream infection develops when microorganisms enter through the insertion site, catheter hub, infusate, or hematogenous seeding and form an intravascular biofilm. The characteristic pattern is fever, chills, hypotension, new organ dysfunction, line erythema or drainage, or rigors during infusion without another clear source. Confirm or stage it with paired peripheral and catheter blood cultures before antibiotics when this does not delay sepsis care; distinguish it from contaminated culture, another sepsis source, infusion reaction, catheter thrombosis, and local exit-site infection by culture pattern and examination. The priority is to begin empiric antimicrobial and sepsis care after cultures because septic shock can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection. can follow if the process advances.

Pathophysiology

Inflammation is present, often from infection, autoimmune injury, obstruction, or tissue damage depending on the condition.

Signs Symptoms

  • fever may be present but older adults/newborns may be atypical

  • localized pain/swelling/drainage when tissue infection is present

Diagnostics

  • paired peripheral and catheter blood cultures before antibiotics when this does not delay sepsis care

  • CBC, lactate, organ tests, and examination for metastatic infection

  • echocardiography or site imaging for persistent bacteremia, thrombosis, or endocarditis risk

  • Distinguish Central line-associated bloodstream infection from contaminated culture, another sepsis source, infusion reaction, catheter thrombosis, and local exit-site infection by culture pattern and examination.

Labs

  • lactate may rise with shock

  • glucose can be immediately reversible

  • potassium and acid-base shifts can kill quickly

  • drug levels matter when available

Treatments

  • begin empiric antimicrobial and sepsis care after cultures

  • remove the line when organism, instability, tunnel infection, or persistent bacteremia requires it

  • narrow therapy and define duration by organism and complications

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for hypotension, shaking rigors, new confusion, rising lactate, purulent drainage, tunnel tenderness, or persistent positive cultures because septic shock, endocarditis, or metastatic infection may be developing.

  • Assess the insertion site, dressing integrity, line necessity, and every lumen before access because breaks in the closed sterile system permit organisms to enter the bloodstream.

  • Perform hand hygiene, disinfect the connector for the required contact time, and use aseptic technique for every entry because hub contamination is a preventable route to CLABSI.

  • Obtain ordered peripheral and line blood cultures before antibiotics when this will not delay treatment because paired cultures help distinguish line infection from another source.

  • Administer prescribed antimicrobials promptly and coordinate line removal or salvage decisions with the treating and vascular-access teams because antibiotics alone may not clear an infected device or biofilm.

  • Trend temperature, rigors during infusion, blood pressure, lactate, mental status, urine output, culture clearance, and local erythema or drainage to detect sepsis and persistent bacteremia.

Complications

  • septic shock can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

  • endocarditis can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

  • septic thrombosis can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

  • osteomyelitis can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

  • emboli can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

  • recurrent bacteremia can complicate Central line-associated bloodstream infection.

Red Flags

  • Rigors or sudden instability during line infusion or flushing

  • Hypotension, rising lactate, new confusion, or oliguria

  • Purulence, spreading erythema, tunnel tenderness, or exposed catheter

  • Persistent bacteremia or fever despite appropriate treatment

Patient Education

  • Explain that a central line provides direct bloodstream access, so clean hands and keeping the dressing dry and intact are essential rather than cosmetic steps.

  • Teach the patient not to manipulate the hubs and to report fever, chills, drainage, pain, a loose dressing, or a change in catheter length promptly.

Nclex Traps

  • A normal insertion site does not exclude an intraluminal bloodstream infection

  • Do not culture every central line without a compatible clinical syndrome
