Definition

Weber test is a bedside tuning-fork test. It is a clue to asymmetric hearing loss. A positive finding means sound lateralizes to the poorer ear in conductive loss and the better ear in sensorineural loss.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Weber test means sound lateralizes to the poorer ear in conductive loss and the better ear in sensorineural loss.

Why It Matters

A positive Weber test result can signal asymmetric hearing loss. Conductive blockage reduces ambient masking and enhances bone perception, while cochlear loss reduces neural input. Weber test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • asymmetric hearing loss

Assessment Method

Place a vibrating tuning fork on the midline forehead or vertex.

Positive Finding

Sound lateralizes to the poorer ear in conductive loss and the better ear in sensorineural loss.

Mechanism

Conductive blockage reduces ambient masking and enhances bone perception, while cochlear loss reduces neural input.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Weber test result raises concern for asymmetric hearing loss because conductive blockage reduces ambient masking and enhances bone perception, while cochlear loss reduces neural input. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Weber test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Weber test to focus evaluation of asymmetric hearing loss and the reviewed alternatives: cerumen, functional hearing loss, skull asymmetry. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place a vibrating tuning fork on the midline forehead or vertex. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Weber test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Symmetric bilateral loss does not lateralize and patient reporting is subjective. Therefore, Weber test cannot confirm or exclude asymmetric hearing loss by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cerumen

  • functional hearing loss

  • skull asymmetry

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as cerumen, functional hearing loss, skull asymmetry.

False Negative Situations

Weber test may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Weber test can adjust concern for asymmetric hearing loss, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Weber test: Symmetric bilateral loss does not lateralize and patient reporting is subjective.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Weber test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place a vibrating tuning fork on the midline forehead or vertex. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Interpret only with Rinne and follow sudden sensorineural loss urgently.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Weber test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Sound lateralizes to the poorer ear in conductive loss and the better ear in sensorineural loss. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Place a vibrating tuning fork on the midline forehead or vertex. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, asymmetric hearing loss, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Weber test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Interpret only with Rinne and follow sudden sensorineural loss urgently. Base escalation for Weber test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
