Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia and chronic musculoskeletal pain; age approvals and formulation indications differ.

Mechanism

Duloxetine inhibits SERT and NET with clinically relevant effects across usual dosing, increasing monoamines in limbic mood circuits and descending projections from brainstem nuclei to the spinal dorsal horn. These descending fibers activate inhibitory interneurons and reduce nociceptive transmission, explaining benefit in neuropathic and centralized pain even when depression is absent. It is extensively metabolized by CYP1A2 and CYP2D6; inhibition or hepatic disease raises exposure. Noradrenergic stimulation increases urethral sphincter tone and can worsen hesitation/retention, while hepatic metabolism and rare idiosyncratic injury explain avoidance in substantial chronic liver disease or heavy alcohol use.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Duloxetine can cause serious liver injury; avoid use in substantial alcohol exposure or chronic liver disease and urgently assess jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant pain or unexplained systemic illness.

Contraindications

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI use or serotonergic linezolid/methylene blue outside emergency guidance

  • Avoid in substantial alcohol use or chronic hepatic impairment; generally avoid severe renal impairment under U.S. labeling

  • Assess urinary obstruction/retention, uncontrolled hypertension, bipolar disorder and glaucoma risk

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure whether pain function, sleep and mobility improve rather than judging only a numeric pain score; descending-pathway benefit develops gradually.

  • Do not crush/open products unless that exact formulation permits it, and taper rather than stop abruptly to prevent dizziness, sensory symptoms, irritability and nausea.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP1A2 inhibitors such as fluvoxamine can markedly raise duloxetine; CYP2D6 inhibitors also increase exposure.

  • Duloxetine inhibits CYP2D6 and can raise susceptible TCAs, antipsychotics and antiarrhythmics.

  • Serotonergic drugs, NSAIDs/anticoagulants, diuretics, alcohol and other BP/retention drugs add predictable toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure/pulse and orthostatic symptoms

  • Liver tests when baseline risk or hepatic symptoms exist; renal function for appropriateness

  • Sodium in high-risk patients, urinary output/retention and mood/pain functional response

Nclex Traps

  • Duloxetine analgesia is a central descending-inhibition effect, not proof that neuropathy has healed.

  • New hesitancy or suprapubic discomfort can be drug-related retention, not merely anxiety.

  • Do not reassure a patient with jaundice because mild nausea is common; the findings have different significance.

Description

Duloxetine inhibits serotonin and norepinephrine transporters to treat depression and anxiety and to strengthen descending brainstem-spinal inhibition of selected chronic pain signals. It does not numb or repair damaged nerves directly; it increases monoamine signaling in circuits that suppress ascending nociceptive transmission. Its signature cautions are nausea, blood-pressure change, withdrawal, liver injury risk, urinary retention and accumulation in severe renal impairment.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, dry mouth, constipation, sweating, dizziness, fatigue and sexual dysfunction

  • Blood-pressure/pulse increase, orthostasis and falls

  • Urinary hesitation/retention and angle-closure glaucoma risk

  • Hepatotoxicity, serotonin syndrome, SIADH/hyponatremia, bleeding and discontinuation syndrome
