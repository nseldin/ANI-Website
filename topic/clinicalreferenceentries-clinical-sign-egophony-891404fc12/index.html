Definition

Egophony is an auscultatory voice sign. It is a clue to compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid. A positive finding means the sound is heard as a nasal “ay”.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Egophony means the sound is heard as a nasal “ay”.

Why It Matters

A positive Egophony response can signal compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid. A thin layer of compressed lung preferentially transmits higher harmonics and filters lower frequencies. Egophony cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to say “ee” while auscultating.

Positive Finding

The sound is heard as a nasal “ay”.

Mechanism

A thin layer of compressed lung preferentially transmits higher harmonics and filters lower frequencies.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Egophony response raises concern for compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid because a thin layer of compressed lung preferentially transmits higher harmonics and filters lower frequencies. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Egophony does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Egophony to focus evaluation of compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid and the reviewed alternatives: bronchophony, nasal speech, thin chest wall. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to say “ee” while auscultating. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Egophony as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Technique, accent, and hearing affect interpretation. Therefore, Egophony cannot confirm or exclude compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • bronchophony

  • nasal speech

  • thin chest wall

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from bronchophony, nasal speech, thin chest wall, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Egophony may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Egophony can adjust concern for compressed or consolidated lung, often above pleural fluid, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Egophony: Technique, accent, and hearing affect interpretation.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Egophony, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to say “ee” while auscultating. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Map the change against other findings and ultrasound rather than treating it as a fluid level.
