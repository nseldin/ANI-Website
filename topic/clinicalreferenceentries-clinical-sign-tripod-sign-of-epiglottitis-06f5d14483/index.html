Definition

Tripod sign of epiglottitis is an observed posture sign. It is a clue to critical upper-airway obstruction. A positive finding means the child sits leaning forward with neck extended, mouth open, and may drool.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tripod sign of epiglottitis means the child sits leaning forward with neck extended, mouth open, and may drool.

Why It Matters

The observed Tripod sign of epiglottitis finding can signal critical upper-airway obstruction. This posture maximizes upper-airway diameter and lets secretions drain when swallowing and supraglottic airflow are impaired. Tripod sign of epiglottitis cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Evidence Limitations

Not every child adopts the posture and severe asthma can also tripod. Therefore, Tripod sign of epiglottitis cannot confirm or exclude critical upper-airway obstruction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as croup, peritonsillar abscess, retropharyngeal abscess.

False Negative Situations

Tripod sign of epiglottitis may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of critical upper-airway obstruction.
