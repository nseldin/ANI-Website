Definition

Jeanne sign is an observed hand-motor sign. It is a clue to ulnar nerve weakness. A positive finding means the thumb MCP joint hyperextends.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Jeanne sign means the thumb MCP joint hyperextends.

Why It Matters

The observed Jeanne sign finding can signal ulnar nerve weakness. Weak ulnar intrinsic stabilization is compensated by extensor pollicis longus and flexor pollicis longus. Jeanne sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ulnar nerve weakness

Assessment Method

Observe thumb MCP posture during a Froment paper-pinch task.

Positive Finding

The thumb MCP joint hyperextends.

Mechanism

Weak ulnar intrinsic stabilization is compensated by extensor pollicis longus and flexor pollicis longus.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Jeanne sign finding raises concern for ulnar nerve weakness because weak ulnar intrinsic stabilization is compensated by extensor pollicis longus and flexor pollicis longus. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Jeanne sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Jeanne sign to focus evaluation of ulnar nerve weakness and the reviewed alternatives: Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, thumb arthritis, normal hypermobility. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe thumb MCP posture during a Froment paper-pinch task. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Jeanne sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Baseline MCP laxity reduces specificity. Therefore, Jeanne sign cannot confirm or exclude ulnar nerve weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Ehlers-Danlos syndrome

  • thumb arthritis

  • normal hypermobility

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, thumb arthritis, normal hypermobility.

False Negative Situations

Jeanne sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of ulnar nerve weakness.

Modern Status

Current adjunct paired with Froment sign. When performed as described, Jeanne sign can adjust concern for ulnar nerve weakness, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Jeanne sign: Baseline MCP laxity reduces specificity.

Safety Precautions

Observe Jeanne sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Observe thumb MCP posture during a Froment paper-pinch task. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Document both MCP and IP compensation rather than calling all thumb bending Froment positive.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Jeanne sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The thumb MCP joint hyperextends. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Observe thumb MCP posture during a Froment paper-pinch task. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, ulnar nerve weakness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Jeanne sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
