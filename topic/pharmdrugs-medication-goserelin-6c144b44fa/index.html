Class

Long-acting GnRH agonist subcutaneous implant producing flare followed by gonadal suppression

Used To Treat

Product- and strength-specific treatment of prostate cancer, endometriosis, endometrial thinning before selected procedures, and advanced breast cancer in selected premenopausal or perimenopausal patients.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Goserelin has no current U.S. boxed warning, but initial tumor flare can worsen bone pain, hypercalcemia, urinary obstruction, ureteral obstruction, or spinal-cord compression. Androgen-deprivation therapy can worsen hyperglycemia, diabetes, cardiovascular risk, and QT prolongation. Women can lose substantial bone mineral density during endometriosis treatment; repeat exposure requires careful risk assessment. Rare pituitary apoplexy presents with sudden severe headache, vomiting, visual or ocular-motor change, altered mental status, or collapse. Implant insertion can cause hemorrhage or vascular injury, especially in a thin patient or one receiving anticoagulation.

Mechanism

Goserelin is a GnRH decapeptide analogue that resists enzymatic breakdown and continuously occupies pituitary GnRH receptors. Initial Gq/PLC/IP3-calcium signaling increases LH and FSH release. With persistent nonpulsatile exposure, receptors uncouple, internalize, and decrease in number; pituitary gonadotrophs release less LH/FSH, and ovarian estradiol/progesterone or testicular testosterone falls. Reduced estrogen slows ectopic endometrial tissue and some breast cancers; reduced androgen slows prostate cancer. The same withdrawal of sex-steroid signaling increases bone resorption and affects thermoregulation, mood, sexual tissue, glucose, lipids, vessels, and QT physiology. The biodegradable polymer controls release over the labeled interval, so dose strength and schedule are formulation identities.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify 3.6-mg versus 10.8-mg product, indication, interval, abdominal insertion site and correct technique. Apply pressure and observe for expanding pain, hypotension, abdominal distention, or bleeding.

  • Teach that symptoms can flare before suppression. New leg weakness, numbness, urinary retention, severe back pain, visual change, or sudden headache is urgent.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity to GnRH analogues or implant components, and product-specific restrictions.

  • Assess spinal metastasis, obstruction, anticoagulation/bleeding risk, body habitus and abdominal anatomy, diabetes, cardiovascular/QT status, mood, and bone health before treatment.

Interactions

  • QT-prolonging drugs and electrolyte abnormalities can compound androgen-deprivation arrhythmia risk.

  • Glucocorticoids, antiseizure drugs, smoking and alcohol add bone loss; antidiabetic therapy may require adjustment.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test when relevant, testosterone or estradiol, PSA or tumor marker, glucose/A1c, lipids, electrolytes, bone-density risk and hemoglobin if insertion bleeding occurs

  • Flare symptoms, neurologic/urinary status, pain and bleeding response, mood, hot flashes, sexual effects, implant site, fracture risk, and on-time repeat dosing

Nclex Traps

  • Goserelin is an agonist that suppresses after continuous exposure; the early flare precedes receptor downregulation.

  • The abdominal implant is a drug-delivery device with insertion injury risk, not merely a slow injection.

Description

Goserelin is a GnRH agonist delivered as a biodegradable subcutaneous abdominal implant. It first stimulates pituitary LH and FSH, briefly increasing testosterone or estradiol, then continuous exposure desensitizes GnRH receptors and suppresses gonadal steroid production. The implant route matters: incorrect deep placement can injure abdominal vessels, and the drug cannot be removed like a daily tablet once released. Hormone-sensitive tumors or endometriosis improve after suppression, while hot flashes, sexual dysfunction, vaginal changes, mood effects, glucose and cardiovascular changes, and bone loss reflect the same low-steroid state. These effects are mechanism-linked because receptor desensitization lowers sex-steroid signaling throughout bone, thermoregulatory, vascular, and sexual tissues; therefore risk monitoring does not mean suppression has failed. A transient tumor or symptom flare during initiation must be anticipated rather than mistaken for treatment failure.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, sweating, headache, emotional lability, depression, decreased libido, vaginal dryness, sexual dysfunction, acne, edema, and implant-site pain

  • Tumor-flare obstruction or cord compression, bone loss/fracture, hyperglycemia, cardiovascular event, QT arrhythmia, severe implant-site vascular injury, anaphylaxis, hypercalcemia, or pituitary apoplexy
