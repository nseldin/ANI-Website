Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, liver disease or prior liver dysfunction, uncontrolled thyroid or adrenal disease, organic intracranial lesion such as pituitary tumor, unexplained uterine bleeding, and ovarian cyst/enlargement not due to PCOS.

  • Evaluate male factor, tubal anatomy when relevant, prolactin, thyroid, adrenal, uterine and ovarian factors before repeating cycles; more stimulation cannot fix every infertility cause.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach the five-day course, expected ovulation window, intercourse or insemination timing, and cycle-specific monitoring. Confirm a negative pregnancy assessment and absence of significant ovarian enlargement before each new course.

  • Tell the patient to stop immediately for visual symptoms and to report rapid weight gain, increasing abdominal girth, severe pelvic pain, vomiting, dyspnea, or oliguria because ovarian complications can progress after dosing ends.

Interactions

  • Formal interaction data are limited, but combining clomiphene with gonadotropins can amplify follicle number, OHSS, and multiple-gestation risk.

  • Other hormones or endocrine-active drugs can alter interpretation of cycle response; do not add supplements marketed for fertility without protocol review.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test, pelvic exam/ultrasound, ovulation confirmation, thyroid, prolactin and other infertility studies as indicated; triglycerides when personal/family hyperlipidemia raises risk

  • Follicle response, pelvic pain and distention, weight, vision, bleeding, timing of ovulation, and pregnancy location once conception occurs

Nclex Traps

  • Clomiphene raises endogenous FSH/LH by blocking estrogen feedback; it does not directly provide FSH and it is not the hCG trigger.

  • Why limit cycles? Benefit falls when repeated correctly timed ovulatory cycles fail, while cumulative visual, ovarian, antiestrogenic, and possible long-term tumor risks continue to rise.

Used To Treat

Ovulatory dysfunction in a nonpregnant patient who desires pregnancy after other impediments to conception have been evaluated; current PCOS guidance generally favors letrozole first when anovulatory PCOS is the only infertility factor.

Mechanism

Clomiphene competes with estradiol at estrogen receptors in hypothalamic feedback circuits. By blocking the expected estrogen signal, it reduces negative feedback on pulsatile GnRH release. Greater GnRH drive increases pituitary FSH and LH secretion; FSH stimulates granulosa-cell proliferation and aromatase, while LH supports theca androgen synthesis and later ovulation. Rising follicular estradiol eventually generates the positive-feedback threshold for an endogenous LH surge. Because clomiphene is a tissue-selective partial agonist/antagonist, receptor effects differ across hypothalamus, cervix, endometrium, bone, and eye. Antiestrogenic cervical-mucus thinning and endometrial effects can offset benefit, and retinal or visual-pathway effects may produce afterimages, flashes, scotomata, or blurred vision that increase with cumulative dose and duration.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Clomiphene has no current U.S. boxed warning, but visual symptoms can be prolonged and possibly irreversible, particularly with higher cumulative dose or duration. Stop treatment and obtain prompt complete ophthalmologic evaluation for blurred vision, flashes, spots, scotomata, afterimages, or any other visual change; driving is unsafe while symptoms persist. Ovarian enlargement, OHSS, torsion, and multiple gestation can occur. Current labeling does not recommend doses above 100 mg daily for 5 days, continued treatment after three failed ovulatory courses, continued treatment after three ovulatory courses without pregnancy, or long-term cyclic therapy beyond about six total cycles.

Description

Clomiphene blocks estrogen feedback at hypothalamic estrogen receptors, increasing GnRH, FSH, and LH release so a dominant follicle can develop and ovulate; pharmacologically, it is an oral selective estrogen-receptor modulator. It is a mixture of enclomiphene and longer-persisting zuclomiphene isomers, helping explain why effects can outlast the five-day dose course. Clomiphene can also antagonize estrogen in cervical mucus and endometrium, so ovulation does not guarantee optimal sperm passage or implantation conditions. Therapy is intentionally cyclic and limited: if ovulation does not occur after three courses or three ovulatory courses do not produce pregnancy, the diagnosis should be reconsidered; long-term cyclic use beyond about six total cycles is not recommended.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, headache, breast discomfort, nausea, mood change, abnormal bleeding, pelvic discomfort, ovarian enlargement, and visual afterimages

  • Persistent or irreversible visual disturbance, OHSS, ovarian torsion or rupture, multiple gestation, severe hypertriglyceridemia or pancreatitis, liver dysfunction, and possible tumor concern with prolonged exposure
