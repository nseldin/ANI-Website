Used To Treat

Hemophilia A from factor VIII deficiency and hemophilia B from factor IX deficiency. Replacement treats or prevents bleeds and supports procedures. Emicizumab provides routine prophylaxis for hemophilia A with or without factor VIII inhibitors, but it does not replace factor IX and is not a universal acute-bleeding drug.

Description

Hemophilia A and B can look clinically similar because both weaken the intrinsic coagulation pathway and prolong aPTT, but the missing proteins are different. Factor VIII is an activated cofactor that organizes factor IXa and factor X on a phospholipid surface; factor IX is the vitamin K-dependent enzyme precursor that becomes IXa. Replacing VIII treats A, while replacing IX treats B. An inhibitor is an antibody that neutralizes infused factor, so an apparently adequate dose may fail. Emicizumab bypasses the need for factor VIII cofactor activity by bridging IXa and X, which makes it useful for prophylaxis in hemophilia A even with inhibitors, but breakthrough-bleed treatment still requires a specific plan.

Mechanism

In intrinsic tenase, activated factor IX is the serine protease and activated factor VIII is its nonenzymatic cofactor. Together on activated platelet phospholipid with calcium, they accelerate factor X activation by orders of magnitude, leading to factor Xa, thrombin, and fibrin. Factor VIII concentrate restores the missing cofactor in hemophilia A; factor IX concentrate restores the missing zymogen in hemophilia B. Neutralizing inhibitors bind the replacement factor and reduce recovery or half-life. Emicizumab is a bispecific antibody with one arm directed toward factor IXa and the other toward factor X, placing enzyme and substrate together without being structurally identical to factor VIII. Because emicizumab continuously changes coagulation potential and shortens aPTT, assay selection and bypassing-agent exposure must be managed deliberately.

Boxed Warning

Emicizumab carries a boxed warning for thrombotic microangiopathy and thromboembolism when high cumulative activated prothrombin complex concentrate is used. Factor concentrates do not share that boxed warning, but hypersensitivity and inhibitor development can make treatment fail or become dangerous. Any head, neck, abdominal, iliopsoas, or airway bleed is an emergency even when external blood loss looks small.

Adverse Effects

  • Inhibitor development with reduced factor recovery or persistent bleeding

  • Hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis, especially important with factor IX products

  • Thrombosis or thrombotic microangiopathy from high-risk bypassing combinations during emicizumab prophylaxis

Contraindications

  • Clarify the diagnosed deficiency before selecting replacement; factor VIII is not factor IX.

  • Do not interpret aPTT-based assays normally during emicizumab treatment.

  • Do not improvise activated prothrombin complex concentrate dosing for breakthrough bleeding during emicizumab prophylaxis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record product, vial potency in IU, body weight, prescribed factor target, infusion time, lot number, clinical response, and any hypersensitivity.

  • Suspect an inhibitor when expected factor activity is not reached or bleeding continues despite correct dosing and administration.

Interactions

  • Aspirin, NSAIDs, anticoagulants, and trauma increase bleeding risk independent of factor level.

  • Tranexamic acid can support mucosal hemostasis in selected situations but does not replace the missing factor for a major deep-tissue bleed.

Key Labs

  • Factor VIII or IX activity, inhibitor titer, CBC, aPTT with assay awareness, and product-specific recovery/half-life assessment

  • Clinical joint, muscle, neurologic, abdominal, airway, and perioperative hemostasis

Nclex Traps

  • Hemophilia A is factor VIII deficiency; hemophilia B is factor IX deficiency.

  • Emicizumab is hemophilia A prophylaxis, not a replacement for factor IX and not permission to use unlimited bypassing agent.
