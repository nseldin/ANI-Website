Used To Treat

These long-acting biologics prevent migraine rather than rescue an attack already underway. Erenumab, fremanezumab, and galcanezumab are subcutaneous; eptinezumab is an IV infusion every 3 months. Galcanezumab also has a distinct adult episodic cluster-headache regimen, and fremanezumab has a pediatric episodic-migraine indication for patients 6 to 17 years who weigh at least 45 kg.

Description

CGRP pathway monoclonal antibodies are target-specific migraine preventives. Erenumab (Aimovig) binds the CGRP receptor; fremanezumab (Ajovy), galcanezumab (Emgality), and eptinezumab (Vyepti) bind the CGRP peptide itself. That receptor-versus-ligand distinction is the central subclass fact. These are not broad immunosuppressants: they do not treat autoimmune disease or cancer, do not require routine TB or hepatitis screening, and do not carry a class rule to avoid live vaccines.

Mechanism

Trigeminal sensory activation releases CGRP, which normally activates the CLR/RAMP1 receptor complex, raises cAMP, and amplifies nociceptive and vasodilatory signaling. Erenumab prevents receptor activation; the ligand antibodies bind CGRP before it can activate the receptor. Because antibodies are catabolized into peptides and amino acids rather than cleared through CYP pathways, conventional CYP interactions are unlikely. Their approximately 27- to 31-day effective half-lives support monthly or quarterly dosing but also explain why benefit and adverse effects fade slowly after a dose is stopped.

Boxed Warning

Current U.S. labels have no boxed warning. All four require attention to serious hypersensitivity, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon. Current AIMOVIG, AJOVY, and VYEPTI labels also warn about severe constipation or constipation with serious complications; current EMGALITY labeling does not list the same dedicated constipation warning. This is product-specific, not permission to ignore bowel symptoms with another agent. Reduced CGRP-mediated vasodilatory or enteric signaling is biologically plausible context, but postmarketing reports do not prove one exclusive mechanism.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reactions are common with the subcutaneous products; nasopharyngitis and hypersensitivity are prominent with IV eptinezumab. Product labels also describe serious or delayed hypersensitivity, hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon.

  • Severe abdominal pain, progressive distention, vomiting, or inability to pass stool or gas can signal ileus or obstruction and requires urgent assessment, especially with erenumab, fremanezumab, or eptinezumab and other constipating drugs.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to the selected antibody or an excipient is the principal labeled contraindication. A reaction can occur during administration or be delayed, and the long half-life means exposure cannot be rapidly removed.

Interactions

  • CYP-mediated interactions are unlikely because monoclonal antibodies undergo proteolytic catabolism, but that does not prove every combination is risk-free. Review additive constipation, blood-pressure, and vasospastic risk and avoid assuming that two CGRP-pathway drugs should be combined without a deliberate evidence-based plan.

  • Monthly and quarterly products cannot be converted by simple arithmetic: formulation, loading dose, injection count, route, and studied exposure differ.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document monthly headache days, functional burden, target, route, loading regimen, injection or infusion technique, bowel and vascular history, pregnancy plans, prior preventive failures, and response over an adequate trial.

  • Use the exact schedule: erenumab monthly; fremanezumab monthly or quarterly in adults and monthly for its pediatric indication; galcanezumab uses a migraine loading dose and a different cluster-headache regimen; eptinezumab is diluted and infused over about 30 minutes every 3 months.

  • Teach that these drugs prevent future attacks and are not acute rescue. A missed dose or schedule change is product specific, and the long half-life means both effect and toxicity may persist for weeks.

Key Labs

  • No routine concentration, CBC, liver test, TB test, or hepatitis panel is required solely because of this class. Follow headache-day burden, disability and rescue use, blood pressure, digital perfusion, bowel function, injection or infusion reactions, and hypersensitivity.

Nclex Traps

  • Erenumab targets the receptor; fremanezumab, galcanezumab, and eptinezumab target the ligand.

  • Galcanezumab's episodic cluster-headache regimen differs from its migraine regimen.

  • These target-specific antibodies are biologics but are not generic immunosuppressants; routine infection screening and live-vaccine avoidance are not class requirements.

Population Risks

  • Type

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Human pregnancy data are insufficient. Long half-lives create prolonged fetal exposure after a recent dose, so pregnancy planning should occur before treatment changes; product pregnancy registries may be available.

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric use

    Note

    Fremanezumab is labeled for prevention of episodic migraine in patients 6 to 17 years weighing at least 45 kg. The other products in this class remain adult indications in current U.S. labeling.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adults

    Note

    Clinical trials included limited numbers of patients 65 and older. Consider vascular disease, constipation, polypharmacy, injection ability, and consequences of a long washout rather than using age alone as a dosing rule.
