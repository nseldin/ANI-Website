Used To Treat

On-demand treatment and control of bleeding, perioperative management, and product-specific routine prophylaxis in von Willebrand disease. Plasma-derived products may contain both VWF and factor VIII; recombinant VWF contains VWF without factor VIII and may require an initial approved factor VIII dose when baseline FVIII is low or unknown.

Description

Von Willebrand factor concentrate is a plasma-derived or recombinant coagulation-factor biologic that replaces the large adhesive protein linking platelets to damaged vessel walls and carrying factor VIII in plasma. VWD therefore affects both primary hemostasis and the stability of secondary coagulation. Giving VWF restores platelet tethering under shear and slows factor VIII clearance. The product distinction matters: some concentrates bring factor VIII with them, while recombinant VWF allows the patient's endogenous factor VIII to rise over several hours and may need immediate factor VIII support for an urgent major bleed. Excessively high VWF or factor VIII can shift treatment from hemostasis toward thrombosis.

Mechanism

VWF multimers bind exposed subendothelial collagen through collagen-binding domains and bind platelet glycoprotein Ib through the A1 domain after shear-dependent conformational change. This slows platelets enough for firm activation and aggregation. VWF also binds factor VIII through its D-prime/D3 region, protecting VIII from proteolysis and rapid clearance. Plasma-derived VWF/FVIII concentrates restore both functions in ratios that vary by product. Recombinant VWF restores VWF without exogenous factor VIII; stabilized endogenous VIII gradually accumulates, so an initial rFVIII dose is required by labeling when immediate hemostatic FVIII is needed and the baseline is below the specified threshold or unknown. ADAMTS13 later cleaves ultra-large multimers, integrating the replacement into normal regulation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, thrombosis, and neutralizing antibodies can occur. Monitor VWF activity and factor VIII during repeated or perioperative dosing because factor VIII can accumulate even when the VWF dose appears appropriate. A thrombotic event is more likely when high factor levels combine with immobility, surgery, estrogen, cancer, or vascular risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, infusion reaction, headache, nausea, or dizziness

  • Thromboembolic events when VWF/FVIII exposure and patient risk converge

  • Neutralizing antibodies with poor response; plasma-derived products also carry a residual infectious-agent concern despite manufacturing safeguards

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to the product, excipients, or product-specific animal proteins

  • Do not assume every VWD subtype responds to desmopressin or that every VWF product contains the same factor VIII amount

  • Persistent bleeding or poor laboratory recovery requires inhibitor and diagnosis reassessment

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify product type, VWF activity units, factor VIII content, patient weight, bleed/procedure target, and whether an initial separate factor VIII dose is required.

  • Trend both clinical hemostasis and VWF/FVIII levels during repeated perioperative treatment because factor VIII accumulation can create thrombosis risk.

Interactions

  • Antifibrinolytics can support mucosal hemostasis but add thrombosis concerns in selected settings.

  • Antiplatelet drugs, NSAIDs, anticoagulants, and estrogen can alter the balance between bleeding and thrombosis.

Key Labs

  • VWF activity, VWF antigen, factor VIII activity, CBC/hemoglobin, and inhibitor studies when response is inadequate

  • Bleeding score/site, surgical drain or wound loss, menstrual/GI bleeding, thrombosis symptoms, and product-specific peak/trough targets

Nclex Traps

  • VWF is both a platelet-adhesion protein and the carrier that stabilizes factor VIII.

  • Recombinant VWF may not provide immediate factor VIII; read the baseline FVIII and urgent-hemostasis plan.
