Definition

Rheumatoid nodule is an observed and palpated subcutaneous sign. It is a clue to seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease. A positive finding means firm usually nontender mobile or fixed nodules occur over elbows or fingers.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rheumatoid nodule means firm usually nontender mobile or fixed nodules occur over elbows or fingers.

Why It Matters

The observed Rheumatoid nodule finding can signal seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease. Palisading granulomatous inflammation forms around fibrinoid necrosis at pressure sites. Rheumatoid nodule cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease

Assessment Method

Inspect extensor pressure points.

Positive Finding

Firm usually nontender mobile or fixed nodules occur over elbows or fingers.

Mechanism

Palisading granulomatous inflammation forms around fibrinoid necrosis at pressure sites.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Rheumatoid nodule finding raises concern for seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease because palisading granulomatous inflammation forms around fibrinoid necrosis at pressure sites. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Rheumatoid nodule does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Rheumatoid nodule to focus evaluation of seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease and the reviewed alternatives: xanthoma, calcinosis, foreign-body granuloma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect extensor pressure points. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Rheumatoid nodule as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Methotrexate can accelerate nodulosis and other nodules resemble it. Therefore, Rheumatoid nodule cannot confirm or exclude seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • xanthoma

  • calcinosis

  • foreign-body granuloma

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as xanthoma, calcinosis, foreign-body granuloma.

False Negative Situations

Rheumatoid nodule may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of seropositive rheumatoid arthritis or related disease.
