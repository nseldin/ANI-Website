Definition

Gaenslen test is a bedside sacroiliac provocation maneuver. It is a clue to sacroiliac-region pain. A positive finding means familiar posterior pelvic pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Gaenslen test means familiar posterior pelvic pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Gaenslen test response can signal sacroiliac-region pain. Opposing hip forces create torsion across the sacroiliac joints and surrounding ligaments. Gaenslen test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • sacroiliac-region pain

Assessment Method

Flex one hip toward the chest while extending the opposite hip off the table with pelvic stabilization.

Positive Finding

Familiar posterior pelvic pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

Opposing hip forces create torsion across the sacroiliac joints and surrounding ligaments.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Gaenslen test response raises concern for sacroiliac-region pain because opposing hip forces create torsion across the sacroiliac joints and surrounding ligaments. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Gaenslen test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Gaenslen test to focus evaluation of sacroiliac-region pain and the reviewed alternatives: hip arthritis, lumbar radiculopathy, pubic symphysis pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex one hip toward the chest while extending the opposite hip off the table with pelvic stabilization. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Gaenslen test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Single-test accuracy is limited and lumbar or hip pain can be provoked. Therefore, Gaenslen test cannot confirm or exclude sacroiliac-region pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip arthritis

  • lumbar radiculopathy

  • pubic symphysis pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hip arthritis, lumbar radiculopathy, pubic symphysis pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Gaenslen test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current provocation adjunct; use a cluster rather than one test. When performed as described, Gaenslen test can adjust concern for sacroiliac-region pain, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Gaenslen test: Single-test accuracy is limited and lumbar or hip pain can be provoked.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Gaenslen test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex one hip toward the chest while extending the opposite hip off the table with pelvic stabilization. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid in pregnancy-related instability, hip fracture, or severe osteoporosis.
