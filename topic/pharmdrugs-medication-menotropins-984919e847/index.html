Used To Treat

Development of multiple follicles as part of assisted reproductive technology under the current U.S. MENOPUR indication; other gonadotropin protocols and products have distinct approved uses and cannot be assumed equivalent.

Description

Menotropins provides purified urinary-derived FSH and LH bioactivity that stimulates granulosa-cell follicular growth and theca-cell androgen production during controlled ovarian stimulation. Historically obtained from postmenopausal urine, its FSH component grows follicles while LH activity supplies androgen substrate that granulosa aromatase converts to estradiol. MENOPUR is used to recruit multiple follicles during assisted reproduction after individualized evaluation and often pituitary suppression. It is not the final ovulation trigger and not the same as pure recombinant FSH. The mixed signal can be useful when LH support is desired, but direct ovarian stimulation requires serial ultrasound and estradiol monitoring because excessive follicle number increases OHSS, torsion, multiple gestation, and cycle-cancellation risk.

Mechanism

The FSH component activates Gs-coupled FSH receptors on granulosa cells, raising cAMP and protein kinase A signaling, increasing aromatase, inhibin, proliferation, and follicular survival. The LH bioactivity activates LH/CG receptors on theca cells, increasing steroidogenic acute regulatory protein, cholesterol transport, and androgen synthesis. Granulosa aromatase converts those androgens to estradiol, so estradiol reflects the growing follicular cohort. After adequate development, a separate hCG or GnRH-agonist trigger induces final maturation and luteinization. If too many follicles are recruited, trigger-related ovarian VEGF can produce capillary leak, ascites, hemoconcentration, thrombosis, and respiratory compromise. Biological International Units express activity and are not simple milligram equivalents.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: MENOPUR has no current U.S. boxed warning, but treatment must be supervised by clinicians experienced in infertility with ultrasound and hormonal monitoring. Major hazards include OHSS, pulmonary and vascular complications, ovarian torsion, abnormal enlargement, multiple gestation, ectopic pregnancy, spontaneous abortion, and congenital-risk context associated with infertility/ART. Do not improvise dose conversion between MENOPUR, recombinant FSH, or another gonadotropin. Reconstitution errors, wrong vial count, and confusion between FSH-growth doses and the later trigger can directly alter cycle safety.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, abdominal pain, pelvic discomfort, nausea, injection-site reaction, bloating, breast symptoms, and ovarian enlargement

  • OHSS, ascites, pleural effusion, hemoconcentration, renal dysfunction, thromboembolism, torsion, ovarian rupture, ectopic pregnancy, and multifetal gestation

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, primary ovarian failure, uncontrolled thyroid/adrenal or other endocrine disease, pituitary or hypothalamic tumor, sex-hormone-dependent tumor, unexplained uterine bleeding, or ovarian cyst/enlargement not explained by PCOS.

  • An unsafe response is a reason to hold or cancel, not to finish the purchased medication. Trigger decisions depend on follicle and estradiol data.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach reconstitution with the supplied diluent and adapter, exact vial count and International Unit dose, subcutaneous technique, timing, storage, and missed-dose contact plan. Have the patient demonstrate the process.

  • Monitor symptoms after treatment ends because OHSS often intensifies after hCG or pregnancy. Severe unilateral pain can be torsion even without dramatic laboratory changes.

Interactions

  • Other gonadotropins, clomiphene, letrozole and hCG can add ovarian stimulation; the whole protocol determines response.

  • No casual substitution is valid between urinary and recombinant gonadotropins because bioactivity, purity, delivery device and prescribed units differ.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test, baseline ultrasound and endocrine evaluation, serial follicle count/size, estradiol, and cycle-specific reproductive testing

  • Weight, abdominal girth, urine output, hematocrit, electrolytes, creatinine, liver tests, oxygenation, ascites, thrombosis, torsion, and pregnancy location when symptomatic

Nclex Traps

  • Menotropins supplies FSH plus LH activity; follitropin alfa supplies recombinant FSH, and hCG provides the later LH-like trigger.

  • A high estradiol value is not merely proof the drug works; it can signal a large follicular cohort and rising OHSS/multiple-gestation risk.
