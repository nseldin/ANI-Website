Definition

Dagger sign is an imaging sign. It is a clue to ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments. A positive finding means a central vertical radiodense line resembles a dagger.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dagger sign means a central vertical radiodense line resembles a dagger.

Why It Matters

The Dagger sign imaging pattern can signal ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments. Ligament ossification forms a continuous midline bony stripe. Dagger sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments

Assessment Method

Review anteroposterior spine radiograph.

Positive Finding

A central vertical radiodense line resembles a dagger.

Mechanism

Ligament ossification forms a continuous midline bony stripe.

Clinical Interpretation

The Dagger sign imaging pattern raises concern for ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments because ligament ossification forms a continuous midline bony stripe. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dagger sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dagger sign to focus evaluation of ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments and the reviewed alternatives: aortic calcification, spinous overlap, ligamentum flavum ossification. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review anteroposterior spine radiograph. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dagger sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Projection and other calcified structures can mimic it. Therefore, Dagger sign cannot confirm or exclude ossification of interspinous and supraspinous ligaments by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • aortic calcification

  • spinous overlap

  • ligamentum flavum ossification

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with aortic calcification, spinous overlap, ligamentum flavum ossification.

False Negative Situations

Dagger sign may be missed when the associated process is early or subtle, the relevant region is not included, or acquisition timing and resolution are inadequate.

Modern Status

Current late axial-spondyloarthritis sign. Interpret Dagger sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Projection and other calcified structures can mimic it.

For Dagger sign, verify patient identity, the indication, acquisition quality, timing, and the relevant imaging plane or electrodiagnostic leads. Apply the reviewed precaution for this pattern: Interpret with sacroiliac and syndesmophyte findings.
