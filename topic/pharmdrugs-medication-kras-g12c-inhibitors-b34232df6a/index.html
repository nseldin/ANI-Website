Used To Treat

Biomarker-confirmed KRAS G12C-mutated cancers, principally selected non-small-cell lung cancers and colorectal cancers under product-specific prior-treatment and combination criteria.

Description

KRAS G12C inhibitors are mutant-selective targeted drugs that covalently bind the acquired cysteine at codon 12 and trap KRAS G12C in its inactive GDP-bound state. They are not interchangeable generic RAS blockers: sotorasib and adagrasib differ in dosing, interaction profile, and dominant toxicities, while colorectal tumors often require concurrent EGFR blockade because EGFR feedback can reactivate downstream signaling.

Mechanism

KRAS cycles between active GTP-bound and inactive GDP-bound conformations. Sotorasib and adagrasib occupy the switch-II pocket and form an irreversible bond with cysteine-12 during the GDP-bound state, preventing return to active signaling through RAF-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT pathways while largely sparing wild-type KRAS.

Boxed Warning

High-yield class safety context: no shared boxed warning defines the class, but hepatotoxicity and ILD/pneumonitis require rapid recognition. Adagrasib also has clinically important GI and QT risk; sotorasib has pH-dependent absorption and major CYP/P-gp/BCRP interactions.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm the exact KRAS G12C result and tumor-specific regimen; a generic KRAS mutation is not sufficient.

  • Trend liver tests and respiratory symptoms for both drugs, then apply product-specific ECG, electrolyte, GI, acid-suppression, and interaction monitoring.

Key Labs

  • KRAS G12C companion-diagnostic result, ALT/AST/alkaline phosphatase/bilirubin, CBC/CMP, ECG and potassium/magnesium for adagrasib, and pulmonary evaluation for new cough or dyspnea.

Nclex Traps

  • KRAS G12C is a specific cysteine substitution, not every KRAS mutation.

  • A targeted therapy can still cause organ toxicity; a new cough or hepatitis pattern is not automatically cancer progression.
