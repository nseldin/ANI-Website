Used To Treat

Adjunct to nutrition and activity for adults with type 2 diabetes. It lowers fasting and postmeal glucose without directly producing major weight loss. It is not for type 1 diabetes or DKA, and the dose must be adjusted for reduced eGFR.

Mechanism

DPP-4 is a serine protease that cleaves active GLP-1 and GIP within minutes. Sitagliptin selectively inhibits this enzyme, increasing intact incretin concentrations. GLP-1/GIP activate Gs-coupled receptors on pancreatic beta cells, raise cAMP, and amplify glucose-triggered calcium-dependent insulin-granule release; GLP-1 also reduces alpha-cell glucagon when glucose is elevated. The effect fades as glucose falls, explaining low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk. Sitagliptin does not directly close beta-cell KATP channels and has little direct gastric-emptying effect at DPP-4 exposures. Most drug is excreted unchanged through renal filtration and active secretion, so accumulation occurs as eGFR falls.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Postmarketing acute pancreatitis, including hemorrhagic or necrotizing cases, has occurred. Stop and evaluate persistent severe abdominal pain, especially if radiating to the back. Heart-failure risk was seen with other DPP-4 inhibitors; assess dyspnea, edema, and rapid weight gain in susceptible patients. Severe arthralgia and bullous pemphigoid can require discontinuation.

Contraindications

  • History of serious hypersensitivity to sitagliptin

  • Not treatment for type 1 diabetes or DKA

  • Clarify eGFR-based dosing before giving; reassess pancreatitis history, heart-failure risk, and active blistering skin disease.

Nursing Essentials

  • Calculate eGFR before initiation and periodically; 100, 50, and 25 mg daily tiers are used under labeling as renal function declines.

  • Teach patients to report persistent abdominal pain, widespread blisters/erosions, facial swelling, severe joint pain, dyspnea, or edema rather than dismissing them as ordinary diabetes symptoms.

Interactions

  • Insulin and secretagogues add hypoglycemia and may need dose reduction.

  • Digoxin exposure can rise modestly; monitor clinically when relevant.

  • Renal function changes can alter sitagliptin exposure more than CYP interactions because metabolism is limited.

Key Labs

  • eGFR/creatinine for dosing

  • A1c and glucose pattern

  • Lipase/imaging only when pancreatitis is clinically suspected; heart-failure and skin assessment

Nclex Traps

  • A DPP-4 inhibitor is not the same as a GLP-1 receptor agonist and usually does not produce comparable weight loss.

  • Low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk disappears when insulin or sulfonylurea exposure is excessive.

  • Sitagliptin needs renal adjustment; linagliptin generally does not.

Description

Sitagliptin is an oral DPP-4 inhibitor that protects the body's own GLP-1 and GIP incretin hormones from rapid enzymatic breakdown. Active incretins then amplify insulin secretion when glucose is elevated and suppress inappropriate glucagon, so monotherapy has low hypoglycemia risk and is generally weight-neutral. It is less potent for weight loss and gastric slowing than an injected or oral GLP-1 receptor agonist because it extends physiologic hormone levels rather than directly driving the receptor at pharmacologic exposure. Renal elimination makes eGFR-based dose selection central.

Adverse Effects

  • Nasopharyngitis, headache, and mild GI symptoms

  • Pancreatitis, severe hypersensitivity including angioedema/SJS, severe joint pain, and bullous pemphigoid

  • Hypoglycemia mainly when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea; renal accumulation if dose is not adjusted
