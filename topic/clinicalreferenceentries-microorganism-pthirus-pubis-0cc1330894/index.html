Definition

Pthirus pubis is a clinically important ectoparasite associated with pubic pediculosis, genital pruritus, and eyelash infestation. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Treat recent partners and household contacts as directed, avoid sexual contact until treatment is complete, and launder recently used clothing and bedding. The reviewed route description is intimate contact transmission. Itching may be delayed after first infestation and occur sooner with repeat exposure; asymptomatic transmission is possible. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

  • Content

    Pubic lice attach to coarse hair and feed on blood, producing itching and excoriations; spread is usually through close intimate contact. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pthirus pubis is associated with pubic pediculosis, genital pruritus, and eyelash infestation. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
