Class

Long-acting HMG-CoA-reductase inhibitor handled mainly by glucuronidation and transporters; moderate-intensity statin

Used To Treat

LDL-C reduction in current label-defined adults and pediatric patients with primary or familial hypercholesterolemia. Doses of 1-4 mg provide low- to moderate-intensity therapy; 4 mg is the maximum and is not high intensity.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cyclosporine is contraindicated, and erythromycin or rifampin requires strict maximum dosing because transporter and clearance effects can cause myopathy or rhabdomyolysis. Never exceed 4 mg daily.

Mechanism

Pitavastatin competitively inhibits hepatic HMG-CoA reductase, lowers intracellular cholesterol, and increases LDL receptors through SREBP2. UGT1A3 and UGT2B7 convert it to a lactone, with minimal CYP2C9/2C8 contribution. OATP1B1 and related transport systems concentrate it in liver; cyclosporine blocks transport and markedly raises systemic exposure, explaining the contraindication despite minimal CYP3A metabolism. Kidney dysfunction also increases exposure and vulnerability, explaining the 1-mg start and 2-mg maximum in moderate or severe impairment and hemodialysis. Its long activity supports flexible time of day, while the small milligram number reflects potency rather than a uniquely small biological effect.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline lipid panel, exact product and dose, expected intensity, liver and thyroid context, kidney function when relevant, pregnancy plans, muscle history, alcohol, and all interacting medicines or supplements.

  • Teach urgent review for severe weakness, dark urine, oliguria, jaundice, or systemic illness. Mild symptoms still deserve assessment, but one episode does not prove permanent intolerance to every statin.

  • Confirm which salt product is used and avoid automatic milligram substitution between Livalo and Zypitamag without product guidance.

  • Check renal function before choosing 2 or 4 mg and explain that the 4-mg maximum is moderate intensity, not permission to pursue high intensity.

Contraindications

  • Active liver failure or decompensated cirrhosis and serious hypersensitivity are major class barriers; use in stable chronic liver disease is individualized rather than automatically prohibited.

  • Most pregnant patients discontinue because therapy is preventive and fetal cholesterol biology matters. Current labeling permits rare individualized high-risk decisions; breastfeeding is generally not recommended during treatment.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine is contraindicated. Erythromycin limits pitavastatin to 1 mg; rifampin limits it to 2 mg under current labeling.

  • Gemfibrozil is avoided; other fibrates and high-dose niacin add muscle risk. Colchicine can add neuromuscular toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Repeat lipids after initiation or dose change to verify the expected percentage LDL reduction. Obtain baseline hepatic assessment; repeat liver tests for symptoms or clinical concern rather than routine asymptomatic monthly screening.

  • Use CK, creatinine, potassium, phosphate, urinalysis, and urine output for significant muscle symptoms or dark urine. Check TSH or other reversible contributors when the presentation suggests them.

Nclex Traps

  • Statins lower LDL mainly by increasing hepatic LDL-receptor clearance after intracellular cholesterol falls; they do not simply dissolve plaque or block intestinal fat.

  • Intensity is defined by expected percentage LDL reduction, not by comparing milligram numbers across drugs.

  • Pitavastatin has little CYP metabolism but a major cyclosporine interaction through transporters.

  • A 4-mg maximum is pharmacologically potent but still not a high-intensity regimen.

Description

Pitavastatin, branded Livalo or Zypitamag in different salt formulations, is a long-acting statin that produces moderate-intensity LDL lowering at 2-4 mg. It undergoes little CYP metabolism and is handled mainly through UGT1A3/UGT2B7 glucuronidation, reversible lactonization, biliary excretion, and hepatic transporters. This lowers classic CYP3A interaction burden but creates important transporter interactions: cyclosporine is contraindicated, and erythromycin or rifampin imposes strict dose limits. Significant renal impairment requires a 1-mg start and 2-mg maximum. The 4-mg ceiling matters because pitavastatin cannot be converted into high-intensity therapy by further escalation; use atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, or combination therapy when at least 50-percent LDL reduction is required. Long activity allows dosing at any consistent time.

Adverse Effects

  • Muscle symptoms can range from aches with normal CK to myopathy and rare rhabdomyolysis with hyperkalemia and myoglobin-mediated acute kidney injury. Risk rises with greater exposure, interacting drugs, CKD, hypothyroidism, frailty, and acute illness.

  • Mild aminotransferase and glucose or A1c increases can occur. Serious liver failure is rare, and the small diabetes signal usually does not outweigh prevention benefit when the indication is strong.
