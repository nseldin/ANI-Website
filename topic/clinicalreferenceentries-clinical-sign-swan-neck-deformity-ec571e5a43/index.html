Definition

Swan-neck deformity is an observed joint-deformity sign. It is a clue to chronic imbalance across finger joints. A positive finding means the PIP joint hyperextends while the DIP joint flexes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Swan-neck deformity means the PIP joint hyperextends while the DIP joint flexes.

Why It Matters

The observed Swan-neck deformity finding can signal chronic imbalance across finger joints. Volar-plate laxity and tendon imbalance redirect extensor forces across the finger. Swan-neck deformity cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chronic imbalance across finger joints

Assessment Method

Inspect finger posture and active correction.

Positive Finding

The PIP joint hyperextends while the DIP joint flexes.

Mechanism

Volar-plate laxity and tendon imbalance redirect extensor forces across the finger.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Swan-neck deformity finding raises concern for chronic imbalance across finger joints because volar-plate laxity and tendon imbalance redirect extensor forces across the finger. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Swan-neck deformity does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

Early flexible deformity differs from fixed late disease. Therefore, Swan-neck deformity cannot confirm or exclude chronic imbalance across finger joints by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as boutonniere deformity, mallet finger, normal hypermobility.

False Negative Situations

Swan-neck deformity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chronic imbalance across finger joints.
