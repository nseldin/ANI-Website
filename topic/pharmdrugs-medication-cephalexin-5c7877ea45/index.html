Used To Treat

Susceptible skin and soft-tissue, streptococcal respiratory, otitis, bone, and genitourinary infections. It is commonly used orally for uncomplicated cellulitis when streptococci or MSSA are likely and MRSA coverage is not required.

Mechanism

Cephalexin binds bacterial PBPs and prevents transpeptidase-mediated peptidoglycan cross-linking. Continued autolytic wall remodeling then causes lysis in susceptible growing organisms. It is acid stable and orally absorbed, then eliminated largely unchanged by the kidney. Renal impairment prolongs exposure; excessive beta-lactam concentrations can reduce inhibitory GABA signaling and provoke seizures. Probenecid raises concentrations by reducing tubular secretion. Cephalexin can also interfere with selected urine glucose assays and may produce a positive direct Coombs test, so laboratory findings require clinical context.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cephalexin has no current U.S. boxed warning. Serious hypersensitivity, C. difficile-associated diarrhea, direct-Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia, prolonged prothrombin time in vulnerable patients, and seizures with renal accumulation are the major safety themes. A mild remote penicillin intolerance is not the same as anaphylaxis, but a prior severe beta-lactam reaction must be clarified before giving.

Contraindications

  • Known serious cephalexin or cephalosporin hypersensitivity; severe immediate or delayed reactions to related beta-lactams require specialist risk assessment.

  • Do not use oral cephalexin alone for sepsis, meningitis, deep uncontrolled abscess, suspected MRSA without susceptibility, or infections requiring Pseudomonas or anaerobic coverage.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess infection depth, purulence, systemic signs, allergy phenotype, renal function, culture need, swallowing and absorption, and whether drainage or other source control is required.

  • Teach completion of the prescribed course, accurate liquid measurement, and prompt reporting of breathing difficulty, blistering/mucosal rash, jaundice, unusual bleeding, or severe/watery/bloody diarrhea.

Interactions

  • Probenecid raises cephalexin exposure; cephalexin can increase metformin concentrations through renal transporter competition, so glucose and adverse effects may need closer review.

  • Anticoagulation can become less stable during illness, poor intake, and antibiotic therapy; trend INR when clinically indicated.

Key Labs

  • Culture and susceptibility when indicated, renal function, temperature and local infection findings, CBC and PT/INR during prolonged or high-risk therapy.

  • Evaluate anemia with a hemolysis pattern and direct antiglobulin test when clinically indicated; assess significant diarrhea for C. difficile.

Nclex Traps

  • Cephalexin is oral and first generation; cefazolin is the common IV first-generation surgical/MSSA drug.

  • Uncomplicated nonpurulent cellulitis may fit cephalexin, but a fluctuant abscess needs drainage and MRSA risk changes empiric coverage.

Description

Cephalexin is an oral first-generation cephalosporin beta-lactam antibiotic that binds bacterial penicillin-binding proteins and blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking, killing susceptible streptococci and MSSA plus a limited set of gram-negative organisms. Its gram-negative lane includes susceptible E. coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Proteus mirabilis, but it does not reliably treat MRSA, Enterococcus, Listeria, Pseudomonas, atypical organisms, or serious infections that require high parenteral exposure and source control. Because it is familiar and orally convenient, it is easy to overgeneralize. The correct question is whether the organism, infection depth, severity, absorption, and renal function match cephalexin's lane.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, rash, urticaria, vaginitis, and candidiasis.

  • Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN or DRESS, C. difficile colitis, hemolytic anemia, neutropenia, interstitial nephritis, prolonged PT, encephalopathy, and seizures.
