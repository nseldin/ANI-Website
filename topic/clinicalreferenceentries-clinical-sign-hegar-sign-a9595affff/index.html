Definition

Hegar sign is a bimanual uterine sign. It is a clue to early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening. A positive finding means the softened isthmus makes cervix and uterine body feel unusually separate.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hegar sign means the softened isthmus makes cervix and uterine body feel unusually separate.

Why It Matters

The observed Hegar sign finding can signal early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening. Hormonal vascular and stromal remodeling softens the lower uterine segment. Hegar sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening

Assessment Method

During an indicated gentle bimanual examination, assess the lower uterine segment.

Positive Finding

The softened isthmus makes cervix and uterine body feel unusually separate.

Mechanism

Hormonal vascular and stromal remodeling softens the lower uterine segment.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hegar sign finding raises concern for early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening because hormonal vascular and stromal remodeling softens the lower uterine segment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hegar sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hegar sign to focus evaluation of early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening and the reviewed alternatives: fibroid uterus, adenomyosis, full bladder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: During an indicated gentle bimanual examination, assess the lower uterine segment. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hegar sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus, fibroids, and examiner technique limit reliability. Therefore, Hegar sign cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • fibroid uterus

  • adenomyosis

  • full bladder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as fibroid uterus, adenomyosis, full bladder.

False Negative Situations

Hegar sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening.

Modern Status

Historical presumptive sign; ultrasound and testing are definitive. Hegar sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening. Apply the reviewed limitation for Hegar sign: Body habitus, fibroids, and examiner technique limit reliability.

Safety Precautions

Observe Hegar sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: During an indicated gentle bimanual examination, assess the lower uterine segment. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not perform aggressive bimanual palpation in pain, bleeding, or suspected ectopic pregnancy.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Hegar sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The softened isthmus makes cervix and uterine body feel unusually separate. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: During an indicated gentle bimanual examination, assess the lower uterine segment. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, early pregnancy-related uterine-isthmus softening, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Hegar sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not perform aggressive bimanual palpation in pain, bleeding, or suspected ectopic pregnancy. Base escalation for Hegar sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.

Common Pitfalls
