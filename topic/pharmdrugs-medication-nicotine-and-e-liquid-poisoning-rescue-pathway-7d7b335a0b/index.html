Used To Treat

Ingestion, inhalation, dermal, ocular, or mucosal exposure to concentrated nicotine, e-liquid, nicotine salts, pouches, gum, patches, tobacco, or occupational wet tobacco leaves.

Description

Nicotine poisoning often has two phases because nicotinic acetylcholine receptors are first stimulated and then become persistently activated and functionally blocked. Early nausea, vomiting, salivation, abdominal pain, sweating, tremor, agitation, tachycardia, and hypertension can progress to weakness, fasciculations, bradycardia, hypotension, dysrhythmia, respiratory-muscle paralysis, coma, and shock. Some patients present directly in the depressive phase. Concentrated e-liquid is especially hazardous because nicotine can be absorbed through the mouth, lungs, eyes, and skin; contaminated clothing can continue dosing the patient and expose caregivers. Vomiting is common and may limit absorption, but it also increases aspiration risk. There is no universal antidote that restores paralyzed nicotinic receptors. Treatment removes ongoing exposure, supports ventilation and perfusion, treats seizures with benzodiazepines, and uses atropine selectively for clinically important bradycardia or excessive secretions without assuming it reverses neuromuscular paralysis.

Mechanism

Nicotine is an agonist at autonomic-ganglion, adrenal-medulla, central, and neuromuscular nicotinic acetylcholine receptors. Initial channel opening releases catecholamines and activates autonomic and motor pathways, causing hypertension, tachycardia, secretions, vomiting, tremor, and fasciculation. Sustained receptor activation then produces depolarization block and desensitization, so sympathetic tone, ganglionic transmission, and skeletal respiratory-muscle function can fail. Removing wet clothing and washing skin lowers the absorbed dose because liquid nicotine continues crossing intact skin. Ventilation replaces failing respiratory-muscle function while nicotine is redistributed and eliminated. Atropine blocks muscarinic effects downstream and can improve bradycardia or secretions, but it cannot reopen desensitized nicotinic receptors, which explains why airway and breathing support remain decisive.

Administration Timing

  • Call 911 for weakness, breathing difficulty, seizure, fainting, severe vomiting, bradycardia, or altered consciousness. Call Poison Help immediately for any child e-liquid exposure or concentrated nicotine contact; do not wait for the second phase.

  • Move to fresh air, avoid direct contact with spilled liquid, remove contaminated clothing, wash skin with soap and water, and irrigate eyes with lukewarm water. Support airway and ventilation, use benzodiazepines for seizure, fluids and vasopressors for shock, and atropine under protocol for symptomatic bradycardia or excessive secretions.

  • Observe symptomatic or significant concentrated exposures through the period of possible late weakness, dysrhythmia, and respiratory depression. Activated charcoal is considered only very early by specialists with a protected airway because spontaneous vomiting and rapid absorption make aspiration a major tradeoff.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify product concentration in milligrams per milliliter, nicotine salt versus freebase, bottle volume, missing amount, route, time, patches or pouches still present, and whether clothing, furniture, or caregivers were contaminated.

  • Use gloves, bag wet clothing, wash rather than spread liquid, and protect staff from dermal exposure because concentrated nicotine continues crossing skin and can poison both the patient and rescuers. Continuously monitor respiratory effort, secretions, muscle strength, ECG, heart rate, blood pressure, oxygenation, mental status, and urine output because toxicity can shift rapidly from stimulation to neuromuscular, respiratory, and cardiovascular failure.

  • Do not let improvement in vomiting or tachycardia end observation prematurely; document transition toward weakness, bradycardia, hypotension, shallow breathing, or declining reflexes because these signal receptor blockade rather than recovery.

Key Labs

  • Bedside glucose, electrolytes, bicarbonate, blood gas and lactate for severe illness, ECG, and renal and hepatic studies as clinically indicated. Chest imaging follows aspiration or pulmonary symptoms.

  • Nicotine or cotinine concentrations are rarely available quickly enough to direct emergency care and do not replace serial neuromuscular, respiratory, and cardiovascular assessment.

Adverse Effects

  • Severe exposure can cause seizures, dysrhythmias, hypoglycemia, hypotension, respiratory-muscle paralysis, bronchorrhea, aspiration, shock, coma, and death. Eye exposure can cause painful corneal injury.

  • Atropine can cause tachycardia, urinary retention, delirium, and hyperthermia; vasopressors can cause ischemia; and charcoal can cause aspiration. These treatments address selected complications, not the whole nicotinic syndrome.

Contraindications

  • Do not induce vomiting, give antacids, or force oral fluids. Do not handle concentrated e-liquid or contaminated clothing barehanded, because secondary dermal absorption can poison a rescuer.

  • Do not assume atropine reverses nicotinic weakness or paralysis, and do not delay assisted ventilation while treating heart rate or secretions. Charcoal is unsafe through an unprotected airway.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Progressive weakness, fasciculation followed by paralysis, apnea, seizure, dysrhythmia, recurrent bradycardia, shock, severe acidosis, or aspiration requires ICU, poison-center, and toxicology care with early airway readiness.

Interactions

  • Other cholinergic pesticides can resemble nicotine but may produce more persistent muscarinic secretions and require oxime therapy; product and exposure setting separate these mechanisms.

  • Stimulants, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, sedatives, cannabis products, or device flavoring chemicals can change heart rate, mental status, and lung findings, so do not attribute every symptom to nicotine alone.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Young children

    Note

    Brightly packaged concentrated liquids, pouches, and refill bottles deliver a large dose per kilogram and require immediate poison-center assessment even before symptoms.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Maternal hypoxia, seizure, and shock threaten the fetus; decontaminate and stabilize immediately, then add obstetric monitoring.

  • Population

    Workers handling wet tobacco or nicotine

    Note

    Dermal absorption can continue through soaked clothing; workplace decontamination, PPE review, and exposure prevention are part of treatment.

Evidence Limitations

  • Human dose thresholds and product concentrations vary widely, and severe-case evidence is largely observational. CDC/NIOSH guidance supports biphasic recognition and supportive rescue, but exact observation duration remains route-, dose-, symptom-, and poison-center dependent.

Nclex Traps

  • Nicotine can cause early stimulation followed by late receptor blockade and respiratory paralysis.

  • Liquid nicotine crosses skin, so removing contaminated clothing and washing can be lifesaving dose control.

  • Atropine may treat bradycardia or secretions but does not reverse nicotinic neuromuscular paralysis.

Regulatory Status

There is no FDA-approved nicotine-poisoning antidote. Atropine, anticonvulsants, fluids, vasopressors, charcoal, and ventilation are complication- and protocol-directed; nicotine-replacement product labeling does not provide a universal poisoning regimen.

Boxed Warning

NICOTINE WARNING: call 911 for weakness, breathing difficulty, seizure, fainting, or severe vomiting, and call Poison Help for every child e-liquid exposure. Remove contaminated clothing and wash skin because nicotine is absorbed dermally; early stimulation can rapidly become bradycardia, paralysis, and respiratory failure.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning identifies the emergency, explains the mechanism of harm, and separates immediate stabilization from specialist antidote or elimination decisions.
