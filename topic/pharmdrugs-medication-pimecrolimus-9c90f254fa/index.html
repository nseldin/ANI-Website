Class

Topical macrophilin-12-calcineurin inhibitor for mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis

Used To Treat

Second-line short-term and non-continuous chronic treatment of mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis in non-immunocompromised adults and children age 2 years and older.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Long-term safety of topical calcineurin inhibitors has not been established. Rare skin malignancy and lymphoma cases have been reported without proof of causation. Avoid continuous long-term use, apply only to involved atopic-dermatitis areas, and do not use in children younger than 2 years.

Mechanism

The pimecrolimus-macrophilin-12 complex inhibits calcineurin's calcium-dependent phosphatase activity. NFAT cannot dephosphorylate and enter the nucleus, reducing T-cell cytokine transcription and mast-cell mediator release. Skin selectivity and low penetration through intact skin limit systemic exposure, but absorption increases with extensive dermatitis, Netherton syndrome or occlusion. The exact relationship between molecular immune suppression and the clinical malignancy reports remains unproven.

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a thin layer twice daily only to active dermatitis and stop when signs resolve; re-examine if not improving by 6 weeks.

  • Teach sun protection and prompt assessment of persistent lymph nodes, nonhealing lesions or viral vesicles.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to pimecrolimus or cream components

  • Not indicated below age 2 or in immunocompromised patients

  • Avoid malignant/premalignant lesions, active untreated infection, Netherton syndrome, severely impaired barrier and occlusion

Interactions

  • Avoid UV phototherapy, tanning and excessive sun exposure.

  • Systemic CYP3A inhibitors may matter with extensive absorption, although routine limited-area interaction is unlikely.

  • Do not combine with systemic immunosuppression without specialist direction.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level

  • Serial skin/lymph-node examination

  • Specialist assessment or drug level only if unusually extensive systemic absorption is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Pimecrolimus is labeled for mild-to-moderate AD; tacrolimus covers moderate-to-severe AD.

  • A boxed warning remains even though causality for malignancy is not established.

  • Do not keep using it continuously on clear skin as if it were a moisturizer.

Description

Pimecrolimus is a steroid-sparing topical calcineurin inhibitor for mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis. It binds macrophilin-12, an immunophilin related to FKBP12, and suppresses calcineurin-NFAT signaling so activated T cells transcribe fewer inflammatory cytokines. It does not thin skin like a corticosteroid, but it is not a general moisturizer or first-line rash cream: continuous long-term use is avoided and a malignancy boxed warning applies.

Adverse Effects

  • Application-site burning, warmth, stinging and pruritus

  • Headache, cough, nasopharyngitis, pyrexia and viral infections

  • Folliculitis, herpes viral skin infection and lymphadenopathy

  • Rare angioedema, anaphylaxis or severe skin reaction
