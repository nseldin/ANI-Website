Used To Treat

Relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis in adults, including clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive MS.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for siponimod in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Siponimod binds S1P1 and S1P5. Repeated S1P1 engagement causes receptor internalization on lymphocytes, preventing egress from lymph nodes and lowering circulating lymphocytes available to enter the CNS.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before therapy: CYP2C9 genotype, CBC, liver tests, ECG/cardiac review, varicella immunity, medication interactions, eye exam when diabetes/uveitis risk, pregnancy counseling, and skin evaluation.

  • Use the required starter titration and first-dose observation when indicated; monitor bradycardia/AV block, BP, infection/PML, macular edema, breathing, liver injury, skin cancer, and rebound after discontinuation.

Description

Siponimod is an S1P1/S1P5 receptor modulator that functionally traps autoreactive lymphocytes in lymph nodes by internalizing S1P1, reducing their migration into the CNS; S1P5 activity may also affect CNS cells. CYP2C9 genotype determines exposure and dose, while first-dose bradycardia, infection, macular edema, liver injury, hypertension, respiratory effects, and rebound disease after stopping shape care.
