Definition

Herald patch is an observed early-lesion sign. It is a clue to pityriasis rosea. A positive finding means one larger plaque precedes the generalized eruption.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Herald patch means one larger plaque precedes the generalized eruption.

Why It Matters

The observed Herald patch finding can signal pityriasis rosea. A localized initial inflammatory lesion appears before the disseminated immune eruption. Herald patch cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pityriasis rosea

Assessment Method

Ask about and inspect for a larger initial oval scaly plaque.

Positive Finding

One larger plaque precedes the generalized eruption.

Mechanism

A localized initial inflammatory lesion appears before the disseminated immune eruption.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Herald patch finding raises concern for pityriasis rosea because a localized initial inflammatory lesion appears before the disseminated immune eruption. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Herald patch does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Herald patch to focus evaluation of pityriasis rosea and the reviewed alternatives: tinea, fixed drug eruption, erythema migrans. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask about and inspect for a larger initial oval scaly plaque. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Herald patch as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Ringworm commonly mimics the solitary stage and a herald patch may never be found. Therefore, Herald patch cannot confirm or exclude pityriasis rosea by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tinea

  • fixed drug eruption

  • erythema migrans

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tinea, fixed drug eruption, erythema migrans.

False Negative Situations

Herald patch may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of pityriasis rosea.

Modern Status

Current supportive clue; absent in many cases. Interpret Herald patch in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Ringworm commonly mimics the solitary stage and a herald patch may never be found.

Safety Precautions

Observe Herald patch with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask about and inspect for a larger initial oval scaly plaque. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not start systemic treatment from one ring-like plaque without checking fungal features.
