Class

Selective complement C5a receptor antagonist

Description

Avacopan is an oral complement-pathway drug for severe active ANCA-associated vasculitis. It blocks the receptor that lets C5a prime and recruit neutrophils into small-vessel walls. This interrupts a major inflammatory amplification loop without blocking formation of the terminal complement membrane-attack complex. It is an adjunct to standard immunosuppressive therapy and can reduce glucocorticoid exposure, but the label specifically warns that it does not eliminate glucocorticoid use.

Mechanism

C5a binds C5a receptor 1 on neutrophils, increasing chemotaxis, adhesion, oxidative burst, and release of enzymes that damage vessel walls. Avacopan selectively antagonizes that receptor. The ANCA-triggered neutrophil response therefore receives less C5a amplification, reducing capillaritis and organ injury. The drug does not remove circulating ANCA, replace induction immunosuppression, or neutralize every complement function.

Used To Treat

Adjunctive treatment of adults with severe active granulomatosis with polyangiitis or microscopic polyangiitis in combination with standard therapy, including glucocorticoids as clinically required.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. Major warnings are serious hepatotoxicity, hypersensitivity including angioedema, hepatitis B reactivation, and serious infection.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to avacopan or an excipient.

  • Not recommended in cirrhosis, especially severe hepatic impairment; active serious infection and current or prior hepatitis B require specialist assessment before treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, headache, hypertension, diarrhea, vomiting, rash, fatigue, upper abdominal pain, dizziness, increased creatinine, and paresthesia.

  • Serious liver injury, angioedema or anaphylaxis, hepatitis B reactivation, and serious infection.

Interactions

  • Avoid strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers because they can lower avacopan exposure.

  • Reduce avacopan with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor according to the label. Avacopan can increase exposure to sensitive CYP3A4 substrates, so narrow-therapeutic-index drugs need review.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, HBsAg, and anti-HBc. Coordinate antiviral monitoring or prophylaxis when prior or current HBV is present.

  • Track renal, pulmonary, skin, neurologic, and constitutional vasculitis findings so disease progression is not mistaken for a medication side effect or vice versa.

  • Stop and urgently assess jaundice, dark urine, right-upper-quadrant pain, facial or tongue swelling, dyspnea, or serious infection.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin before and during therapy

  • HBsAg and anti-HBc before treatment

  • Creatinine, urinalysis, inflammatory and disease-activity measures

Administration

  • Take 30 mg twice daily with food unless the label-directed strong-CYP3A-inhibitor reduction applies. Swallow capsules whole.

  • Use with the complete vasculitis regimen; do not stop glucocorticoids or another immunosuppressant abruptly without the treating team.

Patient Education

  • Report yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe abdominal pain, fever, cough, painful rash, facial swelling, or breathing difficulty immediately.

  • Keep hepatitis, liver-test, infection, and vasculitis follow-up. Avoid grapefruit or new supplements until the interaction profile is reviewed.

Nclex Traps

  • Avacopan reduces C5a-driven inflammation but does not replace the companion immunosuppressive regimen used to control ANCA-associated vasculitis.

  • Liver injury, hepatitis B reactivation, and serious infection can resemble a disease flare; objective testing and escalation are required before simply intensifying therapy.

Routes

  • Oral capsules taken with food according to the scheduled twice-daily regimen in current labeling.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Drug-induced liver injury or severe transaminase and bilirubin elevation.

  • Hepatitis B reactivation, serious infection, or angioedema.

Toxicity Management

  • No specific antidote exists. Stop or hold treatment as directed for serious liver injury, hypersensitivity, or infection; obtain urgent liver tests, viral evaluation, airway support, and specialist guidance based on the presentation.

Special Populations

  • Pregnancy and lactation data are limited; use requires specialist benefit-risk review.

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established. Older adults require the same infection, liver, and polypharmacy review as other adults.

  • Avoid use in severe hepatic impairment, including cirrhosis, and follow current labeling for other hepatic or renal impairment because exposure and safety data are limited.
