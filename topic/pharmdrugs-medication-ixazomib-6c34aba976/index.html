Class

Oral reversible 20S proteasome beta-5 inhibitor prodrug

Used To Treat

With lenalidomide and dexamethasone for multiple myeloma after at least one prior therapy; it is not recommended as maintenance or for newly diagnosed disease outside controlled trials.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Monitor platelets, GI losses, neuropathy, edema, liver injury, severe rash, and TMA; discontinue if TMA is suspected. Increased mortality occurred in maintenance trials, so do not improvise a maintenance indication outside controlled research.

Mechanism

Active ixazomib reversibly binds the beta-5 subunit of the 20S proteasome. Ubiquitinated proteins accumulate, endoplasmic-reticulum stress rises, and myeloma survival signaling fails, producing apoptosis; lenalidomide adds cereblon-mediated protein degradation and dexamethasone adds lymphoid cytotoxicity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give on the same weekly day at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after food; swallow intact and distinguish ixazomib days from lenalidomide/dexamethasone days.

  • Avoid strong CYP3A inducers, verify renal/hepatic dose context, and teach immediate reporting of bleeding, persistent diarrhea/vomiting, neuropathy, rash, jaundice, confusion, or reduced urine output.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets at least monthly and more often early, CMP/liver tests, renal function, weight/edema, and hemolysis/TMA studies when suspected.

Nclex Traps

  • Ixazomib is weekly, not daily; a schedule error can cause fatal overdose.

  • A high-fat meal markedly lowers exposure, which is why empty-stomach timing matters.

Description

Ixazomib is an oral reversible proteasome-inhibitor prodrug that hydrolyzes to active ixazomib, blocks beta-5 chymotrypsin-like proteasome activity, accumulates ubiquitinated proteins, and drives proteotoxic apoptosis in myeloma cells. Weekly empty-stomach dosing is easy to misread, while thrombocytopenia, GI toxicity, neuropathy, edema, severe rash, hepatotoxicity, and thrombotic microangiopathy require monitoring.
