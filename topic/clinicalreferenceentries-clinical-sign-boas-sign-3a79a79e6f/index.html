Definition

Boas sign is a bedside palpation symptom-sign. It is a clue to gallbladder inflammation. A positive finding means focal right infrascapular tenderness or altered sensation is reported.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Boas sign means focal right infrascapular tenderness or altered sensation is reported.

Why It Matters

The provoked Boas sign symptom can signal gallbladder inflammation. Shared lower thoracic sensory pathways can refer gallbladder pain to the posterior trunk. Boas sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gallbladder inflammation

Assessment Method

Ask about altered sensation and gently assess for tenderness below the right scapula.

Positive Finding

Focal right infrascapular tenderness or altered sensation is reported.

Mechanism

Shared lower thoracic sensory pathways can refer gallbladder pain to the posterior trunk.

Clinical Interpretation

The provoked Boas sign symptom raises concern for gallbladder inflammation because shared lower thoracic sensory pathways can refer gallbladder pain to the posterior trunk. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Boas sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Boas sign to focus evaluation of gallbladder inflammation and the reviewed alternatives: myofascial pain, herpes zoster, pleurisy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask about altered sensation and gently assess for tenderness below the right scapula. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Boas sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Diagnostic accuracy is poor and definitions vary. Therefore, Boas sign cannot confirm or exclude gallbladder inflammation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • myofascial pain

  • herpes zoster

  • pleurisy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive response can reflect another painful or sensory process such as myofascial pain, herpes zoster, pleurisy, or a trigger that was not applied as defined.

False Negative Situations

Boas sign may not reproduce when the trigger is incomplete, symptoms are intermittent, treatment has reduced the response, or communication is limited.

Modern Status

Historical and nonspecific. Boas sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude gallbladder inflammation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Boas sign: Diagnostic accuracy is poor and definitions vary.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Boas sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask about altered sensation and gently assess for tenderness below the right scapula. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not diagnose biliary disease from scapular tenderness alone.

Nursing Relevance

Record the patient's description of Boas sign, the exact trigger, distribution, duration, and associated objective findings; the reviewed positive response is: Focal right infrascapular tenderness or altered sensation is reported. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask about altered sensation and gently assess for tenderness below the right scapula. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, gallbladder inflammation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
