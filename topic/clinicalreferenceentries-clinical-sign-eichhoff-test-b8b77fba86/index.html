Definition

Eichhoff test is a bedside tendon-provocation maneuver. It is a clue to de Quervain tenosynovitis. A positive finding means radial styloid pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Eichhoff test means radial styloid pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Eichhoff test response can signal de Quervain tenosynovitis. The combined position tensions first-compartment tendons, often more forcefully than Finkelstein testing. Eichhoff test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • de Quervain tenosynovitis

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to fold the thumb into the palm, wrap fingers over it, and ulnar-deviate the wrist gently.

Positive Finding

Radial styloid pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

The combined position tensions first-compartment tendons, often more forcefully than Finkelstein testing.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Eichhoff test response raises concern for de Quervain tenosynovitis because the combined position tensions first-compartment tendons, often more forcefully than Finkelstein testing. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Eichhoff test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Eichhoff test to focus evaluation of de Quervain tenosynovitis and the reviewed alternatives: Wartenberg syndrome, intersection syndrome, scaphoid injury. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to fold the thumb into the palm, wrap fingers over it, and ulnar-deviate the wrist gently. Evidence Limitations

It commonly provokes pain in healthy wrists. Therefore, Eichhoff test cannot confirm or exclude de Quervain tenosynovitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Wartenberg syndrome

  • intersection syndrome

  • scaphoid injury

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from Wartenberg syndrome, intersection syndrome, scaphoid injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Eichhoff test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct with more false positives and discomfort. When performed as described, Eichhoff test can adjust concern for de Quervain tenosynovitis, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Eichhoff test: It commonly provokes pain in healthy wrists.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Eichhoff test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to fold the thumb into the palm, wrap fingers over it, and ulnar-deviate the wrist gently. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not mislabeled this as Finkelstein or push through severe pain.
