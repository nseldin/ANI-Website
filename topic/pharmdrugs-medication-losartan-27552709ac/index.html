Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric ages; reduction of stroke risk in selected patients with hypertension and left-ventricular hypertrophy; and diabetic nephropathy in type 2 diabetes with hypertension, elevated creatinine, and proteinuria according to labeling.

Mechanism

Losartan and its active metabolite EXP3174 selectively block AT1 receptors. Angiotensin II may rise through loss of feedback, but it cannot efficiently activate AT1-mediated Gq, phospholipase-C, inositol-trisphosphate, intracellular-calcium, vascular-contraction, and aldosterone pathways while receptors are blocked. About 14% of an oral dose is converted through CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 pathways to this metabolite, which is substantially more potent by weight and has greater exposure than the parent. Efferent dilation lowers intraglomerular pressure and albumin leakage, explaining nephropathy benefit and possible early creatinine rise. Lower aldosterone reduces distal potassium secretion, explaining hyperkalemia. Losartan's uricosuric effect is related to renal urate transport rather than AT1 blockade alone and is modest, so it is a useful distinction rather than a substitute for gout evaluation.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Losartan blocks fetal AT1 signaling. Stop promptly when pregnancy is detected; replacing an ACE inhibitor with losartan does not create pregnancy safety.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, evolving AKI, major volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify which indication is being targeted. For nephropathy, trend urine albumin/protein and renal trajectory, not only pressure.

  • Do not promise that losartan prevents every stroke in every population; its LVH indication has population-specific evidence and labeling limitations.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Interactions

  • Fluconazole can reduce formation of the active metabolite through CYP2C9 inhibition, while rifampin can lower parent and metabolite exposure; interpret unexpected loss of control with the medication list.

  • Potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin or protein when nephropathy is treated, and uric acid only when clinically relevant.

  • Assess ECG and urgent potassium treatment when severe hyperkalemia develops rather than waiting for routine follow-up.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Losartan blocks the AT1 receptor; it does not lower angiotensin II production. Angiotensin II can rise while its main harmful receptor is blocked.

  • A small uric-acid reduction is a bonus pharmacologic distinction, not proof that gout is adequately treated.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Losartan is an angiotensin-II receptor blocker that occupies the AT1 receptor, preventing vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, sodium retention, efferent-arteriole constriction, and maladaptive remodeling. It does not inhibit ACE or directly raise bradykinin, so dry cough is much less characteristic than with lisinopril. Losartan undergoes first-pass metabolism to the active carboxylic-acid metabolite EXP3174, also called E-3174, which produces much of the sustained AT1 blockade; the parent half-life is only about two hours while the metabolite persists roughly six to nine hours. Its labeled distinctions are hypertension, diabetic nephropathy in a defined type-2-diabetes population, and stroke-risk reduction in selected hypertensive patients with LVH. Losartan also has a small uricosuric effect that can modestly lower uric acid, but that does not turn it into primary gout therapy or remove the need to monitor potassium and kidney function.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness, orthostatic symptoms, hyperkalemia, creatinine increase, and fatigue can occur; cough incidence is generally near placebo rather than the ACE-inhibitor pattern.

  • The diabetic-nephropathy population has meaningful hyperkalemia risk because impaired renal potassium clearance and reduced aldosterone act together.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
