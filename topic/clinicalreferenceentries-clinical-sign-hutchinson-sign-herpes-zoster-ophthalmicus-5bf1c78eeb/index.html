Definition

Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) is an observed skin-distribution sign. It is a clue to nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster. A positive finding means vesicles involve the tip or side of the nose.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) means vesicles involve the tip or side of the nose.

Why It Matters

The observed Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) finding can signal nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster. The nasociliary branch supplies both nasal skin and important ocular structures, so its involvement raises ocular-risk probability. Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster

Assessment Method

Inspect the nose tip, side, and root in a V1 vesicular eruption.

Positive Finding

Vesicles involve the tip or side of the nose.

Mechanism

The nasociliary branch supplies both nasal skin and important ocular structures, so its involvement raises ocular-risk probability.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) finding raises concern for nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster because the nasociliary branch supplies both nasal skin and important ocular structures, so its involvement raises ocular-risk probability. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) to focus evaluation of nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster and the reviewed alternatives: contact dermatitis, HSV, nasal trauma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the nose tip, side, and root in a V1 vesicular eruption. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Many patients without the sign still develop ocular complications. Therefore, Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) cannot confirm or exclude nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as contact dermatitis, HSV, nasal trauma.

False Negative Situations

Hutchinson sign (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of nasociliary involvement in ophthalmic zoster.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not confuse with Hutchinson nail sign; obtain urgent eye evaluation for ocular symptoms or V1 zoster.
