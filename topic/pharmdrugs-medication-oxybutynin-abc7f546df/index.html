Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Serious hypersensitivity/angioedema

  • Use extreme caution with severe GI dysmotility, myasthenia gravis, dementia, outlet obstruction and autonomic neuropathy

Nursing Essentials

  • Measure residual volume and bowel/cognitive baseline; frequency from overflow retention becomes worse when detrusor contraction is blocked.

  • Teach hydration/oral care without overhydrating heart/renal patients, constipation prevention, heat precautions and no driving until CNS response is known.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, sedatives, opioids and alcohol increase delirium, falls, constipation and retention.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase oxybutynin exposure.

  • Anticholinergic slowing can increase risk from solid oral potassium products and oppose cholinergic drugs.

Key Labs

  • Post-void residual and renal consequences of retention

  • Bowel pattern, cognition, falls and temperature tolerance

  • Glaucoma history and symptom/voiding diary

Nclex Traps

  • A hot, confused patient taking oxybutynin may be unable to sweat normally.

  • Oxybutynin treats storage overactivity, not nonobstructive retention.

  • Transdermal therapy may reduce first-pass active-metabolite exposure but still carries anticholinergic warnings.

Used To Treat

Overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge incontinence; extended-release tablets also treat neurogenic detrusor overactivity in patients age 6 years and older under current labeling. Oral, patch and gel formulations have different exposure and teaching.

Mechanism

Competitive muscarinic antagonism, especially at detrusor M3 receptors, interrupts Gq-phospholipase-C-IP3 calcium release and smooth-muscle contraction. Oxybutynin and its active N-desethyl metabolite are lipophilic tertiary amines that can enter the CNS. Blocking salivary-gland M3 secretion causes dry mouth, while reduced sweat-gland signaling prevents evaporative cooling and explains heat prostration risk; reduced gut and bladder smooth-muscle activity explains ileus and retention.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with urinary retention, gastric retention or uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma. CNS anticholinergic effects can be severe, especially after initiation or dose escalation. Decreased sweating can cause fever and heat prostration. Angioedema may threaten the airway.

Description

Oxybutynin is a CNS-penetrant antimuscarinic that suppresses involuntary detrusor contraction by blocking acetylcholine-driven muscarinic signaling. It can improve urgency but has one of the clearest systemic anticholinergic burdens in the class: dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, urinary retention, impaired sweating/heat illness, somnolence, confusion and hallucinations.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, nausea, blurred vision and dry eyes

  • Somnolence, dizziness, confusion, hallucinations, agitation and memory impairment

  • Urinary retention and increased residual volume

  • Reduced sweating, heat intolerance, tachycardia and rare angioedema

Class Pathway

  • Tertiary-amine bladder antimuscarinic; predominantly M3-mediated detrusor antispasmodic
