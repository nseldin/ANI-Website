Used To Treat

Hypertension and diabetic nephropathy in patients with type 2 diabetes, hypertension, elevated serum creatinine, and proteinuria greater than 300 mg/day according to labeling.

Mechanism

Irbesartan directly and selectively blocks AT1 receptors. Angiotensin II can no longer effectively activate Gq-mediated vascular contraction, aldosterone release, proximal sodium transport, sympathetic facilitation, or profibrotic signaling. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers glomerular capillary pressure, reducing protein passage and long-term barotrauma. In established diabetic nephropathy, this can slow loss of kidney function even though an early hemodynamic eGFR dip occurs. Reduced aldosterone lowers distal potassium secretion, while diabetic hyporeninemic states and CKD may already limit potassium handling; this explains the notable hyperkalemia rate in nephropathy trials. Irbesartan does not inhibit kininase II, so dry cough rates remain near placebo rather than the ACE-inhibitor pattern.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Irbesartan must be discontinued when pregnancy is detected. The drug's kidney-protective adult role becomes kidney-toxic to the fetus because developmental physiology is different.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, acute renal deterioration, marked volume depletion, or symptomatic hypotension.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • For nephropathy, establish the baseline proteinuria, creatinine, eGFR, potassium, pressure, diabetes control, and volume state, then trend kidney outcomes rather than expecting creatinine to improve immediately.

  • Correct major salt or volume depletion before initiation because the lower starting exposure exists to prevent excessive first-dose hypotension.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Interactions

  • Potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class; diabetic CKD magnifies their clinical effect.

  • Amlodipine can lower pressure but does not reproduce irbesartan's intraglomerular and proteinuria mechanism.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin or 24-hour protein context, glucose/A1c, and volume.

  • Evaluate potassium above 6 mEq/L or ECG changes urgently; nephropathy trials demonstrate that severe elevation is not theoretical.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Kidney protection does not mean creatinine must fall. Lower glomerular pressure can cause an early dip while reducing long-term protein-driven injury.

  • Irbesartan's nephropathy indication is defined by type 2 diabetes, hypertension, elevated creatinine, and proteinuria; avoid replacing that precision with generic 'renal drug' language.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Irbesartan is an active, long-acting angiotensin-II AT1-receptor blocker (ARB) that prevents vasoconstrictor and aldosterone signaling. It lowers systemic pressure, aldosterone signaling, and intraglomerular pressure without directly inhibiting ACE or increasing bradykinin. Its defining U.S. indication is diabetic nephropathy in a specific population with type 2 diabetes, hypertension, elevated creatinine, and substantial proteinuria. In that population, irbesartan slows progression measured by doubling of creatinine or end-stage kidney disease. The renal benefit comes from reducing glomerular hypertension and protein leakage, not from increasing filtration. That is why creatinine can rise modestly at first even while long-term nephron stress falls. The same patients are vulnerable to hyperkalemia because diabetes, CKD, and reduced aldosterone converge. Irbesartan can be taken with or without food and is not a prodrug, making its pharmacology simpler than candesartan cilexetil but its monitoring no less important.

Adverse Effects

  • In diabetic nephropathy, hyperkalemia, orthostatic dizziness, and orthostatic hypotension are especially important.

  • Progressive renal decline can occur when volume, pressure, NSAIDs, or renal-artery disease make filtration RAAS dependent.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
