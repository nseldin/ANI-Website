Summary

12-lead ECG (12-lead electrocardiogram) is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Records cardiac electrical activity from 12 views to identify ischemia, rhythm, conduction, chamber, and electrolyte clues.

Quick Answer

12-lead ECG (12-lead electrocardiogram): Records cardiac electrical activity from 12 views to identify ischemia, rhythm, conduction, chamber, and electrolyte clues. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Normal / no acute change

    Meaning

    No obvious rhythm, conduction, or ischemic pattern is captured during the tracing; intermittent ischemia or dysrhythmia can still be missed.

  • Label

    Abnormal / positive finding

    Meaning

    ST elevation/depression, T-wave changes, pathologic Q waves, dysrhythmias, blocks, QT prolongation, hypertrophy, or electrolyte patterns can redirect urgent care.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Interpret with symptoms and timing. Chest pain with dynamic ECG changes is high priority even before biomarkers return.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Records cardiac electrical activity from 12 views to identify ischemia, rhythm, conduction, chamber, and electrolyte clues.

    • Before / preparation

    • Assess chest pain, perfusion, rhythm, oxygenation, allergies, anticoagulant status, kidney function if contrast is possible, and baseline vital signs.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect cardiac test results to perfusion, ischemia, dysrhythmia, heart failure, and immediate safety priorities.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Chest pain with ECG changes, rising cardiac biomarkers, unstable dysrhythmia, hypotension, syncope, pulmonary edema, or post-procedure bleeding.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Treat the client and the trend, not one pretty tracing or one isolated number.
