Class

PEGylated humanized anti-TNF-alpha Fab fragment without an Fc region

Used To Treat

Product-specific Crohn disease, rheumatoid arthritis, polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, nonradiographic axial spondyloarthritis, and plaque psoriasis. It is subcutaneous maintenance therapy, not acute treatment for a flare or infection.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: serious infections and malignancy. TB, bacterial sepsis, invasive fungal infections, and other opportunistic pathogens can lead to hospitalization or death. Test for latent TB and treat before therapy, monitor for active TB even after a negative baseline test, and discontinue for serious infection or sepsis. Lymphoma and other malignancies, some fatal, have occurred in pediatric and adolescent patients receiving TNF blockers. Fc-free structure changes placental transport; it does not remove the host-defense warning.

Mechanism

The Fab prime fragment binds soluble and transmembrane TNF-alpha and sterically prevents TNFR1/TNFR2 engagement, reducing NF-kappaB/MAPK inflammatory signaling, endothelial adhesion, chemokines, and tissue injury. Because no Fc domain is present, the molecule cannot bind C1q or Fc-gamma receptors for complement or ADCC and is not actively carried across placenta by FcRn. PEGylation increases hydrodynamic size, reduces renal filtration, and extends half-life. TNF neutralization still impairs macrophage activation and granuloma integrity, explaining TB and fungal risk independent of Fc. Certolizumab can also interfere with some aPTT assays, producing an erroneously prolonged result without true anticoagulation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify loading versus maintenance schedule, whether a lyophilized clinic product or prefilled syringe is used, injection training, storage, and TB/HBV/vaccine screening.

  • Flag unexpected isolated aPTT prolongation to the laboratory and team because assay interference can trigger unnecessary plasma or delay if mistaken for coagulopathy.

Contraindications

  • Known serious hypersensitivity or uncontrolled serious infection; resolve TB, HBV, vaccine, wound, and endemic-exposure plans first.

  • Use caution with heart failure, demyelination, malignancy, recurrent infection, cytopenia, older age, and laboratory interpretation of aPTT.

Interactions

  • Do not combine with anakinra, abatacept, another biologic, another TNF blocker, or a JAK inhibitor because serious infection rises.

  • Avoid live vaccines. Other immunosuppressants add infection risk; exact pregnancy and infant-vaccine planning remains specialist directed despite low placental transfer.

Key Labs

  • TB and hepatitis B assessment, CBC/liver context, infection evaluation, and disease activity

  • Pregnancy plan, injection reactions, neurologic/heart-failure symptoms, skin surveillance, and confirmation of suspicious aPTT results with an unaffected method

Nclex Traps

  • Certolizumab is the Fc-free PEGylated Fab TNF blocker; PEG replaces Fc-mediated half-life support but not TNF-related infection risk.

  • A prolonged aPTT can be assay interference rather than bleeding physiology, so correlate with the patient and method.

Description

Certolizumab pegol is a humanized antigen-binding fragment that neutralizes TNF-alpha but deliberately lacks the antibody Fc region. A polyethylene-glycol chain prolongs circulation so the fragment can be dosed intermittently. Without Fc, certolizumab does not use complement-dependent cytotoxicity, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, or active neonatal-Fc-receptor transport across the placenta. Clinical pregnancy studies found negligible to low placental transfer in most exposed infants, a meaningful distinction from intact IgG1 TNF antibodies, but it does not erase infection screening or individualized pregnancy decisions. The drug still blocks TNF-dependent granuloma maintenance and therefore retains boxed TB, opportunistic-infection, and malignancy warnings.

Certolizumab pegol

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory or urinary infection, rash, injection-site reaction, headache, and antibody-mediated loss of response

  • TB/HBV reactivation, invasive fungal infection, malignancy, heart-failure worsening, demyelination, cytopenia, lupus-like syndrome, severe hypersensitivity, and erroneous aPTT prolongation
