Definition

Groove sign is an observed inguinal-node sign. It is a clue to lymphogranuloma venereum. A positive finding means the ligament creates a groove between enlarged node groups above and below it.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Groove sign means the ligament creates a groove between enlarged node groups above and below it.

Why It Matters

The observed Groove sign finding can signal lymphogranuloma venereum. Lymphatic infection enlarges both inguinal and femoral nodes separated by the fixed ligament. Groove sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lymphogranuloma venereum

Assessment Method

Inspect enlarged inguinal nodes relative to the inguinal ligament.

Positive Finding

The ligament creates a groove between enlarged node groups above and below it.

Mechanism

Lymphatic infection enlarges both inguinal and femoral nodes separated by the fixed ligament.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Groove sign finding raises concern for lymphogranuloma venereum because lymphatic infection enlarges both inguinal and femoral nodes separated by the fixed ligament. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Groove sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Groove sign to focus evaluation of lymphogranuloma venereum and the reviewed alternatives: bacterial adenitis, hernia, lymphoma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect enlarged inguinal nodes relative to the inguinal ligament. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Groove sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is uncommon and nodal anatomy varies. Therefore, Groove sign cannot confirm or exclude lymphogranuloma venereum by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • bacterial adenitis

  • hernia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as bacterial adenitis, hernia, lymphoma.

False Negative Situations

Groove sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of lymphogranuloma venereum.
