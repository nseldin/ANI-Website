Definition

TACO develops when transfusion-associated circulatory overload raises hydrostatic pulmonary pressure when transfused volume or rate exceeds cardiovascular reserve. The characteristic pattern is dyspnea, orthopnea, hypertension, jugular venous distention, crackles, positive fluid balance, and pulmonary edema during or within hours of transfusion. Confirm or stage it with stop transfusion and assess oxygenation, blood pressure, neck veins, balance, and response to diuresis; distinguish it from TRALI, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, bacterial contamination, and underlying heart failure by pressure, volume signs, fever, hemolysis, and cardiac context. Signs Symptoms

  • Fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, fever, bone pain, lymphadenopathy, weight loss, neurologic change, or transfusion symptoms guide hematology/oncology priority.

  • Escalate neutropenic fever, active bleeding, severe anemia symptoms, tumor lysis signs, hypercalcemia crisis, or transfusion reaction.

Diagnostics

  • stop transfusion and assess oxygenation, blood pressure, neck veins, balance, and response to diuresis

  • chest imaging and natriuretic peptide trend when useful

  • blood-bank reaction evaluation to distinguish other transfusion events

  • Distinguish TACO from TRALI, anaphylaxis, hemolysis, bacterial contamination, and underlying heart failure by pressure, volume signs, fever, hemolysis, and cardiac context.

Labs

  • hemoglobin/hematocrit may fall with anemia or bleeding

  • platelets may fall with bleeding risk

  • ANC below 500 means severe neutropenia risk

  • calcium, potassium, phosphate, and uric acid matter in tumor lysis

Treatments

  • stop the transfusion and sit the patient upright

  • give oxygen and prescribed diuresis while supporting ventilation

  • prevent recurrence with slower rate, smaller aliquots, and individualized volume planning

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for severe hypertension or hypotension, frothy sputum, rapidly falling oxygen saturation, chest pain, or respiratory fatigue because noninvasive or invasive ventilatory support may be required.

  • Stop or pause the transfusion and notify the treating clinician and transfusion service because additional volume can worsen transfusion-associated circulatory overload.

  • Sit the patient upright, apply oxygen, and administer ordered diuretic therapy because reducing venous congestion can improve cardiogenic pulmonary edema.

  • Monitor blood pressure, oxygen saturation, lung sounds, jugular venous pressure, urine output, fluid balance, and weight to detect worsening overload and response.

  • Trend the disease-specific findings of dyspnea, orthopnea, hypertension, jugular venous distention, crackles, positive fluid balance, and pulmonary edema during or within hours of transfusion and escalate immediately for acute pulmonary edema can complicate TACO. because transfusion-associated circulatory overload raises hydrostatic pulmonary pressure when transfused volume or rate exceeds cardiovascular reserve.

Complications

  • acute pulmonary edema can complicate TACO.

  • respiratory failure can complicate TACO.

  • myocardial ischemia can complicate TACO.

  • arrhythmia can complicate TACO.

  • death can complicate TACO.

Red Flags

  • Acute dyspnea with hypertension and pulmonary edema during or after transfusion

  • Frothy sputum, diffuse crackles, or rapidly falling oxygen saturation

  • Marked jugular venous distention or positive fluid balance

  • Chest pain, respiratory fatigue, or hemodynamic instability

Patient Education

  • Explain that TACO results from the circulation receiving volume faster than it can tolerate, which can guide slower rates or divided components later.

  • Teach the patient to report breathlessness, chest pressure, swelling, or sudden cough immediately during future transfusions.

Nclex Traps

  • Hypertension and volume signs favor TACO over TRALI but no single finding is definitive

  • Do not restart the same unit after acute respiratory distress until the reaction is evaluated
