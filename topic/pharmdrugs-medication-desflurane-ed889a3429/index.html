Class

Very-low-blood-gas-solubility pungent volatile fluorinated general anesthetic

Used To Treat

Maintenance of general anesthesia and selected adult induction after airway planning; it is not used for pediatric inhaled induction and is poorly suited to smooth mask induction because it irritates the airway.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in known/suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility and for pediatric inhaled induction. It is not approved for maintenance in nonintubated children because coughing, secretions and laryngospasm are increased. Rapid concentration increases can cause marked tachycardia/hypertension, especially in coronary disease. Replace desiccated CO2 absorbent to prevent carbon-monoxide exposure.

Mechanism

Desflurane suppresses cortical and spinal networks through multiple inhibitory and excitatory ion-channel targets. Very low blood-gas partition means little anesthetic dissolves in blood before alveolar and brain partial pressures equilibrate, explaining fast titration and washout. Airway sensory irritation and abrupt sympathetic activation distinguish it from sevoflurane. Desiccated CO2 absorbents can degrade desflurane and generate carbon monoxide.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the heated desflurane-specific vaporizer, secured airway, fresh absorbent, scavenging and continuous gas/hemodynamic monitoring. Do not substitute into another vaporizer.

  • Avoid abrupt high-concentration increases when tachycardia or hypertension would be dangerous. In children, know the label limits for nonintubated maintenance and airway-reactivity risk.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility, pediatric inhaled induction, general-anesthesia contraindication, halogenated-agent hypersensitivity, or prior unexplained moderate/severe hepatic dysfunction after a halogenated anesthetic.

Interactions

  • Succinylcholine increases malignant-hyperthermia risk. Opioids, nitrous oxide and sedatives lower MAC. Desflurane potentiates nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers. Beta blockers or other cardiovascular drugs alter the response to sympathetic surges.

Key Labs

  • Continuous inspired/end-tidal anesthetic, end-tidal CO2, oxygenation, ECG, blood pressure and temperature

  • Carboxyhemoglobin if unexplained hypoxia follows suspected desiccated-absorbent exposure

  • Malignant-hyperthermia labs including potassium, acid-base, lactate, creatine kinase and urine myoglobin when indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Fast emergence does not mean gentle airway behavior.

  • Desflurane requires its own heated pressurized vaporizer.

  • Do not use it for pediatric inhaled induction.

Description

Desflurane is a pungent volatile general anesthetic that suppresses cortical and spinal neural activity to produce unconsciousness and immobility; exceptionally low blood-gas solubility makes anesthetic-depth changes and emergence rapid. Its low boiling point requires a heated, pressurized agent-specific vaporizer. Pungency provokes coughing, breath-holding and laryngospasm, while rapid concentration increases can trigger sympathetic tachycardia and hypertension. It also carries the volatile-class malignant-hyperthermia, respiratory-depression and perioperative-hyperkalemia risks.

Adverse Effects

  • Airway irritation, cough, breath-holding, laryngospasm, secretions, apnea/respiratory depression, hypotension, tachycardia/hypertension with rapid increases, nausea/vomiting, malignant hyperthermia, perioperative hyperkalemia, QT effects and rare hepatic injury.
