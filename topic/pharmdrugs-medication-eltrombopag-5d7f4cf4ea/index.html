Contraindications

  • Product labeling may list no absolute contraindication, but serious hepatic toxicity or hypersensitivity requires cessation

  • Not indicated for MDS and not intended to normalize platelet counts

  • Food/mineral separation must be feasible; otherwise low exposure can make therapy ineffective

Nursing Essentials

  • Give without a meal or with a low-calcium meal and at least 2 hours before or 4 hours after antacids, dairy/high-calcium foods, iron, calcium, magnesium, aluminum, selenium, or zinc.

  • Trend platelets and liver tests at the indication-specific interval. Watch for abdominal pain, jaundice, dark urine, thrombotic symptoms, or visual change.

Interactions

  • Polyvalent cations chelate eltrombopag and markedly lower absorption.

  • OATP1B1/BCRP substrates such as rosuvastatin can rise; CYP and UGT interactions plus other prothrombotic agents require review.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets, AST/ALT/bilirubin, and smear or marrow studies when indicated

  • Eye assessment for cataract risk, thrombotic symptoms, and food/supplement timing adherence

Nclex Traps

  • Eltrombopag is separated from calcium and minerals; avatrombopag is taken with food and does not share the same teaching.

  • The goal in ITP is enough platelets to reduce bleeding, not a normal count.

Used To Treat

Product-specific persistent or chronic ITP, selected severe aplastic anemia, and historical chronic hepatitis C thrombocytopenia contexts under current labeling. It is not for MDS and is used to reach a count sufficient to reduce bleeding or permit indicated therapy, not to normalize platelets.

Mechanism

Eltrombopag binds the transmembrane domain of human MPL and activates JAK/STAT and MAPK signaling in megakaryocyte progenitors without competing with endogenous thrombopoietin. Proliferation, differentiation, and platelet production increase over days. The molecule has metal-binding groups that form poorly absorbed complexes with polyvalent cations in food and medicines; separation is therefore part of the mechanism of effective delivery. It also inhibits OATP1B1 and BCRP transporters, which can raise exposure to substrates such as rosuvastatin. Hepatic metabolism and biliary handling plus direct liver injury risk explain intensive liver monitoring. Platelet production continues after dose changes long enough that overshoot and thrombosis can occur.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: In chronic hepatitis C, eltrombopag with interferon and ribavirin can increase hepatic decompensation. Eltrombopag can also cause severe and potentially life-threatening hepatotoxicity. Measure liver tests before and during therapy and interrupt or discontinue according to the label. Do not use merely to normalize platelet counts.

Description

Eltrombopag is an oral nonpeptide thrombopoietin-receptor MPL agonist that raises platelet production by driving megakaryocyte-progenitor proliferation and maturation. It binds within the receptor's transmembrane region at a site distinct from endogenous thrombopoietin, so both signals can operate without direct competition. Eltrombopag also chelates calcium, iron, magnesium, aluminum, zinc, and other polyvalent cations. Taking it with dairy, mineral supplements, or antacids can reduce absorption dramatically, creating apparent treatment failure. Its defining hazards are hepatotoxicity, thrombosis, and using platelet count as a dose target rather than a safety threshold.

Adverse Effects

  • Hepatotoxicity or hepatic decompensation, hyperbilirubinemia, nausea, fatigue, or abdominal symptoms

  • Arterial/venous thrombosis and portal-vein thrombosis

  • Cataract, marrow reticulin change, recurrent thrombocytopenia after stopping, or skin discoloration
