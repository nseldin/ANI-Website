Definition

Thready pulse means an arterial pulse feels unusually weak, narrow, and threadlike and is easily lost with light pressure. It is a perfusion warning sign, not a blood-pressure measurement by itself.

Plain Language

The pulse feels faint and is hard to keep under the fingers.

Why It Matters

A new rapid thready pulse can signal low stroke volume from bleeding, dehydration, severe heart failure, tamponade, or another form of shock. When it occurs with confusion, cool mottled skin, delayed capillary refill, low urine output, chest symptoms, or hypotension, circulation may be failing and needs urgent assessment.

Associated Conditions

  • reduced stroke volume or peripheral perfusion from hypovolemia, hemorrhage, shock, myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, tamponade, marked vasoconstriction, or local arterial disease

Assessment Method

Palpate central and peripheral pulses with light graded pressure, compare sides and proximal with distal sites, assess rate, rhythm, amplitude, contour, and pulse deficit, and correlate immediately with blood pressure, mental status, skin color and temperature, capillary refill, urine output, bleeding, breathing, chest symptoms, and Doppler assessment when a pulse is difficult to feel.

Positive Finding

The pulse feels unusually weak, narrow, and threadlike and is easily obliterated by light pressure, often while the rate is rapid.

Mechanism

Low ventricular stroke volume produces a smaller arterial pressure wave, and compensatory peripheral vasoconstriction can further reduce the amplitude reaching the extremities.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Thready pulse finding raises concern for reduced stroke volume or peripheral perfusion from hypovolemia, hemorrhage, shock, myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, tamponade, marked vasoconstriction, or local arterial disease because low ventricular stroke volume produces a smaller arterial pressure wave, and compensatory peripheral vasoconstriction can further reduce the amplitude reaching the extremities. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thready pulse does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thready pulse to focus evaluation of reduced stroke volume or peripheral perfusion from hypovolemia, hemorrhage, shock, myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, tamponade, marked vasoconstriction, or local arterial disease and the reviewed alternatives: pulsus parvus et tardus, pulse deficit, local arterial occlusion, normal small pulse, technical underpalpation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate central and peripheral pulses with light graded pressure, compare sides and proximal with distal sites, assess rate, rhythm, amplitude, contour, and pulse deficit, and correlate immediately with blood pressure, mental status, skin color and temperature, capillary refill, urine output, bleeding, breathing, chest symptoms, and Doppler assessment when a pulse is difficult to feel. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thready pulse as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Edema, obesity, cold, examiner pressure, arterial depth, calcification, and chronic vascular disease alter palpation; pulse presence does not provide a reliable blood-pressure number or exclude serious occlusion. Therefore, Thready pulse cannot confirm or exclude reduced stroke volume or peripheral perfusion from hypovolemia, hemorrhage, shock, myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, tamponade, marked vasoconstriction, or local arterial disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pulsus parvus et tardus

  • pulse deficit

  • local arterial occlusion

  • normal small pulse

  • technical underpalpation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pulsus parvus et tardus, pulse deficit, local arterial occlusion, normal small pulse, technical underpalpation.

False Negative Situations

Thready pulse may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of reduced stroke volume or peripheral perfusion from hypovolemia, hemorrhage, shock, myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, tamponade, marked vasoconstriction, or local arterial disease.

Modern Status

Current urgent contextual perfusion sign but subjective and nonspecific; a rapid thready pulse supports threatened circulation only when integrated with the full shock and vascular assessment. Safety Precautions

Observe Thready pulse with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Palpate central and peripheral pulses with light graded pressure, compare sides and proximal with distal sites, assess rate, rhythm, amplitude, contour, and pulse deficit, and correlate immediately with blood pressure, mental status, skin color and temperature, capillary refill, urine output, bleeding, breathing, chest symptoms, and Doppler assessment when a pulse is difficult to feel. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Treat a new rapid thready pulse with hypotension, confusion, cool mottled skin, delayed capillary refill, oliguria, chest pain, dyspnea, or active bleeding as possible shock; a suddenly absent or markedly weaker unilateral pulse with pain, pallor, numbness, weakness, or coolness is a limb emergency.
