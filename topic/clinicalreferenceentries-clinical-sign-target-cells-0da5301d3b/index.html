Definition

Target cells is a microscopic blood-film sign. It is a clue to excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin. A positive finding means cells show a central hemoglobinized spot, pale ring, and outer rim.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Target cells means cells show a central hemoglobinized spot, pale ring, and outer rim.

Why It Matters

The Target cells laboratory finding can signal excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin. Increased surface-area-to-volume ratio lets hemoglobin distribute in a bullseye pattern. Target cells cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin

Assessment Method

Review peripheral smear.

Positive Finding

Cells show a central hemoglobinized spot, pale ring, and outer rim.

Mechanism

Increased surface-area-to-volume ratio lets hemoglobin distribute in a bullseye pattern.

Clinical Interpretation

The Target cells laboratory finding raises concern for excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin because increased surface-area-to-volume ratio lets hemoglobin distribute in a bullseye pattern. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Target cells does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Target cells to focus evaluation of excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin and the reviewed alternatives: iron deficiency, postsplenectomy state, artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review peripheral smear. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Target cells as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Drying artifact can create target-like cells at smear edges. Therefore, Target cells cannot confirm or exclude excess membrane relative to red-cell volume or altered hemoglobin by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • iron deficiency

  • postsplenectomy state

  • artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with iron deficiency, postsplenectomy state, artifact.

False Negative Situations

Target cells may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
