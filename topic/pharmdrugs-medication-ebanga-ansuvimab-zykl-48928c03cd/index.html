Class

Monoclonal antibody against Orthoebolavirus zairense glycoprotein

Used To Treat

Treatment of infection caused by Orthoebolavirus zairense in adults and pediatric patients, including neonates born to a mother with RT-PCR-confirmed infection. It is not approved for disease caused by other ebolavirus species.

Boxed Warning

SPECIES AND INFUSION WARNING: EBANGA is not a generic Ebola treatment. Confirm O. zairense because activity cannot be assumed for another ebolavirus, prepare the 50 mg/kg infusion exactly, and monitor for reactions because infusion toxicity can resemble disease progression. Continue full high-consequence infection control and critical care because neutralization does not immediately end contagiousness or reverse established organ injury.

Mechanism

Ansuvimab-zykl binds a conserved region of the O. zairense glycoprotein and blocks the conformational and receptor-dependent steps needed for viral entry and fusion. Neutralization reduces new-cell infection, allowing host immunity and organ support to catch up. The epitope and clinical data are tied to O. zairense, so the same mechanism cannot be assumed for a genetically distinct ebolavirus. A single infusion stops new rounds of infection more effectively when given early, but it cannot instantly repair endothelial leak, shock, coagulopathy, or organ injury already present.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify EBANGA, the -zykl suffix, patient weight, prepared concentration, rate, and organism because similar antibody names and species ambiguity are high-risk.

  • Monitor closely during and after infusion for fever, chills, hypotension, tachycardia, bronchospasm, hypoxemia, and rash because these may mimic disease progression.

  • Maintain full infection-control precautions and exposure documentation because neutralization does not establish immediate noninfectiousness.

Contraindications

  • Do not assume EBANGA treats Bundibugyo, Sudan, or another ebolavirus because the syndrome shares the word Ebola.

  • Do not give a second dose or combine with INMAZEB routinely without authoritative expert direction.

Interactions

  • Coordinate live vaccine timing and passive antibody use with outbreak authorities because antibody could affect vaccine immunogenicity.

  • INMAZEB is an alternative approved antibody option for the same species, not routine combination therapy.

Key Labs

  • Public-health RT-PCR with species determination; route all high-risk specimens through designated laboratory procedures.

  • Serial CBC, metabolic panel, glucose, renal and hepatic function, coagulation, lactate, cultures, urine output, and organ-specific tests according to severity.

Nclex Traps

  • EBANGA and ansuvimab-zykl are one canonical product identity.

  • The dose is a single 50 mg/kg infusion, not a vaccine series.

  • Species verification and supportive care remain essential.

Population Risks

    Note

    Use exact weight-based dosing and pediatric critical care; the indication includes specified neonates but fluid and metabolic errors can be especially dangerous.

  • Population

Description

EBANGA is the marketed ansuvimab-zykl product and replaces the shallow Ansuvimab and Ansuvimab-Zykl identity shadows. It is a single monoclonal antibody given as a one-time 50 mg/kg IV infusion. The antibody was selected for potent neutralization of Zaire ebolavirus glycoprotein, but species specificity is clinically decisive: an outbreak labeled simply Ebola may be caused by a virus for which no approved antibody exists. Treatment begins inside a full high-consequence-pathogen response with isolation, observed PPE, public-health coordination, and aggressive supportive care.

Administration Timing

  • Isolate, use appropriate PPE, and notify infection prevention and public health at suspicion; collect specimens through the approved pathway and start supportive care immediately.

  • For qualifying O. zairense infection, give 50 mg/kg once by IV infusion under the current label with equipment and medicines available for infusion reactions.

  • Continue hemodynamic, electrolyte, glucose, renal, hepatic, coagulation, respiratory, and infection monitoring after infusion because clinical deterioration can continue.

Adverse Effects

  • Fever, tachycardia, diarrhea, vomiting, hypotension, chills, and infusion-associated symptoms occur in a disease that produces many of the same findings.

  • Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis are uncommon but require immediate interruption and treatment.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Refractory shock, hemorrhage, encephalopathy, arrhythmia, respiratory failure, renal failure, or a severe infusion reaction requires critical-care escalation.

  • Progression after treatment requires species confirmation and evaluation of timing, viral burden, coinfection, and organ complications rather than assuming inadequate dose.

Evidence Limitations

  • Effectiveness data apply to O. zairense and should not be extrapolated to other species.

  • Outbreak trial conditions and evolving supportive care limit precision for rare subgroups, despite a demonstrated treatment benefit in the studied disease.

Regulatory Status

FDA approved for O. zairense infection in adults and pediatric patients, including specified neonatal use; not approved for other ebolavirus species.
