Class

GalNAc-conjugated transthyretin-directed RNase-H1 antisense oligonucleotide

Used To Treat

Polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis in adults by subcutaneous administration every 4 weeks.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current WAINUA labeling has no boxed warning. Reduced serum vitamin A is the principal labeled warning: supplement only the recommended daily allowance and refer ocular symptoms such as night blindness or dry eyes. Do not import inotersen's REMS into this product, but do not infer that monthly administration makes disease or injection monitoring unnecessary.

Mechanism

GalNAc binds the hepatocyte asialoglycoprotein receptor and concentrates the oligonucleotide in liver cells. Eplontersen base-pairs with TTR mRNA, creating an RNA-DNA duplex cleaved by RNase H1, thereby reducing serum TTR and amyloid precursor. TTR loss also lowers circulating retinol-binding protein and serum vitamin A.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify monthly date, autoinjector versus healthcare-provider prefilled syringe, training, site rotation, room-temperature handling, pregnancy and vitamin A counseling.

  • Trend neuropathic pain, weakness, gait, falls, orthostasis, bowel/bladder function, nutrition and cardiac symptoms; evaluate vomiting or ocular symptoms rather than treating them as expected noise.

Key Labs

  • No routine therapeutic level. Follow clinical neuropathy/autonomic outcomes, renal/liver status as indicated and ophthalmologic evaluation for vitamin A deficiency symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Eplontersen is an antisense oligonucleotide, not siRNA, even though both can use GalNAc hepatocyte targeting.

  • Do not use high-dose vitamin A simply to normalize a serum value altered by reduced TTR transport.

Description

Eplontersen is a monthly GalNAc-conjugated antisense oligonucleotide that targets hepatocyte TTR mRNA, recruits RNase H1, and lowers both mutant and wild-type transthyretin protein in adult hATTR polyneuropathy. GalNAc targeting increases hepatic delivery and distinguishes it from inotersen; current labeling does not carry inotersen's boxed platelet/glomerulonephritis warning or REMS.
