Definition

Thompson test is a bedside tendon-integrity maneuver. It is a clue to Achilles tendon rupture. A positive finding means plantarflexion is absent or markedly reduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Thompson test means plantarflexion is absent or markedly reduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Thompson test response can signal Achilles tendon rupture. Calf muscle force cannot reach the calcaneus through a disrupted Achilles tendon. Thompson test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • Achilles tendon rupture

Assessment Method

With the patient prone or kneeling safely, squeeze the calf while observing the foot.

Positive Finding

Plantarflexion is absent or markedly reduced.

Mechanism

Calf muscle force cannot reach the calcaneus through a disrupted Achilles tendon.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Thompson test response raises concern for Achilles tendon rupture because calf muscle force cannot reach the calcaneus through a disrupted Achilles tendon. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thompson test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thompson test to focus evaluation of Achilles tendon rupture and the reviewed alternatives: tibial nerve palsy, calf pain, prior fusion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient prone or kneeling safely, squeeze the calf while observing the foot. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thompson test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Partial tears and accessory tendons can preserve some motion. Therefore, Thompson test cannot confirm or exclude Achilles tendon rupture by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tibial nerve palsy

  • calf pain

  • prior fusion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from tibial nerve palsy, calf pain, prior fusion, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Thompson test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current high-value sign. Interpret Thompson test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Partial tears and accessory tendons can preserve some motion.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Thompson test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the patient prone or kneeling safely, squeeze the calf while observing the foot. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not squeeze a calf with suspected DVT or compartment syndrome; protect weight bearing.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Thompson test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Plantarflexion is absent or markedly reduced. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: With the patient prone or kneeling safely, squeeze the calf while observing the foot. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, Achilles tendon rupture, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Thompson test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not squeeze a calf with suspected DVT or compartment syndrome; protect weight bearing. Base escalation for Thompson test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
