Used To Treat

Mild-to-moderate Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia treatment or prophylaxis in selected clients, and malaria only when paired with proguanil in the combination product.

Mechanism

Atovaquone mimics ubiquinone and inhibits the parasite mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex, collapsing electron transport needed to regenerate ubiquinone for pyrimidine synthesis. The practical bedside point is that poor absorption or rifamycin interaction can underdose the parasite target and cause failure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food or a fatty meal when ordered because absorption improves significantly.

  • Assess immune status, respiratory symptoms, GI tolerance, hepatic history, and response to therapy.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe hepatic impairment or prior serious hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Rifampin and rifabutin can lower atovaquone levels. Some tetracyclines and metoclopramide may reduce exposure.

Key Labs

  • Liver function if prolonged therapy or hepatic risk. Monitor oxygenation and respiratory status in Pneumocystis infection.

Nclex Traps

  • Food matters. A missed absorption point can look like treatment failure.

Description

Atovaquone is a hydroxynaphthoquinone antiprotozoal and anti-Pneumocystis agent: it mimics ubiquinone and inhibits the parasite mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex, collapsing electron transport needed to regenerate ubiquinone for pyrimidine synthesis. It is used for mild-to-moderate Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia treatment or prophylaxis in selected clients, and malaria only when paired with proguanil in the combination product.
