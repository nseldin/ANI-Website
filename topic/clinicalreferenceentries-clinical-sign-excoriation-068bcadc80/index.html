Definition

Excoriation means a scratch mark: a linear or pinpoint erosion caused by scratching, rubbing, or picking that scrapes away the outer skin surface.

Plain Language

An excoriation is skin that has been scratched or rubbed raw.

Why It Matters

Excoriations show skin-barrier injury and may hide the original rash. Repeated scratching can cause bleeding, crusting, infection, and thickened skin, so the cause of itch or picking must be assessed without blaming the patient.

Associated Conditions

  • superficial skin loss produced by scratching, rubbing, or picking because of itch, irritation, pain, altered sensation, a primary skin disorder, systemic disease, medication or substance effects, or repetitive behavior

Assessment Method

Inspect in neutral light and document linear or punctate shape, number, depth, distribution, symmetry, bleeding, crusting, surrounding rash, infection signs, itch and sleep effects, medications and exposures, and whether an underlying primary lesion remains visible; ask about scratching or picking without blame.

Positive Finding

A linear or punctate superficial erosion removes epidermis and sometimes superficial dermis in a pattern consistent with scratching, rubbing, or picking.

Mechanism

Fingernails or repeated friction mechanically strip away surface skin, disrupt the barrier, and may cause inflammation, bleeding, crusting, secondary infection, or chronic thickening.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Excoriation finding raises concern for superficial skin loss produced by scratching, rubbing, or picking because of itch, irritation, pain, altered sensation, a primary skin disorder, systemic disease, medication or substance effects, or repetitive behavior because fingernails or repeated friction mechanically strip away surface skin, disrupt the barrier, and may cause inflammation, bleeding, crusting, secondary infection, or chronic thickening. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Excoriation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Excoriation to focus evaluation of superficial skin loss produced by scratching, rubbing, or picking because of itch, irritation, pain, altered sensation, a primary skin disorder, systemic disease, medication or substance effects, or repetitive behavior and the reviewed alternatives: erosion, linear abrasion, fissure, laceration. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect in neutral light and document linear or punctate shape, number, depth, distribution, symmetry, bleeding, crusting, surrounding rash, infection signs, itch and sleep effects, medications and exposures, and whether an underlying primary lesion remains visible; ask about scratching or picking without blame. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Excoriation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The original primary lesion may be partly or completely removed, lesions may be self-induced without being deliberate or psychiatric, and morphology alone cannot identify the cause of scratching or picking. Therefore, Excoriation cannot confirm or exclude superficial skin loss produced by scratching, rubbing, or picking because of itch, irritation, pain, altered sensation, a primary skin disorder, systemic disease, medication or substance effects, or repetitive behavior by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • erosion

  • linear abrasion

  • fissure

  • laceration

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as erosion, linear abrasion, fissure, laceration.

False Negative Situations

Excoriation may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of superficial skin loss produced by scratching, rubbing, or picking because of itch, irritation, pain, altered sensation, a primary skin disorder, systemic disease, medication or substance effects, or repetitive behavior.

Modern Status

Current secondary morphology, not a psychiatric diagnosis; recognizing excoriations helps identify skin-barrier injury and prompts evaluation of the cause of itch or picking and of bleeding or infection. Interpret Excoriation in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: The original primary lesion may be partly or completely removed, lesions may be self-induced without being deliberate or psychiatric, and morphology alone cannot identify the cause of scratching or picking.

Safety Precautions

Observe Excoriation with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect in neutral light and document linear or punctate shape, number, depth, distribution, symmetry, bleeding, crusting, surrounding rash, infection signs, itch and sleep effects, medications and exposures, and whether an underlying primary lesion remains visible; ask about scratching or picking without blame. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not shame the patient or diagnose excoriation disorder from scratch marks alone; assess dermatologic, systemic, medication-related, neurologic, and behavioral causes, and promptly escalate uncontrolled bleeding, fever, pus, spreading redness, severe pain, extensive skin loss, or immunocompromise with infection concern.
