Definition

Heinz bodies is a supravital microscopic sign. It is a clue to oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin. A positive finding means round intracellular hemoglobin inclusions are visible.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Heinz bodies means round intracellular hemoglobin inclusions are visible.

Why It Matters

The Heinz bodies laboratory finding can signal oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin. Oxidant stress denatures globin chains, which precipitate on the inner membrane. Heinz bodies cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin

Assessment Method

Examine red cells with an appropriate supravital stain.

Positive Finding

Round intracellular hemoglobin inclusions are visible.

Mechanism

Oxidant stress denatures globin chains, which precipitate on the inner membrane.

Clinical Interpretation

The Heinz bodies laboratory finding raises concern for oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin because oxidant stress denatures globin chains, which precipitate on the inner membrane. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Heinz bodies does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Heinz bodies as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Routine Wright stain may not show them and the spleen removes affected cells. Therefore, Heinz bodies cannot confirm or exclude oxidative denaturation of hemoglobin by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Howell-Jolly bodies

  • Pappenheimer bodies

  • stain precipitate

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with Howell-Jolly bodies, Pappenheimer bodies, stain precipitate.

False Negative Situations

Heinz bodies may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
