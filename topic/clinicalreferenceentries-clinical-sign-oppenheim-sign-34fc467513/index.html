Definition

Oppenheim sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to corticospinal tract dysfunction. A positive finding means the great toe extends with or without fanning.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Oppenheim sign means the great toe extends with or without fanning.

Why It Matters

A positive Oppenheim sign response can signal corticospinal tract dysfunction. Deep cutaneous pressure can evoke an extensor plantar response when corticospinal inhibition is impaired. Oppenheim sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • corticospinal tract dysfunction

Assessment Method

Apply firm downward pressure with knuckles along the anteromedial tibia.

Positive Finding

The great toe extends with or without fanning.

Mechanism

Deep cutaneous pressure can evoke an extensor plantar response when corticospinal inhibition is impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Oppenheim sign response raises concern for corticospinal tract dysfunction because deep cutaneous pressure can evoke an extensor plantar response when corticospinal inhibition is impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Oppenheim sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Oppenheim sign to focus evaluation of corticospinal tract dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: pain withdrawal, local tibial injury, normal variation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply firm downward pressure with knuckles along the anteromedial tibia. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Oppenheim sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Published performance is inconsistent and the maneuver is not a substitute for the standard plantar response. Therefore, Oppenheim sign cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pain withdrawal

  • local tibial injury

  • normal variation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from pain withdrawal, local tibial injury, normal variation, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Oppenheim sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; less standardized than plantar stimulation. Oppenheim sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction. Apply the reviewed limitation for Oppenheim sign: Published performance is inconsistent and the maneuver is not a substitute for the standard plantar response.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Oppenheim sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply firm downward pressure with knuckles along the anteromedial tibia. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid excessive painful pressure and distinguish toe extension from generalized withdrawal.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Apply firm downward pressure with knuckles along the anteromedial tibia.
