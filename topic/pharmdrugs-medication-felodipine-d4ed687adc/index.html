Used To Treat

Hypertension, alone or with other antihypertensives. It is a once-daily extended-release maintenance drug, not an acute blood-pressure rescue or an AV-node rate-control agent.

Mechanism

Felodipine preferentially inhibits Cav1.2 channels in arterial smooth muscle. Reduced calcium-calmodulin activation lowers myosin light-chain kinase activity, relaxes resistance arterioles, lowers systemic vascular resistance, and reduces blood pressure. Abrupt arterial dilation can activate baroreflex sympathetic output and produce palpitations or tachycardia, while precapillary dilation without matched venodilation raises dependent capillary hydrostatic pressure and causes edema. The extended-release system smooths peak exposure, but food and intestinal CYP3A inhibition can disrupt that design. Extensive hepatic first-pass metabolism explains why grapefruit, azole antifungals, macrolides, age, and liver dysfunction materially change concentration.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Excessive hypotension and reflex tachycardia can precipitate angina in susceptible patients. Peripheral edema is common and dose related; in labeling it rose markedly with higher dose and older age. CYP3A inhibitors including grapefruit can produce several-fold exposure increases, while enzyme inducers can make therapy ineffective. Start cautiously in older adults or hepatic impairment, swallow the extended-release tablet whole, and avoid inconsistent high-fat/high-carbohydrate meals that can raise peak exposure.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity; severe hypotension, unstable ischemia, major liver impairment, or high-risk CYP3A interactions require clinician reassessment.

  • Do not crush or chew the extended-release tablet and do not combine casually with grapefruit or a potent CYP3A inhibitor.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess pressure, orthostasis, pulse, edema, chest symptoms, liver disease, food routine, grapefruit use, and all CYP3A drugs. Explain why consistent administration conditions prevent unpredictable peaks.

  • Examine gums during long-term therapy and reinforce brushing, flossing, and dental care; evaluate edema as capillary redistribution while screening for heart failure, renal disease, venous disease, and unilateral red flags.

Interactions

  • Itraconazole has produced about an eightfold AUC increase and erythromycin about a 2.5-fold increase in labeling studies; grapefruit more than doubled AUC/Cmax. Avoid or manage CYP3A inhibitors through the current product plan.

  • Phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital and other inducers can greatly reduce exposure. Other antihypertensives and vasodilators add hypotension.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, pulse, edema/weight context, orthostatic symptoms, liver function when impaired or symptomatic, and dental/gingival assessment.

  • Routine serum felodipine levels are not used; investigate creatinine, electrolytes, albumin, liver/cardiac findings, or venous disease only as the edema differential requires.

Nclex Traps

  • Felodipine is a vascular DHP for maintenance hypertension, not an AV-node rate-control drug.

  • Grapefruit is not a trivial food warning here. Intestinal CYP3A inhibition can more than double and sometimes dramatically raise exposure.

Description

Felodipine is an oral extended-release dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that relaxes arterioles and lowers systemic vascular resistance without serving as a therapeutic AV-node blocker. Its most important practical features are dose-related peripheral edema and strong dependence on CYP3A first-pass metabolism. Grapefruit can more than double felodipine exposure, and potent inhibitors have produced several-fold increases, amplifying hypotension, edema, flushing, and tachycardia. A high-fat or high-carbohydrate meal can raise peak concentration even when total exposure changes less, so the extended-release tablet should be taken consistently on an empty stomach or with a light meal according to the product. Gingival hyperplasia is uncommon but can improve with meticulous dental hygiene.

Adverse Effects

  • Peripheral edema, headache, flushing, dizziness, palpitations, tachycardia, fatigue, nausea, and gingival hyperplasia.

  • Severe hypotension, syncope, angina/ischemia, angioedema, hepatic enzyme elevation, and prolonged vasodilatory shock in overdose.
