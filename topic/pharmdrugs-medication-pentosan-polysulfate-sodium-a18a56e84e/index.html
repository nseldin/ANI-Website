Class

Oral heparin-like glycosaminoglycan analog for interstitial-cystitis bladder pain

Used To Treat

Relief of bladder pain or discomfort associated with interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome; response is delayed and not universal.

Description

Pentosan polysulfate sodium is a weakly anticoagulant glycosaminoglycan analog proposed to coat or reinforce the injured bladder surface and reduce irritant-solute penetration in interstitial cystitis. Only a small fraction is absorbed orally, and its exact therapeutic mechanism remains unproven. Benefit may take months, while cumulative exposure can cause pigmentary maculopathy.

Mechanism

The proposed urothelial-barrier model is physical rather than receptor based: sulfated chains may adhere to the bladder surface and reduce permeability to potassium and other urinary solutes that activate suburothelial nerves. Labeling does not establish this as definitive. Structural similarity to heparin produces weak anticoagulant/fibrinolytic effects, explaining bruising and procedural bleeding. Retinal toxicity appears related to cumulative dose and may progress after drug cessation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Pigmentary maculopathy can impair reading, dark adaptation and central vision; changes may be irreversible and can progress after stopping. Obtain detailed ocular history and baseline OCT/autofluorescence imaging within 6 months, then periodic follow-up. Reassess bleeding risk before surgery or with anticoagulants/antiplatelets.

Adverse Effects

  • Pigmentary maculopathy, blurred vision, reading difficulty and slow dark adaptation

  • Alopecia, diarrhea, nausea, headache and rash

  • Bruising, epistaxis, rectal bleeding and other anticoagulant-like effects

  • Liver-enzyme abnormalities, thrombocytopenia or hypersensitivity

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to pentosan, related compounds or excipients

  • Use cautiously with retinal disease, bleeding disorders, thrombocytopenia, anticoagulants or upcoming invasive procedures

  • Do not substitute for evaluation of hematuria, infection, endometriosis, malignancy or other pelvic-pain causes

Nursing Essentials

  • Document baseline vision and arrange retinal imaging; ask about reading difficulty and adaptation from bright to dim light at every review.

  • Set a time-defined symptom reassessment because prolonged exposure without benefit adds cumulative retinal risk.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs and thrombolytics increase bleeding risk.

  • Take capsules as labeled relative to meals; food can alter absorption.

  • Procedural and dental teams need the medication history.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and periodic retinal examination with OCT and fundus autofluorescence

  • CBC/platelets, coagulation and liver tests when risk or symptoms warrant

  • Pain/voiding diary and cumulative duration/dose review

Nclex Traps

  • Pentosan is not phenazopyridine and does not provide immediate orange-urine analgesia.

  • Retinal injury can progress after discontinuation.

  • The glycosaminoglycan-coating mechanism is plausible but not definitively established.
