Class

Selective beta-3 adrenergic receptor agonist bladder relaxant

Used To Treat

Overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge urinary incontinence in adults, including adult men receiving pharmacologic therapy for BPH under the current indication.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Urinary retention has occurred, particularly with bladder outlet obstruction or concomitant antimuscarinics. Angioedema can involve the tongue or larynx and requires immediate discontinuation/airway care. Monitor digoxin when initiating or changing vibegron.

Mechanism

Vibegron activates Gs-coupled beta-3 receptors on detrusor smooth muscle. Adenylyl cyclase and cyclic AMP rise, protein kinase A reduces contractile calcium signaling, and the filling bladder accommodates more volume before urgency contractions occur. This does not reverse prostate obstruction or strengthen voiding. Its cleaner CYP2D6 profile is pharmacokinetic, not evidence that it is risk free.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess bladder outlet symptoms and residual volume before calling frequency simple OAB.

  • Track urgency/incontinence episodes and digoxin concentration rather than assuming benefit from fewer bathroom trips alone.

Contraindications

  • Known vibegron or formulation hypersensitivity

  • Do not use as treatment for acute retention or mechanical outlet obstruction

  • Clarify severe renal/hepatic disease using current labeling

Interactions

  • Increases digoxin exposure; check concentration before and during therapy as directed.

  • Antimuscarinics add urinary-retention risk.

  • No clinically meaningful CYP2D6 inhibition was demonstrated, an important distinction from mirabegron.

Key Labs

  • Post-void residual and symptom diary

  • Renal/hepatic function when dosing concerns exist

  • Digoxin concentration when coadministered

Nclex Traps

  • Vibegron and mirabegron share beta-3 pharmacology but not every interaction warning.

  • No CYP2D6 inhibition does not remove retention risk.

  • The BPH-associated indication treats OAB symptoms in men already on BPH pharmacotherapy; it does not shrink the prostate.

Description

Vibegron is a selective beta-3 agonist that increases bladder storage by relaxing detrusor muscle during filling. Unlike mirabegron, it has not shown clinically meaningful CYP2D6 inhibition and its current label does not carry a severe-uncontrolled-hypertension warning. It still can cause urinary retention, especially with bladder outlet obstruction or antimuscarinic therapy, and it can increase digoxin exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nasopharyngitis, diarrhea, nausea and urinary tract infection

  • Urinary retention or increased residual volume

  • Rare angioedema/hypersensitivity

  • Hot flush or constipation in selected patients
