Class

Thiazolide antiprotozoal prodrug that disrupts anaerobic redox-dependent energy metabolism

Used To Treat

Diarrhea caused by Giardia duodenalis or Cryptosporidium parvum in immunocompetent patients at least one year old. It is not proven superior to placebo for cryptosporidiosis in people with HIV or major immune suppression.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Nitazoxanide is approved for Giardia and Cryptosporidium diarrhea in immunocompetent patients, not as established effective therapy for severely immunocompromised cryptosporidiosis. Persistent diarrhea requires assessment for dehydration, ongoing infection, reinfection, immune deficiency, another pathogen, or post-infectious symptoms rather than automatic repeated courses. Use cautiously in significant hepatic or renal impairment because pharmacokinetics are not well characterized.

Mechanism

Intestinal and plasma esterases deacetylate nitazoxanide to tizoxanide, which is extensively protein bound and glucuronidated. In Giardia and other anaerobic organisms, tizoxanide interferes with the pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase enzyme-dependent electron-transfer reaction. PFOR normally oxidizes pyruvate and transfers electrons to ferredoxin during anaerobic ATP production; disrupting electron flow impairs energy metabolism. Cryptosporidium lacks a single fully established PFOR-dependent explanation, and thiazolides likely alter additional parasite enzyme, membrane, or stress-response pathways. Because host immunity is central to clearing intracellular Cryptosporidium, direct metabolic inhibition may not produce durable cure in advanced immune deficiency.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with food and rehydrate actively. Monitor intake/output, weight, orthostasis, mucous membranes, and electrolytes in vulnerable patients because diarrhea physiology can be more dangerous than medication toxicity.

  • If symptoms persist, confirm ongoing infection with appropriate stool testing and assess reinfection or another cause. Cryptosporidium can continue shedding after symptoms improve, so swimming restrictions matter.

Contraindications

  • Prior serious hypersensitivity to nitazoxanide or a formulation component.

  • Do not use adult tablets to improvise pediatric doses; the oral suspension is needed for younger weight/age groups.

Interactions

  • Tizoxanide is highly protein bound; use caution with other highly bound narrow-therapeutic-index drugs such as warfarin and monitor clinical effect.

  • Food increases systemic exposure. Severe diarrhea or vomiting may still reduce effective absorption despite correct administration.

Key Labs

  • Hydration and electrolytes, renal/liver function in significant disease, stool antigen/PCR or microscopy, and immune-status assessment

  • Symptom duration, weight, intake/output, blood or fever suggesting another process, recurrence/reinfection, and swimming or infection-control counseling

Nclex Traps

  • Nitazoxanide's proven cryptosporidiosis indication is for immunocompetent patients; severe immune deficiency changes expected benefit.

  • Yellow-green urine can be a benign metabolite effect, but dark concentrated urine with dehydration still requires assessment.

Description

Nitazoxanide is an oral thiazolide prodrug rapidly converted to tizoxanide. In susceptible anaerobic protozoa it disrupts pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase-linked electron transfer and other redox pathways needed to generate energy. This treats Giardia and can shorten cryptosporidial diarrhea in immunocompetent patients. Cryptosporidium control depends heavily on cellular immunity, so the same tablets have uncertain benefit in severe immunosuppression; immune restoration and hydration may matter more than extending the drug. Nitazoxanide should be taken with food because food increases exposure. Urine may turn yellow-green from metabolites, an expected color effect that must be distinguished from dark urine caused by dehydration, hemolysis, or liver disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Abdominal pain, nausea, headache, diarrhea, vomiting, and yellow-green urine discoloration

  • Hypersensitivity, rash, liver-test elevation, and treatment failure when host immunity or absorption is inadequate
