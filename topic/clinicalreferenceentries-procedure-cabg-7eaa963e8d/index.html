Summary

CABG (coronary artery bypass grafting) is a high-yield Cardiovascular diagnostic reference. Coronary artery bypass grafting is open-heart surgical revascularization that routes blood around blocked coronary arteries using graft vessels.

Quick Answer

CABG (coronary artery bypass grafting): Coronary artery bypass grafting is open-heart surgical revascularization that routes blood around blocked coronary arteries using graft vessels. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Coronary artery bypass grafting is open-heart surgical revascularization that routes blood around blocked coronary arteries using graft vessels.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify consent, NPO status, type/crossmatch, baseline vitals, rhythm, lung sounds, renal function, glucose, coagulation status, current anticoagulants/antiplatelets, and teaching about coughing, deep breathing, incentive spirometry, pain control, tubes/lines, and sternal precautions.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Monitor airway/ventilation, hemodynamics, rhythm, chest-tube output, bleeding, tamponade cues, graft perfusion, urine output, electrolytes, glucose, pain, temperature, neuro status, lung expansion, sternal wound integrity, and progressive mobility.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Sudden high or stopped chest-tube output, hypotension, muffled heart sounds, jugular venous distention, new dysrhythmia, low urine output, chest pain, stroke symptoms, fever, sternal instability, wound drainage, or respiratory distress.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • After CABG, the nurse thinks ABCs, bleeding/tamponade, dysrhythmias, perfusion, pain control, pulmonary hygiene, and sternal precautions. Do not treat new bleeding, hypotension, or rhythm change as routine recovery.
