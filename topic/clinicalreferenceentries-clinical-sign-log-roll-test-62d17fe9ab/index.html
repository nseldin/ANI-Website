Definition

Log-roll test is a bedside hip maneuver. It is a clue to intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity. A positive finding means pain or excessive external rotation occurs.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Log-roll test means pain or excessive external rotation occurs.

Why It Matters

A positive Log-roll test response can signal intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity. Passive femoral rotation stresses the hip capsule with minimal surrounding muscle demand. Log-roll test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity

Assessment Method

With the leg relaxed, gently roll it internally and externally.

Positive Finding

Pain or excessive external rotation occurs.

Mechanism

Passive femoral rotation stresses the hip capsule with minimal surrounding muscle demand.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Log-roll test response raises concern for intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity because passive femoral rotation stresses the hip capsule with minimal surrounding muscle demand. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Log-roll test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Log-roll test to focus evaluation of intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity and the reviewed alternatives: lumbar pain, muscle strain, normal anteversion. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the leg relaxed, gently roll it internally and externally. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Log-roll test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain location and guarding affect interpretation. Therefore, Log-roll test cannot confirm or exclude intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • lumbar pain

  • muscle strain

  • normal anteversion

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from lumbar pain, muscle strain, normal anteversion, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Log-roll test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Log-roll test can adjust concern for intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Log-roll test: Pain location and guarding affect interpretation.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Log-roll test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the leg relaxed, gently roll it internally and externally. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use extreme caution after trauma because femoral neck fracture may be occult.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Log-roll test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Pain or excessive external rotation occurs. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: With the leg relaxed, gently roll it internally and externally. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, intra-articular hip pathology or capsular laxity, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Log-roll test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
