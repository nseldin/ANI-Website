Used To Treat

Hypertension, angina, rate/rhythm control, post-MI protection, migraine/tremor uses for selected agents, and heart failure with selected evidence-based beta blockers.

Mechanism

Metoprolol works by blocking beta-adrenergic stimulation, lowering heart rate, contractility, conduction, renin release, and myocardial oxygen demand. Hold/clarify cues are bradycardia, hypotension, heart block, and selected acute decompensation.

Boxed Warning

Boxed warning: abrupt discontinuation can worsen angina and may precipitate myocardial infarction.

Contraindications

  • Severe bradycardia.

  • Second- or third-degree heart block without a pacemaker.

  • Cardiogenic shock or decompensated heart failure.

  • Clarify severe bradycardia, symptomatic hypotension, high-grade heart block without pacing, acute decompensated heart failure, and bronchospasm risk for nonselective agents.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check heart rate and blood pressure before administration.

  • Use caution in heart block, acute decompensated heart failure, and bronchospasm-prone clients.

  • Do not stop abruptly.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Metoprolol is categorized here as Beta-1 selective beta blocker. Pathway: Cardiovascular medication > Beta blocker > Beta-1 selective. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Other rate-slowing drugs such as digoxin, diltiazem, verapamil, amiodarone can worsen bradycardia/AV block.

  • Insulin effects may be masked by reduced tachycardia symptoms.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • glucose in diabetes

  • potassium if arrhythmia risk

  • Heart rate, blood pressure, rhythm/ECG, glucose trends in diabetes, and heart-failure symptoms.

Nclex Traps

  • Beta blockers can mask hypoglycemia tachycardia; sweating may still occur.

  • Beta blockers can mask tachycardia from hypoglycemia and can worsen bradycardia or heart block.

Description

Metoprolol is a beta-1 selective beta blocker: it works by blocking beta-adrenergic stimulation, lowering heart rate, contractility, conduction, renin release, and myocardial oxygen demand. It is used for hypertension, angina, rate control, post-MI protection, dysrhythmia control, migraine/tremor uses for selected agents, heart failure with selected evidence-based beta blockers, and pregnancy hypertension with labetalol when ordered.
