Class

Lipid-conjugated nucleotide analog orthopoxvirus antiviral

Used To Treat

FDA-approved treatment of human smallpox disease in adults and pediatric patients, including neonates. The labeled course is two oral doses one week apart; the drug is not approved as general therapy for mpox, cytomegalovirus, adenovirus, or other viral diseases.

Boxed Warning

FDA BOXED WARNING: an increased risk of mortality was seen when brincidofovir was used for a longer duration in a different disease. Use only for the approved smallpox indication and two-dose schedule unless an authoritative emergency protocol directs otherwise.

Mechanism

After cellular uptake, the lipid conjugate is cleaved to cidofovir, then phosphorylated to cidofovir diphosphate. The active metabolite competes with natural nucleotides at viral DNA polymerase, slows or terminates viral DNA synthesis, and thereby limits replication. Intracellular delivery reduces reliance on a transporter-limited IV cidofovir strategy, but it does not make the drugs interchangeable. Prolonged exposure can also affect host tissues, which helps explain hepatic, gastrointestinal, reproductive, and mortality concerns outside the approved short course.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm smallpox rather than mpox or another rash illness because approval and evidence cannot be transferred by virus-family similarity alone.

  • Verify the seven-day interval and pediatric weight because daily administration would create a severe schedule error.

  • Monitor nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, jaundice, dark urine, and laboratory liver injury because toxicity can undermine hydration and safe completion.

Contraindications

  • Do not use more frequently or longer than the approved two-dose smallpox regimen without expert authority because the boxed mortality signal came from prolonged use in another disease.

  • Do not equate brincidofovir with cidofovir or substitute formulations or dosing schedules.

Interactions

  • Organic anion-transporting polypeptide inhibitors can alter exposure; reconcile current medicines against the active label rather than relying on class memory.

  • Other antivirals and vaccines have distinct timing and evidence; combination treatment is a public-health and specialist decision, not an automatic escalation.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and follow-up ALT, AST, bilirubin, and other hepatic studies according to the label and clinical course.

  • Pregnancy assessment when relevant, weight for pediatric dosing, and diagnostic specimens coordinated through public health.

Nclex Traps

  • TEMBEXA is an alias for brincidofovir, not a separate active card.

  • The smallpox course is two doses one week apart, not daily therapy.

  • Approval for smallpox does not equal approval for mpox.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Neonates and children

    Note

    The label includes pediatric patients, but dose and suspension volume are weight based and require independent verification.

  • Population

    Pregnancy or reproductive potential

    Note

    Animal data support embryo-fetal and reproductive risk, so follow current contraception, pregnancy, and lactation labeling while treating the emergency.

  • Population

    Liver disease

    Note

    Baseline dysfunction can make new hepatotoxicity harder to detect and tolerate, so laboratory and symptom trends are essential.

Description

Brincidofovir, sold as TEMBEXA, is a lipid-conjugated cidofovir prodrug that delivers cidofovir into cells, where its diphosphate metabolite inhibits orthopoxvirus DNA polymerase and suppresses viral DNA synthesis. Smallpox approval rests on the Animal Rule, and the regimen is intentionally brief: two once-weekly oral doses. The boxed warning about increased mortality arose during prolonged use in another disease, which is why the safety signal must remain visible without falsely implying that the approved two-dose smallpox course was tested in human smallpox. Liver injury, gastrointestinal effects, reproductive toxicity, and exact pediatric preparation require active monitoring.

Administration Timing

  • For suspected smallpox, isolate and contact public health and CDC immediately; treatment is part of a coordinated countermeasure response rather than routine outpatient self-diagnosis.

  • Give the exact weight-based oral dose once weekly for two doses using the current tablet or suspension instructions; do not extend therapy without explicit expert and public-health direction.

  • Check hepatic status, pregnancy potential, formulation, and interacting medicines before dosing when possible, but do not delay emergency smallpox coordination.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and elevated aminotransferases are important because severe losses can worsen dehydration.

  • Hepatotoxicity and embryo-fetal or reproductive toxicity require counseling and monitoring; prolonged nonapproved use carried an increased-mortality signal.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Severe diarrhea or vomiting, dehydration, jaundice, marked liver-test elevation, allergy, or clinical progression requires urgent reassessment.

  • Ocular, neurologic, pulmonary, or overwhelming disease requires public-health and specialty management beyond antiviral administration.

Evidence Limitations

  • Human smallpox efficacy could not be studied; approval uses the Animal Rule and human safety information.

  • The boxed increased-mortality finding occurred with prolonged use in a different disease and should be disclosed accurately rather than generalized without context.

Regulatory Status

FDA approved under the Animal Rule for human smallpox in adults and pediatric patients, including neonates; not approved for other viral diseases.
