Class

Rifamycin antitubercular antibiotic

Used To Treat

Active tuberculosis as part of combination therapy, latent TB regimens when ordered, and selected serious susceptible infections.

Mechanism

Inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase in susceptible bacteria, blocking RNA synthesis. The nursing link is TB organism control plus high interaction and liver-safety monitoring.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach orange-red discoloration of urine, sweat, saliva, and tears is expected, but jaundice or dark urine with illness is not normal.

  • Screen medication list carefully because rifampin is a strong enzyme inducer and can reduce effectiveness of many drugs.

Contraindications

  • Clarify significant liver disease, serious prior rifamycin reaction, or critical interacting medications.

Interactions

  • Can reduce effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives, warfarin, some antiretrovirals, azoles, anticonvulsants, and many other drugs.

  • Teach backup nonhormonal contraception when applicable.

Key Labs

  • Liver function tests when indicated.

  • Assess adherence, TB symptom response, bleeding/thrombocytopenia cues, and interaction-related therapy failure.

Nclex Traps

  • Orange secretions are expected; yellow skin/eyes, dark urine with illness, severe fatigue, or right upper quadrant pain are not.

  • The interaction issue is huge. Rifampin can make other medications fail.

Description

Rifampin is a rifamycin antitubercular antibiotic: it inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase in susceptible bacteria, blocking RNA synthesis. It is used for active tuberculosis as part of combination therapy, latent TB regimens when ordered, and selected serious susceptible infections.
