Definition

Diaphoresis means profuse or unexpected sweating. When visible it is a clinical sign; an episode described only by the patient is part of the symptom history. It is not automatically the chronic disorder called hyperhidrosis.

Plain Language

Diaphoresis means unusually heavy sweating, including a sudden cold or clammy sweat.

Why It Matters

Sweating can be normal with heat, exercise, fever, or emotion, but sudden unexplained diaphoresis can accompany hypoglycemia, myocardial infarction, shock, heat illness, medication effects, or withdrawal. Check the surrounding symptoms and urgency instead of assuming anxiety.

Assessment Method

Observe whether sweating is focal or generalized and whether skin or clothing is damp or soaked, then ask about onset, duration, triggers, heat or exertion, night episodes, pain, chest discomfort, dyspnea, dizziness, nausea, fever, medications, substances, and withdrawal; assess temperature, vital signs, skin perfusion, hydration, mental status, and bedside glucose when clinically indicated.

Positive Finding

Sweating is visible and excessive or unexpected for the ambient temperature and activity, with beads of sweat or damp or soaked skin or clothing; it may be focal, generalized, warm, or cold and clammy.

Mechanism

Sympathetic cholinergic nerves activate eccrine sweat glands to dissipate heat or during autonomic stress; fever, falling glucose, ischemia, pain, shock, medications, and withdrawal can increase the same output through different pathways.

Evidence Limitations

Room temperature, exertion, fever, anxiety, clothing, medications, and recent cooling change the finding; an episode may end before examination, anticholinergic exposure or autonomic neuropathy may blunt sweating, and absence of diaphoresis does not exclude myocardial infarction, hypoglycemia, shock, or heat stroke. Therefore, Diaphoresis cannot confirm or exclude normal thermoregulation or emotion, fever, pain, hypoglycemia, myocardial ischemia, shock, heat illness, medications, substance withdrawal, infection, endocrine disease, or autonomic dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Hypoglycemia, Heat exhaustion, Myocardial infarction, Serotonin syndrome.

False Negative Situations

Diaphoresis may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of normal thermoregulation or emotion, fever, pain, hypoglycemia, myocardial ischemia, shock, heat illness, medications, substance withdrawal, infection, endocrine disease, or autonomic dysfunction.

Modern Status
