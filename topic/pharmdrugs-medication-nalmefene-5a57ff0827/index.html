Class

Longer-acting opioid-receptor antagonist for emergency opioid-overdose reversal

Used To Treat

Emergency treatment of known or suspected opioid overdose manifested by respiratory or central nervous system depression in adults and pediatric patients 12 years and older, using the product-specific labeled route, because safety and effectiveness below age 12 have not been established for these products.

Boxed Warning

ACTIVE OVERDOSE WARNING: nalmefene is emergency rescue, not home observation. Call 911, support breathing, use the exact Opvee or Zurnai device as labeled, and repeat only with a new device because incomplete reversal, recurrence, and severe prolonged withdrawal can occur.

Mechanism

Nalmefene competitively blocks opioid receptors, particularly mu receptors, which interrupts agonist suppression of brainstem respiratory drive. Longer antagonist exposure can persist after reversal, but that same duration can prolong severe precipitated withdrawal in a physically dependent person. A tight-binding partial agonist can remain incompletely reversed, and non-opioid sedatives are unaffected; therefore breathing and perfusion, rather than the number of antagonist milligrams, determine whether advanced support is needed.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify Opvee versus Zurnai, patient age, route, strength, and device because nasal spray and auto-injector instructions cannot be combined.

  • Continuously reassess respiratory rate, chest movement, oxygenation, capnography when available, mental status, and circulation because apparent awakening can precede relapse.

  • Prepare for vomiting, agitation, pain, hypertension, and prolonged withdrawal because nalmefene may outlast naloxone.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to nalmefene or a product ingredient is the labeled contraindication because re-exposure can provoke another hypersensitivity reaction.

  • Current Opvee and Zurnai products are not indicated below age 12 because safety and effectiveness have not been established for that age group; use an age-appropriate approved antagonist and airway protocol instead.

  • Do not use nalmefene as a substitute for ventilation or emergency transport because reversal may be incomplete, respiratory depression can recur, and non-opioid causes of coma remain untreated.

Interactions

  • Buprenorphine and pentazocine may be incompletely or gradually reversed because tight receptor binding or mixed agonist-antagonist behavior can persist despite nalmefene.

  • Benzodiazepines, alcohol, xylazine, gabapentinoids, and other co-depressants are not neutralized because nalmefene only antagonizes opioid receptors, so a response cannot end airway support or the diagnostic evaluation.

  • Trying to overcome nalmefene blockade with large or repeated opioid doses can cause delayed opioid intoxication and death when antagonist effect wanes, so precipitated withdrawal must not be self-treated with more opioid.

Key Labs

  • Do not delay nalmefene or ventilation for a toxicology result because overdose reversal is a clinical emergency and prolonged hypoventilation causes hypoxic injury.

  • Trend respiratory rate, chest movement, pulse oximetry, and capnography when available, and obtain a blood gas when ventilation remains impaired because apparent awakening or an acceptable oxygen saturation does not prove adequate carbon-dioxide clearance.

  • Check glucose and use ECG, electrolytes, renal and hepatic tests, toxicology studies, and complication-directed imaging as indicated because co-ingestants, organ failure, aspiration, trauma, or stroke can explain persistent coma and alter monitoring needs.

Nclex Traps

  • Opvee is intranasal and Zurnai is IM or SC; they are not interchangeable devices.

  • Nalmefene is not naltrexone and is not the naloxone in buprenorphine/naloxone.

  • A longer-acting antagonist still does not eliminate recurrence, mixed overdose, or airway risk.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Patients younger than 12 years

    Note

    Current Opvee and Zurnai labels do not establish use in this age group; emergency clinicians should use age-appropriate approved rescue and airway protocols.

  • Population

    Opioid-dependent patients

    Note

    Longer receptor blockade can produce severe and sustained withdrawal, vomiting, pain, autonomic stress, and fetal stress during pregnancy.

  • Population

    Renal or hepatic impairment

    Note

    Altered opioid and antagonist kinetics can make recurrence or prolonged withdrawal less predictable, so extended clinical monitoring may be needed.

Class Pathway

Description

Nalmefene is a longer-acting opioid-receptor antagonist that competitively blocks and displaces opioids from opioid receptors, restoring respiratory drive and reducing central nervous system depression; in the United States, it is supplied as Opvee 2.7 mg/0.1 mL single-use nasal spray and Zurnai 1.5 mg/0.5 mL single-use intramuscular or subcutaneous auto-injector. It is not naloxone, naltrexone, or the naloxone component of buprenorphine/naloxone. Its longer pharmacologic effect may reduce some recurrence risk, but emergency services and ventilation remain mandatory because exposure may be massive, mixed, delayed, or caused by a partial agonist. Both rescue products are prescription products for patients 12 years and older under their current labels.

Adverse Effects

  • Nalmefene can precipitate severe and prolonged opioid withdrawal with pain, agitation, vomiting, diarrhea, hypertension, and tachycardia because competitive receptor blockade abruptly removes opioid signaling and lasts longer than naloxone.

  • Vomiting, agitation, dizziness, and rapid return of pain can cause aspiration, falls, line removal, or injury, so protect the airway and continue supervised emergency care even when breathing improves.

  • Pulmonary edema, dysrhythmia, blood-pressure instability, seizure, or cardiac arrest have been reported after abrupt opioid reversal, especially with cardiovascular disease or other cardiotoxic drugs, so high-risk patients need monitoring because sympathetic stress and preceding hypoxia may both contribute.

Administration Timing

  • Give immediately for suspected opioid respiratory depression while activating 911 and providing ventilation or CPR; diagnostic certainty is not required before lifesaving support.

  • Opvee is one 2.7 mg/0.1 mL spray in one nostril; Zurnai delivers 1.5 mg/0.5 mL intramuscularly or subcutaneously into the anterolateral thigh, through clothing if necessary, and is held in place for the labeled 3 seconds.

  • If the patient does not respond or relapses, use a new device every 2 to 5 minutes according to the exact label while emergency care continues.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Inadequate breathing after labeled repeats, relapse, pulmonary edema, seizure, severe withdrawal, chest pain, arrhythmia, or persistent coma requires advanced resuscitation and toxicology consultation.

Evidence Limitations

  • Longer half-life is a pharmacologic property, not proof that nalmefene is superior to rapidly available naloxone in every community or exposure.

Regulatory Status

Opvee and Zurnai are FDA-approved prescription opioid-overdose rescue products for adults and pediatric patients 12 years and older; they have different routes and strengths.
