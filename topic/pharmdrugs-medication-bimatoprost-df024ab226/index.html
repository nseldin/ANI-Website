Class

Ophthalmic prostamide/FP-pathway agonist outflow enhancer

Used To Treat

Once-daily reduction of elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension; use the exact product schedule and continue optic-nerve/visual-field follow-up because pressure lowering does not restore lost nerve fibers.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Permanent iris darkening, eyelid pigmentation, eyelash growth or misdirection, periorbital fat atrophy/deepened lid sulcus, conjunctival hyperemia, intraocular inflammation, herpetic-keratitis reactivation and cystoid macular edema in susceptible eyes are the key class risks. More frequent dosing can reduce the pressure-lowering response.

Mechanism

Bimatoprost and its hydrolyzed acid engage prostamide-sensitive and FP prostanoid pathways in ciliary muscle and trabecular outflow tissue. Extracellular-matrix and cytoskeletal changes lower resistance through more than one drainage route. Hair follicles spend longer in the growth phase, which explains the eyelash indication and the risk of unwanted hair where solution repeatedly contacts skin.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give once daily at the prescribed time; do not add extra doses for a missed or high-pressure reading. Document baseline iris color, lens/capsule status, inflammation/herpetic history and visual change.

  • Do not interchange the glaucoma bottle technique with the eyelash applicator protocol. Wipe overflow to prevent unwanted skin hair or pigmentation and use a new sterile applicator for each upper lid when the eyelash product is prescribed.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity. Clarify active uveitis/iritis, active herpetic keratitis, aphakia or torn posterior lens capsule, macular-edema risk and whether the glaucoma mechanism fits the patient's angle anatomy.

Interactions

  • Separate other topical eye medicines by at least 5 minutes. Thimerosal-containing drops can precipitate with selected prostaglandin products. Another prostaglandin analog can paradoxically blunt pressure lowering rather than provide additive benefit.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic-nerve examination and visual fields

  • Ocular examination for pigmentation, lash direction, inflammation, corneal disease and macular edema when vision changes occur

Nclex Traps

  • Iris pigmentation may be permanent; eyelid and eyelash changes are more likely to improve after stopping.

  • More frequent dosing is not more effective and can reduce the IOP response.

  • The eyelash product is applied to the upper lid margin, not dropped into the eye as a cosmetic shortcut; glaucoma and eyelash formulations/techniques have different purposes.

Description

Bimatoprost is a prostamide analog used as an eye drop to lower intraocular pressure and in a separate application protocol to increase upper-eyelash prominence. In glaucoma it increases both uveoscleral and trabecular outflow. The same hair and pigment biology that makes lashes longer can cause iris darkening, eyelid pigmentation, misdirected hair and deepening of the upper-lid sulcus.

Adverse Effects

  • Conjunctival hyperemia, stinging, blurred vision, permanent iris pigmentation, eyelid darkening, eyelash length/thickness or misdirection, periorbital fat atrophy, uveitis/iritis, herpetic keratitis reactivation and cystoid macular edema in susceptible eyes.
