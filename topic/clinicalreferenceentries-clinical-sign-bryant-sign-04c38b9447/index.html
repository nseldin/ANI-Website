Definition

Bryant sign is an observed skin sign. It is a clue to retroperitoneal hemorrhage. A positive finding means scrotal ecchymosis develops without direct scrotal trauma.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bryant sign means scrotal ecchymosis develops without direct scrotal trauma.

Why It Matters

The observed Bryant sign finding can signal retroperitoneal hemorrhage. Blood tracks from the retroperitoneum through the inguinal canal into scrotal tissues. Bryant sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • retroperitoneal hemorrhage

Assessment Method

Inspect the scrotum in an appropriate patient with privacy and consent.

Positive Finding

Scrotal ecchymosis develops without direct scrotal trauma.

Mechanism

Blood tracks from the retroperitoneum through the inguinal canal into scrotal tissues.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bryant sign finding raises concern for retroperitoneal hemorrhage because blood tracks from the retroperitoneum through the inguinal canal into scrotal tissues. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bryant sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bryant sign to focus evaluation of retroperitoneal hemorrhage and the reviewed alternatives: testicular trauma, torsion, inguinal hernia. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bryant sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Delayed appearance and local causes limit specificity. Therefore, Bryant sign cannot confirm or exclude retroperitoneal hemorrhage by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • testicular trauma

  • torsion

  • inguinal hernia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as testicular trauma, torsion, inguinal hernia.

False Negative Situations

Bryant sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of retroperitoneal hemorrhage.
