Definition

Crossed adductor reflex is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments. A positive finding means the opposite thigh adducts.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Crossed adductor reflex means the opposite thigh adducts.

Why It Matters

A positive Crossed adductor reflex response can signal spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments. Reflex spread crosses spinal interneurons when descending inhibition is reduced. Crossed adductor reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments

Assessment Method

Tap the medial thigh or patellar tendon while observing the opposite leg.

Positive Finding

The opposite thigh adducts.

Mechanism

Reflex spread crosses spinal interneurons when descending inhibition is reduced.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Crossed adductor reflex response raises concern for spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments because reflex spread crosses spinal interneurons when descending inhibition is reduced. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Crossed adductor reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Crossed adductor reflex to focus evaluation of spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments and the reviewed alternatives: normal hyperreflexia, anxiety, positioning artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Tap the medial thigh or patellar tendon while observing the opposite leg. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Crossed adductor reflex as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It can occur in healthy people with brisk reflexes and does not localize precisely. Therefore, Crossed adductor reflex cannot confirm or exclude spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal hyperreflexia

  • anxiety

  • positioning artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal hyperreflexia, anxiety, positioning artifact, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Crossed adductor reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct for diffuse hyperreflexia. When performed as described, Crossed adductor reflex can adjust concern for spinal cord hyperreflexia above lumbar segments, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Crossed adductor reflex: It can occur in healthy people with brisk reflexes and does not localize precisely.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Crossed adductor reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Tap the medial thigh or patellar tendon while observing the opposite leg. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Ensure the contralateral movement is true adduction rather than transmitted bed motion.
