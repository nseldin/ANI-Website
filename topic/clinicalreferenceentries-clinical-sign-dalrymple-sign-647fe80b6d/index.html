Definition

Dalrymple sign is an observed ocular sign. It is a clue to thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity. A positive finding means the upper lid is retracted with sclera visible above the cornea.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Dalrymple sign means the upper lid is retracted with sclera visible above the cornea.

Why It Matters

The observed Dalrymple sign finding can signal thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity. Increased sympathetic tone and levator fibrosis elevate the upper lid. Dalrymple sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity

Assessment Method

Observe upper-lid position in primary gaze.

Positive Finding

The upper lid is retracted with sclera visible above the cornea.

Mechanism

Increased sympathetic tone and levator fibrosis elevate the upper lid.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Dalrymple sign finding raises concern for thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity because increased sympathetic tone and levator fibrosis elevate the upper lid. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Dalrymple sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Dalrymple sign to focus evaluation of thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity and the reviewed alternatives: Collier sign, proptosis, facial retraction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe upper-lid position in primary gaze. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Dalrymple sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal lid anatomy and other neurologic disease can mimic it. Therefore, Dalrymple sign cannot confirm or exclude thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Collier sign

  • proptosis

  • facial retraction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Collier sign, proptosis, facial retraction.

False Negative Situations

Dalrymple sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thyroid-associated sympathetic or levator overactivity.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Assess vision, pain, color perception, and corneal exposure rather than cosmetic appearance alone.
