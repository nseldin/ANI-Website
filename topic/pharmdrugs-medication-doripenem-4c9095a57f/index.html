Used To Treat

Historically labeled for susceptible complicated intra-abdominal and complicated urinary infections, including pyelonephritis. U.S. availability may be limited, and it should not be used for ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia because a clinical trial showed increased mortality and lower cure.

Mechanism

Doripenem enters Gram-negative bacteria and acylates PBPs, with fT>MIC driving killing. It is stable to human renal dehydropeptidase and does not need cilastatin. Renal elimination requires dose adjustment, while carbapenemases, porin loss, efflux, and target change cause resistance. The VABP harm signal may reflect an inadequate studied regimen and difficult organisms or disease severity, but because mortality was higher and cure lower, the practical consequence is to avoid the indication rather than speculate away the trial result.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Doripenem is not approved for and should not be used to treat ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia because increased mortality and lower cure were observed in a trial.

Contraindications

  • Do not use for VABP because the observed mortality and cure signal makes the risk clinically decisive.

  • Serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity and carbapenem-valproate coadministration require avoidance or specialist planning.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and current product availability before preparation because a remembered carbapenem spectrum does not override the VABP warning.

  • Check renal function, CNS history, valproate, cultures, infusion completion, and source control because exposure and organism fit still determine non-VABP treatment.

Interactions

  • Doripenem lowers valproate concentrations as a carbapenem class effect and can precipitate seizures.

  • Probenecid increases doripenem exposure; nephrotoxins can reduce clearance and amplify neurologic risk.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC and carbapenemase data, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, mental status, and valproate concentration when exposed.

  • Clinical deterioration in pneumonia requires an active alternative because extending an unsupported doripenem regimen leaves infection undertreated and repeats the exposure linked to worse VABP outcomes.

Nclex Traps

  • Doripenem has antipseudomonal activity when susceptible, but it should not be used for ventilator-associated pneumonia because a clinical trial found higher mortality and lower cure.

  • The warning comes from worse clinical outcomes, not from a claim that doripenem lacks all in-vitro lung-pathogen activity.

Description

Doripenem is an IV carbapenem beta-lactam antibiotic that binds multiple PBPs, blocks bacterial wall cross-linking, resists many ESBL and AmpC enzymes, and covers susceptible Enterobacterales, Pseudomonas, and anaerobes. Its most important defining fact is a negative one: a shortened high-dose regimen in ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia produced higher mortality and lower clinical cure than imipenem/cilastatin, leading to a strong warning that doripenem is not approved and should not be used for that indication. This is not proof that every doripenem course is intrinsically fatal; it means disease, dose, duration, exposure, and trial outcome matter, and the VABP regimen failed to provide a favorable benefit-risk profile.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, diarrhea, phlebitis, rash, liver-test elevation, candidiasis, and CDI.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, cytopenias, interstitial nephritis, encephalopathy, myoclonus, and seizures can occur, especially with renal accumulation.
