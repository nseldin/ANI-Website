Used To Treat

Add-on maintenance for severe eosinophilic asthma from age six, adult chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, inadequately controlled eosinophilic COPD in adults, eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, and hypereosinophilic syndrome meeting label criteria. It prevents disease activity over time and does not relieve acute bronchospasm.

Mechanism

Mepolizumab is a humanized IgG1 kappa antibody that binds soluble IL-5 with high affinity and prevents IL-5 from engaging IL-5 receptor alpha. The receptor normally pairs with a common beta chain and activates JAK/STAT, Ras-MAPK, and PI3K signaling that supports eosinophil maturation, trafficking, activation, and resistance to apoptosis. Ligand neutralization removes this survival input, so eosinophil numbers fall within days and remain suppressed between doses. Mepolizumab does not bind IL-5 receptor alpha directly and does not recruit NK-cell ADCC to deplete eosinophils the way benralizumab does. Residual eosinophils and other inflammatory pathways explain why response is substantial but not universal.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis, can occur within hours or be delayed for days. It is not treatment for acute asthma or COPD symptoms. Do not abruptly stop corticosteroids because adrenal insufficiency or previously suppressed disease can emerge. Herpes zoster has occurred, and IL-5 contributes to helminth defense; treat pre-existing helminth infection and interrupt therapy if infection persists despite treatment.

Contraindications

  • History of severe hypersensitivity to mepolizumab or excipients

  • Not for acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus; treat significant helminth infection before initiation

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and dose: 40 mg pediatric asthma, 100 mg for several indications, and 300 mg for EGPA or HES are not interchangeable assumptions.

  • Trend exacerbations, steroid exposure, eosinophils, organ manifestations, injection reactions, zoster status, and delayed hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Do not abruptly reduce systemic or inhaled corticosteroids; taper under supervision.

  • No classic CYP interactions are expected, but unstudied biologic combinations should not be improvised.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and trend absolute eosinophil count interpreted with recent corticosteroids, exacerbations, spirometry, and disease-specific organ assessment

  • Zoster risk, helminth history, injection reaction, and corticosteroid burden

Nclex Traps

  • Mepolizumab binds IL-5 ligand; benralizumab binds IL-5 receptor alpha.

  • A lower eosinophil count predicts target engagement, not instant relief of a current asthma or COPD attack.

Description

Mepolizumab is a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody that captures interleukin-5 before IL-5 can bind the alpha chain of its receptor on eosinophils. IL-5 is a major signal for eosinophil growth, differentiation, recruitment, activation, and survival, so neutralizing it lowers blood and tissue eosinophils and reduces eosinophil-driven exacerbations or organ injury. It is not a broad immunosuppressant and does not directly relax airway muscle. In 2025 the U.S. indication expanded to selected eosinophilic COPD, making phenotype documentation more important than the broad diagnosis. Different diseases use different doses, and a 300-mg dose is delivered as three separate 100-mg injections rather than one improvised larger injection.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, injection-site reaction, back pain, fatigue, or indication-specific pharyngitis, arthralgia, diarrhea, and cough

  • Hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis, herpes zoster, helminth concern, opportunistic infection, or antibody development with reduced effect
