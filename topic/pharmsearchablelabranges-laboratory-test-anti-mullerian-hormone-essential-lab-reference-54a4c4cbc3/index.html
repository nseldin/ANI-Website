Range

An Anti-Mullerian hormone (AMH) result helps guide counseling about ovarian reserve, meaning the remaining ovarian follicle pool, and expected response to ovarian stimulation. A higher AMH result is associated with a larger recruitable follicle pool and may occur with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), while a lower result suggests fewer recruitable follicles in the appropriate age and clinical context. Laboratories may report ng/mL or pmol/L, but assays, populations, and reference limits differ; interpret the performing laboratory's method and range with age, menstrual-cycle context, current or recent hormonal contraception, ovarian surgery, and prior treatment response rather than applying one universal cutoff.

Why

AMH is produced by granulosa cells around small ovarian follicles. In infertility care, AMH helps predict egg yield and low or excessive response to controlled ovarian stimulation; age remains a stronger general predictor of reproductive success, and AMH does not measure egg quality. Assess age, menstrual and ovulation history, duration of infertility, pregnancy goals, prior ovarian surgery or gonadotoxic chemotherapy or radiation, current or recent hormonal contraception, and prior stimulation response. Common evaluation includes antral follicle count by transvaginal ultrasound and, when indicated, FSH with estradiol plus ovulation, uterine or tubal, and semen evaluation. An isolated AMH result has no universal emergency cutoff; the clinical question, not the number alone, sets urgency, so communicate the result within the active fertility-treatment or gonadotoxic-treatment plan. One AMH result does not diagnose infertility, PCOS, primary ovarian insufficiency, or menopause and cannot by itself predict natural conception, egg quality, pregnancy, live birth, or the exact age of menopause.

High Causes

  • A larger recruitable ovarian follicle pool for age

  • A PCOS pattern in an adult when interpreted within the full diagnostic algorithm

  • Greater ovarian response to controlled stimulation

  • Population, assay, menstrual-cycle, or other biologic variation

Low Causes

  • A smaller recruitable ovarian follicle pool for age

  • Advancing reproductive age

  • Prior ovarian surgery or gonadotoxic chemotherapy or radiation

  • Current or recent hormonal contraception that suppresses the measured value

Critical Concerns

  • A low AMH value is not a stand-alone infertility verdict and should not be used to deny fertility treatment.

  • AMH is not a single-test diagnosis of PCOS or primary ovarian insufficiency.

  • No AMH result has a universal emergency cutoff; the clinical question and active treatment plan determine follow-up timing.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess age, menstrual and ovulation history, infertility duration, pregnancy goals, prior ovarian surgery or gonadotoxic treatment, current or recent hormonal contraception, and prior stimulation response.

  • Verify the performing assay, units, population-specific reference information, and whether prior results used the same method before comparing values.

  • Coordinate antral follicle count and other indicated fertility evaluation rather than counseling from AMH alone.

Nclex Traps

  • Ovarian reserve describes oocyte quantity, not egg quality.

  • AMH predicts ovarian-stimulation response better than natural fertility or live birth.

  • One AMH result does not diagnose infertility, PCOS, primary ovarian insufficiency, or menopause.
