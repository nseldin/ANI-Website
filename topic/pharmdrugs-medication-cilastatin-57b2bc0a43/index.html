Mechanism

Cilastatin works by inhibiting renal dehydropeptidase I, the brush-border kidney enzyme that degrades imipenem. Cilastatin has no meaningful antibacterial killing by itself; it protects imipenem exposure and reduces nephrotoxic metabolite formation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Cilastatin is not the antibacterial agent. The nursing focus remains infection response, renal dosing, seizure risk, and allergy screening for the carbapenem product.

  • Monitor kidney function because renal impairment changes dosing and toxicity risk.

Contraindications

  • Clarify serious carbapenem or beta-lactam allergy and seizure history when given with imipenem.

Interactions

  • Valproic acid levels can fall with carbapenems, raising seizure risk. Avoid or clarify therapy if applicable.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine, cultures, WBC trend, temperature, and neurologic status if seizure risk exists.

Nclex Traps

  • Cilastatin protects imipenem from kidney breakdown. It does not make imipenem seizure-risk-free.

Description

Cilastatin is a renal dehydropeptidase inhibitor: it works by inhibiting renal dehydropeptidase I, the brush-border kidney enzyme that degrades imipenem. It is used for imipenem protection from renal dehydropeptidase breakdown, improving imipenem recovery and reducing toxic renal metabolite formation.
