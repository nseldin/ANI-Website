Definition

Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign is an auscultatory abdominal sign. It is a clue to portal hypertension with recanalized paraumbilical veins. A positive finding means dilated abdominal veins accompany a continuous venous hum.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign means dilated abdominal veins accompany a continuous venous hum.

Why It Matters

A positive Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign response can signal portal hypertension with recanalized paraumbilical veins. Portal blood diverts through reopened paraumbilical veins into systemic abdominal-wall veins. Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • portal hypertension with recanalized paraumbilical veins

Assessment Method

Inspect the abdominal wall and auscultate over the umbilicus or epigastrium.

Positive Finding

Dilated abdominal veins accompany a continuous venous hum.

Mechanism

Portal blood diverts through reopened paraumbilical veins into systemic abdominal-wall veins.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign response raises concern for portal hypertension with recanalized paraumbilical veins because portal blood diverts through reopened paraumbilical veins into systemic abdominal-wall veins. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

The hum may be absent and abdominal bruits have other causes. Therefore, Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign cannot confirm or exclude portal hypertension with recanalized paraumbilical veins by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hepatic artery bruit, AV fistula, innocent venous hum, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Cruveilhier-Baumgarten sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
