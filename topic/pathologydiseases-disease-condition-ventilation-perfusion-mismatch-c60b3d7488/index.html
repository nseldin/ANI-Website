Pronunciation

ven-tih-LAY-shun per-FYOO-zhun MIS-match

Plain Meaning

Airflow to alveoli and blood flow through pulmonary capillaries are not matched well enough for efficient gas exchange.

Word Parts

  • Root

    ventilation

    Meaning

    air movement into and out of alveoli

  • Root

    perfusion

    Meaning

    blood flow through capillaries

Word Origin

The term compares V for ventilation with Q for perfusion, the conventional physiology symbol for blood flow.

Pathology

Gas exchange works best when alveoli receive both air and blood. Low V/Q means blood reaches poorly ventilated alveoli, as in asthma, COPD, pneumonia, or edema. High V/Q means air reaches poorly perfused alveoli, as in pulmonary embolism or low pulmonary blood flow. Severe low V/Q can behave like shunt.

Etiology

Asthma, COPD, pneumonia, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, pulmonary embolism, shock, ARDS, and pulmonary vascular disease.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, low SpO2, abnormal breath sounds, pleuritic pain with PE, wheezing with obstruction, or crackles with edema/pneumonia

  • CO2 may rise when ventilation failure is significant

Diagnostics

  • Pulse oximetry/ABG

  • Chest imaging

  • CT pulmonary angiography or V/Q scan when PE is suspected

  • PFTs for chronic obstructive patterns

  • Response to bronchodilator, oxygen, PEEP, or anticoagulation depends on cause

Treatments

  • Treat the cause: bronchodilators, antibiotics, diuresis, anticoagulation, recruitment/PEEP, positioning, or perfusion support

  • Supplemental oxygen often helps low V/Q mismatch more than true shunt

Nursing Priorities

  • Ask which side of the match is failing: air, blood flow, or both

  • Escalate sudden dyspnea, pleuritic pain, syncope, hemoptysis, severe hypoxemia, or rising CO2

  • Trend response to the intervention matched to the mechanism

Complications

  • Hypoxemia

  • Hypercapnia

  • Respiratory failure

  • Right-heart strain

  • Cardiac arrest

Patient Education

  • Take lung and clot-prevention medications as prescribed and report sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or oxygen drops.

Nclex Traps

  • V/Q mismatch is not one disease; it is a gas-exchange pattern.

  • Pulmonary embolism is a high V/Q problem: ventilated alveoli have reduced perfusion.

  • Pneumonia/atelectasis/edema are low V/Q or shunt-like problems.
