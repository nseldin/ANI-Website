Definition

Nail pitting is an observed nail sign. It is a clue to disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization. A positive finding means multiple shallow pits interrupt the nail surface.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Nail pitting means multiple shallow pits interrupt the nail surface.

Why It Matters

The observed Nail pitting finding can signal disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization. Transient inflammatory foci in the nail matrix produce defective plate formation. Nail pitting cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization

Assessment Method

Inspect several nail plates for small depressions.

Positive Finding

Multiple shallow pits interrupt the nail surface.

Mechanism

Transient inflammatory foci in the nail matrix produce defective plate formation.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Nail pitting finding raises concern for disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization because transient inflammatory foci in the nail matrix produce defective plate formation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Nail pitting does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Nail pitting to focus evaluation of disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization and the reviewed alternatives: trauma, lichen planus, habit-tic deformity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect several nail plates for small depressions. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Nail pitting as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A few pits can occur in healthy people and pattern matters. Therefore, Nail pitting cannot confirm or exclude disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • trauma

  • lichen planus

  • habit-tic deformity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as trauma, lichen planus, habit-tic deformity.

False Negative Situations

Nail pitting may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of disordered proximal nail-matrix keratinization.
