Used To Treat

Serious susceptible pneumonia, febrile neutropenia, complicated urinary, skin, and intra-abdominal infections with companion anaerobic coverage when needed, including selected Pseudomonas infections.

Mechanism

Cefepime is zwitterionic, which supports rapid passage through selected gram-negative porins. It binds multiple PBPs and blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking, producing bactericidal wall failure. Relative stability against chromosomal AmpC and lower affinity for some beta-lactamases support activity against selected hospital gram-negative organisms, but ESBLs, carbapenemases, porin loss, efflux, and target changes can still defeat it. More than most bedside teaching acknowledges, efficacy depends on maintaining free concentration above the MIC while avoiding excessive trough exposure. Renal accumulation raises CSF drug and antagonizes GABA-A inhibition, producing cortical hyperexcitability and nonconvulsive seizures.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Cefepime has no current U.S. boxed warning, but life-threatening or fatal neurotoxicity is a prominent label warning. Most cases involve renal impairment or unadjusted dosing, although cases occur despite adjustment. New encephalopathy, aphasia, hallucinations, myoclonus, or seizure requires immediate drug and renal review, discontinuation when suspected, supportive care, and possible EEG or hemodialysis. Severe hypersensitivity and C. difficile colitis remain major class hazards.

Contraindications

  • Known immediate hypersensitivity to cefepime, cephalosporins, penicillins, or other beta-lactams under the product label; characterize the prior reaction precisely.

  • Do not use cefepime alone when MRSA, Enterococcus, Listeria, atypical, or anaerobic coverage is required, and do not continue broad empiric therapy after data support a narrower drug.

Nursing Essentials

  • Calculate creatinine clearance before the first dose and repeatedly during sepsis, shock, diuresis, dialysis, or AKI. A lagging serum creatinine does not guarantee stable clearance.

  • Perform baseline and serial mental-status, speech, tremor/myoclonus, and seizure assessment; obtain cultures and source control, and request narrowing when susceptibility permits.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides and potent diuretics can add nephrotoxicity, indirectly raising cefepime exposure; other neurotoxic drugs can obscure recognition of the syndrome.

  • Dialysis removes cefepime and changes post-dialysis dosing; verify modality and schedule rather than treating all renal replacement therapies alike.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, renal function and calculated clearance, urine output, CBC, liver tests, and stool assessment.

  • Neurologic checks, medication timing, dialysis timing, and EEG for unexplained encephalopathy because nonconvulsive status may have no dramatic motor seizure.

Nclex Traps

  • Cefepime neurotoxicity can mimic ICU delirium or worsening sepsis. Renal decline plus confusion, aphasia, or myoclonus should make the drug a leading cause.

  • Fourth generation does not mean MRSA or anaerobic coverage; spectrum must be stated organism by organism.

Description

Cefepime is a fourth-generation IV cephalosporin that binds bacterial penicillin-binding proteins and blocks cell-wall cross-linking, combining strong aerobic gram-negative activity including susceptible Pseudomonas with better streptococcal and MSSA activity than ceftazidime. It is often used empirically in severe hospital infection or febrile neutropenia, then narrowed when cultures identify the organism. It does not reliably cover MRSA, Enterococcus, Listeria, atypicals, or anaerobes. Its signature danger is neurotoxicity from high CNS exposure, especially when renal function falls: confusion, hallucinations, aphasia, myoclonus, seizures, coma, and nonconvulsive status epilepticus can develop even after a previously appropriate dose becomes excessive during acute kidney injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion reactions, rash, pruritus, diarrhea, nausea, fever, headache, positive Coombs test, and transient liver or coagulation-test changes.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, cytopenias, encephalopathy, aphasia, hallucinations, myoclonus, nonconvulsive status epilepticus, coma, and death.
