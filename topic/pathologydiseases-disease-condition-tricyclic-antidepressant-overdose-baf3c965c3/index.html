Definition

Tricyclic antidepressant overdose develops when fast sodium-channel blockade slows depolarization while anticholinergic and alpha-blocking effects cause seizures, coma, tachycardia, and hypotension. The characteristic pattern is altered mental status, dry flushed skin, dilated pupils, seizures, hypotension, and a widened QRS with a terminal rightward axis. Confirm or stage it with immediate and serial electrocardiograms with QRS and aVR assessment; distinguish it from antihistamine, antipsychotic, carbamazepine, cocaine, and other sodium-channel blocker toxicity by exposure and ECG pattern. The priority is to secure ventilation while avoiding acidosis because ventricular dysrhythmia can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Timeline and trigger matter: exposure, ingestion, submersion, temperature stress, anesthesia, procedure, bite, or postoperative immobility plus vital-sign, neurologic, respiratory, skin, pain, and perfusion changes.

  • Escalate altered mental status, airway trouble, shock, dysrhythmia, severe temperature abnormality, rapidly spreading tissue injury, respiratory distress, or worsening pain/swelling.

Diagnostics

  • immediate and serial electrocardiograms with QRS and aVR assessment

  • electrolytes, glucose, blood gas, temperature, and coingestant testing

  • continuous rhythm, blood pressure, and neurologic monitoring

  • Distinguish Tricyclic antidepressant overdose from antihistamine, antipsychotic, carbamazepine, cocaine, and other sodium-channel blocker toxicity by exposure and ECG pattern.

Labs

  • lactate may rise with shock

  • glucose can be immediately reversible

  • potassium and acid-base shifts can kill quickly

  • drug levels matter when available

Treatments

  • secure ventilation while avoiding acidosis

  • give intravenous sodium bicarbonate for QRS widening, ventricular dysrhythmia, or hypotension

  • treat seizures promptly and use vasopressors when bicarbonate and fluids are insufficient

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for widening QRS, ventricular arrhythmia, refractory hypotension, seizure, severe agitation, coma, or cardiac arrest because toxicology-led resuscitation and advanced support are time-critical.

  • Secure the airway as needed and place the patient on continuous cardiac and seizure monitoring because sodium-channel blockade can cause abrupt ventricular dysrhythmia and coma.

  • Obtain serial electrocardiograms and administer ordered intravenous sodium bicarbonate for QRS widening, ventricular arrhythmia, or hypotension because alkalinization reduces cardiotoxic sodium-channel blockade.

  • Monitor QRS duration, rhythm, blood pressure, pH, sodium, potassium, temperature, consciousness, and seizure activity to detect recurrent toxicity and treatment complications.

Complications

  • ventricular dysrhythmia can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose.

  • refractory shock can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose.

  • seizure can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose.

  • aspiration can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose.

  • cardiac arrest can complicate Tricyclic antidepressant overdose.

  • hypernatremia or alkalemia during bicarbonate therapy

  • rapid deterioration after an initially alert period

Red Flags

  • Widening QRS or ventricular arrhythmia

  • Refractory hypotension or cardiac arrest

  • Seizure, coma, or rapidly declining consciousness

  • Recurrent electrocardiographic abnormality after initial improvement

Patient Education

  • Teach families to treat any suspected tricyclic antidepressant ingestion as an emergency and contact poison services rather than waiting for symptoms.

  • After recovery, arrange medication-safety and mental-health follow-up because overdose prevention requires attention to access and intent.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not use physostigmine for a tricyclic pattern with conduction delay

  • QRS widening is more useful for toxicity risk than the drug concentration
