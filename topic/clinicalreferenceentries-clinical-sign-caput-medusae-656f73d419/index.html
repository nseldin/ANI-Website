Definition

Caput medusae is an observed venous sign. It is a clue to portal hypertension. A positive finding means dilated tortuous veins radiate from the umbilicus.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Caput medusae means dilated tortuous veins radiate from the umbilicus.

Why It Matters

The observed Caput medusae finding can signal portal hypertension. High portal pressure reopens paraumbilical collaterals that drain into superficial systemic veins. Caput medusae cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • portal hypertension

Assessment Method

Inspect the abdominal wall for radiating veins and flow direction.

Positive Finding

Dilated tortuous veins radiate from the umbilicus.

Mechanism

High portal pressure reopens paraumbilical collaterals that drain into superficial systemic veins.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Caput medusae finding raises concern for portal hypertension because high portal pressure reopens paraumbilical collaterals that drain into superficial systemic veins. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Caput medusae does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Caput medusae to focus evaluation of portal hypertension and the reviewed alternatives: IVC obstruction, pregnancy veins, abdominal wall AVM. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the abdominal wall for radiating veins and flow direction. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Caput medusae as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity can hide veins and IVC obstruction creates a different flow pattern. Therefore, Caput medusae cannot confirm or exclude portal hypertension by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • IVC obstruction

  • pregnancy veins

  • abdominal wall AVM

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as IVC obstruction, pregnancy veins, abdominal wall AVM.

False Negative Situations

Caput medusae may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of portal hypertension.
