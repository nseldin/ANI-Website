Used To Treat

Type 2 diabetes when functioning pancreatic beta cells remain. These agents lower fasting and/or postmeal glucose by increasing endogenous insulin; they do not work when beta-cell insulin production is absent and do not treat DKA.

Description

Glipizide and glyburide are second-generation sulfonylureas with longer insulin-secretory effects. Repaglinide is a meglitinide designed for rapid, short meal-linked secretion. All can cause hypoglycemia because they can close the beta-cell potassium channel even when glucose or food is insufficient. Glyburide is particularly hazardous in older adults and renal impairment because active metabolites and prolonged action can cause recurrent lows. Repaglinide follows the meal: take before eating and skip the dose when the meal is skipped.

Mechanism

The beta-cell KATP channel contains the Kir6.2 pore and SUR1 regulatory subunit. ATP generated from glucose normally closes this channel, depolarizing the membrane, opening voltage-gated calcium channels, and triggering insulin-granule exocytosis. Sulfonylureas and repaglinide bind SUR1 and pharmacologically close KATP, bypassing part of glucose sensing. This explains both efficacy and danger: insulin can be released despite fasting, exercise, alcohol intake, or reduced renal/hepatic clearance. Repaglinide binds a related SUR1 site with rapid kinetics, aligning effect more closely with meals, but it still causes hypoglycemia.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: All insulin secretagogues can cause severe and prolonged hypoglycemia. Glyburide is especially problematic in older or renally impaired patients; repaglinide is contraindicated with gemfibrozil because CYP2C8/OATP-related exposure rises markedly.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypoglycemia, weight gain, nausea, dizziness, and headache

  • Sulfonamide-type hypersensitivity or blood dyscrasias rarely; hemolysis risk with G6PD deficiency

  • Repaglinide interactions can sharply increase exposure; glyburide lows can recur after initial correction

Contraindications

  • Type 1 diabetes, DKA, or inability to produce endogenous insulin

  • Active hypoglycemia and high-risk fasting without a clear medication plan

  • Repaglinide with gemfibrozil; conservative or alternative therapy for glyburide in older age or renal impairment

Nursing Essentials

  • Link each dose to meal timing and teach the patient to carry rapid glucose; recurrent hypoglycemia needs regimen correction, not repeated rescue alone.

  • After a glyburide-related severe low, continue observation because insulin secretion can outlast the first carbohydrate treatment.

Interactions

  • Insulin and other glucose-lowering drugs add hypoglycemia.

  • Alcohol, poor intake, exercise, renal/hepatic decline, azole antifungals, and selected CYP inhibitors increase risk.

  • Beta blockers can hide tremor/palpitations; gemfibrozil is prohibited with repaglinide.

Key Labs

  • Glucose/CGM pattern and A1c

  • Renal/hepatic function and meal reliability

  • Frequency, timing, severity, awareness, and recurrence of hypoglycemia

Nclex Traps

  • Glucose-dependent incretin drugs and glucose-independent secretagogues do not carry the same hypoglycemia risk.

  • Do not give repaglinide when the meal is skipped.

  • Glyburide is not preferred merely because it is potent; prolonged hypoglycemia can outweigh convenience.
