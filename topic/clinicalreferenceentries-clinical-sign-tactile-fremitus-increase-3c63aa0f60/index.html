Definition

Tactile fremitus increase is a palpation sign. It is a clue to lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus. A positive finding means vibration is stronger over a focal region.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Tactile fremitus increase means vibration is stronger over a focal region.

Why It Matters

A positive Tactile fremitus increase response can signal lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus. Dense lung transmits low-frequency vocal vibration better than air-filled alveoli when the airway remains open. Tactile fremitus increase cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus

Assessment Method

Place ulnar hands symmetrically on the chest while the patient repeats a low-pitched phrase.

Positive Finding

Vibration is stronger over a focal region.

Mechanism

Dense lung transmits low-frequency vocal vibration better than air-filled alveoli when the airway remains open.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Tactile fremitus increase response raises concern for lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus because dense lung transmits low-frequency vocal vibration better than air-filled alveoli when the airway remains open. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tactile fremitus increase does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tactile fremitus increase to focus evaluation of lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus and the reviewed alternatives: thin chest wall, atelectasis with patent airway, cavity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place ulnar hands symmetrically on the chest while the patient repeats a low-pitched phrase. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tactile fremitus increase as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Voice pitch, obesity, and hand pressure affect comparison. Therefore, Tactile fremitus increase cannot confirm or exclude lung consolidation connected to a patent bronchus by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • thin chest wall

  • atelectasis with patent airway

  • cavity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from thin chest wall, atelectasis with patent airway, cavity, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Tactile fremitus increase may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Apply the reviewed limitation for Tactile fremitus increase: Voice pitch, obesity, and hand pressure affect comparison.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Tactile fremitus increase, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place ulnar hands symmetrically on the chest while the patient repeats a low-pitched phrase. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Compare matching levels; absolute vibration without symmetry is unreliable.
