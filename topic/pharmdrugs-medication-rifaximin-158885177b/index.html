Used To Treat

Noninvasive E. coli travelers' diarrhea in age-approved patients, reduction of recurrent overt hepatic encephalopathy in adults, and adult IBS with diarrhea; indication and dose are not interchangeable.

Mechanism

Rifaximin binds the beta subunit encoded by bacterial rpoB, preventing RNA-chain initiation and downstream protein synthesis. In travelers' diarrhea it targets susceptible noninvasive E. coli. In hepatic encephalopathy and IBS-D, clinical benefit reflects altered microbial activity and metabolite production rather than systemic tissue antibiotic levels. rpoB mutation can confer rifamycin resistance.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to rifaximin, another rifamycin or formulation components

  • Do not use for invasive travelers' diarrhea with fever or blood in stool

  • Clarify severe hepatic impairment, C. difficile history and major transporter inhibitors

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the exact indication before checking dose: the travelers' diarrhea, HE and IBS-D regimens differ.

  • For HE, assess mental status, adherence to lactulose, stool output and common precipitants; rifaximin recurrence prevention does not prove the current confusion is hepatic.

Interactions

  • Cyclosporine inhibits P-glycoprotein/OATP transport and greatly increases rifaximin exposure.

  • Warfarin INR changes have been reported; monitor when therapy starts or stops.

  • Other rifamycins do not substitute automatically because systemic exposure, induction and indications differ.

Key Labs

  • Mental status and recurrence history for HE; hydration/stool pattern for diarrhea indications

  • Liver tests and clinical hepatic severity when exposure risk is high

  • INR with warfarin; stool testing or infection workup for persistent/worsening diarrhea

Nclex Traps

  • Rifaximin is not the red-orange-fluid, strong enzyme-inducing rifampin card.

  • Bloody diarrhea or fever argues against rifaximin travelers' diarrhea treatment.

  • In HE, clinical status and precipitant correction matter more than simply adding another bowel drug.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not use the travelers' diarrhea regimen for fever, bloody stool or suspected invasive Campylobacter, Shigella or Salmonella disease. Stop and reassess worsening/persistent diarrhea and consider C. difficile. Severe hepatic dysfunction and cyclosporine can markedly increase systemic exposure despite the usual 'minimally absorbed' description.

Description

Rifaximin is a gut-predominant rifamycin antibiotic that binds bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and blocks transcription. Minimal oral absorption produces high intraluminal exposure that, in hepatic encephalopathy, changes gut bacterial nitrogen metabolism, reduces the ammonia-generating microbial burden, and helps prevent recurrent overt episodes, usually alongside lactulose. It does not repair hepatic failure or replace the search for a precipitating cause, and its travelers' diarrhea and IBS-D indications use different clinical logic and regimens.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, abdominal symptoms and dizziness

  • HE population: peripheral edema, fatigue and ascites; IBS-D: ALT elevation and nausea

  • C. difficile-associated diarrhea, hypersensitivity/angioedema/anaphylaxis and resistant organisms
