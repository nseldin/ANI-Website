Used To Treat

Medically indicated induction of labor when delivery is appropriate and vaginal birth is not contraindicated. Dinoprostone or protocol-based misoprostol can ripen an unfavorable cervix; oxytocin then initiates or augments coordinated contractions when the cervix and obstetric situation support it.

Description

Labor induction has two different biological jobs. Cervical ripening softens, shortens, and opens a collagen-rich cervix; contraction induction makes the myometrium contract in a coordinated pattern. Dinoprostone, a prostaglandin E2 product, directly promotes cervical matrix remodeling and sensitizes the uterus to oxytocin. Misoprostol, a prostaglandin E1 analogue, can also ripen the cervix and stimulate contractions under obstetric protocols, although its U.S. tablet label is for NSAID-ulcer prevention. Oxytocin is strongest at coordinated uterine contraction but does not reliably transform a very unfavorable cervix by itself. That is why Bishop score, prior uterine surgery, fetal status, presentation, and the reason for delivery come before choosing a drug.

Mechanism

Prostaglandin signaling increases matrix metalloproteinase activity, redistributes collagen, raises water and glycosaminoglycan content, and recruits inflammatory remodeling in cervical tissue. Dinoprostone activates EP receptors and increases local PGF2-alpha production, which also sensitizes myometrium to oxytocin. Misoprostol acid activates EP receptors with formulation-, dose-, route-, and gestational-context-dependent cervical and uterine effects. Oxytocin activates Gq-coupled oxytocin receptors, generating inositol trisphosphate and calcium-calmodulin activation of myosin-light-chain kinase. Near term, estrogen-driven receptor expression and gap-junction formation make the uterus more responsive. Excess stimulation shortens resting intervals, reduces uteroplacental reperfusion between contractions, and can produce fetal heart-rate abnormalities.

Boxed Warning

Induction agents require an environment able to continuously assess uterine activity and fetal status and perform urgent delivery. Dinoprostone vaginal insert is removed for active labor, tachysystole, hypertonicity, fetal distress, or before amniotomy and must be removed at least 30 minutes before an IV oxytocic. Misoprostol can cause abortion, premature birth, congenital injury after failed exposure, tachysystole, and uterine rupture; risk rises with advancing gestation and a uterine scar. Oxytocin is indicated for medical rather than convenience-only elective induction under its U.S. label and can cause rupture, fetal hypoxia, water intoxication, or maternal cardiovascular effects when used improperly.

Adverse Effects

  • Uterine tachysystole, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fever, shivering, headache, and fetal heart-rate changes

  • Uterine rupture, placental abruption, fetal hypoxia, amniotic-fluid embolism syndrome, DIC, severe hyponatremia, or emergency operative delivery

Contraindications

  • Do not induce vaginal labor when vaginal birth itself is contraindicated, fetal distress requires immediate delivery, presentation or placentation is unsafe, or uterine integrity cannot tolerate prolonged contraction.

  • Prior cesarean or major uterine surgery changes prostaglandin selection and rupture risk; use current obstetric protocol rather than transferring a dose from another indication.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document Bishop score elements, indication, membrane status, presentation, placental location, uterine-scar history, baseline fetal tracing, and informed plan before the first dose.

  • Track contraction frequency, duration, resting tone, and fetal response. Stop or remove the agent and follow tachysystole protocol when recovery time becomes inadequate.

Interactions

  • Prostaglandins potentiate oxytocin. Respect product-specific removal and waiting intervals rather than stacking agents because labor appears slow.

  • Other drugs that alter blood pressure, fluid balance, or uterine tone can magnify maternal and fetal consequences.

Key Labs

  • No laboratory value substitutes for obstetric assessment; obtain CBC, type and screen, and indication-specific studies when clinically required

  • Bishop score, contraction pattern, resting tone, fetal heart-rate category, membrane status, maternal vital signs, intake/output, and labor progress

Nclex Traps

  • Cervical ripening and uterine contraction are related but not identical tasks; oxytocin is not simply a stronger cervical ripener.

  • Tachysystole matters because uteroplacental blood flow recovers between contractions. Too little relaxation can deprive the fetus of oxygen even when each contraction looks effective.
