Definition

Weight loss means measured body weight has decreased from a verified baseline. Whether it is intended, how much changed, how quickly it changed, and whether muscle, fat, or fluid was lost determine what it means.

Plain Language

The person's measured weight is lower than before, and the cause may be intentional change, fluid loss, reduced intake, illness, or tissue wasting.

Why It Matters

Unintentional or progressive weight loss can reveal swallowing difficulty, malabsorption, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, chronic infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effects, or poor access to food. Severe or rapid loss, dehydration, bleeding, fever, a mass, profound weakness, or functional decline needs prompt evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • intentional energy change or unintentional reduced intake, impaired swallowing, malabsorption, gastrointestinal or renal loss, hypermetabolism, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effect, substance use, or fluid loss

Assessment Method

Verify serial weights under comparable conditions and calculate amount, percentage, and time course; ask whether the change was intended and assess appetite, intake and food access, swallowing, gastrointestinal and urinary losses, fever, pain, mood, medications, substances, activity, edema, and functional change.

Positive Finding

Body weight falls from a verified prior baseline; concern increases when the loss is unintentional, progressive, rapid, accompanied by muscle loss, or disproportionate to an expected change in fluid or treatment.

Mechanism

Weight decreases when energy and nutrient intake or absorption falls below expenditure and losses, when catabolism accelerates, when tissue is lost, or when excess body water is removed.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Weight loss finding raises concern for intentional energy change or unintentional reduced intake, impaired swallowing, malabsorption, gastrointestinal or renal loss, hypermetabolism, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effect, substance use, or fluid loss because weight decreases when energy and nutrient intake or absorption falls below expenditure and losses, when catabolism accelerates, when tissue is lost, or when excess body water is removed. Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Weight loss to focus evaluation of intentional energy change or unintentional reduced intake, impaired swallowing, malabsorption, gastrointestinal or renal loss, hypermetabolism, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effect, substance use, or fluid loss and the reviewed alternatives: intentional weight reduction, dehydration, diuresis, scale or documentation error. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Weight loss as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Different scales, clothing, time of day, hydration, edema, diuresis, amputation, and unreliable recall can create apparent change; stable weight can conceal simultaneous muscle loss and fluid gain. Therefore, Weight loss cannot confirm or exclude intentional energy change or unintentional reduced intake, impaired swallowing, malabsorption, gastrointestinal or renal loss, hypermetabolism, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effect, substance use, or fluid loss by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • intentional weight reduction

  • dehydration

  • diuresis

  • scale or documentation error

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as intentional weight reduction, dehydration, diuresis, scale or documentation error.

False Negative Situations

Weight loss may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of intentional energy change or unintentional reduced intake, impaired swallowing, malabsorption, gastrointestinal or renal loss, hypermetabolism, hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, infection, inflammation, cancer, depression, medication effect, substance use, or fluid loss.
