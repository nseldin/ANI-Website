Plain Meaning

Steatosis occurs when abnormal fat droplets accumulate inside cells, especially triglyceride droplets inside liver hepatocytes.

Word Parts

  • Root

    steat- / steato-

    Meaning

  • Root

    Meaning

    condition, process, or abnormal state

Word Origin

Steatosis uses Greek-derived medical roots: steato- points to fat, while -osis signals a pathologic condition or process.

Pathology

In hepatic steatosis, fatty acid delivery or synthesis exceeds oxidation and export, so triglyceride droplets collect inside hepatocytes. In macrovesicular steatosis, a large fat droplet can push the nucleus to the side. In microvesicular steatosis, many tiny droplets suggest more severe mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation stress. Steatosis is the fat-storage stage; steatohepatitis adds inflammation and hepatocyte injury.

Etiology

Common drivers include insulin resistance, obesity, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, alcohol use, rapid weight change, malnutrition or refeeding contexts, pregnancy-related fatty liver syndromes, toxins, and selected medications such as amiodarone, valproate, methotrexate, tamoxifen, glucocorticoids, and some antiretroviral drugs.

Signs Symptoms

  • Often silent and found on imaging or mild liver enzyme evaluation

  • Possible fatigue, hepatomegaly, or right upper quadrant fullness

  • Metabolic clues such as central adiposity, high triglycerides, diabetes, or hypertension

  • Symptoms of advanced liver disease are not expected from simple steatosis alone and suggest progression or another process

Diagnostics

  • ALT and AST may be normal or mildly elevated and do not reliably stage fibrosis

  • Ultrasound may report increased echogenicity consistent with fatty infiltration

  • MRI-PDFF and controlled attenuation parameter measurements estimate fat burden more quantitatively when used

  • Elastography helps separate fat accumulation from fibrosis risk

  • Biopsy, when used, can distinguish simple steatosis from steatohepatitis and stage fibrosis

Treatments

  • Treat the driver: metabolic risk control, alcohol avoidance when indicated, medication/toxin review, nutrition support, and weight management when appropriate

  • Exercise and improved insulin sensitivity can reduce hepatic fat even before major weight loss

  • Escalate evaluation when steatosis occurs with thrombocytopenia, rising INR, low albumin, jaundice, ascites, or suspected steatohepatitis

Nursing Priorities

  • Explain that steatosis is fat inside cells, not simply fat around an organ

  • Watch for progression language: steatosis plus inflammation becomes steatohepatitis; repeated injury can drive fibrosis and cirrhosis

  • Connect liver findings with diabetes, lipids, blood pressure, alcohol history, medications, and viral hepatitis risk

Complications

  • Steatohepatitis

  • Fibrosis

  • Cirrhosis

  • Portal hypertension

  • Hepatocellular carcinoma when advanced chronic liver disease develops

Patient Education

  • Follow the plan for diabetes, lipid, weight, alcohol, and medication safety because simple steatosis can be reversible.

  • Keep follow-up even if symptoms are absent.

  • Seek care for jaundice, abdominal swelling, confusion, black stools, vomiting blood, or severe weakness.

Nclex Traps

  • Steatosis is not the same as steatohepatitis. The latter includes inflammation and cell injury.

  • Normal AST/ALT does not prove the liver is free of fibrosis.

  • The clinical danger rises when fat storage is paired with inflammation, scarring, or impaired synthetic function.
