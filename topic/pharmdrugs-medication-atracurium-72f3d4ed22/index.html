Class

Intermediate-acting benzylisoquinolinium nondepolarizing neuromuscular blocker

Used To Treat

Adjunct to general anesthesia for tracheal intubation and skeletal-muscle relaxation during surgery or mechanical ventilation when continuous sedation, analgesia, ventilation and quantitative blockade monitoring are present.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Accidental administration can cause fatal respiratory paralysis. It must be given only with immediate ventilation/intubation capability, continuous sedation and analgesia, and objective neuromuscular monitoring. Rapid bolus or higher doses can release histamine and produce marked hypotension, tachycardia, flushing or bronchospasm. Anaphylaxis is possible with any neuromuscular blocker.

Mechanism

Atracurium occupies postsynaptic Nm nicotinic receptor sites without opening the ion channel, so acetylcholine cannot depolarize the muscle end plate. Skeletal muscle becomes flaccid, including respiratory muscle. Hofmann elimination is a temperature- and pH-dependent nonenzymatic breakdown, supplemented by ester hydrolysis. Laudanosine crosses the blood-brain barrier and is excitatory at high concentrations, although clinical seizures are uncommon at routine exposure.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm adequate sedation/analgesia before every dose and throughout paralysis. Provide ventilation, eye protection, pressure-injury prevention and communication safeguards for awareness.

  • Use quantitative train-of-four monitoring. Temperature and pH alter Hofmann degradation: hypothermia and acidosis can slow breakdown and prolong effect despite normal renal function.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to atracurium or benzyl alcohol-containing formulation components. Clarify severe asthma, cardiovascular instability, neuromuscular disease, electrolyte/acid-base disturbance, hypothermia and medications that potentiate blockade.

Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics, aminoglycosides, magnesium, lithium, some antiarrhythmics and acid-base/electrolyte disturbances can potentiate blockade. Chronic antiseizure therapy or burns can produce resistance. Neostigmine can reverse appropriate residual block; sugammadex does not encapsulate atracurium.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative train-of-four ratio, continuous ventilation and hemodynamics

  • Temperature, acid-base status, potassium, magnesium and calcium

  • Clinical recovery and weakness assessment after discontinuation

Nclex Traps

  • Atracurium paralyzes but does not sedate or relieve pain.

  • Hofmann elimination is organ-independent but still depends on temperature and pH.

  • Sugammadex is not a universal paralytic reversal drug.

Description

Atracurium is a nondepolarizing paralytic that competitively blocks nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction. It does not cause sleep, amnesia or analgesia. Spontaneous Hofmann degradation and nonspecific ester hydrolysis make duration less dependent on liver or kidney clearance than many alternatives, but histamine release can cause hypotension, flushing and bronchospasm, and its metabolite laudanosine can accumulate during prolonged high exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Prolonged paralysis or weakness, hypotension, tachycardia, flushing, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis, injection-site reaction and laudanosine accumulation with prolonged infusion.
