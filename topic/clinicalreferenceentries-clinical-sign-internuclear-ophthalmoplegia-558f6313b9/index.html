Definition

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia is an observed ocular motor sign. It is a clue to medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion. A positive finding means the adducting eye is weak while the abducting eye may show nystagmus.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Internuclear ophthalmoplegia means the adducting eye is weak while the abducting eye may show nystagmus.

Why It Matters

The observed Internuclear ophthalmoplegia finding can signal medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion. A medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion disconnects the abducens internuclear signal from the opposite medial rectus nucleus. Internuclear ophthalmoplegia cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to look rapidly to each side.

Positive Finding

The adducting eye is weak while the abducting eye may show nystagmus.

Mechanism

A medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion disconnects the abducens internuclear signal from the opposite medial rectus nucleus.

The observed Internuclear ophthalmoplegia finding raises concern for medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion because a medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion disconnects the abducens internuclear signal from the opposite medial rectus nucleus. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Internuclear ophthalmoplegia does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Internuclear ophthalmoplegia to focus evaluation of medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion and the reviewed alternatives: pseudo-INO from myasthenia, third nerve palsy, restrictive eye disease. Evidence Limitations

Convergence may be variably preserved and subtle cases need expert examination. Therefore, Internuclear ophthalmoplegia cannot confirm or exclude medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pseudo-INO from myasthenia

  • third nerve palsy

  • restrictive eye disease

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as pseudo-INO from myasthenia, third nerve palsy, restrictive eye disease.

False Negative Situations

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of medial longitudinal fasciculus lesion.
