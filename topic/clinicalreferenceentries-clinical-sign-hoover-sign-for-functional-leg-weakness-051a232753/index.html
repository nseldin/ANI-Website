Definition

Hoover sign for functional leg weakness is a bedside motor consistency test. It is a clue to functional limb weakness. A positive finding means weak voluntary hip extension improves when the opposite hip flexes against resistance.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Hoover sign for functional leg weakness means weak voluntary hip extension improves when the opposite hip flexes against resistance.

Why It Matters

A positive Hoover sign for functional leg weakness result can signal functional limb weakness. Normal crossed synergistic motor activation persists despite impaired voluntary output in functional weakness. Hoover sign for functional leg weakness cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • functional limb weakness

Assessment Method

Place a hand under each heel and compare involuntary hip extension during contralateral hip flexion against resistance.

Positive Finding

Weak voluntary hip extension improves when the opposite hip flexes against resistance.

Normal crossed synergistic motor activation persists despite impaired voluntary output in functional weakness.
