Definition

Oculovestibular reflex is a brainstem reflex test. It is a clue to pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma. A positive finding means expected tonic eye deviation is absent or asymmetric.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Oculovestibular reflex means expected tonic eye deviation is absent or asymmetric.

Why It Matters

A positive Oculovestibular reflex result can signal pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma. Thermal convection changes vestibular firing, which travels through brainstem vestibulo-ocular pathways to move the eyes. Oculovestibular reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma

Assessment Method

After confirming an intact tympanic membrane and safe ear canal, irrigate with protocol-directed cold or warm water while observing eye movement.

Positive Finding

Expected tonic eye deviation is absent or asymmetric.

Mechanism

Thermal convection changes vestibular firing, which travels through brainstem vestibulo-ocular pathways to move the eyes.

Evidence Limitations

Ear disease, perforation, ocular palsy, and technique affect results; this is not a casual bedside screen. Therefore, Oculovestibular reflex cannot confirm or exclude pontine and midbrain pathway integrity in coma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as vestibular loss, temporal bone injury, drug intoxication.

False Negative Situations

Oculovestibular reflex may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.
