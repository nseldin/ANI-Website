Definition

Arlt triangle is an observed corneal sign. It is a clue to granulomatous anterior uveitis. A positive finding means keratic precipitates form a base-down triangle inferiorly.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Arlt triangle means keratic precipitates form a base-down triangle inferiorly.

Why It Matters

The observed Arlt triangle finding can signal granulomatous anterior uveitis. Aqueous convection carries inflammatory cells toward the inferior corneal endothelium. Arlt triangle cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • granulomatous anterior uveitis

Assessment Method

Use slit-lamp examination of the corneal endothelium.

Positive Finding

Keratic precipitates form a base-down triangle inferiorly.

Mechanism

Aqueous convection carries inflammatory cells toward the inferior corneal endothelium.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Arlt triangle finding raises concern for granulomatous anterior uveitis because aqueous convection carries inflammatory cells toward the inferior corneal endothelium. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Arlt triangle does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Arlt triangle to focus evaluation of granulomatous anterior uveitis and the reviewed alternatives: non-granulomatous uveitis, endothelial deposits, old pigment. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Use slit-lamp examination of the corneal endothelium. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Arlt triangle as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The pattern is not etiologically specific and may be incomplete. Therefore, Arlt triangle cannot confirm or exclude granulomatous anterior uveitis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • non-granulomatous uveitis

  • endothelial deposits

  • old pigment

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as non-granulomatous uveitis, endothelial deposits, old pigment.

False Negative Situations

Arlt triangle may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of granulomatous anterior uveitis.
