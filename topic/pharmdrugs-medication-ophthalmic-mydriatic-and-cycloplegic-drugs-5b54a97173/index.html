Used To Treat

Pupil dilation for ocular examination or procedures and, with antimuscarinics, cycloplegic refraction or therapeutic ciliary rest according to duration and age.

Description

Ophthalmic mydriatic drugs increase pupil diameter; cycloplegic drugs also block ciliary-muscle accommodation so near focusing temporarily stops. Antimuscarinics such as tropicamide, cyclopentolate and atropine produce both effects by blocking the iris sphincter and ciliary muscle. Phenylephrine stimulates the radial dilator through alpha-1 receptors, so it dilates without reliably paralyzing accommodation.

Mechanism

Parasympathetic M3 signaling constricts the pupil and contracts ciliary muscle for near focus. Blocking it creates unopposed dilation and cycloplegia. Alpha-1 stimulation contracts radial dilator muscle but leaves the muscarinic ciliary pathway largely intact. Systemic absorption can therefore produce either anticholinergic CNS/cardiac toxicity or adrenergic hypertension and dysrhythmia.

Boxed Warning

No class boxed warning. These agents can precipitate acute angle closure in anatomically narrow angles. Antimuscarinics can cause delirium, agitation, fever, tachycardia and cardiorespiratory collapse, especially in infants/children; concentrated phenylephrine can cause severe hypertension, reflex bradycardia, arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia or pulmonary edema.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen for narrow-angle risk and explain blurred near vision, photophobia and impaired driving until accommodation and pupil size recover. Use the lowest effective pediatric concentration and monitor systemic behavior and vital signs when risk is high.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Key Labs

  • Pupil size/reactivity, accommodation and intraocular-pressure/angle context; heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, mental status and respiratory status if systemic toxicity is suspected.

Nclex Traps

  • Phenylephrine dilates but does not provide dependable cycloplegia.

  • A painful red eye, halos, headache and nausea after dilation can be acute angle closure, not an expected nuisance effect.

  • Punctal occlusion is especially important in infants and small children.
