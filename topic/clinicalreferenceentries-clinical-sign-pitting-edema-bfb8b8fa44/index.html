Definition

Pitting edema is swelling in which steady pressure pushes fluid aside and leaves a temporary indentation in the skin or soft tissue. It describes how the edema behaves; it does not identify the cause.

Plain Language

Pressing the swollen area leaves a dent that takes time to fill back in.

Why It Matters

Pitting helps confirm mobile fluid in tissue and its distribution can guide evaluation for heart, kidney, liver, venous, medication-related, and other causes. Sudden one-sided painful swelling can indicate a deep-vein clot, while edema with breathing difficulty or instability can signal serious systemic disease.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

Inspect dependent and symptomatic areas bilaterally, then press a consistent site over firm tissue or bone for several seconds; document location, symmetry, skin condition, indentation depth and recovery, circumference or weight trend when relevant, breathing findings, jugular venous pressure, medications, renal and hepatic clues, and pain or warmth.

Positive Finding

Pressure displaces the swollen tissue and leaves an indentation that persists briefly after the finger is removed.

Mechanism

Relatively mobile interstitial fluid is pushed aside by pressure and returns gradually when capillary filtration exceeds venous and lymphatic removal.

Evidence Limitations

Pressure duration, location, tissue thickness, chronic fibrosis, examiner technique, body position, and inconsistent grading scales change the apparent depth; absence of a pit does not exclude edema, while a pit does not identify the cause. Therefore, Pitting edema cannot confirm or exclude mobile excess interstitial fluid from increased hydrostatic pressure, reduced plasma oncotic pressure, sodium retention, capillary leakage, venous disease, medication effect, or early lymphatic dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Non-pitting edema, lymphedema, lipedema, obesity, localized soft-tissue swelling.

False Negative Situations

Pitting edema may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of mobile excess interstitial fluid from increased hydrostatic pressure, reduced plasma oncotic pressure, sodium retention, capillary leakage, venous disease, medication effect, or early lymphatic dysfunction.

Modern Status

Current descriptive edema subtype; the presence and pattern help narrow the cause but do not prove simple fluid overload, and early lymphedema may still pit.
