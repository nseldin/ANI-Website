Class

CYP3A pharmacokinetic enhancer for selected HIV protease inhibitors

Description

Cobicistat is a drug booster, not an antiretroviral that suppresses HIV by itself. It deliberately inhibits metabolism and transport so atazanavir or darunavir remains at an effective concentration longer. That makes dosing more practical, but it also turns the medication list into a major safety issue: the same CYP3A blockade can dangerously raise unrelated drugs or make essential therapy ineffective when an inducer lowers cobicistat exposure.

Mechanism

Cobicistat inhibits CYP3A enzymes and several transporters, including P-glycoprotein. It has no meaningful anti-HIV activity of its own. By slowing CYP3A-mediated clearance, it increases systemic exposure to coadministered atazanavir or once-daily darunavir. It also inhibits renal tubular creatinine secretion, so serum creatinine may rise and estimated creatinine clearance may fall without a true fall in glomerular filtration. That predictable laboratory effect must still be distinguished from real kidney injury, especially when tenofovir disoproxil fumarate is present.

Used To Treat

Used with atazanavir or once-daily darunavir and a complete antiretroviral regimen to increase protease-inhibitor exposure in adults and eligible pediatric patients. It is not interchangeable with ritonavir for every boosted regimen and is not HIV monotherapy.

Boxed Warning

Cobicistat has no boxed warning in the current TYBOST label. The dominant hazards are severe or fatal drug interactions, loss of virologic response, serum-creatinine interpretation errors, and true renal injury when used with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated combinations include drugs whose CYP3A-dependent accumulation can cause serious toxicity and strong inducers that can cause loss of antiviral effect; the exact list must be checked against the current TYBOST and companion-drug labels.

  • Not recommended during pregnancy because cobicistat and boosted protease-inhibitor exposure falls substantially; use a pregnancy-appropriate regimen rather than simply continuing the same booster.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea and jaundice or scleral icterus when paired with atazanavir.

  • Serum creatinine increase from inhibited tubular secretion; acute kidney injury and Fanconi syndrome can occur in tenofovir disoproxil fumarate-containing regimens.

  • Adverse effects from excessive exposure to interacting drugs or virologic failure when inducers reduce the boosted regimen.

Interactions

  • Cobicistat is a potent CYP3A inhibitor and affects P-gp and other transporters; sedatives, antiarrhythmics, statins, anticonvulsants, anticoagulants, transplant drugs, steroids, and many other agents require exact interaction screening.

  • Strong CYP3A inducers such as rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, and St. John's wort can reduce cobicistat and protease-inhibitor exposure and promote virologic failure or resistance.

  • It cannot be substituted automatically for ritonavir because the supported protease inhibitor, dose, age, and interaction evidence differ.

Nursing Essentials

  • Complete medication reconciliation must include prescriptions, OTC products, inhaled or injected steroids, contraceptives, and supplements before every start or change.

  • Establish creatinine and estimated creatinine clearance before therapy. With tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, monitor urine glucose and protein plus phosphorus when kidney disease is present.

  • Verify that cobicistat is paired with the intended atazanavir or darunavir regimen and taken with food; it provides no antiviral coverage when given alone.

Key Labs

  • Serum creatinine and estimated creatinine clearance

  • Urinalysis for glucose and protein and serum phosphorus when TDF-related renal risk is present

  • HIV viral load and CD4 trend

  • Liver tests according to the companion regimen

Administration

  • Take once daily with food at the same time as the selected atazanavir or darunavir dose and the rest of the antiretroviral regimen.

  • Do not start, stop, or change interacting medications without a complete regimen-level review.

Patient Education

  • Explain that a small creatinine rise can reflect blocked secretion, but reduced urine, swelling, weakness, or a continuing rise still needs prompt assessment.

  • Do not miss doses or take herbal products such as St. John's wort. Tell every clinician and pharmacist that the HIV regimen contains a strong CYP3A inhibitor.

Nclex Traps

  • Cobicistat is a booster with no independent anti-HIV activity; never count it as the active antiviral regimen.

  • A creatinine rise can reflect inhibited tubular secretion, but that expected effect does not rule out true kidney injury when symptoms, urine findings, or continued deterioration are present.

Routes

  • Oral tablet taken with food at the same time as the supported protease inhibitor.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Severe or fatal toxicity from contraindicated CYP3A interactions or virologic failure from strong induction.

  • True renal injury, including Fanconi syndrome, when a tenofovir disoproxil fumarate regimen is used in a susceptible patient.

Toxicity Management

  • No specific antidote exists. Stop the suspected interacting or toxic exposure under HIV-specialist guidance, monitor organ function and rhythm as indicated, and provide supportive care; do not interrupt the remaining antiretroviral regimen casually because incomplete therapy can promote resistance.

Special Populations

  • Cobicistat-boosted regimens are not recommended during pregnancy because booster and protease-inhibitor exposure falls.

  • Pediatric use is limited to the age, weight, protease inhibitor, and regimen supported by current labeling; it is not a universal booster.

  • Renal restrictions depend on companion drugs, especially tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, and severe hepatic impairment is not recommended. Older adults require close interaction and renal review because polypharmacy magnifies CYP3A risk.
