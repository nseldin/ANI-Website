Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric patients age six years and older. The standalone label does not establish a universal heart-failure or diabetic-nephropathy outcome indication.

Mechanism

Esterases in the gut wall convert olmesartan medoxomil to active olmesartan, which selectively blocks AT1 receptors. This prevents angiotensin-II-mediated Gq vascular contraction, aldosterone release, sodium retention, sympathetic facilitation, and remodeling. Efferent-arteriole dilation lowers glomerular capillary pressure, and reduced aldosterone raises potassium. It does not inhibit ACE, so cough is less characteristic. The exact mechanism of olmesartan-associated sprue-like enteropathy is not fully established; delayed onset, villous atrophy, and improvement after withdrawal support an immune-mediated or cell-mediated intestinal injury rather than simple dose-related diarrhea. Because the latency can be months to years, the adverse effect cannot be ruled out by an uneventful first month.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Olmesartan blocks fetal renal AT1 signaling. Stop when pregnancy is detected and do not confuse pediatric hypertension approval with fetal safety.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy, serious hypersensitivity, and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes.

  • Use below age one is unsafe for developmental RAAS reasons; current hypertension labeling begins at age six.

  • Pregnancy and aliskiren coadministration in diabetes. Avoid routine duplicate ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI therapy.

  • Clarify severe hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, evolving AKI, marked volume depletion, or suspected bilateral renal-artery stenosis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask specifically about chronic diarrhea, weight loss, weakness, dehydration, and duration of olmesartan exposure. Long latency strengthens rather than excludes the medication clue.

  • If enteropathy is suspected, assess volume, electrolytes, kidney function, nutrition, stool or celiac differential, and prescriber-directed substitution.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, albuminuria or heart-failure goal, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that less cough than an ACE inhibitor does not remove fetal, potassium, pressure, or kidney risks; report facial or tongue swelling urgently.

Interactions

  • Potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow the class; diarrhea can magnify diuretic and renal-perfusion risk.

  • Colesevelam can reduce olmesartan exposure; separate administration according to current labeling.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and other RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • Sacubitril/valsartan already contains valsartan. A separate ARB duplicates AT1 blockade.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, and during chronic diarrhea: sodium, bicarbonate, magnesium, albumin, weight, hydration, and nutritional markers.

  • Celiac serology, endoscopy, biopsy, and infectious testing belong to the diagnostic plan, but medication withdrawal response can be a critical causal clue.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Olmesartan's signature adverse effect is sprue-like enteropathy with severe chronic diarrhea, weight loss, and villous atrophy after months to years.

  • An ARB can be the cause even when the patient tolerated it for years; delayed immune injury differs from immediate dose intolerance.

  • ARBs block AT1 receptors; they do not lower angiotensin II production and do not directly inhibit bradykinin breakdown.

  • A -sartan ending identifies the class, not an identical indication. Match the agent to its labeled evidence.

Description

Olmesartan medoxomil is an oral prodrug rapidly converted during absorption to the active AT1 blocker olmesartan. It lowers vascular resistance and aldosterone signaling without directly inhibiting bradykinin breakdown. Its card must immediately flag a unique delayed danger: severe chronic diarrhea with substantial weight loss and intestinal villous atrophy can appear months to years after treatment begins. This sprue-like enteropathy can resemble celiac disease or chronic infection, so medication history is essential even after long tolerance. The label advises excluding other causes and using alternative antihypertensive therapy when no other etiology is found. Olmesartan's primary standalone indication is hypertension, including selected pediatric patients age six and older. Like every ARB it can also cause hypotension, hyperkalemia, creatinine change, and fetal renal injury.

Adverse Effects

  • Dizziness and ordinary diarrhea can occur, but persistent high-volume diarrhea with weight loss is a separate red-flag syndrome.

  • Sprue-like enteropathy can cause villous atrophy, malabsorption, dehydration, electrolyte loss, AKI, and hospitalization months to years after initiation.

  • Class effects include dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, and a hemodynamic creatinine increase; cough is much less characteristic than with ACE inhibitors.

  • Severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, rare angioedema, shock in volume depletion, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
