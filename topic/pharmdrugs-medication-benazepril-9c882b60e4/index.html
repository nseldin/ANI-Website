Class

Ester-prodrug ACE inhibitor for hypertension

Used To Treat

Hypertension in adults and labeled pediatric ages. Benazepril is also an ingredient in combinations such as amlodipine/benazepril, but the standalone U.S. label should not be inflated into universal heart-failure or post-MI indications.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Benazepril's hypertension indication does not soften its fetal danger. Stop as soon as pregnancy is detected.

Mechanism

Benazepril is absorbed and hydrolyzed, primarily in the liver, to benazeprilat. Benazeprilat binds ACE and reduces angiotensin-II formation while preserving bradykinin. Lower AT1 signaling reduces vasoconstriction, aldosterone, sodium retention, and remodeling; bradykinin increases nitric-oxide and prostacyclin signaling while creating cough and angioedema risk. Efferent dilation can lower glomerular pressure, so the usual creatinine and potassium logic applies. Benazeprilat reaches steady state after repeated once-daily dosing and is cleared predominantly through the kidney. Mild-to-moderate renal dysfunction may have less effect than severe impairment, but active-metabolite accumulation and hemodynamic AKI still require monitoring.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm whether the product is benazepril alone or amlodipine/benazepril because the second ingredient changes edema, pressure, and interaction assessment.

  • Do not teach unlabeled class uses as if they were the standalone product's approved indications; connect each use to the prescriber's clinical rationale.

  • Check pressure and orthostasis, volume, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, pregnancy status, cough, facial or tongue swelling, and every NSAID, diuretic, potassium product, lithium drug, or duplicate RAAS blocker.

  • Teach that persistent dry cough should be evaluated, while lip, tongue, throat, voice, or swallowing change is an emergency because bradykinin swelling can obstruct the airway.

Contraindications

  • Previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, hereditary or idiopathic angioedema, pregnancy, and hypersensitivity.

  • Do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or within 36 hours, or with aliskiren in diabetes.

  • Pregnancy, previous ACE-inhibitor angioedema, and hereditary or idiopathic angioedema where specified by labeling.

  • Do not combine with sacubitril/valsartan or administer within 36 hours; do not combine aliskiren with an ACE inhibitor in diabetes.

Interactions

  • Amlodipine combination products add vasodilator edema and hypotension, while potassium, NSAID, diuretic, lithium, and duplicate RAAS interactions follow benazepril.

  • mTOR inhibitors and neprilysin inhibition can add angioedema concern through peptide pathways.

  • Potassium supplements or salt substitutes, MRAs, potassium-sparing diuretics, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, diuretics, lithium, and duplicate RAAS blockers can produce hyperkalemia, AKI, hypotension, or lithium toxicity.

  • ACE inhibitor plus sacubitril/valsartan is contraindicated because overlapping peptide-degradation blockade increases angioedema risk.

Key Labs

  • Pressure, orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume, and CBC when collagen-vascular or neutropenia risk is present.

  • When combination therapy is used, assess edema and pulse in addition to ACE-inhibitor safety markers.

  • Baseline and follow-up potassium and creatinine/eGFR, blood pressure/orthostasis, volume, and urine albumin when kidney protection is the goal.

  • Investigate a major or progressive renal change for dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics, sepsis, obstruction, hypotension, or renal-artery disease.

Nclex Traps

  • Lotrel is amlodipine plus benazepril. Ankle edema may come from amlodipine's precapillary dilation rather than total-body fluid overload.

  • Benazepril is a prodrug; benazeprilat is the active ACE inhibitor.

  • ACE inhibition lowers angiotensin II and raises bradykinin; those are separate consequences of inhibiting the same enzyme.

  • A modest early creatinine rise can reflect lower intraglomerular pressure, but oliguria, severe hyperkalemia, symptoms, or a large progressive decline is not routine adaptation.

Description

Benazepril is an oral ACE-inhibitor prodrug used primarily for hypertension. Hepatic ester cleavage converts it to benazeprilat, the much more active ACE inhibitor. The active metabolite lowers angiotensin II and aldosterone, raises bradykinin, relaxes arteries, and lowers intraglomerular pressure. Benazepril's most important educational distinction is scope: its current standalone label is hypertension, so a generic database sentence claiming every ACE inhibitor treats heart failure, post-MI remodeling, diabetic nephropathy, and CKD overstates agent-specific approval. It remains a full RAAS drug with fetal toxicity, cough, angioedema, hyperkalemia, and kidney-perfusion concerns. The fixed combination Lotrel adds amlodipine; a learner must recognize both ingredients because edema and hypotension can come from different mechanisms.

Adverse Effects

  • Cough, dizziness, headache, hypotension, potassium or creatinine increase, and combination-specific edema when paired with amlodipine.

  • Rare neutropenia is a concern in collagen-vascular disease with renal impairment because the class history does not guarantee benazepril is exempt.

  • Class effects include dry cough, dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalemia, creatinine increase, and less commonly rash or taste change.

  • Airway angioedema, severe AKI, dangerous potassium elevation, neutropenia in susceptible patients, hepatic injury, and fetal toxicity are high-consequence events.
