Definition

Clonus is a rhythmic series of involuntary muscle contractions and relaxations triggered by a sudden maintained stretch. It is usually assessed at the ankle or kneecap as part of the neurologic reflex examination.

Plain Language

After a muscle is stretched, the joint repeatedly and rhythmically beats instead of settling.

Why It Matters

In an adult, sustained clonus can signal an upper-motor-neuron problem in the brain or spinal cord. Widespread clonus with fever, agitation, sweating, diarrhea, or unstable vital signs can indicate serotonin syndrome and requires emergency treatment.

Associated Conditions

  • hyperexcitable stretch-reflex circuits from reduced descending upper-motor-neuron inhibition, spinal or cerebral injury, or toxicologic states such as serotonin syndrome

Assessment Method

Ensure the joint is safe and the limb relaxed, apply a brisk muscle stretch and maintain gentle pressure using the site-specific ankle, patellar, or other trained technique, then document site, side, rhythm, number of beats, sustained or unsustained response, tone, reflexes, weakness, sensation, medications, and toxic or autonomic findings.

Positive Finding

A sudden maintained stretch produces rhythmic alternating muscle contractions and relaxations that continue beyond the initial stimulus.

Mechanism

Reduced descending inhibition makes the spinal stretch-reflex circuit excessively excitable, so one stretch repeatedly triggers the next contraction-relaxation cycle.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Clonus response raises concern for hyperexcitable stretch-reflex circuits from reduced descending upper-motor-neuron inhibition, spinal or cerebral injury, or toxicologic states such as serotonin syndrome because reduced descending inhibition makes the spinal stretch-reflex circuit excessively excitable, so one stretch repeatedly triggers the next contraction-relaxation cycle. Evidence Limitations

Relaxation, anxiety, technique, joint disease, age, and a brief unsustained response affect interpretation; clonus alone does not localize the lesion or identify its cause. Therefore, Clonus cannot confirm or exclude hyperexcitable stretch-reflex circuits from reduced descending upper-motor-neuron inhibition, spinal or cerebral injury, or toxicologic states such as serotonin syndrome by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from tremor, myoclonus, voluntary rhythmic movement, muscle spasm, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Clonus may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current upper-motor-neuron and toxicologic sign; age, site, number of beats, symmetry, and accompanying findings determine significance, and brief clonus can occur developmentally in infants. Safety Precautions

Before assessing Clonus, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ensure the joint is safe and the limb relaxed, apply a brisk muscle stretch and maintain gentle pressure using the site-specific ankle, patellar, or other trained technique, then document site, side, rhythm, number of beats, sustained or unsustained response, tone, reflexes, weakness, sensation, medications, and toxic or autonomic findings. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not force an injured or painful joint; new focal clonus with weakness, sensory loss, severe headache, or bladder and bowel dysfunction requires urgent neurologic evaluation, while diffuse clonus with agitation, fever, diaphoresis, diarrhea, or autonomic instability can signal serotonin syndrome and requires emergency treatment.
