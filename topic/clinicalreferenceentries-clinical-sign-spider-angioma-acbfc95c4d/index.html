Definition

Spider angioma is an observed vascular skin sign. It is a clue to hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease. A positive finding means a central arteriole with radiating vessels blanches from the center outward.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Spider angioma means a central arteriole with radiating vessels blanches from the center outward.

Why It Matters

The observed Spider angioma finding can signal hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease. A dilated superficial arteriole feeds radial capillaries under hormonal and angiogenic influence. Spider angioma cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease

Assessment Method

Inspect the face, upper chest, and arms and apply central pressure.

Positive Finding

A central arteriole with radiating vessels blanches from the center outward.

Mechanism

A dilated superficial arteriole feeds radial capillaries under hormonal and angiogenic influence.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Spider angioma finding raises concern for hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease because a dilated superficial arteriole feeds radial capillaries under hormonal and angiogenic influence. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Spider angioma does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Spider angioma to focus evaluation of hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease and the reviewed alternatives: hereditary telangiectasia, rosacea, cherry angioma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the face, upper chest, and arms and apply central pressure. Evidence Limitations

One or two lesions can be normal; number does not precisely grade liver disease. Therefore, Spider angioma cannot confirm or exclude hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as hereditary telangiectasia, rosacea, cherry angioma.

False Negative Situations

Spider angioma may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of hyperestrogenic or vasodilated states including chronic liver disease.
