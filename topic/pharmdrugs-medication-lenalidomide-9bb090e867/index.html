Class

Cereblon-binding immunomodulatory imide drug (IMiD)

Used To Treat

Multiple myeloma, transfusion-dependent del(5q) myelodysplastic syndrome, and selected mantle-cell, follicular, or marginal-zone lymphomas under regimen-specific criteria.

Boxed Warning

Embryo-fetal toxicity, hematologic toxicity, and venous/arterial thromboembolism: pregnancy is contraindicated and Lenalidomide REMS requirements are mandatory. Monitor neutrophils and platelets closely; DVT, PE, MI, and stroke risk rises especially with dexamethasone, so use risk-based antithrombotic prophylaxis.

Mechanism

Lenalidomide-bound cereblon changes CRL4-CRBN substrate selection, causing ubiquitination and proteasomal destruction of Ikaros/Aiolos and CK1-alpha in susceptible cells. Downstream effects include myeloma-cell apoptosis, altered transcription, increased IL-2 and T/NK activation, enhanced antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, and antiangiogenic signaling.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify REMS authorization, pregnancy testing/contraception, male condom and donation rules, and renal dose before dispensing.

  • Trend CBC, infection/bleeding, renal function, DVT/PE/MI/stroke symptoms, rash/DRESS/SJS-TEN, liver injury, thyroid function when ordered, and tumor-flare risk in lymphoma.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing per REMS, CBC with differential/platelets, creatinine clearance/eGFR, liver tests, thyroid tests when indicated, and thrombosis assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Lenalidomide is primarily renally eliminated, so kidney function directly changes dose.

  • Thromboprophylaxis does not cancel the need to evaluate unilateral swelling, chest pain, dyspnea, or focal neurologic deficit urgently.

Description

Lenalidomide is a thalidomide-derived immunomodulatory imide drug that binds cereblon and retargets an E3 ubiquitin ligase to degrade Ikaros, Aiolos, and context-specific proteins such as CK1-alpha. This targeted protein degradation suppresses malignant hematopoietic cells and strengthens T/NK activity, but profound teratogenicity, neutropenia/thrombocytopenia, venous and arterial thrombosis, renal accumulation, serious rash, and secondary malignancy require structured controls.
