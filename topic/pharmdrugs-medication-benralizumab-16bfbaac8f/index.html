Class

Afucosylated anti-IL-5 receptor alpha monoclonal antibody that recruits NK-cell ADCC to deplete eosinophils

Used To Treat

Add-on maintenance treatment of severe eosinophilic asthma in label-specified patients from age six and treatment of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis in adults. It prevents future eosinophil-driven disease and is not a rescue treatment for acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, may occur within hours or have delayed onset. Benralizumab is not for acute symptoms. Do not abruptly reduce systemic or inhaled corticosteroids. Treat pre-existing helminth infections; if infection develops and does not respond to anti-helminth treatment, interrupt benralizumab until it resolves.

Mechanism

Benralizumab is a humanized afucosylated IgG1 kappa antibody with high-affinity binding to IL-5 receptor alpha on eosinophils and basophils. Its Fc region binds Fc-gamma-RIIIa on natural killer cells more strongly because core fucose is absent. The NK cell releases perforin and granzymes, producing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and apoptosis of the receptor-bearing cell. Blood eosinophils can fall to a median near zero after treatment. Blocking receptor signaling also removes IL-5 survival input. This direct cellular depletion explains the rapid, profound biomarker effect and the extended every-eight-week maintenance interval after loading in asthma; it does not prove that every symptom is eosinophil-mediated.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify loading versus maintenance schedule and pediatric weight-based product strength; do not confuse every-four-week loading with long-term interval.

  • Monitor injection reactions, delayed hypersensitivity, eosinophils, exacerbations, steroid burden, and disease-specific EGPA organ findings.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to benralizumab or an excipient

  • Not for acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus; unresolved significant helminth infection requires management

Interactions

  • Corticosteroid taper must be supervised; abrupt withdrawal can cause adrenal crisis or unmask vasculitic/eosinophilic disease.

  • No classic CYP interactions are expected, but combining biologics without specialist evidence is not routine.

Key Labs

  • Absolute eosinophil count, exacerbation history, spirometry, corticosteroid exposure, and EGPA organ assessment

  • Helminth history, injection reaction, and hypersensitivity surveillance

Nclex Traps

  • Benralizumab binds IL-5 receptor alpha and depletes eosinophils through NK-cell ADCC.

  • Near-zero eosinophils do not mean an acute asthma attack has been treated or that every controller can be stopped.

Description

Benralizumab is a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody that binds IL-5 receptor alpha on eosinophils and basophils and then uses its engineered Fc region to recruit natural killer cells. The antibody is afucosylated, which strengthens Fc-gamma-RIII binding and drives antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, rapidly reducing eosinophils toward zero. This is mechanistically different from mepolizumab, which neutralizes the soluble IL-5 ligand but does not directly mark eosinophils for killing. The depletion can reduce severe eosinophilic asthma exacerbations and EGPA activity, but it does not immediately relax airway muscle. Corticosteroids must be tapered gradually because symptom improvement can otherwise expose adrenal insufficiency or previously suppressed disease.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, pharyngitis, fever, injection-site reaction, or hypersensitivity

  • Anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria, helminth-related concern, or immunogenic loss of response
