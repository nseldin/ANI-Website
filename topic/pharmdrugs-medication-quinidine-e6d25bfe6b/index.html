Boxed Warning

Drug-specific/class antiarrhythmic warning: increased mortality has been reported with antiarrhythmic therapy for non-life-threatening dysrhythmias. Quinidine can cause torsades de pointes, syncope, severe hypotension, thrombocytopenia, and cinchonism.

Contraindications

  • History of quinidine-associated thrombocytopenia, thrombocytopenic purpura, or hypersensitivity.

  • Myasthenia gravis.

  • Complete AV block without a pacemaker, prolonged QT/torsades history, or severe digoxin toxicity requires clarification before giving.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor ECG, QTc, QRS, heart rate, blood pressure, syncope, palpitations, tinnitus, hearing changes, visual changes, nausea/diarrhea, and bleeding/bruising.

  • Keep potassium and magnesium normal; low electrolytes raise torsades risk.

  • Check platelet count if bruising, petechiae, or bleeding occurs.

Nclex Traps

  • Tinnitus plus GI upset can be cinchonism, not anxiety.

  • QT prolongation with syncope or palpitations points to torsades risk.

  • New bruising or petechiae can mean immune thrombocytopenia and needs urgent follow-up.

Generic

Class

Class IA antiarrhythmic; sodium-channel blocker with potassium-channel effects

Used To Treat

Selected atrial or ventricular dysrhythmias when ordered by a specialist; use is less common because safer or better-tolerated options often exist.

Mechanism

Blocks fast sodium channels and also prolongs repolarization through potassium-channel effects. This slows conduction, prolongs the action potential, and can widen QRS and prolong QT.

Interactions

  • Digoxin levels can rise substantially; monitor for nausea, visual changes, dysrhythmias, and ordered digoxin levels.

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs, diuretics causing hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia, CYP inhibitors, and warfarin can increase risk or require close monitoring.

Key Labs

  • ECG/QTc and QRS.

  • Potassium and magnesium.

  • CBC/platelets when toxicity or bleeding cues occur.

  • Digoxin level if combined.

  • Liver and renal function when clinically indicated.

Description

Quinidine is a class IA antiarrhythmic and sodium-channel blocker with potassium-channel effects: it blocks fast sodium channels and also prolongs repolarization through potassium-channel effects. It is used for selected atrial or ventricular dysrhythmias when ordered by a specialist. Use is less common because safer or better-tolerated options often exist.
