Definition

Asboe-Hansen sign is a bedside blister-pressure maneuver. It is a clue to intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension. A positive finding means the blister spreads laterally into surrounding skin.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Asboe-Hansen sign means the blister spreads laterally into surrounding skin.

Why It Matters

A positive Asboe-Hansen sign response can signal intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension. Pressure displaces blister fluid along the weakest tissue cleavage plane. Asboe-Hansen sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension

Assessment Method

Apply very gentle vertical pressure to the top of an intact blister only if clinically justified.

Positive Finding

The blister spreads laterally into surrounding skin.

Mechanism

Pressure displaces blister fluid along the weakest tissue cleavage plane.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Asboe-Hansen sign response raises concern for intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension because pressure displaces blister fluid along the weakest tissue cleavage plane. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Asboe-Hansen sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Asboe-Hansen sign to focus evaluation of intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension and the reviewed alternatives: friction blister, burn blister, epidermolysis bullosa. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply very gentle vertical pressure to the top of an intact blister only if clinically justified. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Asboe-Hansen sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Several blistering disorders can be positive and technique is not standardized. Therefore, Asboe-Hansen sign cannot confirm or exclude intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • friction blister

  • burn blister

  • epidermolysis bullosa

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from friction blister, burn blister, epidermolysis bullosa, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Asboe-Hansen sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Limited adjunct; can enlarge injury. When performed as described, Asboe-Hansen sign can adjust concern for intraepidermal or subepidermal blister extension, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Asboe-Hansen sign: Several blistering disorders can be positive and technique is not standardized.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Asboe-Hansen sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply very gentle vertical pressure to the top of an intact blister only if clinically justified. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid testing tense, infected, painful, or diagnostically obvious blisters.
