Used To Treat

Blastomycosis, histoplasmosis, selected aspergillosis, sporotrichosis, and onychomycosis or other product-specific indications. Capsules, solution, and SUBA-itraconazole products differ in absorption and approved uses.

Mechanism

CYP51 blockade disrupts fungal membrane sterols. Itraconazole is highly lipophilic, extensively protein bound, and metabolized by CYP3A4 to active hydroxy-itraconazole. Gastric pH, formulation, food, GI disease, and CYP induction therefore create large exposure differences. Negative inotropy has been demonstrated in animals and healthy volunteers, providing a mechanism for edema and heart-failure exacerbation. Long tissue persistence supports treatment of some deep mycoses but also means interactions and toxicity do not disappear immediately after the last dose. Trough monitoring including active metabolite can verify exposure in invasive or prolonged therapy.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - CONGESTIVE HEART FAILURE, CARDIAC EFFECTS, AND DRUG INTERACTIONS: Do not use itraconazole capsules for onychomycosis in patients with ventricular dysfunction or a history of CHF; discontinue if heart-failure symptoms occur. Numerous CYP3A substrates are contraindicated because accumulation can cause life-threatening arrhythmia, vascular, sedative, renal, or other toxicity.

Contraindications

  • Ventricular dysfunction for onychomycosis treatment, serious hypersensitivity, pregnancy for onychomycosis, and numerous CYP3A-dependent co-medications under boxed labeling.

  • Avoid or closely manage acid suppression with conventional capsules; do not substitute capsule, solution, or Tolsura formulations without product-specific conversion.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask about dyspnea, orthopnea, rapid weight gain, edema, cardiac history, liver symptoms, neuropathy, hearing change, blood pressure, and every medication. Stop and escalate new CHF signs.

  • Document formulation and administration: conventional capsules with a full meal and acidic environment; solution fasting. Obtain troughs for invasive/prolonged therapy or uncertain absorption.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibition raises many statins, antiarrhythmics, sedatives, calcium-channel blockers, immunosuppressants, and oncology agents; some combinations are contraindicated.

  • Rifampin, rifabutin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, and other inducers can make itraconazole ineffective. PPIs/H2 blockers reduce conventional capsule absorption.

Key Labs

  • Itraconazole plus hydroxy-itraconazole trough when indicated, AST/ALT/bilirubin, blood pressure, potassium, weight/edema, cardiac assessment, and organism/site response.

  • A low trough should trigger formulation, meal, acid, adherence, GI, and inducer review before reflex dose escalation.

Nclex Traps

  • Itraconazole capsules and solution are not interchangeable and have opposite food instructions.

  • The heart-failure warning follows negative inotropy; edema is not merely a cosmetic adverse effect.

  • Fungal CYP51 inhibition

  • Ergosterol depletion

  • CYP3A-dependent exposure with variable oral absorption

Description

Itraconazole is a broad systemic triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51, depletes ergosterol, and impairs membrane function; it is used especially for endemic dimorphic fungi, selected Aspergillus disease, sporotrichosis, and onychomycosis. Its practical pharmacology is dominated by variable absorption and CYP3A interactions. Conventional capsules need gastric acidity and food; oral solution is absorbed better fasting and is not interchangeable milligram for milligram with capsules; newer formulations have their own rules. Itraconazole can reduce cardiac contractility and carries a boxed warning against treating onychomycosis in patients with ventricular dysfunction or heart failure history. It also strongly inhibits CYP3A4, so dangerous partner accumulation is a core property, not a footnote.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea/constipation, headache, rash, edema, hypertension, hypokalemia, and elevated liver tests.

  • CHF/pulmonary edema, severe hepatotoxicity, neuropathy, hearing loss, severe skin reaction, QT-mediated interaction toxicity, and pseudoaldosteronism with hypertension/hypokalemia.
