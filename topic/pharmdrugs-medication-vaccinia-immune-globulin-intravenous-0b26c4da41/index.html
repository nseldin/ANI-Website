Class

Human vaccinia-neutralizing immune globulin

Used To Treat

Specified complications of vaccinia vaccination: eczema vaccinatum, progressive vaccinia, severe generalized vaccinia, vaccinia infection in patients with certain skin conditions, and aberrant vaccinia infections other than isolated keratitis. It has no established benefit for postvaccinial encephalitis and is not routine prophylaxis after smallpox vaccination.

Boxed Warning

BOXED-WARNING-LEVEL PRODUCT HAZARD: maltose in VIGIV can cause falsely high readings on incompatible glucose systems, leading to inappropriate insulin, profound hypoglycemia, coma, or death. Use only a glucose-specific method and verify the device before infusion.

Mechanism

Donor IgG binds vaccinia surface antigens, neutralizes extracellular virions, limits attachment and entry into new cells, promotes opsonization, and supports immune clearance. Passive antibody acts immediately but decays over time and does not create durable active immunity. Efficacy depends on reaching virus that has not already produced irreversible tissue injury, explaining why early CDC consultation and repeated clinical assessment matter. Maltose in the formulation is not metabolized to glucose in the sample but can react with certain glucose-dehydrogenase test systems, creating a dangerous analytic—not physiologic—interaction.

Nursing Essentials

  • Quarantine every point-of-care glucose meter and strip system that cannot be proven glucose specific because maltose interference can produce a false high and lethal insulin administration.

  • Monitor blood pressure, pulse, oxygenation, airway, temperature, urine output, infusion site, and symptoms throughout infusion because anaphylaxis, hemolysis, thrombosis, renal injury, or aseptic meningitis can emerge.

  • Use contact precautions and lesion containment directed by infection prevention because VIGIV treats the patient but does not remove infectious vaccinia from dressings, hands, or close contacts.

  • Map, photograph, and measure lesions and assess eye involvement urgently because isolated keratitis has a different risk-benefit profile and topical or systemic decisions require ophthalmology and CDC input.

Contraindications

  • Patients with IgA deficiency and antibodies to IgA or a history of severe immune-globulin reaction are at increased anaphylaxis risk; urgent use requires CDC and allergy-risk planning rather than casual infusion.

  • VIGIV is not indicated for isolated vaccinia keratitis because immune globulin can increase corneal-scarring concern, or for postvaccinial encephalitis because benefit against that syndrome is unestablished; do not use it as routine prophylaxis in an asymptomatic vaccine recipient because no treatment benefit has been demonstrated without a qualifying complication.

Interactions

  • Maltose interferes with GDH-PQQ and certain other non-glucose-specific testing systems; use a glucose-specific method and never administer insulin from an unverified meter result.

  • Immune globulin can reduce the response to live-virus vaccines for a product-specific interval, while other nephrotoxic or thrombogenic medicines can add to renal or vascular risk; reconcile vaccination and medication plans with CDC and pharmacy.

Key Labs

  • Use a laboratory or point-of-care glucose method known to be glucose specific; trend CBC, creatinine/eGFR, urinalysis, electrolytes, liver tests, LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, and direct antiglobulin testing when hemolysis is suspected.

  • Monitor viscosity or thrombosis-related studies, urine output, weight, and fluid balance when indicated because immune globulin can increase viscosity and renal or volume stress; monitor lesion PCR or culture through public health and serial clinical progression because an antibody titer alone does not show whether vaccinia replication and tissue injury are controlled.

Nclex Traps

  • VIGIV is vaccinia immune globulin, not routine IVIG and not VariZIG for varicella.

  • Maltose can create a false high glucose; confirm the meter method before giving insulin.

  • Isolated vaccinia keratitis and postvaccinial encephalitis are not labeled indications.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Patients with diabetes

    Note

    Insulin treatment magnifies the danger of a maltose-falsely-elevated glucose result; verify the exact meter chemistry before VIGIV reaches the unit.

  • Population

    Patients with renal disease, hyperviscosity, or thrombosis risk

    Note

    Immune globulin can increase viscosity and renal stress, so use the minimum practical rate, adequate hydration when safe, and close kidney and vascular monitoring.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, children, and immunocompromised patients

    Note

    These groups may have severe vaccinia disease and limited trial data; public-health and specialty guidance must balance immediate viral risk against immune-globulin uncertainty.

Description

Vaccinia immune globulin intravenous is pooled human IgG with high vaccinia-neutralizing antibody titers. It supplies immediate passive immunity when uncontrolled vaccine-virus replication exceeds the patient's ability to contain it. Because infused antibody is distributed systemically, it can limit new viral spread but cannot instantly repair necrotic skin, corneal injury, or organ damage. The product contains maltose, which some glucose meters misread as glucose; an erroneous high value can trigger insulin and fatal hypoglycemia, making laboratory-method verification a core safety task.

Administration Timing

  • Contact the CDC and relevant public-health emergency pathway immediately when a qualifying vaccinia complication is suspected; obtain access and dosing instructions without waiting for viral confirmation if progressive disease is clinically credible.

  • Verify weight, indication, product, infusion rate, compatible line, renal and thrombosis risks, IgA history, and the glucose-testing method before infusion; begin slowly and titrate only under the current label.

  • Reassess lesions, systemic disease, and laboratory toxicity after the initial course; use repeat dosing only through CDC and label guidance because antibody exposure and product risks accumulate.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, nausea, fever, chills, dizziness, back pain, infusion reactions, aseptic meningitis, hemolysis, and transfusion-related lung injury can occur.

  • Anaphylaxis, acute kidney injury, thrombosis, and maltose-related glucose-monitoring error with inappropriate insulin and severe hypoglycemia are the highest-consequence risks.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Airway swelling, hypotension, hypoxemia, chest pain, focal neurologic deficit, oliguria, hemoglobinuria, or a suspected glucose-meter discrepancy requires immediate infusion cessation and emergency evaluation.

  • Expanding lesions, new organ involvement, continued viral replication, or absent response after initial treatment requires renewed CDC consultation, repeat-dose assessment, and consideration of additional investigational antiviral support.

Evidence Limitations

  • Qualifying vaccinia complications are rare, so efficacy evidence comes from historical experience, pharmacology, and nonrandomized data rather than modern randomized trials.

  • Diagnosis, release, redosing, and adjunct antiviral decisions require CDC coordination; this entry cannot substitute for the emergency public-health pathway.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled for specified complications due to vaccinia vaccination. Distribution and case management occur through the applicable CDC/public-health emergency pathway.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
