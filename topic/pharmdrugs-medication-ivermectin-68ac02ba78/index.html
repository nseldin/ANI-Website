Used To Treat

Oral strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis; topical 0.5% lotion treats head lice and topical 1% cream treats inflammatory rosacea lesions. Oral scabies treatment is common evidence-based off-label use, not a U.S. tablet-label indication.

Mechanism

High-affinity binding opens invertebrate glutamate-gated chloride channels and can enhance other ligand-gated chloride currents at higher exposure, producing flaccid paralysis and death. Mammalian selectivity depends on absent glutamate channels, low affinity at mammalian GABA receptors and P-glycoprotein-supported CNS exclusion. It kills microfilariae rather than adult Onchocerca worms, so retreatment is needed. In rosacea, the approved clinical effect should not be reduced to an unproven single target.

Contraindications

  • Known ivermectin or formulation hypersensitivity

  • Topical lice products are not used under age 6 months; formulations have separate restrictions

  • Clarify Loa loa exposure, blood-brain-barrier/P-gp vulnerability, pregnancy/lactation, severe liver disease and interacting CNS drugs

Nursing Essentials

  • Name the formulation and target before teaching: tablet, rosacea cream and lice lotion are different medications in practice.

  • For oral parasitic disease, document weight-based dose, epidemiology, follow-up stool/skin/eye testing and inflammatory reactions.

Interactions

  • CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibitors can increase exposure; CNS depressants may compound neurologic toxicity.

  • Warfarin INR elevation has been reported; monitor when used together.

  • Do not layer multiple lice/scabies drugs without a protocol because irritation can mimic failure.

Key Labs

  • Stool examinations for strongyloidiasis and skin/eye follow-up for onchocerciasis

  • Loa loa blood testing when epidemiologic risk exists

  • Liver tests, neurologic assessment, blood pressure and INR when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Ivermectin opens parasite glutamate-gated chloride channels; humans do not have that target.

  • Oral ivermectin is not labeled in the U.S. for scabies even though it is widely used off-label.

  • Killing microfilariae can make the patient temporarily more inflamed because parasite antigens trigger a Mazzotti reaction.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Before oral ivermectin in people with Central/West African exposure, assess possible Loa loa coinfection: heavy microfilaremia has caused serious or fatal encephalopathy. Onchocerciasis treatment can trigger Mazzotti inflammation and ophthalmic reactions. Stop and assess severe neurologic change, hypotension, extensive rash or systemic hypersensitivity.

Description

Ivermectin is an avermectin antiparasitic and invertebrate glutamate-gated chloride-channel activator that paralyzes susceptible nematodes and ectoparasites; oral tablets treat strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis, 0.5% lotion kills head lice, 1% cream treats inflammatory rosacea lesions, and oral dosing is commonly used off label for scabies. Channel opening increases chloride influx, hyperpolarizing parasite nerve and muscle until movement, feeding and reproduction fail. Humans lack these channels and therapeutic drug crosses the intact blood-brain barrier poorly. The cream's rosacea benefit is not fully explained and may combine Demodex reduction with anti-inflammatory effects.

Adverse Effects

  • Oral: dizziness, nausea, diarrhea, rash, pruritus and hypotension

  • Mazzotti reaction with fever, lymph-node tenderness, edema, arthralgia and tachycardia

  • Rare encephalopathy, seizures, severe skin reaction or hepatotoxicity

  • Topical: burning, irritation, conjunctivitis or scalp/skin symptoms
