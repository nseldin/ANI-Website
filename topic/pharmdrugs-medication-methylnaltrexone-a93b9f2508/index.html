Class

Peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA)

Used To Treat

Opioid-induced constipation in adults with chronic non-cancer pain and in advanced illness/palliative-care clients when laxative response is inadequate, according to formulation and labeling.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for methylnaltrexone in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Methylnaltrexone competitively blocks peripheral mu-opioid receptors in the enteric nervous system, restoring acetylcholine release, peristalsis, secretion, and intestinal transit while largely preserving central opioid analgesia.

Nursing Essentials

  • Exclude known or suspected mechanical obstruction and assess conditions that weaken the bowel wall before dosing.

  • Monitor bowel movement timing, severe/persistent abdominal pain, diarrhea, dehydration, withdrawal symptoms, analgesia change, and renal/hepatic dose context.

Key Labs

  • Renal and hepatic function for dosing, hydration/electrolytes if diarrhea is significant, and bowel/abdominal assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Methylnaltrexone is not naltrexone for addiction treatment. It is designed to stay peripheral and treat opioid-induced constipation.

Description

Methylnaltrexone is a peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA) that blocks enteric mu receptors, restoring secretion and motility without normally crossing the blood-brain barrier enough to reverse analgesia. Quaternary methylation keeps it peripheral, so bowel motility can return rapidly; obstruction, GI perforation risk, severe diarrhea, and opioid-withdrawal symptoms still require screening.
