Used To Treat

Learning the practical identities of common cephalosporins without assuming that a higher generation is universally stronger, safer, or broader for every organism and site.

Description

Cephalosporin generations describe broad historical spectrum trends, not a ladder where each step replaces the one before it. Cefazolin and cephalexin are strong first-generation choices for susceptible streptococci and MSSA; cefazolin is also a major surgical-prophylaxis drug. Cefuroxime expands selected respiratory gram-negative coverage and its oral axetil prodrug has formulation and absorption rules. Ceftriaxone offers long exposure and useful CNS and disseminated-infection roles but has neonatal bilirubin/calcium and biliary-sludge cautions. Ceftazidime emphasizes gram-negative and Pseudomonas activity but is weaker for MSSA. Cefepime combines broad gram-negative, Pseudomonas, and better gram-positive activity yet can cause renal-accumulation neurotoxicity because falling kidney clearance raises CNS exposure. Ceftaroline binds MRSA PBP2a but is not a Pseudomonas drug.

Mechanism

Every agent acylates PBPs and blocks peptidoglycan transpeptidation, but side chains change porin entry, PBP affinity, beta-lactamase stability, protein binding, renal or biliary handling, and tissue penetration. Those structural differences create the real bedside identities. Ceftazidime and cefepime can reach susceptible Pseudomonas through gram-negative outer-membrane pathways; ceftaroline's flexible binding interaction permits high-affinity engagement of altered MRSA PBP2a. Ceftriaxone's long half-life and high protein binding support less frequent dosing but also explain bilirubin displacement and precipitation with calcium in neonates. Cefepime clearance is predominantly renal, so accumulation can antagonize CNS GABA-A signaling and produce encephalopathy, myoclonus, or nonconvulsive status epilepticus.

Boxed Warning

There is no one cephalosporin boxed warning. Shared hazards include serious beta-lactam hypersensitivity, C. difficile-associated diarrhea, cytopenias, and renal accumulation. Drug-specific stop rules matter more than the generation label: ceftriaxone plus IV calcium in neonates can form fatal precipitates; cefepime or ceftazidime accumulation can cause severe neurotoxicity; prolonged ceftaroline can cause neutropenia; and no listed agent should be assumed to cover Enterococcus, Listeria, atypicals, or every anaerobe.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, rash, candidiasis, infusion or injection reactions, positive direct Coombs testing, and transient liver-test changes.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reactions, C. difficile colitis, hemolytic anemia or neutropenia, seizures or encephalopathy, biliary or urinary precipitation, and treatment failure from a spectrum mismatch.

Contraindications

  • A prior immediate anaphylactic or severe delayed beta-lactam reaction requires precise phenotype review and agent-specific risk assessment rather than a vague allergy checkbox.

  • Do not choose by generation alone. Confirm organism, site, local susceptibility, renal function, CNS penetration need, Pseudomonas or MRSA requirement, and source control.

Nursing Essentials

  • Say each drug's defining identity before giving it: cefazolin MSSA/surgery, cephalexin oral skin/strep, cefuroxime respiratory oral prodrug, ceftriaxone long-acting with neonatal cautions, ceftazidime Pseudomonas, cefepime broad Pseudomonas with neurotoxicity, ceftaroline MRSA without Pseudomonas.

  • Trend response and culture data, then de-escalate. Broad empiric coverage is a temporary hypothesis, not proof that the chosen drug matches the organism.

Interactions

  • Probenecid can raise exposure to selected agents; anticoagulation and vitamin-K status matter with some cephalosporins; nephrotoxic combinations magnify renal risk.

  • Calcium-containing IV products create a unique neonatal ceftriaxone hazard, while other interactions are drug and formulation specific rather than generation-wide.

Key Labs

  • Cultures, WBC, temperature, renal function and calculated clearance, CBC during prolonged therapy, liver tests when indicated, coagulation context, stool assessment, and neurologic status with cefepime or ceftazidime.

  • For ceftriaxone, assess neonatal age/bilirubin/calcium exposure and biliary symptoms; for ceftaroline, trend neutrophils during prolonged courses.

Nclex Traps

  • Ceftaroline covers MRSA but not Pseudomonas; ceftazidime and cefepime cover susceptible Pseudomonas but are not interchangeable MRSA drugs.

  • Ceftriaxone's convenient interval does not make it safe in hyperbilirubinemic neonates or neonates requiring IV calcium.
