Summary

PET/CT fusion imaging (positron emission tomography / computed tomography fusion imaging) is a high-yield General Imaging diagnostic reference. Hybrid metabolic/anatomic imaging combining PET tracer uptake with CT localization, often used in oncology.

Quick Answer

PET/CT fusion imaging (positron emission tomography / computed tomography fusion imaging): Hybrid metabolic/anatomic imaging combining PET tracer uptake with CT localization, often used in oncology. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Hybrid metabolic/anatomic imaging combining PET tracer uptake with CT localization, often used in oncology.

    • Before / preparation

    • Screen pregnancy/breastfeeding status, allergies, kidney concerns if contrast is paired, and ability to remain still for scan timing.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Teach that a small tracer may be used, encourage ordered hydration after some studies, and monitor for delayed or unexpected symptoms.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Unstable symptoms during testing, severe pain, allergic symptoms, or results suggesting embolus, obstruction, ischemia, or metastatic disease.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Nuclear studies often show function, not just anatomy. Match the test to the clinical question.
