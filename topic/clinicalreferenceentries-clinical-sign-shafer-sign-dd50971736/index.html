Definition

Shafer sign is a slit-lamp vitreous sign. It is a clue to retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment. A positive finding means brown pigment cells appear like tobacco dust.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Shafer sign means brown pigment cells appear like tobacco dust.

Why It Matters

The observed Shafer sign finding can signal retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment. A retinal tear releases retinal pigment epithelial cells into the vitreous. Shafer sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment

Assessment Method

Examine the anterior vitreous after dilation.

Positive Finding

Brown pigment cells appear like tobacco dust.

Mechanism

A retinal tear releases retinal pigment epithelial cells into the vitreous.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Shafer sign finding raises concern for retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment because a retinal tear releases retinal pigment epithelial cells into the vitreous. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Shafer sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Shafer sign to focus evaluation of retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment and the reviewed alternatives: vitreous hemorrhage, uveitis pigment, melanoma cells. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine the anterior vitreous after dilation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Shafer sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Absence does not exclude a tear and prior surgery or inflammation can add pigment. Therefore, Shafer sign cannot confirm or exclude retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • vitreous hemorrhage

  • uveitis pigment

  • melanoma cells

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as vitreous hemorrhage, uveitis pigment, melanoma cells.

False Negative Situations

Shafer sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of retinal break associated with acute posterior vitreous detachment.

Safety Precautions

Observe Shafer sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Examine the anterior vitreous after dilation. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: New flashes or floaters with this sign need urgent dilated retinal examination.
