Used To Treat

Resuscitation from local-anesthetic systemic toxicity, especially seizures, ventricular dysrhythmia, or cardiovascular collapse, and selected refractory lipophilic poisonings under toxicology guidance. It is not routine parenteral nutrition in this context.

Mechanism

A circulating lipid phase partitions lipophilic local anesthetic away from cardiac and neural tissue, creating a lipid shuttle toward metabolizing organs. It may also supply fatty-acid substrate and improve cardiomyocyte energetics and ion-channel function. Because hypoxia and acidemia increase local-anesthetic binding and cardiovascular toxicity, airway control and ventilation are mechanistic treatment rather than background support. The emulsion does not remove all drug instantly, so relapse can occur as tissue and lipid compartments re-equilibrate.

Contraindications

  • There is no simple nutritional contraindication that should delay rescue in cardiovascular collapse, but severe egg or soybean allergy and disorders of fat metabolism require risk-benefit judgment; use only 20% IV lipid under a resuscitation protocol.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify 20% lipid, patient weight, cumulative volume, infusion rate, last local-anesthetic dose, and time because nutrition emulsions and toxicology rescue are different workflows.

  • Use the LAST-specific resuscitation checklist: benzodiazepines for seizures, smaller epinephrine doses at or below 1 microgram/kg, and avoidance of vasopressin, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, and additional local anesthetic.

  • Alert the laboratory and extracorporeal team because lipemia can invalidate assays and impair oxygenators or dialysis circuitry.

Interactions

  • Vasopressin, beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, and large epinephrine doses can worsen LAST resuscitation and are avoided under the ASRA checklist.

  • Lipid can sequester other lipophilic medications, interfere with laboratory assays, and compromise ECMO or dialysis filters, so teams must coordinate timing and interpretation.

Key Labs

  • Use continuous ECG, hemodynamic, and oxygenation monitoring; obtain frequent or serial blood gas, lactate, glucose, electrolytes, triglycerides, and amylase or lipase, track the cumulative lipid dose, and flag visibly lipemic specimens because trends show resuscitation response and can detect lipid-related metabolic, pancreatic, dose-related, or laboratory-interference complications.

Nclex Traps

  • LAST means local-anesthetic systemic toxicity only when context supports it; the bare word last is not enough.

  • Lipid rescue changes ACLS medication choices and does not replace ventilation.

  • A parenteral-nutrition lipid infusion is not automatically a rescue bolus.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    Weight-based bolus, infusion, and 12 mL/kg cumulative ceiling require independent calculation because a small volume error is clinically important.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Regional anesthesia exposure is common, and emergency lipid rescue should not be delayed when severe LAST is suspected because restoring maternal oxygenation and circulation is the best immediate fetal support.

  • Population

    Severe hypertriglyceridemia, pancreatitis, or extracorporeal support

    Note

    Complication risk and circuit dysfunction are higher, but collapse requires an individualized rescue decision rather than applying nutrition-infusion rules.

Boxed Warning

LAST RESUSCITATION WARNING: stop the local anesthetic, call 911 or activate the operating-room emergency response, ventilate, treat seizures, and use 20% lipid only through a clinician checklist. Track cumulative dose because fat overload, assay interference, relapse, and ECMO-circuit problems can occur.

Description

Intravenous lipid emulsion is a 20% fat emulsion that shuttles lipophilic local anesthetic away from cardiac and neural tissue as a rescue component for local-anesthetic systemic toxicity. AHA describes a 1.5 mL/kg bolus, up to 100 mL, followed by 0.25 mL/kg/min for up to 30 minutes. ASRA simplifies this to about 100 mL over 2 to 3 minutes then about 250 mL over 15 to 20 minutes for patients over 70 kg, or 1.5 mL/kg then 0.25 mL/kg/min below 70 kg, with repeat bolus or doubled infusion for persistent instability, continuation at least 15 minutes after stability, and a maximum 12 mL/kg. These are clinician protocols, not instructions for unsupervised use.

Administration Timing

  • For severe LAST, stop local anesthetic injection, call for help and the LAST kit, secure oxygenation and ventilation, suppress seizures, and give 20% lipid promptly under ASRA/AHA protocol.

  • Common clinician dosing is 1.5 mL/kg bolus followed by 0.25 mL/kg/min; repeat or increase only for persistent instability and keep cumulative dose within the protocol maximum of 12 mL/kg.

  • Continue infusion for at least 15 minutes after hemodynamic stability and observe for recurrence because redistributed local anesthetic can re-enter the active compartment.

Adverse Effects

  • Fat overload, hypertriglyceridemia, pancreatitis, acute lung injury, infection risk, hypersensitivity, laboratory interference, and extracorporeal-circuit dysfunction can occur.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Persistent seizure, ventricular dysrhythmia, shock, or arrest despite protocol therapy requires immediate cardiopulmonary bypass or VA-ECMO planning and expert toxicology support.

Evidence Limitations

  • Evidence is strongest for LAST but relies heavily on animal studies, case reports, and consensus; effectiveness for non-local-anesthetic poisonings is inconsistent and toxin-specific.
