Definition

Lancisi sign is an observed jugular waveform sign. It is a clue to severe tricuspid regurgitation. A positive finding means marked systolic venous expansion produces a single prominent wave.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lancisi sign means marked systolic venous expansion produces a single prominent wave.

Why It Matters

The observed Lancisi sign finding can signal severe tricuspid regurgitation. Severe tricuspid backflow merges c and v components into a large systolic cv wave. Lancisi sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe tricuspid regurgitation

Assessment Method

Observe the jugular venous pulse and neck veins during systole.

Positive Finding

Marked systolic venous expansion produces a single prominent wave.

Mechanism

Severe tricuspid backflow merges c and v components into a large systolic cv wave.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Lancisi sign finding raises concern for severe tricuspid regurgitation because severe tricuspid backflow merges c and v components into a large systolic cv wave. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lancisi sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lancisi sign to focus evaluation of severe tricuspid regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: carotid pulse, cannon a waves, venous obstruction. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the jugular venous pulse and neck veins during systole. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lancisi sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The eponym adds little beyond precise waveform description. Therefore, Lancisi sign cannot confirm or exclude severe tricuspid regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • carotid pulse

  • cannon a waves

  • venous obstruction

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as carotid pulse, cannon a waves, venous obstruction.

False Negative Situations

Lancisi sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe tricuspid regurgitation.

Modern Status
