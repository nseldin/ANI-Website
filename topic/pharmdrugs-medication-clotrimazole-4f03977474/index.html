Class

Local imidazole antifungal; fungal CYP51 and membrane-synthesis inhibitor

Used To Treat

Topical dermatophyte and Candida skin infections, vaginal candidiasis with formulation-specific products, and treatment or prophylaxis of oropharyngeal candidiasis with slowly dissolved prescription troches.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Use the formulation only at its intended site. Persistent mouth lesions, dysphagia, fever, spreading rash, scalp/nail disease, pregnancy questions, or recurrent infection need diagnosis rather than indiscriminate over-the-counter retreatment.

Mechanism

CYP51 inhibition depletes ergosterol and accumulates abnormal sterols, disrupting membrane enzymes and permeability. At lower local concentrations clotrimazole is mainly fungistatic; higher concentrations can become fungicidal in vitro. The formulation creates the exposure: troche dissolution bathes oral mucosa, cream penetrates stratum corneum, and vaginal products concentrate locally. None reliably produces systemic blood levels for invasive disease. Symptom improvement can precede complete fungal clearance, explaining full-course adherence.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach troche dissolution over about 15-30 minutes without chewing or swallowing whole. For skin therapy, clean/dry the site, apply beyond the visible border, and continue for the labeled course.

  • Reassess if no improvement: steroid-modified tinea, bacterial disease, eczema, resistant/nonfungal rash, scalp/nail involvement, or poor adherence can explain failure.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to clotrimazole or components.

  • Troches are not suitable for patients unable to dissolve them safely or for systemic disease; topical products are not ophthalmic unless specifically labeled.

Interactions

  • Systemic interactions are uncommon with topical use. Oral troches can inhibit CYP3A enough to affect selected drugs, so transplant and high-risk medication lists still deserve review.

  • Avoid adding topical corticosteroid without a defined indication because it can suppress inflammation while fungal burden expands.

Key Labs

  • No routine labs for ordinary topical use. Liver tests can be considered for prolonged troche therapy or hepatic symptoms; KOH/culture helps with recurrent or atypical disease.

  • Clinical site and formulation are as important as organism name when judging response.

Nclex Traps

  • A troche is topical oral administration, not a swallowed systemic tablet.

  • Lotrimin brand families contain different ingredients; verify clotrimazole on the label rather than relying on the brand alone.

Description

Clotrimazole is a mainly local imidazole antifungal that inhibits fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase (CYP51), reduces ergosterol, alters membrane permeability, and suppresses growth on skin, vaginal, or oral mucosa. A 10-mg troche is designed to dissolve slowly in the mouth, creating sustained salivary exposure for thrush; swallowing or chewing it defeats the dosage form. Topical cream treats susceptible dermatophytes and Candida at the skin surface but does not treat scalp, nail, bloodstream, or deep-organ infection simply because those conditions share a fungal name. Limited systemic absorption keeps interaction burden lower than systemic azoles, although troches can still affect liver tests and drug metabolism in selected patients.

Adverse Effects

  • Topical burning, stinging, erythema, peeling, itching, or contact dermatitis; troche nausea, altered taste, mouth discomfort, and liver-test elevation.

  • Rare hypersensitivity or clinically significant hepatic abnormality.
