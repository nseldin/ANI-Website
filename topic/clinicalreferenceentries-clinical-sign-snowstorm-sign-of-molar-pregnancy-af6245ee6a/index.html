Definition

Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy is an ultrasound imaging sign. It is a clue to hydatidiform mole. A positive finding means numerous echogenic cystic spaces create a snowstorm or cluster-of-grapes appearance.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy means numerous echogenic cystic spaces create a snowstorm or cluster-of-grapes appearance.

Why It Matters

The Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy imaging pattern can signal hydatidiform mole. Hydropic chorionic villi and trophoblastic proliferation form many fluid-filled vesicles. Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hydatidiform mole

Assessment Method

Review uterine ultrasound with quantitative hCG and clinical context.

Positive Finding

Numerous echogenic cystic spaces create a snowstorm or cluster-of-grapes appearance.

Mechanism

Hydropic chorionic villi and trophoblastic proliferation form many fluid-filled vesicles.

Clinical Interpretation

The Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy imaging pattern raises concern for hydatidiform mole because hydropic chorionic villi and trophoblastic proliferation form many fluid-filled vesicles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy to focus evaluation of hydatidiform mole and the reviewed alternatives: failed pregnancy, placental mesenchymal dysplasia, fibroid. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review uterine ultrasound with quantitative hCG and clinical context. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Early disease and partial moles may lack the classic pattern. Therefore, Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy cannot confirm or exclude hydatidiform mole by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • failed pregnancy

  • placental mesenchymal dysplasia

  • fibroid

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with failed pregnancy, placental mesenchymal dysplasia, fibroid.

False Negative Situations

Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy may be missed when the associated process is early or subtle, the relevant region is not included, or acquisition timing and resolution are inadequate.

Modern Status

Current characteristic imaging sign; early moles may look different. Interpret Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Early disease and partial moles may lack the classic pattern.

Safety Precautions

For Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy, verify patient identity, the indication, acquisition quality, timing, and the relevant imaging plane or electrodiagnostic leads. Apply the reviewed precaution for this pattern: Escalate bleeding, hyperthyroid, preeclampsia, or respiratory signs and arrange post-evacuation hCG follow-up.

Nursing Relevance

Verify how and when Snowstorm sign of molar pregnancy was acquired, record the exact reported pattern, and compare it with the patient's condition and the positive criteria: Numerous echogenic cystic spaces create a snowstorm or cluster-of-grapes appearance. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Review uterine ultrasound with quantitative hCG and clinical context. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, hydatidiform mole, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.
