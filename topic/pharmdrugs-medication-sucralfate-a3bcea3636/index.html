Used To Treat

Short-term healing of active duodenal ulcer and reduced-dose maintenance after healing; it protects the mucosal defect but does not eradicate H. pylori or provide strong acid suppression.

Mechanism

Acid promotes polymerization and cross-linking, and the resulting complex adheres preferentially to injured mucosal protein for hours. The film impedes hydrogen-ion diffusion, adsorbs bile salts and reduces pepsin activity. Acid-neutralizing capacity is modest, so healing depends mainly on barrier formation. The same nonspecific binding reduces absorption of many coadministered drugs.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to sucralfate or formulation components

  • Clarify severe renal failure/dialysis, bowel obstruction or markedly impaired gastric emptying

  • Use extra caution with dysphagia, reduced gag/cough reflex, tracheostomy or enteral feeding

Nursing Essentials

  • Administer on an empty stomach as ordered and separate other oral drugs; do not mix timing casually with tube feeds or antacids.

  • Assess ulcer symptoms and bleeding red flags while confirming H. pylori testing/eradication and acid-suppression strategy when indicated.

Interactions

  • Can reduce absorption of fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, levothyroxine, phenytoin, digoxin, quinidine, ketoconazole, theophylline and other drugs.

  • Giving the interacting drug at least 2 hours before eliminated several documented interactions, but some products require longer spacing.

  • Aluminum-containing antacids increase aluminum burden; avoid close antacid administration that disrupts acid-dependent binding.

Key Labs

  • Renal function before prolonged use in at-risk patients

  • Aluminum level only when toxicity is clinically suspected; monitor cognition, bone pain and anemia

  • CBC/occult bleeding or endoscopic/H. pylori evaluation when ulcer complications or persistent symptoms exist

Nclex Traps

  • Sucralfate coats an ulcer; it does not substantially suppress acid or kill H. pylori.

  • A local GI drug can cause systemic aluminum toxicity when renal clearance is poor.

  • Binding interactions are prevented by timing, not by assuming minimal absorption means no interaction.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Use caution in chronic kidney failure or dialysis because absorbed aluminum can accumulate and cause encephalopathy, osteomalacia or anemia; dialysis does not efficiently remove protein-bound aluminum. Separate interacting drugs and protect patients with dysphagia, tracheostomy, recent intubation or poor cough/gag reflex from tablet aspiration or bezoar formation.

Description

Sucralfate is a minimally absorbed sulfated-sucrose aluminum mucosal protectant that polymerizes in an acidic stomach and forms an ulcer-adherent complex with proteins in the ulcer exudate. The barrier blocks hydrogen-ion diffusion, pepsin and bile salts. Because it also binds medications, many interacting drugs are given at least 2 hours before sucralfate, with longer product-specific spacing when required.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, dry mouth, nausea, dyspepsia and abdominal discomfort

  • Bezoar or GI obstruction in patients with impaired motility

  • Aspiration with respiratory complications

  • Aluminum accumulation/toxicity in significant renal impairment
