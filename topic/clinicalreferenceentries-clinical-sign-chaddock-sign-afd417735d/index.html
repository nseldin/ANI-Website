Definition

Chaddock sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to corticospinal tract dysfunction. A positive finding means the great toe extends, often with toe fanning.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Chaddock sign means the great toe extends, often with toe fanning.

Why It Matters

A positive Chaddock sign response can signal corticospinal tract dysfunction. Alternative cutaneous stimulation activates the same disinhibited extensor plantar circuitry as Babinski sign. Chaddock sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • corticospinal tract dysfunction

Assessment Method

Stroke the skin below the lateral malleolus and along the lateral foot without injuring the skin.

Positive Finding

The great toe extends, often with toe fanning.

Mechanism

Alternative cutaneous stimulation activates the same disinhibited extensor plantar circuitry as Babinski sign.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Chaddock sign response raises concern for corticospinal tract dysfunction because alternative cutaneous stimulation activates the same disinhibited extensor plantar circuitry as Babinski sign. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Chaddock sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Chaddock sign to focus evaluation of corticospinal tract dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: normal withdrawal, local foot pain, peripheral nerve disease. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Stroke the skin below the lateral malleolus and along the lateral foot without injuring the skin. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Chaddock sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Technique and patient arousal alter the response; diagnostic accuracy is variable. Therefore, Chaddock sign cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal withdrawal

  • local foot pain

  • peripheral nerve disease

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal withdrawal, local foot pain, peripheral nerve disease, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Chaddock sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with limited incremental value. When performed as described, Chaddock sign can adjust concern for corticospinal tract dysfunction, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Chaddock sign: Technique and patient arousal alter the response; diagnostic accuracy is variable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Chaddock sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Stroke the skin below the lateral malleolus and along the lateral foot without injuring the skin. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Keep the stimulus below the malleolus and do not call nonspecific leg withdrawal positive.
