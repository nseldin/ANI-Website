Class

Multikinase inhibitor targeting FLT3 and KIT

Used To Treat

With standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy for newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML, and as therapy for aggressive systemic mastocytosis, systemic mastocytosis with an associated hematologic neoplasm, or mast-cell leukemia.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: no boxed warning is listed. Monitor for new cough, dyspnea, hypoxia, or infiltrates and discontinue for drug-related interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis. Prevent fetal exposure, control severe nausea/vomiting, and review strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers that can markedly alter exposure.

Mechanism

Midostaurin and active metabolites inhibit FLT3-ITD/TKD and wild-type FLT3, KIT including D816V, PDGFR-alpha/beta, and PKC-family kinases. Blocking receptor phosphorylation reduces proliferative and survival signaling, promotes apoptosis in FLT3-driven leukemia, and suppresses KIT-driven mast-cell proliferation and histamine release.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm FLT3 companion-diagnostic positivity and AML cycle days; midostaurin is given with induction/consolidation rather than replacing chemotherapy.

  • Give with food and scheduled antiemetic support; monitor pulmonary status, infection, bleeding, cytopenias, ECG/electrolytes when QT risk is stacked, and mastocytosis symptom/tryptase response.

Key Labs

  • FLT3 result for AML, CBC, CMP/liver function, pregnancy testing when applicable, ECG and potassium/magnesium for QT-risk combinations, and tryptase/mast-cell disease markers when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Midostaurin targets both FLT3-mutated AML and KIT-driven mastocytosis, but the dose and treatment context differ.

  • It is not approved as single-agent AML induction; omitting the chemotherapy backbone changes the regimen fundamentally.

Description

Midostaurin is an oral multikinase inhibitor that suppresses wild-type and mutant FLT3 signaling in AML and KIT, including KIT D816V, in advanced systemic mastocytosis. It is not single-agent AML induction: timing with cytarabine/daunorubicin matters. Severe GI intolerance, pulmonary toxicity, fetal harm, cytopenic/infectious complications from the regimen, and strong CYP3A interactions require active management.
