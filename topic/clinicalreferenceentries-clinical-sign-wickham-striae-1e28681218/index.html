Definition

Wickham striae is an observed surface-pattern sign. It is a clue to lichen planus. A positive finding means fine white reticular lines cross the violaceous surface.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Wickham striae means fine white reticular lines cross the violaceous surface.

Why It Matters

The Wickham striae pattern can signal lichen planus. Focal hypergranulosis creates opaque white lines over inflamed epidermis. Wickham striae cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lichen planus

Assessment Method

Inspect papules under good light or dermoscopy.

Positive Finding

Fine white reticular lines cross the violaceous surface.

Mechanism

Focal hypergranulosis creates opaque white lines over inflamed epidermis.

Clinical Interpretation

The Wickham striae pattern raises concern for lichen planus because focal hypergranulosis creates opaque white lines over inflamed epidermis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Wickham striae does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Wickham striae to focus evaluation of lichen planus and the reviewed alternatives: leukoplakia, candidiasis, lichen sclerosus. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect papules under good light or dermoscopy. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Wickham striae as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Subtle striae may be absent and other mucosal disorders can appear reticular. Therefore, Wickham striae cannot confirm or exclude lichen planus by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • leukoplakia

  • candidiasis

  • lichen sclerosus

False Positive Situations

A false-positive label can result from grouping nonspecific components caused by leukoplakia, candidiasis, lichen sclerosus without confirming that the defined pattern is present.

False Negative Situations

Wickham striae may be absent even when lichen planus is present because the complete named pattern need not occur.

Safety Precautions

Observe Wickham striae with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect papules under good light or dermoscopy. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Oral ulceration or persistent white lesions need appropriate malignancy assessment.

Nursing Relevance

For Wickham striae, document which defined components are present or absent, their timing and trend, and whether the positive pattern is met: Fine white reticular lines cross the violaceous surface. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Inspect papules under good light or dermoscopy. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, lichen planus, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Wickham striae on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
