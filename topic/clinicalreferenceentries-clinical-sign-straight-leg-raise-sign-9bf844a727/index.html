Definition

Straight-leg raise sign is a bedside neural-tension maneuver. It is a clue to lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1. A positive finding means familiar radiating pain below the knee occurs in a typical angle range.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Straight-leg raise sign means familiar radiating pain below the knee occurs in a typical angle range.

Why It Matters

A positive Straight-leg raise sign response can signal lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1. Hip flexion tensions the sciatic nerve and lumbosacral roots around a disc lesion. Straight-leg raise sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1

Assessment Method

Raise the relaxed straight leg by flexing the hip while keeping the knee extended.

Positive Finding

Familiar radiating pain below the knee occurs in a typical angle range.

Mechanism

Hip flexion tensions the sciatic nerve and lumbosacral roots around a disc lesion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Straight-leg raise sign response raises concern for lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1 because hip flexion tensions the sciatic nerve and lumbosacral roots around a disc lesion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Straight-leg raise sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Straight-leg raise sign to focus evaluation of lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1 and the reviewed alternatives: hip disease, hamstring strain, peripheral neuropathy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Raise the relaxed straight leg by flexing the hip while keeping the knee extended. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Straight-leg raise sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Angle, pain description, and technique affect accuracy. Therefore, Straight-leg raise sign cannot confirm or exclude lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1 by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip disease

  • hamstring strain

  • peripheral neuropathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hip disease, hamstring strain, peripheral neuropathy, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Straight-leg raise sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; back or hamstring pain alone is not positive. When performed as described, Straight-leg raise sign can adjust concern for lumbosacral nerve-root irritation, usually L5 or S1, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Straight-leg raise sign: Angle, pain description, and technique affect accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Straight-leg raise sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Raise the relaxed straight leg by flexing the hip while keeping the knee extended. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Stop for severe pain and do not bounce or force the leg.
