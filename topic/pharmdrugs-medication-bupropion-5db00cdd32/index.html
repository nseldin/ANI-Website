Used To Treat

Major depressive disorder and seasonal affective disorder; under smoking-cessation products, reduction of nicotine withdrawal and craving. Formulations and total daily doses are not interchangeable without accounting for release rate.

Mechanism

Bupropion and active hydroxybupropion inhibit norepinephrine and dopamine transporters to a clinically relevant degree and noncompetitively antagonize several neuronal nicotinic receptor subtypes. Reduced nicotinic reward signaling plus increased catecholamine tone weakens craving and withdrawal during smoking cessation. It has little direct SERT inhibition, so its adverse pattern differs from SSRIs. CYP2B6 forms hydroxybupropion, while bupropion/hydroxybupropion inhibit CYP2D6. Immediate peaks and excessive total exposure increase neuronal excitability, explaining why crushing extended-release tablets, closely spaced doses or combining Wellbutrin and Zyban can provoke seizures.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. All bupropion-containing products count toward one total daily exposure; duplicate brands or altered extended-release tablets can produce dangerous peak concentrations and seizures.

Contraindications

  • Seizure disorder or current/prior bulimia or anorexia nervosa

  • Abrupt discontinuation of alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates or antiseizure drugs

  • Concurrent/recent MAOI, another bupropion product, or product hypersensitivity

Nursing Essentials

  • Reconcile every brand and indication because a patient may unknowingly receive Wellbutrin for depression and Zyban for smoking, duplicating the same molecule.

  • Give sustained/extended-release doses on schedule and swallow intact; dose spacing limits peak concentration and therefore seizure risk.

Interactions

  • CYP2B6 inducers/inhibitors change bupropion and hydroxybupropion exposure; ritonavir-type regimens may lower active exposure.

  • CYP2D6 inhibition can raise metoprolol, several antipsychotics, TCAs and antiarrhythmics and reduce tamoxifen activation.

  • Other seizure-threshold-lowering or dopaminergic/stimulant drugs and nicotine replacement add neurologic or blood-pressure risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure/pulse, weight, sleep and agitation

  • Seizure-risk, alcohol/sedative use, eating-disorder and duplicate-product review

  • Mood, suicidality, mania/psychosis and smoking status

Nclex Traps

  • Bupropion is avoided in bulimia/anorexia because electrolyte instability and the drug's excitatory effect compound seizure risk.

  • Lower sexual adverse-effect burden does not mean no serious interactions or suicide monitoring.

  • Zyban and Wellbutrin are the same active drug.

Description

Bupropion is a non-SSRI antidepressant that enhances norepinephrine/dopamine signaling and antagonizes neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors. This explains its activating profile, smoking-cessation benefit, and relatively low rate of serotonergic sexual dysfunction or weight gain. The same catecholaminergic and electrophysiologic activation lowers seizure threshold in a dose- and peak-concentration-dependent way, making seizure disorder, eating disorders, abrupt sedative/alcohol withdrawal and accidental duplicate-brand dosing central safety issues.

Adverse Effects

  • Insomnia, activation/anxiety, dry mouth, nausea, tremor, sweating and headache

  • Hypertension and tachycardia, especially with nicotine replacement or other stimulants

  • Dose-related seizures and rare psychosis/mania

  • Smoking-cessation neuropsychiatric symptoms, angle-closure glaucoma and serious hypersensitivity
