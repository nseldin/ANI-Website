Definition

Galeazzi sign is an observed limb-length sign. It is a clue to developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy. A positive finding means one knee sits lower than the other.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Galeazzi sign means one knee sits lower than the other.

Why It Matters

The observed Galeazzi sign finding can signal developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy. Unilateral proximal femoral displacement or femoral shortening reduces apparent knee height. Galeazzi sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy

Assessment Method

With hips and knees flexed and feet on the surface, compare knee heights.

Positive Finding

One knee sits lower than the other.

Mechanism

Unilateral proximal femoral displacement or femoral shortening reduces apparent knee height.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Galeazzi sign finding raises concern for developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy because unilateral proximal femoral displacement or femoral shortening reduces apparent knee height. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Galeazzi sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Galeazzi sign to focus evaluation of developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy and the reviewed alternatives: leg positioning error, tibial shortening, contracture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With hips and knees flexed and feet on the surface, compare knee heights. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Galeazzi sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Bilateral dislocation and tibial discrepancy can escape or alter the sign. Therefore, Galeazzi sign cannot confirm or exclude developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • leg positioning error

  • tibial shortening

  • contracture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as leg positioning error, tibial shortening, contracture.

False Negative Situations

Galeazzi sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of developmental hip dislocation or femoral length discrepancy.

Current pediatric screening clue; misses bilateral disease. Interpret Galeazzi sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Bilateral dislocation and tibial discrepancy can escape or alter the sign.

Safety Precautions

Observe Galeazzi sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: With hips and knees flexed and feet on the surface, compare knee heights. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Align heels and pelvis; do not press unstable infant hips repeatedly.
