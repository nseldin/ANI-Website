Definition

Terry nails is an observed nail sign. It is a clue to systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes. A positive finding means most of the nail is white with a narrow distal pink or brown band.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Terry nails means most of the nail is white with a narrow distal pink or brown band.

Why It Matters

The observed Terry nails finding can signal systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes. Reduced nail-bed vascularity and connective-tissue change alter visible color. Terry nails cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes

Assessment Method

Inspect the nail bed with pressure released.

Positive Finding

Most of the nail is white with a narrow distal pink or brown band.

Mechanism

Reduced nail-bed vascularity and connective-tissue change alter visible color.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Terry nails finding raises concern for systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes because reduced nail-bed vascularity and connective-tissue change alter visible color. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Terry nails does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Terry nails to focus evaluation of systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes and the reviewed alternatives: half-and-half nails, leukonychia, onychomycosis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the nail bed with pressure released. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Terry nails as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age and normal variation strongly confound the association. Therefore, Terry nails cannot confirm or exclude systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • half-and-half nails

  • leukonychia

  • onychomycosis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as half-and-half nails, leukonychia, onychomycosis.

False Negative Situations

Terry nails may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of systemic disease including cirrhosis, heart failure, and diabetes.
