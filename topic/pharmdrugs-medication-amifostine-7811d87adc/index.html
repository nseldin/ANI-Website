Class

Normal-tissue cytoprotective aminothiol prodrug

Used To Treat

Label-directed reduction of cumulative cisplatin renal toxicity in specified advanced ovarian-cancer treatment and reduction of moderate-to-severe xerostomia during postoperative radiation for head and neck cancer. It prevents selected injury; it does not reverse established kidney failure or salivary-gland fibrosis and is not appropriate in every curative-treatment setting.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): amifostine can cause abrupt severe hypotension, anaphylaxis, and life-threatening skin reactions. Give only with hydration, required blood-pressure monitoring, and immediate resuscitation capability.

Mechanism

Membrane-bound alkaline phosphatase removes amifostine's phosphate group and forms WR-1065, a free thiol that donates reducing capacity, scavenges radiation-generated radicals, and binds reactive platinum species before they damage DNA, membranes, and proteins. Better perfusion, pH, and enzyme activity in many normal tissues favor local conversion compared with some tumors, which explains cytoprotection but does not prove absolute tumor selectivity. The same vasodilatory and autonomic effects can abruptly lower blood pressure, while thiol chemistry can trigger serious hypersensitivity.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record supine blood pressure before and during infusion and keep the patient positioned safely because abrupt hypotension can cause syncope, ischemia, and falls within minutes.

  • Confirm hydration and reconcile antihypertensive medicines before dosing because volume depletion and additive vasodilation magnify the blood-pressure fall.

  • Give a label-directed antiemetic regimen and track emesis because severe nausea and vomiting can worsen dehydration and make later hypotension more likely.

  • Inspect skin and mucosa and monitor airway, oxygenation, and hemodynamics because anaphylaxis and severe cutaneous reactions can begin during or after exposure.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity to aminothiol compounds is a labeled contraindication.

  • Do not administer to a dehydrated or hypotensive patient, and do not generalize use to curative chemotherapy or definitive radiotherapy when the label warns that tumor protection or reduced efficacy has not been excluded.

Interactions

  • Antihypertensives, nitrates, diuretics, dehydration, and other vasodilators can add to amifostine-associated hypotension; follow the label's withholding and hydration plan rather than making an unsupervised medication change.

  • Cisplatin and radiotherapy are the intended injury-producing partners, but timing is deliberate. Giving amifostine outside the validated interval or treatment setting could reduce protection or theoretically protect tumor tissue.

Key Labs

  • Serum creatinine/eGFR, electrolytes, calcium, magnesium, fluid balance, urine output, and cisplatin-specific renal monitoring when used for nephroprotection.

  • Blood pressure at label-defined intervals; CBC and treatment-specific cancer monitoring, plus salivary and oral assessment when used during head-and-neck radiotherapy.

Nclex Traps

  • Amifostine is given before the injurious treatment; it does not repair established renal or salivary damage.

  • Hydration and blood-pressure monitoring are core parts of the medicine, not optional comfort measures.

  • Do not assume a cytoprotectant is appropriate whenever cisplatin or radiation appears in a regimen.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Older adults or patients with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease

    Note

    Reduced perfusion reserve makes a short blood-pressure drop more likely to cause ischemia, falls, or arrhythmia, so eligibility, hydration, monitoring, and recovery thresholds require particular care.

  • Population

    Patients with low calcium or disorders of calcium regulation

    Note

    Amifostine can lower serum calcium, and repeated dosing increases the need for biochemical and neuromuscular surveillance because symptomatic tetany or arrhythmia can follow a deeper decline.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, and pediatric care

    Note

    Evidence and labeled experience are limited, while the underlying chemotherapy or radiation carries major reproductive and developmental implications; use only through the specialist oncology protocol.

Description

Amifostine is converted by tissue alkaline phosphatase to the active thiol WR-1065, which scavenges reactive species and binds electrophilic metabolites shortly before selected cisplatin or radiation exposure. Preferential activation and uptake in many normal tissues can reduce injury while attempting to preserve antitumor effect, but that selectivity is incomplete. Profound hypotension, hypersensitivity, hypocalcemia, nausea, and vomiting make hydration, blood-pressure eligibility, exact timing, and continuous observation central to safe use.

Administration Timing

  • Confirm that the cancer indication and treatment setting match the current ETHYOL label, because the label limits use when chemotherapy or radiotherapy is potentially curative or survival-improving outside an approved or studied setting.

  • Hydrate, withhold antihypertensive medicines for the label-directed interval when clinically safe, verify baseline blood pressure and calcium, and give the infusion in the exact pre-cisplatin or pre-radiation window because protection depends on active thiol being present during injury.

  • Monitor blood pressure at the required frequency during infusion, stop for the label threshold, position and support the patient, and restart only under the product's explicit recovery criteria.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, nausea, vomiting, flushing, dizziness, somnolence, sneezing, hiccups, and hypocalcemia are clinically important and can interrupt treatment.

  • Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, angioedema, cardiac ischemia or arrhythmia during hypotension, and severe cutaneous reactions including Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis can occur.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Syncope, persistent hypotension, chest pain, dysrhythmia, hypoxemia, airway swelling, blistering rash, or mucosal lesions requires immediate cessation and emergency treatment.

  • Rising creatinine despite prophylaxis or progressive xerostomia requires oncology reassessment because amifostine lowers risk but does not guarantee organ protection or justify continuing an injurious regimen unchanged.

Evidence Limitations

  • Benefit is indication- and schedule-specific. Evidence for one normal tissue cannot be generalized to prevention of every chemotherapy or radiation toxicity.

  • Normal-tissue selectivity is relative rather than absolute; the label's limitations around curative or definitive treatment must remain visible rather than being softened into a generic cytoprotectant claim.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled for specified cisplatin nephroprotection and postoperative head-and-neck-radiation xerostomia reduction, with explicit limitations on use in potentially curative or definitive treatment settings.

Boxed Warning Specificity

Specific educational safety warning; verify whether the current product carries an FDA boxed warning.
