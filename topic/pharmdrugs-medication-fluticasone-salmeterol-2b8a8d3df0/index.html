Used To Treat

Scheduled maintenance treatment of asthma in product-specific ages and product/strength-specific maintenance treatment of COPD. It is not a rescue inhaler, not AIR, and not MART because salmeterol does not begin bronchodilation rapidly enough for symptom relief.

Mechanism

Fluticasone binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors with high affinity, moves to the nucleus, induces anti-inflammatory genes, and represses NF-kappaB and AP-1 pathways, reducing inflammatory-cell survival and mediator release. Salmeterol activates beta2-Gs-adenylyl cyclase-cAMP-PKA signaling to reduce myosin light-chain kinase and calcium-dependent smooth-muscle contraction. Its lipophilic side chain anchors near a receptor exosite and prolongs action, but receptor access is too slow for rescue use. Corticosteroid signaling can increase beta2-receptor expression and LABA signaling can support glucocorticoid-receptor function, explaining maintenance synergy. Neither component immediately substitutes for emergency treatment of severe airflow obstruction.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: The fixed combination has no current U.S. boxed warning, but LABA monotherapy remains unsafe in asthma and salmeterol is never a rescue medicine. Do not add another LABA. Acute or rapidly worsening symptoms require the rapid-relief action plan. Severe milk-protein allergy contraindicates lactose-containing Diskus/Inhub powders, and strong CYP3A4 inhibition can cause systemic corticosteroid excess and cardiovascular salmeterol toxicity.

Contraindications

  • Primary treatment of status asthmaticus or acute episodes requiring intensive measures

  • Severe milk-protein allergy for lactose dry powders, ingredient hypersensitivity, or concurrent use of another LABA

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach twice-daily or product-specific scheduled use, rinse and spit, and keep a separate rapid reliever. Never direct extra Advair for an attack.

  • Assess mouth, voice, inhaler technique, dose counter, symptoms, rescue use, exacerbations, pneumonia cues, and adherence.

Interactions

  • Ritonavir, cobicistat, ketoconazole, and other strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can greatly increase fluticasone and salmeterol exposure.

  • Other LABAs or sympathomimetics add toxicity; nonselective beta blockers oppose bronchodilation; diuretics can magnify hypokalemia.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, peak flow/spirometry, exacerbations, rescue use, oral exam, technique, and adherence

  • Potassium, glucose, adrenal function, growth, bone density, and eye assessment when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Advair is not a rescue inhaler and cannot be used for MART because salmeterol is slow-onset.

  • Severe milk-protein allergy is different from lactose intolerance, but both should be clarified rather than guessed.

Description

Fluticasone/salmeterol is a scheduled inhaled corticosteroid plus long-acting beta2 agonist controller that suppresses airway inflammation with fluticasone and relaxes airway smooth muscle for about 12 hours with salmeterol. Fluticasone reduces cytokine transcription, eosinophilic inflammation, edema, mucus signaling, and exacerbation risk. Salmeterol keeps airway smooth muscle relaxed but has a slower onset than formoterol. That timing difference makes Advair-like products scheduled controllers only: taking extra Diskus doses during an attack delays albuterol or another prescribed rapid reliever. The fixed combination protects against LABA monotherapy in asthma, but it can still cause thrush, dysphonia, pneumonia in COPD, adrenal effects, and beta-agonist toxicity. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can markedly raise fluticasone exposure.

Adverse Effects

  • Oral candidiasis, dysphonia, throat irritation, headache, tremor, palpitations, muscle cramps, or upper-respiratory symptoms

  • Pneumonia in COPD, adrenal suppression, reduced bone density or growth, cataract/glaucoma, hyperglycemia, hypokalemia, arrhythmia, paradoxical bronchospasm, or hypersensitivity
