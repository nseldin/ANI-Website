Class

Phenoxyacetic-acid non-sulfonamide loop diuretic

Used To Treat

Edema in selected patients when a potent loop effect is required, especially when the chemical structure of sulfonamide-type loops is undesirable after a serious reaction. Its risk profile prevents it from being the routine first choice.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Ethacrynic acid can cause profound diuresis and severe electrolyte or volume depletion. Deafness may be permanent, especially with renal impairment or concurrent aminoglycosides, other ototoxic drugs, or another loop. If sudden severe watery diarrhea occurs, discontinue the drug; the current label advises that it not be given again because the reaction can be dangerous.

Mechanism

After proximal tubular secretion into the lumen, ethacrynic acid and its active sulfhydryl-related metabolite inhibit salt transport in the thick ascending limb, functionally suppressing the NKCC2 pathway and medullary-gradient generation. Sodium, chloride, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and water excretion increase. Greater distal sodium delivery and volume-mediated aldosterone signaling increase potassium and hydrogen secretion, producing hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis. Ethacrynic acid's alpha,beta-unsaturated ketone chemistry can react with sulfhydryl groups and may contribute to its distinctive toxicity. Hearing injury reflects disruption of ion transport in the stria vascularis and is more likely with rapid IV exposure, renal impairment, high doses, and another ototoxin. The severe watery diarrhea warning is drug specific and should not be diluted into a generic loop side-effect list.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record weight, congestion, pressure, orthostasis, intake/output, recent response, creatinine/eGFR, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, and magnesium before aggressive dosing.

  • Reassess within the expected route-specific effect window. Teach daily weight and symptom thresholds, morning timing when feasible, fall prevention, and urgent reporting of hearing change or severe weakness.

  • Ask why ethacrynic acid was selected and document the exact allergy history. Stop and escalate sudden profuse diarrhea, tinnitus, muffled hearing, vertigo, or abrupt imbalance.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and hypersensitivity are core label contraindications. Severe sodium, potassium, chloride, or volume depletion and progressive oliguria or azotemia require correction or reassessment before more drug.

  • Cirrhosis with ascites requires cautious titration because an abrupt fluid or electrolyte shift can precipitate hepatic encephalopathy.

  • The label contraindicates use in infants and directs permanent discontinuation after severe watery diarrhea. Avoid combination with furosemide because hearing toxicity can be additive.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs can blunt natriuresis and worsen kidney perfusion; aminoglycosides, cisplatin, and other ototoxins increase hearing risk; lithium clearance falls with sodium depletion.

  • RAAS blockers and vasodilators add hypotension and filtration effects, while low potassium or magnesium increases digoxin and arrhythmia risk.

  • Avoid concurrent aminoglycosides or other highly ototoxic therapy whenever possible; another loop adds hearing and depletion risk.

Key Labs

  • Daily weight, intake/output, blood pressure and orthostasis, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, and calcium or uric acid when clinically relevant.

  • Judge response by decongestion with preserved perfusion and acceptable chemistry, not urine volume alone.

Nclex Traps

  • Loop drugs increase distal sodium delivery, so potassium and hydrogen are lost together and hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis can result.

  • They increase urinary calcium and magnesium; thiazides decrease urinary calcium.

  • Non-sulfonamide does not mean low toxicity. Ethacrynic acid has greater practical hearing concern and a distinctive no-rechallenge severe-diarrhea warning.

Description

Ethacrynic acid, historically branded Edecrin, is a non-sulfonamide loop diuretic that inhibits thick-ascending-limb salt transport and can produce profound sodium and water loss. Lacking the sulfonamide group found in furosemide, bumetanide, and torsemide makes it a possible alternative after a serious, carefully characterized sulfonamide-related reaction, although nonantibiotic sulfonamide cross-reactivity is uncommon and the allergy history should be clarified rather than treated as a slogan. It has a particularly important association with ototoxicity and can cause abrupt, severe watery diarrhea; the label directs discontinuation and avoidance of rechallenge when that diarrhea occurs. It is therefore not a generically safer loop. It trades one structural issue for distinctive gastrointestinal and hearing risks.

Adverse Effects

  • Dose-related volume depletion, orthostasis, hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, hypomagnesemia, hyperuricemia, and kidney-function change arise from potent salt loss and downstream aldosterone signaling.

  • Tinnitus or hearing loss becomes more likely with high or rapid IV exposure, kidney impairment, low albumin, or another ototoxin; severe rash, interstitial nephritis, cytopenia, and pancreatitis are less common.

  • Sudden severe watery diarrhea is a distinctive potentially serious reaction. Gastrointestinal bleeding, pancreatitis, hepatic injury, agranulocytosis, and thrombocytopenia are rare but important.
