Definition

Sandifer sign pattern is an observed dystonic-posture sign. It is a clue to gastroesophageal reflux in an infant or child. A positive finding means brief dystonic head and back postures occur around feeding while consciousness is preserved.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Sandifer sign pattern means brief dystonic head and back postures occur around feeding while consciousness is preserved.

Why It Matters

The observed Sandifer sign pattern finding can signal gastroesophageal reflux in an infant or child. Posturing may reduce painful esophageal acid exposure through learned positioning. Sandifer sign pattern cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • gastroesophageal reflux in an infant or child

Assessment Method

Observe timing of episodic neck extension and trunk arching relative to feeds.

Brief dystonic head and back postures occur around feeding while consciousness is preserved.

Mechanism

Posturing may reduce painful esophageal acid exposure through learned positioning.
