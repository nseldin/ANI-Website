Class

Nrf2-pathway-activating Friedreich-ataxia therapy

Used To Treat

Friedreich ataxia in adults and adolescents 16 years of age and older.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current SKYCLARYS labeling has no boxed warning. Aminotransferases can rise, BNP can rise with fluid-overload events, and LDL and total cholesterol can increase. Obtain ALT, AST, bilirubin, BNP and lipids before treatment and at labeled intervals; evaluate weight gain, edema, dyspnea, palpitations or chest symptoms rather than treating BNP as an isolated number.

Mechanism

Friedreich ataxia impairs frataxin-dependent iron-sulfur-cluster biology, promoting mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress while Nrf2 signaling is relatively suppressed. Omaveloxolone has been shown to activate Nrf2 in vitro and in animals and humans; Nrf2 translocates to the nucleus and binds antioxidant-response elements. The label does not establish one molecular binding event as the complete therapeutic mechanism.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give 150 mg once daily on an empty stomach at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after food; a high-fat meal markedly raises peak exposure. Verify hepatic dose adjustment, strong/moderate CYP3A interactions, pregnancy and contraceptive efficacy.

  • Trend gait/ataxia and function, liver symptoms, edema/heart-failure physiology and lipid burden. Stop and escalate according to aminotransferase/bilirubin thresholds rather than assuming every rise reflects disease.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST and bilirubin before treatment, monthly for the first 3 months then periodically; BNP before and during therapy, fasting lipids, weight/volume status and clinical Friedreich-ataxia outcomes.

Nclex Traps

  • Omaveloxolone activates a stress-response pathway but does not replace frataxin or correct the FXN expansion.

  • BNP elevation may occur without obvious heart failure, yet symptoms or fluid gain demand prompt cardiac evaluation.

Description

Omaveloxolone is a once-daily oral therapy for Friedreich ataxia that activates the Nrf2 cellular stress-response pathway, increasing transcriptional programs involved in antioxidant defense, redox balance, mitochondrial function and intermediary metabolism. The exact mechanism that produces clinical benefit is unknown, and treatment requires early liver tests, BNP and lipid surveillance. It should not be described as repairing the frataxin gene or directly replacing deficient frataxin.
