Class

Antimuscarinic motion-sickness antiemetic

Used To Treat

Prevention of motion sickness and selected postoperative nausea/vomiting prevention, commonly by transdermal patch.

Mechanism

Blocks muscarinic acetylcholine signaling from vestibular pathways to the vomiting center, reducing motion-triggered nausea before it starts.

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply patch behind ear as directed and remove old patches. Assess blurred vision, dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, confusion, glaucoma risk, heat intolerance, and accidental eye exposure after touching the patch.

Contraindications

  • Clarify narrow-angle glaucoma, urinary retention/BPH, bowel obstruction/ileus, older-adult delirium risk, or hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, antihistamines, tricyclics, antipsychotics, and CNS depressants increase confusion, urinary retention, constipation, and sedation risk.

Key Labs

  • No routine lab; monitor mental status, urinary output/retention symptoms, vision complaints, and bowel pattern.

Nclex Traps

  • Scopolamine prevents vestibular/motion nausea; it is not a general rescue antiemetic for every vomiting pathway. Anticholinergic toxicity is the safety pattern.

Description

Scopolamine is an antimuscarinic motion-sickness antiemetic: it blocks muscarinic acetylcholine signaling from vestibular pathways to the vomiting center, reducing motion-triggered nausea before it starts. It is used for prevention of motion sickness and selected postoperative nausea/vomiting prevention, commonly by transdermal patch.
