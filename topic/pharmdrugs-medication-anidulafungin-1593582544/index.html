Used To Treat

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for anidulafungin in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Anidulafungin noncompetitively inhibits fungal beta-1,3-D-glucan synthase, weakening the cell wall and causing osmotic instability. Mammalian cells lack glucan cell walls, which creates selective toxicity; FKS mutations can confer echinocandin resistance.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain cultures and evaluate source control/line removal; give the loading dose and do not exceed the labeled infusion rate.

  • Monitor flushing, rash, bronchospasm, hypotension/anaphylaxis, liver tests, potassium, treatment response, and hereditary fructose intolerance risk.

Key Labs

  • Blood/tissue cultures and susceptibility, liver tests, potassium, CBC, renal function for overall illness, and temperature/hemodynamic response.

Nclex Traps

  • Anidulafungin targets fungal cell-wall glucan, not ergosterol. It has no established dosing for Candida infection in the eye or CNS.

Description

Anidulafungin is an IV echinocandin antifungal that noncompetitively inhibits beta-1,3-D-glucan synthase, weakening a fungal cell-wall structure that human cells do not possess. It is active against many Candida species and avoids renal dose adjustment, but infusion-related histamine reactions, anaphylaxis, liver injury, and fructose-containing formulation risk in hereditary fructose intolerance require attention.
