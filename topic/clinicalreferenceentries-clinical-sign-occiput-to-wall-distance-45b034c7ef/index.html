Definition

Occiput-to-wall distance is a bedside posture measurement. It is a clue to fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis. A positive finding means the occiput cannot reach the wall.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Occiput-to-wall distance means the occiput cannot reach the wall.

Why It Matters

The observed Occiput-to-wall distance finding can signal fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis. Structural kyphosis and cervical involvement shift the head anteriorly. Occiput-to-wall distance cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis

Assessment Method

Have the patient stand with heels and back against a wall without forced extension and measure occiput gap.

Positive Finding

The occiput cannot reach the wall.

Mechanism

Structural kyphosis and cervical involvement shift the head anteriorly.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Occiput-to-wall distance finding raises concern for fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis because structural kyphosis and cervical involvement shift the head anteriorly. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Occiput-to-wall distance does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Occiput-to-wall distance to focus evaluation of fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis and the reviewed alternatives: Parkinson posture, vertebral fracture, normal kyphosis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Have the patient stand with heels and back against a wall without forced extension and measure occiput gap. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Occiput-to-wall distance as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, height, and voluntary posture affect distance. Therefore, Occiput-to-wall distance cannot confirm or exclude fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Parkinson posture

  • vertebral fracture

  • normal kyphosis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as Parkinson posture, vertebral fracture, normal kyphosis.

False Negative Situations

Occiput-to-wall distance may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of fixed thoracic kyphosis or cervical flexion in axial spondyloarthritis.

Safety Precautions

Observe Occiput-to-wall distance with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Have the patient stand with heels and back against a wall without forced extension and measure occiput gap. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not push the head backward in fragile cervical spine or severe osteoporosis.
