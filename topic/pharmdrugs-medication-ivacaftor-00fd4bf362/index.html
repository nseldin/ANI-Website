Class

Cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) potentiator

Used To Treat

Cystic fibrosis in clients with at least one CFTR mutation shown to respond to ivacaftor; genotype or approved in-vitro responsiveness must be confirmed.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for ivacaftor in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Ivacaftor binds CFTR protein at the cell surface and increases channel open probability. More chloride and bicarbonate cross epithelial membranes, improving mucus hydration, mucociliary clearance, sweat chloride, pancreatic/GI physiology, and other organ effects when responsive CFTR is present.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify responsive genotype, give with fat-containing food, avoid grapefruit/Seville oranges, and review strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers.

  • Monitor ALT/AST/bilirubin on schedule, respiratory symptoms/exacerbations, weight/pancreatic status, rash, and baseline/follow-up eye exams in pediatric clients for cataracts.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, bilirubin, sweat chloride or pulmonary function as ordered, weight/nutrition, and pediatric ophthalmic exams.

Nclex Traps

  • Ivacaftor is a potentiator: it opens responsive CFTR at the surface. It cannot correct absent or severely misfolded protein by itself.

Description

Ivacaftor helps CFTR channels already present at the epithelial surface stay open longer, improving chloride/bicarbonate transport and airway-surface hydration. It is highly effective for gating and other responsive mutations but cannot rescue a mutation that produces no usable channel; liver injury, CYP3A interactions, pediatric cataracts, and fat-containing-food administration define safe use.
