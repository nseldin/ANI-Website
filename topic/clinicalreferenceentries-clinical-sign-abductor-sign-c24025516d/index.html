Definition

Abductor sign is a bedside motor consistency test. It is a clue to functional lower-limb weakness. A positive finding means involuntary abduction of the weak leg is stronger than its voluntary effort.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Abductor sign means involuntary abduction of the weak leg is stronger than its voluntary effort.

Why It Matters

A positive Abductor sign result can signal functional lower-limb weakness. Bilateral hip-abductor synergy exposes a mismatch between automatic and requested movement. Abductor sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • functional lower-limb weakness

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to abduct each hip and observe the allegedly weak leg during resisted abduction of the opposite leg.

Positive Finding

Involuntary abduction of the weak leg is stronger than its voluntary effort.

Mechanism

Bilateral hip-abductor synergy exposes a mismatch between automatic and requested movement.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Abductor sign result raises concern for functional lower-limb weakness because bilateral hip-abductor synergy exposes a mismatch between automatic and requested movement. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Abductor sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Abductor sign to focus evaluation of functional lower-limb weakness and the reviewed alternatives: hip arthritis, pain inhibition, bilateral neurologic weakness. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to abduct each hip and observe the allegedly weak leg during resisted abduction of the opposite leg. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Abductor sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The test requires preserved comprehension and one reasonably strong limb. Therefore, Abductor sign cannot confirm or exclude functional lower-limb weakness by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hip arthritis

  • pain inhibition

  • bilateral neurologic weakness

False Positive Situations

A false-positive test can result from method deviation, subjective interpretation, or an alternative cause such as hip arthritis, pain inhibition, bilateral neurologic weakness.

False Negative Situations

Abductor sign may be missed when technique, timing, cooperation, or disease stage prevents the defined response.

Modern Status

Current adjunct for functional weakness. When performed as described, Abductor sign can adjust concern for functional lower-limb weakness, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Abductor sign: The test requires preserved comprehension and one reasonably strong limb.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Abductor sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Ask the patient to abduct each hip and observe the allegedly weak leg during resisted abduction of the opposite leg. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use alone or confront the patient; integrate multiple positive functional signs.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Abductor sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Involuntary abduction of the weak leg is stronger than its voluntary effort. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Ask the patient to abduct each hip and observe the allegedly weak leg during resisted abduction of the opposite leg. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, functional lower-limb weakness, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
