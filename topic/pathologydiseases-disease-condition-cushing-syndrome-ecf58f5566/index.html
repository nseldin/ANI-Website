Pathology

Cushing syndrome is prolonged exposure to excessive cortisol, either made by the body or caused by long-term corticosteroid therapy. Think: too much cortisol. This is glucocorticoid excess: cortisol is high or the body is being exposed to too much glucocorticoid effect. Cortisol excess raises glucose and blood pressure, weakens protein/collagen tissue, suppresses immune response, and causes fat redistribution.

Etiology

Most commonly caused by long-term glucocorticoid therapy. Endogenous causes include pituitary ACTH excess called Cushing disease, adrenal cortisol-producing tumors, and ectopic ACTH production.

Signs Symptoms

  • Central weight gain

  • moon face

  • buffalo hump

  • thin skin

  • purple striae

  • easy bruising

  • proximal muscle weakness

  • hypertension

  • hyperglycemia

  • infection risk

  • osteoporosis

  • mood changes

Diagnostics

  • Medication history for steroid exposure

  • cortisol exposure is high or inappropriate for normal feedback

  • late-night salivary cortisol, 24-hour urine free cortisol, or dexamethasone suppression testing when ordered

  • ACTH level helps localize source

  • glucose high

  • blood pressure high

  • potassium may be low in selected causes

  • bone density and infection assessment as indicated

Treatments

  • Taper exogenous steroids only under provider direction

  • surgery, radiation, or cortisol-lowering medications for selected endogenous causes

  • manage hypertension, hyperglycemia, infection risk, skin fragility, and bone loss

Nursing Priorities

  • Assess glucocorticoid medicines and injections, weight distribution, proximal weakness, skin fragility, bruising, infection, mood, sleep, menstrual or sexual change, fractures, and growth because chronic cortisol excess affects nearly every organ system.

  • Coordinate ordered cortisol confirmation, ACTH-directed cause testing, imaging, glucose, electrolytes, bone, cardiovascular, and infection assessment because exogenous exposure, pituitary, adrenal, and ectopic causes require different treatment.

  • Protect fragile skin and bones, prevent falls and infection, manage prescribed glucose, pressure, potassium, and thrombosis precautions, and prepare cause-directed therapy because cardiovascular, infectious, and thrombotic complications drive substantial morbidity.

  • Monitor blood pressure, glucose, potassium, weight, edema, temperature, wounds, mood, muscle strength, treatment adherence, and post-treatment cortisol status because improvement is gradual while therapy can abruptly produce adrenal insufficiency.

  • Escalate for hypotension with vomiting or profound weakness after treatment, fever with infection, critical hypokalemia or dysrhythmia, severe hyperglycemia, chest pain, dyspnea, suicidality, or acute neurologic change because adrenal crisis and cortisol-related complications are time critical.

Complications

  • Severe infection

  • poor wound healing

  • diabetes/hyperglycemia

  • hypertension

  • thromboembolism

  • osteoporosis/fracture

  • adrenal insufficiency after treatment or abrupt steroid stop

Patient Education

  • Never stop long-term glucocorticoids suddenly because suppressed adrenal glands may not produce enough cortisol during physiologic stress.

  • Carry the prescribed steroid emergency information after treatment because illness or surgery may require temporary stress dosing.

Nclex Traps

  • Cushing syndrome is cortisol excess. Do not confuse it with Addison disease, which is cortisol deficiency. Cushing cues include hypertension, hyperglycemia, weight gain, moon face, buffalo hump, thin skin, muscle wasting, osteoporosis, and poor wound healing.

  • Memory trick: Cushing = cushion. Too much cortisol creates the cushioned appearance, but the safety issues are infection, glucose, BP, skin breakdown, and fractures.

Definition

Cushing syndrome is prolonged exposure to excessive cortisol, either made by the body or caused by long-term corticosteroid therapy. Think: too much cortisol. This is glucocorticoid excess: cortisol is high or the body is being exposed to too much glucocorticoid effect. Cortisol excess raises glucose and blood pressure, weakens protein/collagen tissue, suppresses immune response, and causes fat redistribution.

Red Flags

  • Hypotension, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, or profound weakness after cortisol-lowering treatment

  • Fever, rapidly spreading infection, poor wound healing, or sepsis physiology

  • Critical hypokalemia, dysrhythmia, severe hyperglycemia, chest pain, or acute dyspnea

  • Suicidal intent, psychosis, acute focal deficit, fracture, or sudden severe back pain

    Expansion
    • Cue

      Cushion outside

      Meaning

      Cortisol excess can redistribute fat toward the trunk, face, and supraclavicular or dorsocervical regions while blood pressure and glucose rise.

    • Cue

      Catabolism underneath

      Meaning

      Protein and connective-tissue breakdown helps explain proximal weakness, thin fragile skin, bruising, purple striae, poor healing, and bone loss despite the outward fat pattern.

    Id

    mnemonic:cushing-cushion

    Limitations

    • Body shape alone cannot diagnose Cushing syndrome, and common obesity does not automatically indicate pathologic cortisol excess.

    • Exogenous glucocorticoid exposure is a common cause and must be distinguished from endogenous pituitary, adrenal, or ectopic ACTH disease.

    • The cue does not supply screening criteria, dynamic-test interpretation, or a treatment plan.
