Class

Continuous IV prostacyclin (PGI2) analog

Used To Treat

Severe pulmonary arterial hypertension to improve exercise capacity, hemodynamics, symptoms, and survival in appropriate specialist-managed clients.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current FLOLAN and VELETRI labeling has no boxed warning, but abrupt dose reduction or withdrawal is immediately life threatening. Brief delivery interruption can cause dyspnea, dizziness, asthenia, rebound pulmonary hypertension, and death because epoprostenol disappears within minutes. Never stop the infusion to troubleshoot. Maintain delivery with the verified backup pump, cassette, and access plan while the problem is corrected.

Mechanism

Epoprostenol binds the prostacyclin IP receptor on vascular smooth muscle and platelets. The Gs-coupled receptor activates adenylyl cyclase, raises cAMP, and activates protein kinase A, which reduces calcium-dependent smooth-muscle contraction and platelet activation. Pulmonary vascular resistance falls, so the right ventricle ejects against less afterload. The same systemic vasodilation causes hypotension, flushing, and headache, while platelet inhibition increases bleeding. Rapid hydrolysis and metabolism explain both fine titratability and the exceptional danger of interruption.

Nursing Essentials

  • Treat the infusion as uninterrupted life support. At every handoff verify drug and brand, concentration in ng/mL, prescribed ng/kg/min, dosing weight, mL/hour, dedicated central lumen, pump settings, cassette volume and expiration, battery, backup pump, backup medication, clamps, connectors, and emergency contacts.

  • If the pump alarms or the line fails, assess the patient and immediately preserve delivery through the established backup pathway; do not flush a medication-filled line into the patient and do not pause therapy while investigating.

  • Monitor systemic pressure, syncope, oxygenation, right-heart status, bleeding, line infection, catheter position, jaw pain, GI effects, and pulmonary edema. Titrate only in a monitored setting according to symptoms and adverse effects.

Contraindications

  • Chronic use in congestive heart failure caused by severe left-ventricular systolic dysfunction; pulmonary edema during dose initiation; or hypersensitivity to epoprostenol or structurally related compounds.

  • Pulmonary edema during initiation can indicate pulmonary veno-occlusive disease, where increased arterial inflow meets obstructed pulmonary venous outflow.

Interactions

  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs can add bleeding risk because epoprostenol inhibits platelet aggregation; other vasodilators add hypotension and syncope.

  • Product-specific diluent, concentration, stability, temperature, and pump instructions are not interchangeable between FLOLAN, VELETRI, or improvised solutions.

Key Labs

  • Continuous or frequent hemodynamic and oxygenation assessment during initiation; CBC and bleeding assessment; blood cultures and sepsis evaluation when indicated; BNP/NT-proBNP, functional class, six-minute-walk distance, echocardiography, and right-heart hemodynamics for response.

Nclex Traps

  • Never abruptly stop epoprostenol, even when the line appears infected or the pump is malfunctioning; establish backup delivery first.

  • Do not flush the dedicated line, because the catheter dead-space can contain a concentrated bolus.

  • Epoprostenol is not a generic IV vasodilator for left-sided systolic heart failure; that population had increased mortality and is contraindicated.

Class Pathway

PAH therapy > Prostacyclin analog > IP receptor-Gs-cAMP > Continuous ultra-short-acting IV infusion

Description

Epoprostenol is an ultra-short-acting continuous IV prostacyclin analog that activates IP receptors, raises cAMP, rapidly dilates pulmonary arteries, and reduces right-ventricular afterload in advanced WHO Group 1 PAH. It also dilates systemic arteries and inhibits platelet aggregation while the infusion is running. Because the drug is cleared within minutes, the pump and dedicated central line function as life support: an empty cassette, dead battery, clamp, disconnection, programming error, or dislodged catheter can remove pulmonary vasodilator support and trigger abrupt rebound PAH, right-ventricular collapse, and death.

Adverse Effects

  • Headache, flushing, jaw pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness, hypotension, tachycardia or bradycardia, musculoskeletal pain, and bleeding reflect potent prostacyclin signaling.

  • Long-term IV therapy adds central-line infection, sepsis, catheter thrombosis or dislodgement, pump or cassette failure, and dosing-error risk.
