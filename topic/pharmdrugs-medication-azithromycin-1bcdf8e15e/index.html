Used To Treat

Susceptible infections based on organism, infection site, cultures, allergies, renal function, and local resistance patterns.

Mechanism

Azithromycin works by binding 23S rRNA on the 50S ribosomal subunit and blocking translocation during bacterial protein synthesis. It is often used for atypical respiratory pathogens; QT prolongation, CYP interactions with erythro/clarithro, GI effects, and local resistance matter.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning commonly emphasized; QT prolongation and hepatotoxicity warnings matter.

Contraindications

  • Known macrolide hypersensitivity.

  • History of cholestatic jaundice/hepatic dysfunction from azithromycin.

  • High-risk QT prolongation requires review.

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess allergy, diarrhea, liver symptoms, QT risk.

  • Complete prescribed course.

  • Use caution in dysrhythmia risk.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Azithromycin is categorized here as Macrolide 50S ribosomal inhibitor antibiotic. Pathway: Anti-infective medication > Protein synthesis inhibitor > Macrolide 50S inhibitor. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Other QT-prolonging drugs increase risk.

  • Warfarin effect may increase; monitor INR.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • LFTs if symptoms/risk

  • INR if on warfarin

  • ECG/QT risk

  • Allergies, cultures before first dose when ordered, renal function for renally cleared drugs, liver function for hepatotoxic drugs, CBC, diarrhea/C. difficile cues, and therapeutic levels for aminoglycosides/vancomycin when ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Palpitations/syncope on QT-risk meds need attention, not reassurance.

  • Do not delay urgent antibiotics for sepsis, but obtain cultures first if ordered and it does not delay care.

Description

Azithromycin is a macrolide 50S ribosomal inhibitor antibiotic: it works by binding 23S rRNA on the 50S ribosomal subunit and blocking translocation during bacterial protein synthesis. It is used for antibiotic therapy only when the suspected or proven organism, infection site, severity, allergies, pregnancy/age context, renal/hepatic function, and local resistance pattern match the exact subclass.
