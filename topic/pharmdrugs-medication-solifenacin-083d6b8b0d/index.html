Class

Long-acting M3-preferring bladder antimuscarinic

Used To Treat

Adult overactive bladder with urgency, frequency and urge incontinence; the separate VESIcare LS suspension has pediatric neurogenic-detrusor indications and is not automatically interchangeable with tablets.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated with urinary or gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma. Angioedema/anaphylaxis can occur after the first or later doses. Avoid in patients at high QT-prolongation risk. Severe constipation or abdominal pain can signal dangerous GI hypomotility.

Mechanism

Competitive blockade of M3 receptors interrupts Gq, phospholipase C, IP3 and intracellular calcium signaling in detrusor smooth muscle. The bladder stores more before contracting, while the same pathway is suppressed in salivary glands, bowel, ciliary muscle and sweat glands. CYP3A4 metabolism and a long elimination half-life explain why inhibitors and organ impairment can increase sustained exposure and QT/anticholinergic risk.

Nursing Essentials

  • Ask specifically about constipation lasting 3 days, abdominal pain, reduced stream and inability to void.

  • Review QT history, interacting drugs and formulation before dose escalation from 5 to 10 mg.

Contraindications

  • Urinary retention, gastric retention and uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma

  • Known hypersensitivity

  • Avoid severe hepatic impairment; cap dose with severe renal impairment, moderate hepatic impairment or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors require a 5 mg maximum.

  • Other anticholinergics add GI, cognitive and urinary toxicity.

  • QT-prolonging drugs increase repolarization risk.

Key Labs

  • Renal and hepatic function

  • Post-void residual and bowel function

  • ECG and electrolytes when QT risk exists

Nclex Traps

  • M3 preference is relative, not organ exclusivity.

  • A long half-life means holding one dose may not end toxicity immediately.

  • QT risk separates solifenacin from a generic 'dry-mouth drug' description.

Description

Solifenacin is a long-acting, M3-preferring antimuscarinic that reduces detrusor contraction during filling. Its long half-life supports once-daily dosing but also means adverse effects can persist. In addition to class-wide dry mouth, constipation and retention, solifenacin has specific angioedema/anaphylaxis and QT-prolongation warnings, with a 5 mg ceiling in severe renal impairment, moderate hepatic impairment or strong CYP3A4 inhibition.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision and urinary tract infection

  • Urinary retention and kidney injury downstream of outlet obstruction

  • Somnolence, confusion and hallucinations

  • Angioedema/anaphylaxis, QT prolongation and rare torsades reports
