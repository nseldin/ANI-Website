Definition

Lymphadenopathy means one or more lymph nodes are enlarged or otherwise abnormal for the patient's age and body location. It may be localized to one drainage region or generalized across separate regions.

Plain Language

The small immune-system filters often called glands are swollen or feel abnormal.

Why It Matters

Many enlarged nodes are reactive to infection, but persistent, generalized, supraclavicular, hard, fixed, matted, or rapidly growing nodes can signal systemic infection, autoimmune disease, medication reaction, lymphoma, or metastatic cancer. Fever, drenching sweats, unexplained weight loss, breathing or swallowing difficulty, or immune compromise increases urgency.

Associated Conditions

  • local or systemic infection, immune or granulomatous inflammation, autoimmune disease, medication reaction, hematologic malignancy, metastatic cancer, or less commonly a storage disorder

Assessment Method

With consent, inspect and gently palpate relevant cervical, supraclavicular, axillary, epitrochlear, and inguinal regions as indicated; document exact location, number, size, tenderness, warmth, consistency, mobility, matting, overlying skin, duration, drainage territory, generalized distribution, medications, exposures, fever, night sweats, weight loss, and organ enlargement.

Positive Finding

One or more lymph nodes are enlarged or otherwise abnormal for the patient's age and anatomic site, with possible tenderness, firmness, fixation, matting, or involvement of two or more noncontiguous regions.

Mechanism

Immune-cell proliferation and edema during antigen response, granulomatous inflammation, malignant lymphoid growth, metastatic infiltration, hemorrhage, or storage material increases node size or changes texture.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Lymphadenopathy finding raises concern for local or systemic infection, immune or granulomatous inflammation, autoimmune disease, medication reaction, hematologic malignancy, metastatic cancer, or less commonly a storage disorder because immune-cell proliferation and edema during antigen response, granulomatous inflammation, malignant lymphoid growth, metastatic infiltration, hemorrhage, or storage material increases node size or changes texture. Evidence Limitations

Normal node size varies by site and age, deep nodes are not palpable, obesity and muscle obscure examination, and tenderness, softness, firmness, or mobility cannot by itself rule malignancy in or out. Therefore, Lymphadenopathy cannot confirm or exclude local or systemic infection, immune or granulomatous inflammation, autoimmune disease, medication reaction, hematologic malignancy, metastatic cancer, or less commonly a storage disorder by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as salivary-gland enlargement, neck cyst, lipoma, vascular mass.

False Negative Situations

Lymphadenopathy may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of local or systemic infection, immune or granulomatous inflammation, autoimmune disease, medication reaction, hematologic malignancy, metastatic cancer, or less commonly a storage disorder.
