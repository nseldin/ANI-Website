Class

Subcutaneous bispecific factor IXa- and factor X-directed antibody that mimics factor VIII cofactor function

Used To Treat

Routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce bleeding episodes in adults and children, including newborns, with hemophilia A with or without factor VIII inhibitors. It is not hemophilia B therapy and is not an on-demand single-dose antidote for an acute bleed; breakthrough management follows a patient-specific factor or bypassing-agent plan.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Thrombotic microangiopathy and thrombotic events occurred when, on average, a cumulative amount greater than 100 units/kg per 24 hours of activated prothrombin complex concentrate was given for 24 hours or more during HEMLIBRA prophylaxis. Monitor if aPCC is used; discontinue aPCC and suspend emicizumab if TMA or thrombosis symptoms occur.

Mechanism

One emicizumab arm binds activated factor IX and the other binds factor X, creating a ternary complex that positions enzyme and substrate in a factor VIIIa-like orientation. It does not require thrombin activation and is not regulated identically to native factor VIII, but it restores enough factor X activation to improve thrombin generation in hemophilia A. The antibody does not share structural homology with factor VIII, so anti-FVIII inhibitors generally do not bind it. Emicizumab shortens aPTT and can make one-stage aPTT-based factor assays falsely reassuring; chromogenic assays using bovine reagents are often needed for selected measurements. Its long half-life means assay and interaction effects persist after discontinuation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before the first dose, document when bypassing agents should stop and exactly which agent/dose to use for breakthrough bleeding. Keep this plan available in emergency care.

  • Teach and assess TMA/thrombosis cues: confusion, weakness, limb swelling, abdominal/back pain, jaundice, reduced urine, chest pain, dyspnea, focal neurologic change, or painful swollen extremity.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but serious hypersensitivity requires discontinuation

  • Do not use routine aPTT or aPTT-based factor VIII assays as if emicizumab were absent

  • Avoid high cumulative aPCC exposure and follow the hemophilia center's breakthrough-bleed plan

Interactions

  • Activated prothrombin complex concentrate is the defining dangerous interaction; cumulative dose and duration matter.

  • Recombinant activated factor VII and factor VIII use require specialist planning but do not share the same interaction profile as aPCC.

Key Labs

  • CBC/platelets, hemolysis markers, creatinine/urinalysis, and imaging or organ studies when TMA/thrombosis is suspected

  • Bovine-reagent chromogenic factor VIII or inhibitor assays when indicated; avoid uncritical aPTT-based interpretation

Nclex Traps

  • Emicizumab is for hemophilia A prophylaxis with or without inhibitors; it does not replace factor IX in hemophilia B.

  • A short aPTT during emicizumab does not prove normal coagulation or exclude a clinically important bleed.

Description

Emicizumab is a long-acting subcutaneous bispecific antibody that performs the spatial job of activated factor VIII: it brings factor IXa and factor X close enough for IXa to activate X efficiently. Because it is not factor VIII, factor VIII inhibitors do not neutralize it. This makes prophylaxis possible even when replacement factor has failed from inhibitors. Its continuous procoagulant effect also changes emergency management. High cumulative activated prothrombin complex concentrate exposure can produce thrombotic microangiopathy or thrombosis, and conventional aPTT becomes misleadingly short for months after the last dose.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection-site reaction, headache, arthralgia, fever, or antibody development with loss of efficacy

  • Thrombotic microangiopathy with thrombocytopenia, hemolysis, renal or neurologic injury

  • Venous or arterial thromboembolism when high-risk bypassing therapy is added
