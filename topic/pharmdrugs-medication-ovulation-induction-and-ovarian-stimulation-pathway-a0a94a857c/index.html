Used To Treat

Anovulatory infertility and controlled ovarian stimulation after evaluation of the infertility cause, ovarian reserve, semen factors, tubal anatomy when relevant, and pregnancy status. Letrozole or clomiphene increases endogenous FSH; exogenous gonadotropins directly stimulate follicles; hCG mimics the LH surge that triggers final oocyte maturation and ovulation.

Description

Fertility drugs work at different levels of the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis. Letrozole transiently lowers estrogen synthesis, and clomiphene blocks estrogen feedback at the hypothalamus; both make the brain perceive insufficient estrogen and release more GnRH, FSH, and LH. Follitropin alfa supplies recombinant FSH directly, while menotropins supplies FSH plus LH activity. Chorionic gonadotropin then activates the LH receptor to complete oocyte maturation and trigger ovulation after follicles are ready. The farther therapy moves downstream, the more direct and powerful ovarian stimulation becomes, but the greater the need for ultrasound and estradiol monitoring because multiple follicles, multiple gestation, ovarian torsion, and ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome can result.

Mechanism

Lower estrogen feedback increases hypothalamic GnRH pulse output and pituitary FSH/LH secretion. Letrozole inhibits CYP19A1 aromatase, decreasing conversion of theca-cell androgens to estrogens for several days. Clomiphene is a selective estrogen-receptor modulator whose hypothalamic antagonism prolongs perceived estrogen deprivation; its isomers persist differently and can produce antiestrogenic cervical or endometrial effects. FSH receptor activation on granulosa cells uses Gs-cAMP-protein kinase A signaling to increase aromatase, follicular growth, and inhibin. LH activity supports theca androgen synthesis, which granulosa aromatase converts to estradiol. hCG binds the LH/CG receptor and triggers meiotic resumption, follicular rupture, and luteinization. Excess follicle number and hCG-driven ovarian VEGF explain the vascular permeability of OHSS.

Boxed Warning

There is no single class boxed warning, but specialist monitoring is a core safety requirement. Gonadotropins and hCG can cause ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome with ovarian enlargement, third spacing, ascites, pleural effusion, hemoconcentration, renal dysfunction, thrombosis, respiratory compromise, torsion, and multiple gestation. hCG can worsen an already excessive ovarian response, which is why a trigger may be withheld. Clomiphene visual symptoms can be prolonged or possibly irreversible and rise with cumulative dose or duration. Letrozole is contraindicated during an established pregnancy under its oncology label even though short early-cycle use is guideline-supported for selected ovulation induction.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, headache, mood or visual symptoms, pelvic discomfort, injection-site effects, ovarian enlargement, and altered bleeding

  • OHSS, ovarian torsion or rupture, thrombosis, multiple gestation, ectopic pregnancy, persistent visual injury, or fetal exposure from use after conception

Contraindications

  • Exclude pregnancy, primary ovarian failure, uncontrolled thyroid/adrenal disease, pituitary or ovarian tumor, unexplained bleeding, and contraindications to pregnancy before stimulation.

  • Do not trigger solely because a calendar says so; follicle number and size, estradiol trend, symptoms, and the planned cycle determine whether hCG is safe.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach the exact sequence and purpose of each medicine. Confusing a follicle-growth drug with the ovulation trigger can ruin timing and increase risk.

  • Escalate rapid weight gain, increasing abdominal girth, severe pelvic pain, vomiting, dyspnea, oliguria, leg swelling, or neurologic symptoms because OHSS can worsen after the last dose or after conception.

Interactions

  • Other ovarian stimulants have additive effects; protocol substitutions are not dose-equivalent across units or products.

  • Clomiphene and letrozole interact with the endocrine context, while enzyme modifiers and concurrent hormone therapies can change exposure or response.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test, baseline pelvic ultrasound, ovarian reserve and endocrine evaluation, serial follicular ultrasound, estradiol, and semen/tubal evaluation as indicated

  • Weight, abdominal girth, urine output, hematocrit, electrolytes, renal and liver function, oxygenation, thrombosis symptoms, and pregnancy location when OHSS or ectopic pregnancy is possible

Nclex Traps

  • hCG is the trigger that mimics LH; FSH-containing products grow follicles. Giving more growth signal is not the same as timing final maturation.

  • OHSS often intensifies after hCG because luteinized ovaries release VEGF, making capillaries leaky even though the medication course may already be over.
