Definition

Cryptococcus neoformans is a clinically important fungus associated with pulmonary cryptococcosis, cryptococcal meningitis, and disseminated cryptococcosis. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Cryptococcus neoformans is a fungus with a yeast clinical morphology, nonmotile motility classification, and facultative intracellular host-cell relationship; its reviewed oxygen profile is obligate aerobe. Life stage, specimen source, and host context may change what is visible, so morphology is a clue rather than a stand-alone diagnosis.

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Routine person-to-person isolation is not indicated; reduce avoidable high-burden environmental exposure in profoundly immunocompromised patients and support early symptom evaluation. The reviewed route description is environmental inhalation. Clinical disease can follow recent exposure or delayed reactivation, so one exposure-to-symptom interval is not reliable. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Inhaled yeast can persist in lung and disseminate, especially with impaired cellular immunity; the capsule and intracellular survival support immune evasion. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Cryptococcus neoformans is associated with pulmonary cryptococcosis, cryptococcal meningitis, and disseminated cryptococcosis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
