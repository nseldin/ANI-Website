Contraindications

  • Pregnancy and serious hypersensitivity; oncology use is generally for postmenopausal disease, while fertility use requires a distinct early-cycle protocol.

  • Before ovulation induction, evaluate infertility causes and ovarian reserve; letrozole cannot overcome primary ovarian failure, major tubal blockage, or untreated male factor.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm which indication is being treated. A daily long-term cancer regimen and a brief fertility cycle use the same molecule for opposite endocrine goals and must never be conflated.

  • For chronic therapy, track bone and lipid risk, musculoskeletal function, and adherence. For fertility therapy, teach exact cycle days, ovulation timing, pregnancy testing, and when ultrasound monitoring is required.

Interactions

  • Tamoxifen and estrogen-containing therapies can reduce letrozole's estrogen-deprivation effect; avoid unreviewed estrogen supplements in oncology treatment.

  • CYP metabolism interactions are less dramatic than with many agents, but strong modifiers and other ovarian stimulants require protocol review.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy test before reproductive-age exposure; bone mineral density, lipids, liver function and cancer surveillance for chronic therapy

  • Follicular response and ovulation timing in fertility care; arthralgia, fracture risk, vasomotor symptoms, adherence, and signs of recurrence in oncology care

Nclex Traps

  • Letrozole lowers estrogen synthesis; clomiphene blocks estrogen feedback at the receptor. Both can raise FSH, but they do so at different molecular steps.

  • Why is fertility use brief? The goal is temporary early-cycle feedback release. Continuing after conception would add fetal risk without improving ovulation that already occurred.

Used To Treat

Adjuvant, extended-adjuvant, and advanced hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal patients under U.S. labeling; off-label first-line ovulation induction for many patients with anovulatory PCOS and no other infertility factor under current evidence-based guidance.

Mechanism

Letrozole competitively and reversibly binds the heme group of aromatase, inhibiting CYP19A1 conversion of androstenedione to estrone and testosterone to estradiol in ovary, adipose tissue, and tumor-associated tissue. Lower estrogen reduces estrogen-receptor-driven tumor transcription in hormone-sensitive breast cancer. During early follicular use, lower estradiol removes negative feedback at hypothalamus and pituitary, increasing GnRH-driven FSH release. FSH stimulates granulosa-cell aromatase and follicular growth; as letrozole clears and a follicle matures, estradiol production returns and can support endometrial development and the physiologic LH surge. Chronic estrogen depletion increases osteoclast-mediated bone turnover and alters lipids, while short-cycle fertility exposure has a different duration and risk profile.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Letrozole has no current U.S. boxed warning, but it is contraindicated during pregnancy and can cause fetal harm. Exclude pregnancy before treatment and keep the oncology regimen separate from a specialist early-cycle ovulation-induction course. Chronic therapy can reduce bone mineral density and raise cholesterol; monitor according to cancer-treatment duration and baseline risk. Dizziness, fatigue, and somnolence can impair driving. In fertility care, multiple follicle development, multiple gestation, ovarian enlargement, and uncommon OHSS remain possible even though the risk is generally lower than with injectable gonadotropins.

Description

Letrozole is an oral nonsteroidal inhibitor of aromatase, the CYP19A1 enzyme that converts androgens into estrogens. In postmenopausal breast cancer it deprives estrogen-receptor-positive tumor cells of their major estrogen source. In early-cycle fertility treatment, a short course transiently lowers estradiol, releases hypothalamic-pituitary negative feedback, and raises endogenous FSH enough to recruit a follicle. Because letrozole clears relatively quickly and does not occupy estrogen receptors throughout the cycle, it often produces less persistent antiestrogenic effect on cervical mucus and endometrium than clomiphene. It must not be continued into an established pregnancy; the fertility use depends on brief preovulatory exposure and correct cycle timing, not on the oncology daily regimen.

Adverse Effects

  • Hot flashes, arthralgia, myalgia, fatigue, headache, dizziness, nausea, sweating, edema, and hypercholesterolemia

  • Bone loss and fracture, severe fetal toxicity with pregnancy exposure, cardiovascular or thrombotic events in susceptible oncology patients, hepatic dysfunction, ovarian enlargement, and multiple gestation
