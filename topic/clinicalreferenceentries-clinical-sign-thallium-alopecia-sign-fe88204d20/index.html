Definition

Thallium alopecia sign is an observed hair sign. It is a clue to systemic thallium poisoning. A positive finding means marked alopecia develops days to weeks after exposure.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Thallium alopecia sign means marked alopecia develops days to weeks after exposure.

Why It Matters

The observed Thallium alopecia sign finding can signal systemic thallium poisoning. Thallium disrupts sulfhydryl-dependent keratin formation and rapidly dividing hair-matrix cells. Thallium alopecia sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • systemic thallium poisoning

Assessment Method

Assess rapid diffuse hair shedding after gastrointestinal or neurologic illness.

Positive Finding

Marked alopecia develops days to weeks after exposure.

Mechanism

Thallium disrupts sulfhydryl-dependent keratin formation and rapidly dividing hair-matrix cells.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Thallium alopecia sign finding raises concern for systemic thallium poisoning because thallium disrupts sulfhydryl-dependent keratin formation and rapidly dividing hair-matrix cells. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thallium alopecia sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thallium alopecia sign to focus evaluation of systemic thallium poisoning and the reviewed alternatives: severe illness effluvium, arsenic exposure, autoimmune alopecia. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess rapid diffuse hair shedding after gastrointestinal or neurologic illness. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thallium alopecia sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Hair loss is delayed and nonspecific. Therefore, Thallium alopecia sign cannot confirm or exclude systemic thallium poisoning by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • severe illness effluvium

  • arsenic exposure

  • autoimmune alopecia

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as severe illness effluvium, arsenic exposure, autoimmune alopecia.

False Negative Situations

Thallium alopecia sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of systemic thallium poisoning.

Observe Thallium alopecia sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Assess rapid diffuse hair shedding after gastrointestinal or neurologic illness. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not wait for alopecia when painful neuropathy and exposure suggest thallium.
