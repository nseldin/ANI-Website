Definition

Suprasternal retraction is an observed respiratory sign. It is a clue to upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort. A positive finding means the notch retracts inward on inspiration.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Suprasternal retraction means the notch retracts inward on inspiration.

Why It Matters

The observed Suprasternal retraction finding can signal upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort. Large negative inspiratory pressure pulls compliant tissue inward above the sternum. Suprasternal retraction cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort

Assessment Method

Inspect the suprasternal notch during breathing.

Positive Finding

The notch retracts inward on inspiration.

Mechanism

Large negative inspiratory pressure pulls compliant tissue inward above the sternum.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Suprasternal retraction finding raises concern for upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort because large negative inspiratory pressure pulls compliant tissue inward above the sternum. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Suprasternal retraction does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Suprasternal retraction to focus evaluation of upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort and the reviewed alternatives: tracheomalacia, normal infant soft tissue, Campbell sign. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the suprasternal notch during breathing. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Suprasternal retraction as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Neck anatomy and crying affect appearance. Therefore, Suprasternal retraction cannot confirm or exclude upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tracheomalacia

  • normal infant soft tissue

  • Campbell sign

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tracheomalacia, normal infant soft tissue, Campbell sign.

False Negative Situations

Suprasternal retraction may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of upper-airway obstruction or severe inspiratory effort.
