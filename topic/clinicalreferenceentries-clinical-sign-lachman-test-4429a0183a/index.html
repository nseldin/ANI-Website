Definition

Lachman test is a bedside knee-stability maneuver. It is a clue to anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. A positive finding means anterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft compared with the other knee.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Lachman test means anterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft compared with the other knee.

Why It Matters

A positive Lachman test response can signal anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. A torn ACL no longer restrains anterior tibial translation near extension. Lachman test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency

Assessment Method

With the knee flexed about 20 to 30 degrees, stabilize the femur and translate the tibia anteriorly.

Positive Finding

Anterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft compared with the other knee.

Mechanism

A torn ACL no longer restrains anterior tibial translation near extension.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Lachman test response raises concern for anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency because a torn ACL no longer restrains anterior tibial translation near extension. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Lachman test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Lachman test to focus evaluation of anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: PCL injury, constitutional laxity, tibial plateau fracture. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the knee flexed about 20 to 30 degrees, stabilize the femur and translate the tibia anteriorly. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Lachman test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Acute pain, hamstring guarding, and examiner hand size affect performance. Therefore, Lachman test cannot confirm or exclude anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • PCL injury

  • constitutional laxity

  • tibial plateau fracture

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from PCL injury, constitutional laxity, tibial plateau fracture, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Lachman test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current preferred bedside ACL test; swelling and guarding reduce accuracy. Interpret Lachman test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Acute pain, hamstring guarding, and examiner hand size affect performance.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Lachman test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the knee flexed about 20 to 30 degrees, stabilize the femur and translate the tibia anteriorly. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not stress an acutely fractured or grossly unstable knee.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Lachman test observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Anterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft compared with the other knee. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: With the knee flexed about 20 to 30 degrees, stabilize the femur and translate the tibia anteriorly. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Lachman test, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not stress an acutely fractured or grossly unstable knee. Base escalation for Lachman test on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
