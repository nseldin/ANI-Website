Class

Plasma colloid oncotic agent; intravascular volume expander

Used To Treat

Selected intravascular volume or oncotic support situations such as large-volume paracentesis protocols, burns, shock adjunct use, hypoalbuminemia with edema/third spacing, or cirrhosis-related protocols when ordered.

Description

Albumin is an IV plasma protein colloid that raises oncotic pressure, pulling or holding fluid in the vascular space instead of treating a receptor or enzyme target. Nurses should immediately connect it to intravascular volume support versus fluid overload: lung sounds, oxygenation, BP/MAP, edema, urine output, sodium, and the clinical reason for giving it.

Mechanism

Albumin molecules remain largely intravascular and create colloid osmotic pressure, drawing water from interstitial spaces into circulation when capillary integrity and clinical context allow. The same intravascular expansion can precipitate pulmonary edema or hypertension if the client cannot tolerate the volume shift.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess why albumin is ordered: paracentesis replacement, shock adjunct, burns, cirrhosis protocol, nephrotic/third-spacing context, or other specialty plan.

  • Monitor lung sounds, work of breathing, SpO2, BP/MAP, edema, daily weight, intake/output, urine output, and IV reaction symptoms.

  • Do not assume swollen means intravascularly full; edema can coexist with low effective circulating volume.

Contraindications

  • Clarify severe anemia with cardiac failure, decompensated heart failure, pulmonary edema, uncontrolled hypertension, or history of albumin reaction.

Interactions

  • Effect is hemodynamic rather than CYP-based. Concurrent fluids, diuretics, vasopressors, and sodium strategy change the bedside response.

Key Labs

  • Serum albumin/total protein when relevant.

  • Sodium, creatinine/eGFR, urine output.

  • Hemoglobin/hematocrit and hemodynamic trend depending on context.

Nclex Traps

  • Albumin is not a generic cardiovascular drug; it is oncotic pressure in a bottle.

  • The danger is moving fluid into the vascular space faster than the heart/lungs can tolerate.
