Contraindications

  • Unpaced sick sinus syndrome or second-/third-degree AV block, severe hypotension/cardiogenic shock, accessory-pathway atrial fibrillation/flutter, ventricular tachycardia, serious hypersensitivity, and close IV beta-blocker administration.

  • Do not initiate IV therapy in acute decompensated heart failure. Oral product contraindications also include acute MI with pulmonary congestion for selected labels; verify the exact formulation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before IV use, verify rhythm diagnosis, QRS width, evidence of pre-excitation, ventricular function, pressure, heart rate, PR interval, and recent beta blocker/digoxin/antiarrhythmic exposure. Use continuous ECG and blood-pressure monitoring with resuscitation capability.

  • For oral therapy, verify immediate- versus extended-release product and whether it may be opened, split, or crushed. Monitor pulse, pressure, edema, dyspnea, weight, and bowel tolerance; do not apply one formulation's instructions to another.

Interactions

  • Beta blockers, digoxin, ivabradine, clonidine, anesthetics, and other conduction/contractility depressants add bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, and heart failure. IV beta-blocker proximity is specifically contraindicated in current injection labeling.

  • Diltiazem is a CYP3A4 substrate and inhibitor and affects P-glycoprotein. It can raise exposure to simvastatin/lovastatin, ranolazine, cyclosporine, carbamazepine, and other substrates; inhibitors raise diltiazem and inducers reduce it.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG, heart rate, PR/QRS/rhythm, pressure, perfusion, and IV site during injection/infusion; ventricular function and heart-failure signs when relevant.

  • Liver tests for symptoms or chronic high-risk use, renal context for frailty/interactions, and digoxin or interacting-drug concentrations when clinically indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • Diltiazem is an AV-node blocker. It may slow the ventricular response in ordinary atrial fibrillation but can be dangerous in atrial fibrillation conducted through WPW.

  • A lower heart rate is not automatically success if pressure, mentation, urine output, or heart failure worsens. Rate control must preserve perfusion.

Description

Diltiazem is a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that slows SA-node firing, prolongs AV-node conduction and refractoriness, weakens myocardial contraction, and relaxes arteries. Its cardiac effect makes it a Class IV antiarrhythmic and a useful rate-control drug for selected supraventricular rhythms, but also creates its central hazards: bradycardia, high-grade AV block, hypotension, and worsening systolic heart failure. IV diltiazem is for a rhythm that has been identified, not merely a high displayed heart rate. In ventricular tachycardia or pre-excited atrial fibrillation, AV-node blockade can cause hemodynamic deterioration or ventricular fibrillation. Extended-release products have different delivery systems and schedules, so brand/formulation verification is clinically meaningful.

Used To Treat

IV diltiazem temporarily controls rapid ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation/flutter and can convert selected paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia. Oral immediate- or extended-release products treat hypertension and chronic angina depending on formulation. It is not appropriate for every fast rhythm.

Mechanism

Diltiazem binds open and inactivated L-type calcium channels in nodal, myocardial, and vascular cells. SA- and AV-nodal phase-0 depolarization depends on inward calcium current, so less current slows conduction and prolongs AV-node refractoriness. Use dependence means rapid repeated depolarization increases block, allowing diltiazem to slow tachycardias involving the AV node more than normal-rate conduction. In ventricular myocytes, reduced trigger calcium decreases calcium-induced calcium release and contractility; in arterioles, reduced calcium-calmodulin-myosin light-chain kinase signaling lowers resistance and pressure. Adding a beta blocker or digoxin suppresses the same conduction system through another pathway and can convert useful rate control into severe bradycardia, block, or pump failure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: IV diltiazem is contraindicated in severe hypotension/cardiogenic shock, unpaced sick sinus syndrome or second-/third-degree AV block, atrial fibrillation/flutter with an accessory bypass tract, and ventricular tachycardia. A wide-complex tachycardia must be distinguished from ventricular tachycardia before administration. Do not initiate in acute decompensated heart failure or cardiogenic shock because diltiazem is a negative inotrope. IV beta blockers and IV diltiazem should not be given together or in close proximity. Monitor continuously for hypotension, bradycardia, AV block, and heart-failure deterioration.

Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia, first-degree AV block, hypotension, edema, headache, dizziness, fatigue, rash, nausea, and IV-site burning or pruritus.

  • Second-/third-degree AV block, asystole, worsening heart failure, ventricular fibrillation after rhythm misdiagnosis, severe hypotension, acute hepatic injury, and severe skin reactions.
