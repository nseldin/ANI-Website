Used To Treat

Maintenance of remission of ulcerative colitis in adults who are intolerant of sulfasalazine; it is not labeled as a general induction drug for an acute severe flare.

Mechanism

Bacterial azoreduction releases mesalamine, which acts topically to reduce eicosanoid, cytokine, NF-kappa-B-related and oxidative inflammatory signaling in the colonic mucosa. Intact olsalazine and metabolites can increase small-intestinal water/electrolyte secretion, producing watery diarrhea before or apart from inflammatory relapse.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected hypersensitivity to salicylates, aminosalicylates or product excipients

  • Clarify renal impairment, nephrotoxic drugs, liver disease, severe diarrhea, prior mesalamine intolerance or active severe skin reaction

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify that the goal is remission maintenance and document the prior sulfasalazine intolerance.

  • Assess stool blood, inflammatory symptoms, hydration and timing relative to dosing before labeling diarrhea as a UC relapse.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs and other nephrotoxic drugs add renal risk.

  • Azathioprine/6-mercaptopurine can increase myelosuppression.

  • Selected heparins/heparinoids and varicella vaccine require label-specific review; mesalamine can interfere with urinary normetanephrine assays.

Key Labs

  • Creatinine/eGFR and urinalysis

  • CBC and liver tests when clinically indicated

  • Stool pattern, hydration and electrolytes if diarrhea is significant

Nclex Traps

  • Olsalazine diarrhea can be pharmacologic rather than inflammatory.

  • Its labeled role is maintenance of remission, not automatic treatment of an acute severe flare.

  • Two linked mesalamine molecules still share systemic mesalamine-class toxicities.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Assess renal function before treatment and periodically. Discontinue and evaluate renal decline, mesalamine acute-intolerance syndrome, myocarditis/pericarditis, hepatic failure, severe cutaneous reactions or nephrolithiasis. Distinguish olsalazine-associated secretory diarrhea from recurrent inflammatory bloody diarrhea.

Description

Olsalazine is a symmetric azo-bonded prodrug that colonic bacteria cleave to release two topical mesalamine molecules onto inflamed epithelium. Its defining clinical distinction is maintenance of ulcerative-colitis remission in sulfasalazine-intolerant adults, while dose-limiting secretory diarrhea can resemble disease recurrence.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea or loose stool, abdominal pain, nausea, dyspepsia, headache and rash/pruritus

  • Acute intolerance syndrome, interstitial nephritis/renal failure, nephrolithiasis, pancreatitis, myocarditis/pericarditis, hepatitis and severe skin reactions

  • Volume or electrolyte loss if diarrhea is severe
