Class

Oral selective vasopressin V2-receptor antagonist and aquaretic

Used To Treat

As Samsca, clinically significant euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia, including sodium below 125 mEq/L or symptomatic less-marked hyponatremia that resisted fluid restriction. As Jynarque, slowing kidney-function decline in adults at risk of rapidly progressing ADPKD under a separate dose, indication, and REMS program.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - SAMSCA INITIATION AND RAPID SODIUM CORRECTION: Initiate and reinitiate Samsca only in a hospital where serum sodium can be monitored closely. Overly rapid correction can cause osmotic demyelination with dysarthria, mutism, dysphagia, lethargy, weakness, seizures, coma, and death; malnutrition, alcoholism, and advanced liver disease increase vulnerability. Samsca is not approved for ADPKD. ADPKD treatment uses Jynarque under a separate REMS because serious and potentially fatal liver injury can occur.

Mechanism

Vasopressin binds V2 receptors on collecting-duct principal cells and activates Gs, adenylyl cyclase, cAMP, and protein kinase A, moving aquaporin-2 channels into the apical membrane. Tolvaptan competitively blocks V2, reducing aquaporin-2 insertion and collecting-duct water permeability. Urine becomes more dilute, electrolyte-free water clearance rises, and serum sodium increases without adding sodium. In ADPKD, lower V2-cAMP signaling also reduces cyst-cell proliferation and chloride-driven cyst fluid secretion, slowing structural and functional progression in selected patients. CYP3A metabolism creates major interaction risk: strong inhibitors markedly raise exposure, while inducers reduce effect. Rapid correction occurs when aquaresis is large, fluid intake is restricted, another sodium-raising therapy is added, or the underlying vasopressin stimulus resolves. That causal chain explains hospital initiation for Samsca, frequent sodium checks, access to water, and avoidance of fluid restriction during the first 24 hours.

Nursing Essentials

  • For Samsca, obtain baseline sodium, serum and urine osmolality, volume state, urine output, neurologic status, liver context, medications, and a correction plan. Initiate or reinitiate in hospital and monitor sodium and volume frequently.

  • Allow access to water and avoid fluid restriction during the first 24 hours unless a specialist protocol explicitly directs otherwise. If sodium rises too quickly, stop the drug and manage active relowering or free-water replacement under protocol.

Contraindications

  • Urgent need to raise sodium acutely, inability to sense or appropriately respond to thirst, hypovolemic hyponatremia, anuria, and strong CYP3A inhibitors are key Samsca contraindications.

  • Do not use Samsca for ADPKD or longer than 30 days. Avoid use in underlying liver disease when treating hyponatremia because recovery from liver injury may be impaired.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated; moderate inhibitors increase exposure and inducers reduce efficacy. Grapefruit can increase exposure and should be avoided under product guidance.

  • Other diuretics, hypertonic saline, oral urea, sudden SIADH resolution, or fluid restriction can accelerate correction. Tolvaptan can increase exposure to selected P-glycoprotein substrates.

Key Labs

  • Serum sodium at frequent protocol-defined intervals, serum and urine osmolality, urine output, fluid balance, pressure, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, and neurologic examination; liver tests and REMS monitoring are central for Jynarque.

  • Track the rate from the pretreatment sodium rather than celebrating a normal endpoint. The speed of correction determines osmotic-demyelination risk.

Nclex Traps

  • Samsca is limited to 30 days for hyponatremia because of liver-injury concern; Jynarque is the separate chronic ADPKD product under a REMS.

  • Tolvaptan raises sodium by removing water, not by delivering sodium. It is an aquaretic, not a salt tablet or loop diuretic.

Description

Tolvaptan is an oral selective V2 antagonist that produces aquaresis: excretion of relatively electrolyte-free water. Samsca and Jynarque contain the same molecule but are not the same treatment program. Samsca is initiated or reinitiated in hospital for selected euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia because sodium can rise too quickly; treatment is limited to 30 days because of liver-injury concern and is not approved for ADPKD. Jynarque is chronic ADPKD therapy under a liver-safety REMS with different split dosing and monitoring. Tolvaptan is not for hypovolemic hyponatremia and not a substitute for urgent hypertonic saline when immediate correction is needed. Patients must be able to sense thirst and reach water because thirst is a major defense against excessive free-water loss, dehydration, hypernatremia, and osmotic demyelination.

Display Name

Tolvaptan

Adverse Effects

  • Thirst, dry mouth, polyuria, nocturia, dehydration, hypotension, hypernatremia, constipation, and overly rapid sodium correction follow V2 blockade.

  • Serious liver injury can occur, particularly with chronic ADPKD exposure. Osmotic demyelination is the catastrophic consequence of excessive sodium correction.
