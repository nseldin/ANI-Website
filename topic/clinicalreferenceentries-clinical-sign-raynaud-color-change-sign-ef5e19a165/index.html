Definition

Raynaud color-change sign is an observed vascular sign. It is a clue to episodic digital vasospasm. A positive finding means sharply demarcated pallor or cyanosis followed by reactive redness occurs.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Raynaud color-change sign means sharply demarcated pallor or cyanosis followed by reactive redness occurs.

Why It Matters

The observed Raynaud color-change sign finding can signal episodic digital vasospasm. Excess digital arterial vasoconstriction reduces flow, deoxygenation darkens blood, and reperfusion causes erythema. Raynaud color-change sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • episodic digital vasospasm

Assessment Method

Observe or document cold- or stress-triggered finger color change and distribution.

Positive Finding

Sharply demarcated pallor or cyanosis followed by reactive redness occurs.

Mechanism

Excess digital arterial vasoconstriction reduces flow, deoxygenation darkens blood, and reperfusion causes erythema.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as acrocyanosis, erythromelalgia, blue-toe ischemia.

False Negative Situations

Raynaud color-change sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of episodic digital vasospasm.
