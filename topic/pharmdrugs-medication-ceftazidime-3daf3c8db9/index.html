Used To Treat

Serious susceptible gram-negative infections, including selected Pseudomonas pneumonia, bacteremia, meningitis, urinary, bone/joint, skin, and intra-abdominal infections with appropriate companion anaerobic coverage.

Mechanism

Ceftazidime crosses gram-negative porins and binds PBPs, with important PBP3 activity that disrupts septal peptidoglycan synthesis and cell division. Beta-lactamase stability and outer-membrane penetration support activity against selected Pseudomonas, but ESBLs, AmpC derepression, carbapenemases, porin loss, efflux, and PBP changes can confer resistance. The drug is cleared mainly by glomerular filtration. When renal function falls, prolonged high CNS exposure antagonizes GABA-A-mediated inhibition and increases neuronal excitability, explaining myoclonus, seizures, and nonconvulsive status. Hemodialysis can remove drug in severe accumulation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Ceftazidime has no current U.S. boxed warning. Severe neurotoxicity is reported especially in renal impairment and can include encephalopathy, coma, asterixis, myoclonus, seizures, and nonconvulsive status epilepticus. Stop and urgently evaluate suspected neurotoxicity. Hypersensitivity, C. difficile colitis, cytopenias, and selection of resistant organisms remain important.

Contraindications

  • Known serious ceftazidime or cephalosporin hypersensitivity; severe relevant beta-lactam reactions require avoidance or specialist evaluation.

  • Do not use as sole therapy when gram-positive or anaerobic coverage is essential, and do not infer susceptibility from the word Pseudomonas without culture or local data.

Nursing Essentials

  • Calculate current renal clearance at initiation and during acute illness; reassess the dose after creatinine, urine output, dialysis modality, or neurologic status changes.

  • Obtain cultures, assess source control, and de-escalate when possible. Pair anaerobic or gram-positive therapy only when the syndrome requires it.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides and potent diuretics can increase kidney injury, making ceftazidime accumulation more likely; combination antipseudomonal therapy must have a clear reason.

  • Aztreonam shares a similar side chain, so a true ceftazidime allergy deserves specific cross-reactivity caution even when other beta-lactams are avoided.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, creatinine clearance, urine output, CBC and liver tests during prolonged therapy, and stool assessment for significant diarrhea.

  • Perform repeated mental-status and neuromuscular assessment; EEG may be needed because nonconvulsive status can present as unexplained confusion or reduced responsiveness.

Nclex Traps

  • Ceftazidime covers susceptible Pseudomonas but is comparatively weak for MSSA; cefepime has a broader gram-positive balance.

  • Renal beta-lactam neurotoxicity can look like sepsis delirium. Myoclonus or aphasia should trigger dose and drug review immediately.

Description

Ceftazidime is an IV antipseudomonal third-generation cephalosporin beta-lactam antibiotic that binds bacterial penicillin-binding proteins and blocks peptidoglycan cross-linking, with its spectrum weighted toward aerobic gram-negative bacteria including susceptible Pseudomonas aeruginosa. That strength comes with a tradeoff: its gram-positive activity, especially against MSSA, is weaker than ceftriaxone or cefepime, and it does not cover MRSA, Enterococcus, Listeria, atypicals, or reliable anaerobes. It is therefore not simply 'ceftriaxone plus Pseudomonas.' Renal elimination is dominant. Accumulation can cause encephalopathy, aphasia, myoclonus, seizures, or nonconvulsive status epilepticus, making a new mental-status change in a patient with kidney injury a medication emergency rather than routine delirium.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, rash, pruritus, injection-site pain or phlebitis, candidiasis, and transient blood-count or liver-test changes.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reaction, C. difficile colitis, neutropenia or thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, interstitial nephritis, encephalopathy, myoclonus, coma, and seizures.
