Definition

Luria fist-edge-palm sign is a bedside executive-motor sign. It is a clue to frontal lobe dysfunction. A positive finding means the sequence cannot be learned, maintained, or inhibited despite adequate strength.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Luria fist-edge-palm sign means the sequence cannot be learned, maintained, or inhibited despite adequate strength.

Why It Matters

The observed Luria fist-edge-palm sign finding can signal frontal lobe dysfunction. Frontal premotor and executive networks cannot organize and switch a multistep motor program. Luria fist-edge-palm sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • frontal lobe dysfunction

Assessment Method

Demonstrate a repeating fist, edge, palm hand sequence and ask the patient to imitate it.

Positive Finding

The sequence cannot be learned, maintained, or inhibited despite adequate strength.

Mechanism

Frontal premotor and executive networks cannot organize and switch a multistep motor program.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Luria fist-edge-palm sign finding raises concern for frontal lobe dysfunction because frontal premotor and executive networks cannot organize and switch a multistep motor program. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Luria fist-edge-palm sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Use Luria fist-edge-palm sign to focus evaluation of frontal lobe dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: limb apraxia, motor weakness, delirium. Evidence Limitations

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as limb apraxia, motor weakness, delirium.

False Negative Situations

Luria fist-edge-palm sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of frontal lobe dysfunction.
