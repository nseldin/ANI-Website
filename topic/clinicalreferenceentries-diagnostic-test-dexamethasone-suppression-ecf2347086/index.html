Summary

Dexamethasone suppression is a high-yield Endocrine diagnostic reference. Dynamic cortisol suppression test used in Cushing syndrome evaluation.

Quick Answer

Dexamethasone suppression: Dynamic cortisol suppression test used in Cushing syndrome evaluation. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Suppressed cortisol

    Meaning

    Cortisol falls appropriately after dexamethasone, making autonomous cortisol excess less likely in the tested protocol.

  • Label

    No suppression

    Meaning

    Cortisol remains inappropriately high, supporting Cushing syndrome evaluation when the protocol and clinical picture fit.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Stress, alcohol use, depression, obesity, estrogen therapy, antiseizure drugs, and incorrect timing can affect results.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Dynamic cortisol suppression test used in Cushing syndrome evaluation.

    • Before / preparation

    • Check timing, fasting status, current endocrine medications, steroid use, pregnancy status when relevant, and whether dynamic testing requires serial specimens.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Connect the result to symptoms, glucose safety, thyroid/adrenal crisis risk, and medication teaching.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Severe hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia, thyroid storm/myxedema cues, adrenal crisis, altered mental status, dehydration, or unstable vital signs.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • This is not a treatment trial. It is a dynamic endocrine test tied to cortisol regulation.
