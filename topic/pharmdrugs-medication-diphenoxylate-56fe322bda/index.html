Class

Opioid antidiarrheal

Used To Treat

Short-term control of diarrhea when infectious, inflammatory, or toxic megacolon concerns have been ruled out.

Mechanism

Slows intestinal motility through opioid receptor activity in the gut. Atropine is often included in products to discourage misuse.

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess for fever, bloody stool, severe abdominal pain, dehydration, C. difficile risk, and infectious diarrhea before giving.

  • Monitor sedation, respiratory status in high-risk clients, bowel pattern, and hydration.

Contraindications

  • Avoid or clarify suspected C. difficile, invasive bacterial diarrhea, obstructive jaundice, toxic megacolon, or significant pediatric safety risk.

Interactions

  • Other CNS depressants can increase sedation and respiratory risk.

Key Labs

  • Electrolytes and renal function if dehydration is present. Stool testing may be needed for infectious diarrhea.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not slow the bowel when the body needs to clear infection or toxin. Fever, bloody diarrhea, or C. difficile risk should stop and clarify the order.

Description

Diphenoxylate is an opioid antidiarrheal: it slows intestinal motility through opioid receptor activity in the gut. It is used for short-term control of diarrhea when infectious, inflammatory, or toxic megacolon concerns have been ruled out.
