Class

Quinazoline thiazide-like diuretic that retains distal activity at reduced GFR

Used To Treat

Salt-water retention associated with heart failure or renal disease including nephrotic syndrome, and adult hypertension under current labeling. In heart failure, add-on use with a loop is generally reserved for inadequate response to moderate- or high-dose loop therapy.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: The current U.S. label prominently states DO NOT INTERCHANGE metolazone products equivalent to Zaroxolyn with products equivalent to older Mykrox formulations because they are not bioequivalent. Sequential use with a loop can cause sudden, severe sodium, potassium, magnesium, chloride, volume, and kidney-function changes, so the next dose must depend on reassessment rather than habit.

Mechanism

Metolazone is secreted into the nephron and inhibits NCC in the distal convoluted tubule. Chronic loop exposure increases distal sodium-reabsorbing capacity; metolazone removes that adaptation, so sodium that escapes NKCC2 cannot be reclaimed efficiently. Increased collecting-duct sodium still drives potassium and hydrogen loss through ENaC and aldosterone, while combined volume contraction intensifies RAAS activation. This explains the combination's characteristic hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis. Because metolazone can act despite low GFR, it is useful in resistant renal or heart-failure edema, but less filtration and narrow perfusion reserve increase the consequences of overshoot. Its prolonged action means losses can continue after the desired urine response is reached. The formulation bioavailability warning matters because equal milligrams can produce different exposure and an unexpected depth or duration of diuresis.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check pressure, orthostasis, weight, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, and uric acid when relevant before and after initiation or escalation.

  • Teach earlier-day dosing, slow position changes, photosensitivity precautions, and urgent reporting of confusion, seizure, severe weakness, palpitations, eye pain, or sudden visual change.

  • Document product, dose, timing relative to the loop, pre-dose labs, urine response, weight target, and hold criteria. Repeat chemistry at an interval matched to acuity before allowing automatic continuation.

Contraindications

  • Anuria and hypersensitivity are core contraindication patterns. Symptomatic hyponatremia, major potassium or magnesium loss, hypotension, or evolving kidney injury requires reassessment.

  • Do not assume every thiazide is ineffective below one eGFR threshold; agent, dose, route, indication, and actual response matter.

  • The label contraindicates anuria and hepatic coma or precoma. Do not interchange non-bioequivalent metolazone formulations.

Interactions

  • Lithium exposure can rise as sodium depletion increases proximal lithium reabsorption; NSAIDs can blunt effect and worsen kidney function.

  • SSRIs, carbamazepine, desmopressin, and high water intake magnify hyponatremia; low potassium increases digoxin-related arrhythmia risk.

  • Loop diuretics create intended synergy but also severe depletion risk; corticosteroids and ACTH worsen potassium loss, and curare-type neuromuscular blockers may be potentiated.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, orthostasis, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, magnesium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, glucose, uric acid, weight, and urine calcium when that is the indication.

  • Check sooner in older adults, low body mass, high water intake, CKD, or interacting medication use because severe sodium decline can appear quickly.

Nclex Traps

  • Thiazides lower urinary calcium while raising uric acid; those effects come from different transport responses.

  • They can cause profound hyponatremia because the diluting segment is blocked while concentrating ability and vasopressin action remain.

  • Metolazone is not just 'a stronger thiazide.' Its high-yield role is downstream blockade of loop-induced sodium delivery, and its formulations may not be bioequivalent.

Description

Metolazone is a potent, long-acting thiazide-like NCC inhibitor notable for retaining useful natriuretic activity when GFR is reduced and for strongly synergizing with loop diuretics. It blocks distal sodium rescue after a loop sends a large sodium load downstream, so a small oral dose can transform a weak loop response into profound diuresis. That same mechanism can rapidly cause hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic alkalosis, hypotension, and acute kidney injury. The current label contains an unusual prominent instruction: metolazone tablet formulations equivalent to Zaroxolyn and those equivalent to older Mykrox products must not be interchanged because bioavailability differs. Timing it about 30 minutes before a loop is a common practice intended to establish NCC blockade before loop delivery, but absorption varies and no universal minute rule replaces the prescribed protocol and measured response.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyponatremia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis, volume depletion, hyperuricemia or gout, photosensitivity, and glucose intolerance follow NCC blockade and compensatory distal transport.

  • Severe neurologic hyponatremia, arrhythmia, pancreatitis, severe skin reactions, and acute angle-closure glaucoma or myopia require urgent action.

  • Combined loop therapy can produce exceptionally rapid electrolyte depletion, prerenal azotemia, syncope, seizure, or ventricular arrhythmia.
