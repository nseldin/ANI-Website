Definition

D'Espine sign is an auscultatory pediatric sign. It is a clue to posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis. A positive finding means whispered sounds become unusually clear below the expected upper-thoracic level.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive D'Espine sign means whispered sounds become unusually clear below the expected upper-thoracic level.

Why It Matters

A positive D'Espine sign response can signal posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis. Enlarged posterior mediastinal nodes transmit vocal vibration toward the spine. D'Espine sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis

Assessment Method

Auscultate whispered voice down the thoracic spine.

Positive Finding

Whispered sounds become unusually clear below the expected upper-thoracic level.

Mechanism

Enlarged posterior mediastinal nodes transmit vocal vibration toward the spine.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive D'Espine sign response raises concern for posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis because enlarged posterior mediastinal nodes transmit vocal vibration toward the spine. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; D'Espine sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use D'Espine sign to focus evaluation of posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis and the reviewed alternatives: pneumonia, thin chest wall, normal voice transmission. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate whispered voice down the thoracic spine. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating D'Espine sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Technique, age, and lung disease make it unreliable. Therefore, D'Espine sign cannot confirm or exclude posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • pneumonia

  • thin chest wall

  • normal voice transmission

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from pneumonia, thin chest wall, normal voice transmission, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

D'Espine sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and obsolete with imaging. D'Espine sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude posterior mediastinal lymphadenopathy, historically tuberculosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for D'Espine sign: Technique, age, and lung disease make it unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing D'Espine sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate whispered voice down the thoracic spine. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not use this historical sign to exclude or diagnose tuberculosis.
