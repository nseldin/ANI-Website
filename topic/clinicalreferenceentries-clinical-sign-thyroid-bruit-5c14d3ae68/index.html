Definition

Thyroid bruit is an auscultatory vascular sign. It is a clue to marked thyroid hypervascularity. A positive finding means a systolic or continuous bruit is heard over the gland.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Thyroid bruit means a systolic or continuous bruit is heard over the gland.

Why It Matters

A positive Thyroid bruit response can signal marked thyroid hypervascularity. Autoimmune stimulation increases thyroid blood flow enough to create turbulence. Thyroid bruit cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marked thyroid hypervascularity

Assessment Method

Auscultate gently over each thyroid lobe before palpation in a quiet room.

Positive Finding

A systolic or continuous bruit is heard over the gland.

Mechanism

Autoimmune stimulation increases thyroid blood flow enough to create turbulence.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Thyroid bruit response raises concern for marked thyroid hypervascularity because autoimmune stimulation increases thyroid blood flow enough to create turbulence. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Thyroid bruit does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Thyroid bruit to focus evaluation of marked thyroid hypervascularity and the reviewed alternatives: carotid stenosis, venous hum, toxic multinodular goiter. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate gently over each thyroid lobe before palpation in a quiet room. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Thyroid bruit as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It is absent in many cases and carotid sounds transmit nearby. Therefore, Thyroid bruit cannot confirm or exclude marked thyroid hypervascularity by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • carotid stenosis

  • venous hum

  • toxic multinodular goiter

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from carotid stenosis, venous hum, toxic multinodular goiter, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Thyroid bruit may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct in active Graves disease. When performed as described, Thyroid bruit can adjust concern for marked thyroid hypervascularity, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Thyroid bruit: It is absent in many cases and carotid sounds transmit nearby.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Thyroid bruit, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate gently over each thyroid lobe before palpation in a quiet room. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not compress the carotid sinus or mistake transmitted vascular sound for a gland finding.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Thyroid bruit observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: A systolic or continuous bruit is heard over the gland. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Auscultate gently over each thyroid lobe before palpation in a quiet room. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, marked thyroid hypervascularity, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

For Thyroid bruit, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not compress the carotid sinus or mistake transmitted vascular sound for a gland finding. Base escalation for Thyroid bruit on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
