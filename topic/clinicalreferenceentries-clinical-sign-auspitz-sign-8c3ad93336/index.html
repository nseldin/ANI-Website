Definition

Auspitz sign is a bedside scale-removal sign. It is a clue to psoriasis plaque capillary exposure. A positive finding means pinpoint bleeding appears.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Auspitz sign means pinpoint bleeding appears.

Why It Matters

The observed Auspitz sign finding can signal psoriasis plaque capillary exposure. Thinned suprapapillary epidermis exposes dilated papillary dermal capillaries beneath adherent scale. Auspitz sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • psoriasis plaque capillary exposure

Assessment Method

After clinically appropriate gentle scale removal, inspect the surface.

Positive Finding

Pinpoint bleeding appears.

Mechanism

Thinned suprapapillary epidermis exposes dilated papillary dermal capillaries beneath adherent scale.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Auspitz sign finding raises concern for psoriasis plaque capillary exposure because thinned suprapapillary epidermis exposes dilated papillary dermal capillaries beneath adherent scale. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Auspitz sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Auspitz sign to focus evaluation of psoriasis plaque capillary exposure and the reviewed alternatives: eczema, lichen planus, seborrheic dermatitis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: After clinically appropriate gentle scale removal, inspect the surface. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Auspitz sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sensitivity and specificity are poor and trauma can cause bleeding in many lesions. Therefore, Auspitz sign cannot confirm or exclude psoriasis plaque capillary exposure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • eczema

  • lichen planus

  • seborrheic dermatitis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as eczema, lichen planus, seborrheic dermatitis.

False Negative Situations

Auspitz sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of psoriasis plaque capillary exposure.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; intentionally scraping lesions is usually unnecessary. Auspitz sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude psoriasis plaque capillary exposure. Apply the reviewed limitation for Auspitz sign: Sensitivity and specificity are poor and trauma can cause bleeding in many lesions.

Safety Precautions

Observe Auspitz sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: After clinically appropriate gentle scale removal, inspect the surface. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not scrape anticoagulated, infected, painful, or diagnostically clear skin.
