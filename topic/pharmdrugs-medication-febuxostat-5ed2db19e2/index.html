Contraindications

  • Concomitant azathioprine or mercaptopurine because xanthine-oxidase inhibition can cause life-threatening accumulation; serious prior febuxostat hypersensitivity.

  • Clarify cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, heart failure, liver/kidney function, prior allopurinol dose and reaction, transplant immunosuppressants, pregnancy, and flare-prophylaxis plan.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document why allopurinol is inadequate or inadvisable, obtain baseline urate and liver tests, assess cardiovascular risk, and recheck urate after initiation for treat-to-target titration.

  • Provide indicated flare prophylaxis and teach patients to continue febuxostat through a flare unless a true serious adverse reaction occurs; monitor for hepatic and severe skin symptoms.

Interactions

  • Azathioprine and mercaptopurine are contraindicated; theophylline and other xanthine-oxidase substrates require current-label review.

  • Review hepatotoxic medicines and cardiovascular therapies; interaction absence does not remove the boxed cardiovascular-risk comparison with allopurinol.

Key Labs

  • Serum urate with a usual gout target below 6 mg/dL, AST/ALT, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, and medication review

  • Cardiovascular symptoms and events, rash/mucosal or systemic hypersensitivity signs, flare frequency, tophus burden, and adherence

Nclex Traps

  • Febuxostat is not the routine first choice before an adequate allopurinol trial because of its boxed cardiovascular-death warning.

  • A flare after starting febuxostat reflects crystal mobilization, not increased urate production; continue urate lowering and treat inflammation unless another safety issue requires stopping.

Used To Treat

Chronic hyperuricemia in adults with gout who had an inadequate response to a maximally titrated allopurinol dose, cannot tolerate allopurinol, or should not receive it. It is not recommended for asymptomatic hyperuricemia and is not first-line merely because once-daily dosing is convenient.

Mechanism

Xanthine oxidoreductase catalyzes the final two steps of purine degradation: hypoxanthine to xanthine and xanthine to uric acid. Febuxostat is a nonpurine inhibitor that occupies the enzyme's molybdenum-pterin access channel and inhibits both oxidized and reduced xanthine oxidase. Uric-acid production falls while more soluble upstream purines rise. The drug is metabolized mainly by conjugation with contributions from CYP1A2, CYP2C8, and CYP2C9, so it is less dependent on renal excretion than oxypurinol but still needs renal and hepatic dose review. Because xanthine oxidase also metabolizes azathioprine and mercaptopurine, febuxostat can cause extreme accumulation and fatal marrow toxicity when combined; that interaction is a contraindication, not a minor dose-timing issue.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: In gout patients with established cardiovascular disease, febuxostat produced a higher rate of cardiovascular death than allopurinol in a cardiovascular outcomes study. Use it only when a maximally titrated allopurinol regimen produced inadequate response, allopurinol was not tolerated, or allopurinol is not advisable. Discuss the uncertainty and individual risk before starting or continuing therapy. Teach urgent evaluation for chest pain, dyspnea, rapid or irregular heartbeat, unilateral weakness, facial droop, speech difficulty, sudden severe headache, or dizziness; do not interpret good urate control as proof that cardiovascular risk is acceptable.

Description

Febuxostat is a nonpurine xanthine oxidase inhibitor that lowers uric-acid production without being converted into a purine-like active metabolite. It binds a channel in xanthine oxidase and inhibits both oxidized and reduced forms of the enzyme, lowering conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to urate. Like allopurinol, sustained urate reduction permits crystal deposits to dissolve and can initially mobilize crystals enough to trigger flares, so prophylaxis and continued therapy matter. Its major differentiator is safety positioning: in gout patients with established cardiovascular disease, a large outcomes trial found more cardiovascular deaths than with allopurinol. The boxed warning therefore reserves febuxostat for patients in whom adequately titrated allopurinol failed, was not tolerated, or is not advisable.

Adverse Effects

  • Early gout flares, nausea, arthralgia, rash, and liver-enzyme abnormalities

  • Cardiovascular death, serious hepatic injury, SJS/TEN or DRESS, anaphylaxis, and profound azathioprine/mercaptopurine toxicity
