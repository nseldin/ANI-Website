Definition

Bull neck sign is an observed neck sign. It is a clue to severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy. A positive finding means diffuse neck swelling creates a thick bull-neck appearance.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bull neck sign means diffuse neck swelling creates a thick bull-neck appearance.

Why It Matters

The observed Bull neck sign finding can signal severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy. Toxin-mediated inflammation, soft-tissue edema, and lymphadenopathy expand the neck. Bull neck sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy

Assessment Method

Inspect neck contour and airway status using appropriate isolation.

Positive Finding

Diffuse neck swelling creates a thick bull-neck appearance.

Mechanism

Toxin-mediated inflammation, soft-tissue edema, and lymphadenopathy expand the neck.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bull neck sign finding raises concern for severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy because toxin-mediated inflammation, soft-tissue edema, and lymphadenopathy expand the neck. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bull neck sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bull neck sign to focus evaluation of severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy and the reviewed alternatives: epiglottitis, Ludwig angina, lymphoma. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect neck contour and airway status using appropriate isolation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bull neck sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Other infections and edema can look identical. Therefore, Bull neck sign cannot confirm or exclude severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as epiglottitis, Ludwig angina, lymphoma.

False Negative Situations

Bull neck sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe diphtheria with cervical edema and adenopathy.
