Used To Treat

Mechanism-based explanation of why antidepressant adverse effects can begin early while mood, anxiety, sleep architecture, pain, or compulsive symptoms improve over weeks.

Description

Transporter or receptor occupancy begins after the first doses, but depression is not simply a low-serotonin tank that refills. Acute SERT/NET blockade changes synaptic monoamines immediately and can cause nausea, activation, sleep change, sexual effects, blood-pressure change, or anxiety before benefit. Therapeutic response requires slower autoreceptor desensitization, second-messenger and gene-expression changes, altered stress-network processing, and synaptic plasticity across mood circuits. That delay explains why early tolerability and safety monitoring matter even when the patient says the medicine is not working yet.

Mechanism

SSRIs block SERT; SNRIs block SERT and NET; bupropion affects NET/DAT; mirtazapine blocks inhibitory alpha-2 receptors and selected serotonin receptors; trazodone blocks 5-HT2A and weakly inhibits SERT. Initially, increased serotonin activates presynaptic 5-HT1A/1B autoreceptors that restrain firing and release. Repeated exposure desensitizes this brake and permits more sustained downstream signaling. Receptor trafficking, BDNF-related plasticity, sleep/circadian effects, and network learning then evolve over weeks. The mechanism is therefore a cascade, not a single transmitter concentration.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. Screen for bipolar disorder because antidepressant monotherapy can precipitate mania or mixed symptoms. Serotonin syndrome, discontinuation syndromes, hyponatremia and bleeding risk are mechanism-linked but drug dependent.

Adverse Effects

  • Early nausea, activation, headache, sleep change and sexual dysfunction

  • Serotonin syndrome with interacting serotonergic drugs

  • Discontinuation symptoms, especially with short-half-life agents

  • Hyponatremia/SIADH, bleeding, mania activation or drug-specific organ toxicity

Contraindications

  • Current/recent MAOI use according to product-specific washout intervals

  • Unrecognized bipolar mania/mixed state requires a different treatment plan

  • Apply drug-specific seizure, hepatic, cardiac, pregnancy and interaction contraindications

Nursing Essentials

  • Give a timeline: adverse effects may appear in days; meaningful benefit often needs several weeks at a therapeutic dose.

  • Ask about suicidality, activation, sleep, impulsivity and function at every early follow-up rather than waiting only for a depression score.

Interactions

  • MAOIs, linezolid, IV methylene blue and multiple serotonergic drugs can produce serotonin syndrome.

  • NSAIDs, antiplatelets and anticoagulants add bleeding risk when platelet serotonin uptake is inhibited.

  • CYP inhibition and long half-lives change washout requirements substantially.

Key Labs

  • Sodium when older age, diuretics or symptoms raise SIADH risk

  • Blood pressure for SNRIs and bupropion

  • Drug-specific liver, renal, ECG, pregnancy or concentration monitoring

Nclex Traps

  • Immediate transporter blockade does not mean immediate antidepressant benefit.

  • Early energy improvement can increase the ability to act before hopelessness improves, so suicide assessment remains active.

  • Abruptly stopping a short-half-life agent can mimic relapse or neurologic illness.
