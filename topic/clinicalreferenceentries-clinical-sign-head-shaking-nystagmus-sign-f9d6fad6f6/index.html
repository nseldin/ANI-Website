Definition

Head-shaking nystagmus sign is a bedside vestibular sign. It is a clue to dynamic vestibular asymmetry. A positive finding means transient nystagmus appears or changes after shaking.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Head-shaking nystagmus sign means transient nystagmus appears or changes after shaking.

Why It Matters

The observed Head-shaking nystagmus sign finding can signal dynamic vestibular asymmetry. Rapid head motion temporarily stores asymmetric vestibular velocity signals that discharge when movement stops. Head-shaking nystagmus sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • dynamic vestibular asymmetry

Assessment Method

With cervical safety confirmed, oscillate the head horizontally using a validated technique and then observe the eyes.

Positive Finding

Transient nystagmus appears or changes after shaking.

Mechanism

Rapid head motion temporarily stores asymmetric vestibular velocity signals that discharge when movement stops.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Head-shaking nystagmus sign finding raises concern for dynamic vestibular asymmetry because rapid head motion temporarily stores asymmetric vestibular velocity signals that discharge when movement stops. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Head-shaking nystagmus sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Head-shaking nystagmus sign to focus evaluation of dynamic vestibular asymmetry and the reviewed alternatives: spontaneous nystagmus, cervical proprioceptive nystagmus, poor fixation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With cervical safety confirmed, oscillate the head horizontally using a validated technique and then observe the eyes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Head-shaking nystagmus sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Sensitivity is variable and central patterns can occur. Therefore, Head-shaking nystagmus sign cannot confirm or exclude dynamic vestibular asymmetry by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • spontaneous nystagmus

  • cervical proprioceptive nystagmus

  • poor fixation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as spontaneous nystagmus, cervical proprioceptive nystagmus, poor fixation.

False Negative Situations

Head-shaking nystagmus sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of dynamic vestibular asymmetry.

When performed as described, Head-shaking nystagmus sign can adjust concern for dynamic vestibular asymmetry, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis.
