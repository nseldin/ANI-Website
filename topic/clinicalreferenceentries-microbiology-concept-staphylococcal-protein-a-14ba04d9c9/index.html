  • Content

    Staphylococcal protein A is a cell-wall-associated and secreted virulence factor of Staphylococcus aureus that binds immunoglobulins and alters effective antibody-mediated host defense.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    Protein A binds the Fc region of immunoglobulin G. Normally, Fc regions point outward after antibodies bind a microbe so phagocytes can recognize and ingest the coated organism. Protein A disrupts that useful orientation and weakens opsonization and phagocytic clearance; interactions with B-cell receptors can also distort adaptive immune responses.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Its classic exam association is Fc binding by S. aureus. That association explains immune evasion, but it should be integrated with adhesins, toxins, enzymes, biofilm, host barriers, and resistance rather than memorized as the organism's only pathogenic tool.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Protein A helps explain how S. aureus can persist despite an antibody response and why colonization can progress to invasive disease when barriers fail. It is a mechanism concept, not a standalone diagnostic result or a medication target in routine bedside care.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Virulence depends on strain expression, infection site, inoculum, and host defenses. Detecting or recalling protein A does not determine methicillin susceptibility, prove invasive infection, or replace culture and susceptibility testing.
