Class Pathway

  • Bind negatively charged bile acids in the intestinal lumen

  • Increase fecal bile-acid loss and hepatic CYP7A1 demand

  • Lower hepatic cholesterol and increase LDL receptors

Used To Treat

Adjunctive LDL lowering when triglycerides are acceptable; cholestatic pruritus and bile-acid diarrhea for selected agents; and glycemic improvement in type 2 diabetes for colesevelam under its specific label.

Description

Cholestyramine, colestipol, and colesevelam are large positively charged resins that remain in the gut and bind negatively charged bile acids. The bile acid-resin complex is excreted instead of recycled through the terminal ileum. The liver must convert more cholesterol into bile acids through cholesterol 7-alpha-hydroxylase, lowering hepatocyte cholesterol and increasing LDL receptors. Because the drugs are not systemically absorbed, they can be useful when systemic exposure is undesirable, but the intestine becomes the site of nearly every problem: constipation, bloating, obstruction, drug binding, and reduced absorption of fat-soluble vitamins. They can raise triglycerides because hepatic cholesterol and bile-acid signaling changes can increase VLDL production. That is why a drug that lowers LDL can be inappropriate when triglycerides are already high.

Mechanism

Normally about 95 percent of bile acids are reabsorbed and returned to the liver. Resin binding expands fecal loss, reducing the hepatic bile-acid pool. Lower FXR signaling and increased CYP7A1 activity drive conversion of cholesterol to new bile acids, while SREBP2 increases LDL-receptor expression. Hepatic HMG-CoA reductase also increases compensatorily, explaining synergy with statins. Physical adsorption is nonselective: warfarin, levothyroxine, digoxin, thiazides, anticonvulsants, vitamins A/D/E/K, folate, and many other compounds may be trapped or have altered absorption. Separating doses protects bioavailability. In bile-acid diarrhea, binding prevents excess colonic bile acids from stimulating secretion; in cholestasis, reducing recirculating bile acids can lessen pruritus even though the obstruction itself remains.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Powder resins must never be swallowed dry because they can swell and obstruct or injure the esophagus. Severe constipation, dysphagia, abdominal distension, vomiting, absent stool or gas, bleeding, and high triglycerides require prompt review.

Adverse Effects

  • Constipation, bloating, nausea, dyspepsia, fecal impaction, bowel obstruction, hemorrhoidal bleeding, and hypertriglyceridemia are class effects.

  • Long exposure can reduce fat-soluble vitamins and folate, contributing to bleeding or nutritional problems; colesevelam tablets can create a large pill burden and dysphagia risk.

Contraindications

  • Complete biliary obstruction prevents bile from reaching the intestine and defeats the mechanism. Severe hypertriglyceridemia, prior pancreatitis from triglycerides, bowel obstruction, and severe swallowing or motility problems are major product-specific concerns.

  • Do not use a resin to treat generalized pruritus without diagnosing cholestasis or bile-acid involvement.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check baseline triglycerides, bowel pattern, swallowing, obstruction history, medications, vitamins, anticoagulation, and the exact formulation. Mix powders completely in adequate fluid or appropriate soft food.

  • Create a written spacing schedule rather than saying 'separate other medicines.' Many products require other drugs at least one hour before or four to six hours after, while colesevelam has drug-specific four-hour rules.

Interactions

  • Physical binding can reduce absorption of levothyroxine, warfarin, digoxin, thiazides, anticonvulsants, transplant drugs, oral contraceptives, vitamins, and many others. Consult the exact label.

  • Statins can add LDL lowering; triglyceride-raising drugs or uncontrolled diabetes can make the resin-induced VLDL response more dangerous.

Key Labs

  • Lipid panel with triglycerides, INR when warfarin timing changes, TSH for levothyroxine, drug levels for narrow-index medicines when indicated, and fat-soluble vitamins or folate during prolonged high-risk use.

  • A rising triglyceride trend is a mechanistic warning, not a cosmetic laboratory change, because severe elevation can increase pancreatitis risk.

Nclex Traps

  • These drugs lower LDL but can raise triglycerides. Always inspect the whole lipid panel before and after therapy.

  • Nonabsorbed does not mean interaction free. Intestinal binding can make another essential drug clinically absent.
