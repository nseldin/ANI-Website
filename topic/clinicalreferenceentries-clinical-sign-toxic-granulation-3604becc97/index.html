Definition

Toxic granulation is a microscopic neutrophil sign. It is a clue to accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation. A positive finding means coarse dark primary granules are prominent.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Toxic granulation means coarse dark primary granules are prominent.

Why It Matters

The Toxic granulation laboratory finding can signal accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation. Inflammatory cytokines shorten maturation time and retain abundant azurophilic granules. Toxic granulation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation

Assessment Method

Review neutrophil cytoplasm.

Positive Finding

Coarse dark primary granules are prominent.

Mechanism

Inflammatory cytokines shorten maturation time and retain abundant azurophilic granules.

Clinical Interpretation

The Toxic granulation laboratory finding raises concern for accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation because inflammatory cytokines shorten maturation time and retain abundant azurophilic granules. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Toxic granulation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Toxic granulation to focus evaluation of accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation and the reviewed alternatives: normal granules, stain artifact, Chediak-Higashi syndrome. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review neutrophil cytoplasm. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Toxic granulation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The term does not mean toxin exposure and treatment can cause it. Therefore, Toxic granulation cannot confirm or exclude accelerated granulopoiesis during major inflammation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal granules

  • stain artifact

  • Chediak-Higashi syndrome

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with normal granules, stain artifact, Chediak-Higashi syndrome.

False Negative Situations

Toxic granulation may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
