Class

Dual OX1R/OX2R orexin receptor antagonist hypnotic

Used To Treat

Adult insomnia characterized by difficulty with sleep onset, sleep maintenance or both.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current QUVIVIQ labeling has no boxed warning. It is contraindicated in narcolepsy. Warn about CNS depression and driving impairment, worsening depression/suicide risk, complex sleep behaviors requiring immediate discontinuation, sleep paralysis/hallucinations/cataplexy-like symptoms, respiratory uncertainty and CYP3A interactions; alcohol adds psychomotor impairment.

Mechanism

Daridorexant binds and inhibits both orexin receptors, suppressing a hypothalamic system that sustains wakefulness. The effect permits sleep rather than directly forcing global neuronal inhibition, but too much orexin blockade can produce sleep paralysis, hallucinations or cataplexy-like weakness and can worsen the orexin deficiency of narcolepsy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Administer 25 or 50 mg no more than once nightly within 30 minutes of bed with at least 7 hours remaining. Review hepatic function, strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, other depressants, falls, respiratory disease, depression and substance-use history.

  • Assess actual next-day function, driving and sleep behavior; persistent insomnia warrants evaluation for medical, psychiatric, medication, circadian or sleep-disorder causes.

Key Labs

  • No routine drug level. Monitor alertness/driving, falls, respiratory symptoms, mood/suicidality, REM-transition effects, complex behaviors and CYP3A/CNS-depressant exposure.

Nclex Traps

  • Do not give a second dose in the same night after awakening.

  • A patient may be impaired without recognizing it; subjective alertness does not clear driving.

Description

Daridorexant is a Schedule IV dual orexin receptor antagonist that lowers wake drive by blocking orexin A and B signaling at OX1R and OX2R. It is taken once nightly for adult sleep-onset or maintenance insomnia. Although it does not enhance GABA-A signaling, it can still cause daytime impairment, falls, additive alcohol/CNS-depressant effects, complex sleep behaviors and narcolepsy-like REM-transition symptoms.
