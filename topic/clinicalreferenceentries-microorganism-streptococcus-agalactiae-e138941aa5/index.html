Definition

Streptococcus agalactiae is a clinically important bacterium associated with early-onset neonatal sepsis, late-onset neonatal meningitis, maternal peripartum infection, and invasive infection in adults. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Streptococcus agalactiae is a Gram-positive coccus with a facultative anaerobe oxygen profile. It is not acid-fast, not known to form spores, nonmotile, and extracellular in its reviewed clinical behavior. These traits help narrow an early laboratory differential, but no trait pattern alone proves identity or susceptibility.

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Genital or gastrointestinal colonization plus an antiphagocytic capsule enables vertical transmission and invasive disease in neonates and vulnerable adults. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

  • Content

    Streptococcus agalactiae is associated with early-onset neonatal sepsis, late-onset neonatal meningitis, maternal peripartum infection, and invasive infection in adults. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
