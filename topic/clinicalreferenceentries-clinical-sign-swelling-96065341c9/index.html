Definition

Swelling is visible or palpable enlargement of a body part or musculoskeletal region. It may result from interstitial fluid, joint fluid, blood, inflammation, enlarged tissue, venous or lymphatic obstruction, or a mass, so it is a finding rather than a diagnosis.

Plain Language

Swelling means an area looks or feels larger than usual because fluid, blood, inflammation, or tissue has increased its volume.

Why It Matters

The location, speed, warmth, pain, firmness, pitting, and effect on circulation help separate routine from urgent causes. Rapidly expanding or tense swelling, pain out of proportion, weak pulses, numbness, fever with spreading redness, or sudden one-sided leg swelling with chest symptoms can signal compartment syndrome, severe infection, bleeding, or venous thromboembolism.

Assessment Method

Inspect and compare sides, define the exact location and boundaries, measure consistently when trending, and assess warmth, color, tenderness, firmness, pitting, fluctuance, pulses, sensation, motion, trauma, fever, medications, and whether the enlargement is within a joint or surrounding tissue.

Positive Finding

A body part or musculoskeletal region is visibly or palpably enlarged compared with baseline or the opposite side.

Mechanism

Fluid, blood, inflammatory cells, tissue growth, or impaired venous or lymphatic drainage increases tissue volume and changes contour.

Clinical Interpretation

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus, normal asymmetry, positioning, dependent edema, and imprecise landmarks affect comparison; swelling alone does not distinguish edema, effusion, hematoma, infection, or mass. Therefore, Swelling cannot confirm or exclude localized accumulation of interstitial fluid, joint fluid, blood, inflammatory exudate, enlarged tissue, or a mass after injury, infection, inflammation, venous or lymphatic obstruction, or systemic disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as edema, joint effusion, hematoma, soft-tissue mass.

False Negative Situations

Swelling may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of localized accumulation of interstitial fluid, joint fluid, blood, inflammatory exudate, enlarged tissue, or a mass after injury, infection, inflammation, venous or lymphatic obstruction, or systemic disease.
