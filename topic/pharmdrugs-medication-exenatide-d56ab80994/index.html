Used To Treat

Type 2 diabetes, chronic weight management for selected agents, and cardiovascular/kidney risk reduction for selected clients/agents.

Mechanism

Exenatide works by amplifying incretin signaling. GLP-1 receptor activity increases glucose-dependent insulin release, suppresses glucagon when glucose is high, slows gastric emptying, and increases satiety; tirzepatide also activates GIP signaling. That explains A1c/weight benefit plus nausea, dehydration risk, pancreatitis/gallbladder red flags, and peri-procedure delayed-gastric-emptying nuance.

Boxed Warning

Many agents carry boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumor risk in rodents and contraindication with personal/family MTC or MEN2.

Contraindications

  • Personal/family medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 for many agents

  • serious hypersensitivity

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Teach injection/use schedule if injectable.

  • Monitor GI effects, pancreatitis symptoms, glucose with other diabetes meds.

  • Assess thyroid cancer/MEN2 contraindication.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Exenatide is categorized here as GLP-1 receptor agonist incretin medication. Pathway: Endocrine medication > Diabetes/weight medication > Incretin pathway > GLP-1 receptor agonist. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Insulin/sulfonylureas increase hypoglycemia risk.

  • Slowed gastric emptying can affect oral meds.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Key Labs

  • glucose/A1c

  • renal function if dehydration

  • lipase if pancreatitis symptoms

  • Use medication-specific labs when ordered. For high-risk drugs, connect renal function, liver function, electrolytes, CBC, coagulation studies, drug levels, ECG, and vital-sign trends to safety.

Nclex Traps

  • Severe abdominal pain radiating to back is pancreatitis until proven otherwise.

  • The NCLEX trap is memorizing the name without connecting class, contraindication, assessment, and priority adverse effect.

Description
