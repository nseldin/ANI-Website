Class

Selective relaxant-binding agent for aminosteroid neuromuscular blockade reversal

Used To Treat

Reversal of rocuronium- or vecuronium-induced neuromuscular blockade during surgery. It does not reverse succinylcholine, cisatracurium, opioid sedation, or neurologic weakness.

Boxed Warning

ANESTHESIA SAFETY WARNING: verify rocuronium or vecuronium and objective blockade depth before dosing. Maintain airway support and monitor for recurrent paralysis, anaphylaxis, profound bradycardia, and cardiac arrest; sugammadex does not reverse succinylcholine or cisatracurium. Because sugammadex can lower hormonal contraceptive exposure, use additional nonhormonal contraception for 7 days.

Mechanism

Sugammadex forms a tight water-soluble inclusion complex around free rocuronium or vecuronium. Lowering the free plasma concentration creates a gradient that draws relaxant away from the neuromuscular junction, so nicotinic receptors become available and muscle transmission returns. The mechanism is molecule-specific; it cannot encapsulate succinylcholine or benzylisoquinolinium agents such as cisatracurium. Underdosing at deep blockade can leave enough free drug for recurrence, which is why objective monitoring and depth-matched dosing matter even when movement appears to return.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the original blocker, dose, time, actual body weight, and quantitative train-of-four or post-tetanic count because drug identity and depth determine whether sugammadex works and which dose is appropriate.

  • Monitor ECG, heart rate, blood pressure, oxygenation, ventilation, airway strength, and hypersensitivity because marked bradycardia, cardiac arrest, and anaphylaxis can develop within minutes.

  • Explain seven days of additional nonhormonal contraception because sugammadex can reduce exposure to hormonal progestogens.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity is contraindicated. Current labeling does not recommend use in severe renal impairment because clearance of drug and complex is substantially reduced.

Interactions

  • Toremifene can displace rocuronium or vecuronium from the complex and delay recovery; monitor ventilation closely.

  • Hormonal contraceptive exposure may fall, so use an additional nonhormonal method for seven days. Re-administration timing for rocuronium or vecuronium also depends on prior sugammadex dose.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative train-of-four ratio is the central response measure; assess renal function because the sugammadex-relaxant complex is renally eliminated, and obtain ECG/hemodynamic studies for bradycardia or instability.

Nclex Traps

  • Sugammadex reverses rocuronium and vecuronium, not every paralytic.

  • Dose follows blockade depth, not a routine one-size dose.

  • A recovered twitch does not replace airway and ventilation assessment.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Severe renal impairment

    Note

    Reduced elimination can prolong complex exposure and makes current labeling advise against routine use; specialist planning and postoperative monitoring are essential.

  • Population

    Obesity

    Note

    The U.S. label bases dosing on actual body weight because underdosing risks recurrent blockade; quantitative monitoring prevents visual guessing.

  • Population

    Pregnancy, lactation, or hormonal contraception

    Note

    Human pregnancy data are limited, and hormonal contraception requires seven days of additional nonhormonal protection after exposure.

Description

Sugammadex is a modified gamma-cyclodextrin that binds rocuronium and vecuronium in plasma. The dose is chosen from the measured depth of blockade and actual body weight, not from a vague statement that surgery is over. Labeled dosing is 2 mg/kg when the second train-of-four response has reappeared, 4 mg/kg at one to two post-tetanic counts with no train-of-four response, and 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal about 3 minutes after rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg. Because the product removes free aminosteroid relaxant rather than stimulating acetylcholine, it avoids the muscarinic mechanism of neostigmine but has distinct anaphylaxis, bradycardia, recurrence, renal, and contraception risks.

Adverse Effects

  • Pain, nausea, vomiting, headache, cough, dysgeusia, recurrence of blockade, marked bradycardia or cardiac arrest, and serious hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis can occur.

Administration Timing

  • Use quantitative neuromuscular monitoring and give 2 mg/kg at T2 reappearance or 4 mg/kg at one to two post-tetanic counts for rocuronium or vecuronium.

  • Use 16 mg/kg only for labeled immediate reversal approximately 3 minutes after rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg; immediate reversal after vecuronium has not been established.

  • Continue airway and neuromuscular monitoring after administration because recovery can recur or remain inadequate, especially after a dose below the measured depth.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Falling ventilation, a reduced train-of-four ratio, weakness, anaphylaxis, severe bradycardia, or arrest requires immediate airway support, resuscitation, and anesthesiology reassessment.

Evidence Limitations

  • Rapid pharmacologic reversal does not prove airway patency, adequate ventilation, or absence of residual anesthetic and opioid effects; clinical recovery still must be assessed.

  • Immediate reversal with 16 mg/kg has not been studied in pediatric patients.

Regulatory Status

FDA-approved U.S. prescription drug for reversal of rocuronium- and vecuronium-induced neuromuscular blockade; current product labeling was revised in 2026.
