Used To Treat

First-line IV therapy for many patients with candidemia or invasive candidiasis and selected salvage or combination roles in Aspergillus, with agent-specific indications and dosing.

Description

Echinocandins are IV lipopeptides that inhibit the Fks catalytic subunits of beta-(1,3)-D-glucan synthase. Glucan is a load-bearing fungal cell-wall polymer absent from mammalian cells, so its loss weakens the wall and causes osmotic instability. They are fungicidal against most Candida and inhibit active Aspergillus hyphal growth, but they do not reliably treat Cryptococcus, Mucorales, or endemic dimorphic fungi and are not primary monotherapy for invasive aspergillosis. Caspofungin uses a loading dose and has hepatic dose considerations; micafungin does not require renal adjustment and is used for candidemia and selected Candida prophylaxis; anidulafungin undergoes slow nonenzymatic degradation; rezafungin has a prolonged half-life enabling weekly dosing for its labeled indication.

Mechanism

Fks1/Fks2 sit in the fungal plasma membrane and extrude newly synthesized glucan into the wall. Echinocandin binding lowers glucan, exposing cells to osmotic rupture and compensatory chitin remodeling. Mutations in FKS hot-spot regions reduce drug binding and raise MICs; prior echinocandin exposure and species such as Candida glabrata make this concern more important. Candida parapsilosis naturally has higher echinocandin MICs, which does not automatically prove clinical failure but makes species and susceptibility meaningful. Because echinocandins are large, highly protein-bound molecules with poor urinary and CSF penetration, a bloodstream-active drug may still be the wrong choice for Candida cystitis, prostatitis, endophthalmitis, or meningitis.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious hypersensitivity and infusion reactions can occur. Hepatic laboratory abnormalities and species-specific resistance require review; echinocandins are not a universal answer to any positive Candida culture.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion-related histamine symptoms, rash, facial swelling, bronchospasm, phlebitis, fever, headache, nausea, and hepatic enzyme elevation can occur.

  • Breakthrough infection can arise through FKS mutation, inadequate source control, protected biofilm or device burden, sanctuary-site penetration, or a non-susceptible organism.

Contraindications

  • Do not use echinocandin monotherapy for cryptococcal meningitis or mucormycosis; the target or clinical activity is inadequate.

  • Do not assume renal failure requires dose reduction: these agents are not primarily renally cleared, but hepatic and product-specific factors still differ.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain cultures before therapy when feasible, identify species, repeat blood cultures until clearance, inspect/remove infected intravascular devices when appropriate, and evaluate persistent candidemia for metastatic foci.

  • Infuse through the recommended time and monitor for histamine-like reactions. Trend liver tests and clinical response; transition to oral fluconazole only when stability, culture clearance, and susceptibility criteria are met.

Interactions

  • Caspofungin exposure can fall with selected enzyme inducers and change with cyclosporine; tacrolimus levels can fall. Micafungin can raise selected CYP3A substrates such as sirolimus or nifedipine modestly.

  • Interaction burden is generally lower than with azoles, which can make echinocandins useful while a complex medication list is being stabilized.

Key Labs

  • Candida species and susceptibility when indicated, serial blood cultures, AST/ALT/bilirubin, CBC, renal function for host status, and focused imaging or eye evaluation when the syndrome requires it.

  • A cleared blood culture starts the clock for uncomplicated candidemia duration only after symptoms resolve and metastatic infection is excluded.

Nclex Traps

  • Echinocandins are cell-wall drugs. Humans lack beta-(1,3)-D-glucan, which explains their selective target.

  • They are strong candidemia drugs but poor urinary drugs; bloodstream efficacy does not guarantee urine exposure.

Source Note

Current U.S. caspofungin and micafungin labels and IDSA candidiasis guidance.
