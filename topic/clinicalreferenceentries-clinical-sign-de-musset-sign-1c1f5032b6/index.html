Definition

de Musset sign is an observed pulsation sign. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure. A positive finding means the head nods rhythmically with each heartbeat.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive de Musset sign means the head nods rhythmically with each heartbeat.

Why It Matters

The observed de Musset sign finding can signal severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure. Large stroke-volume and pulse-pressure swings transmit systolic acceleration to the head and neck. de Musset sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure

Assessment Method

Observe the seated patient's head at rest.

Positive Finding

The head nods rhythmically with each heartbeat.

Mechanism

Large stroke-volume and pulse-pressure swings transmit systolic acceleration to the head and neck.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed de Musset sign finding raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure because large stroke-volume and pulse-pressure swings transmit systolic acceleration to the head and neck. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; de Musset sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use de Musset sign to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure and the reviewed alternatives: essential tremor, parkinsonism, anxiety. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Observe the seated patient's head at rest. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating de Musset sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Absence is expected in many patients and head movement has many causes. Therefore, de Musset sign cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • essential tremor

  • parkinsonism

  • anxiety

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as essential tremor, parkinsonism, anxiety.

False Negative Situations

de Musset sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; uncommon and nonspecific. de Musset sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation with wide pulse pressure. Apply the reviewed limitation for de Musset sign: Absence is expected in many patients and head movement has many causes.
