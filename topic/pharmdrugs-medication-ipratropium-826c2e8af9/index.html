Used To Treat

Scheduled maintenance treatment of bronchospasm in COPD and, with a rapid beta2 agonist, adjunctive treatment of moderate or severe acute asthma exacerbations. Its slower onset makes it inappropriate as the only immediate rescue medicine.

Mechanism

Vagal nerves release acetylcholine onto M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle and glands. M3 couples to Gq, activates phospholipase C, forms IP3 and diacylglycerol, raises intracellular calcium, activates myosin light-chain kinase, and contracts the airway. Ipratropium competitively and reversibly blocks muscarinic receptors, preventing this calcium signal and reducing cholinergic bronchomotor tone. It also blocks prejunctional M2 autoreceptors, but the net inhaled effect is bronchodilation. Because it is permanently charged, systemic absorption and blood-brain penetration are limited. Aerosol reaching the eye can still inhibit iris and ciliary muscarinic receptors, dilate the pupil, narrow the drainage angle, and precipitate acute angle closure in a susceptible eye.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Immediate hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, paradoxical bronchospasm, acute narrow-angle glaucoma from ocular exposure, and urinary retention can be serious. Eye pain, halos, blurred vision, red eye, nausea, or headache after nebulization requires urgent evaluation. It is not a rapid standalone rescue drug for acute respiratory distress.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to ipratropium, atropine derivatives, or product components

  • Use caution with narrow-angle glaucoma, bladder-neck obstruction, prostatic hyperplasia, or substantial anticholinergic burden

Nursing Essentials

  • Use a mouthpiece when possible and keep nebulized mist out of the eyes. Assess eye pain and urinary symptoms explicitly.

  • In acute asthma, give with the ordered SABA and reassess air movement, work of breathing, oxygenation, and response; do not delay systemic therapy or escalation.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics add dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision, and cognitive burden.

  • Albuterol combination is intentional because receptor pathways differ; reconcile combination products to avoid duplicate dosing.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, spirometry or peak flow, breath sounds, oxygenation, inhaler technique, urinary output, and ocular symptoms

  • No routine serum level; assess renal function and medication burden when systemic effects appear

Nclex Traps

  • Ipratropium is an adjunct to albuterol in a serious asthma attack, not a replacement for it.

  • A quaternary anticholinergic has limited CNS entry but can still trigger glaucoma if sprayed into the eye.

Description

Ipratropium is an inhaled quaternary ammonium antimuscarinic that blocks vagally mediated bronchoconstriction. It competes with acetylcholine at airway muscarinic receptors, especially the M3 receptor that contracts bronchial smooth muscle and promotes secretion. In COPD, where cholinergic tone is an important reversible component, scheduled ipratropium improves airflow. During a significant asthma exacerbation, adding it to albuterol blocks a second constrictor pathway and can improve bronchodilation, but ipratropium starts more slowly and does not replace the SABA, systemic corticosteroid assessment, or escalation plan. Limited CNS penetration does not prevent local glaucoma or urinary-retention toxicity.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, cough, bitter taste, throat irritation, headache, dizziness, nausea, constipation, or tachycardia

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, hypersensitivity, acute angle-closure glaucoma, blurred vision, urinary retention, or worsening prostatic obstruction
