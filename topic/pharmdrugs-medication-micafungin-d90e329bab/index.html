Used To Treat

Candidemia, acute disseminated candidiasis, Candida peritonitis/abscess, esophageal candidiasis, and prophylaxis of Candida infection in hematopoietic stem-cell transplant recipients under product-specific age and dose labeling.

Mechanism

Micafungin binds the Fks glucan-synthase complex, reducing wall glucan and causing osmotic instability. Mammalian cells lack the target, explaining relatively selective toxicity. FKS mutation can reduce susceptibility, and Candida parapsilosis naturally has higher MICs than many other Candida species. High protein binding limits glomerular filtration, while molecular size limits urine and CNS penetration. Histamine release during infusion can cause rash, pruritus, facial swelling, or vasodilation, which is why infusion rate and observation matter.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to micafungin, another echinocandin, or components.

  • Do not select it solely from a positive urine Candida result because low urinary drug exposure may not treat true Candida UTI.

Nursing Essentials

  • Infuse over at least the label-specified time to reduce histamine reactions; never give as an IV push. Monitor line, skin, airway, blood pressure, and symptoms during administration.

  • Trend liver tests, CBC/hemolysis clues, renal function, potassium, serial cultures, species/susceptibility, and source control. No renal adjustment does not mean no renal monitoring in a critically ill patient.

Interactions

  • Micafungin can increase exposure to sirolimus, nifedipine, and itraconazole; monitor and reduce partner dose when toxicity appears under labeling.

  • Its interaction burden is generally lower than systemic azoles, which can be useful in transplant and oncology patients while medication plans are stabilized.

Key Labs

  • AST/ALT/alkaline phosphatase/bilirubin, CBC/hemoglobin, creatinine, potassium, serial blood cultures, species/susceptibility, and focused source/deep-infection evaluation.

  • Hemoglobin fall plus hemoglobinuria or hemolysis markers requires evaluation rather than being attributed automatically to critical illness.

Nclex Traps

  • Micafungin has no renal dose adjustment but is not a urinary antifungal.

  • A selective fungal-wall target lowers human toxicity; it does not eliminate hepatic, infusion, or hypersensitivity risk.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Anaphylaxis, serious skin reactions, hemolysis/hemoglobinuria, renal effects, and hepatic injury can occur. Evaluate significant persistent liver abnormalities and use the lowest effective exposure and duration for the indication.

Class Pathway

  • Fks glucan-synthase inhibition

  • Candida cell-wall failure

  • IV treatment and selected prophylaxis without renal dose adjustment

Description

Micafungin is an IV echinocandin that inhibits beta-(1,3)-D-glucan synthesis, weakening a fungal cell-wall scaffold absent from human cells. It is fungicidal against many Candida species and useful as initial candidemia therapy while species and susceptibility are pending. It has no meaningful urinary or CSF concentration and does not treat Cryptococcus or Mucorales. Micafungin is highly protein bound, metabolized mainly through non-CYP pathways, and does not require renal or dialysis adjustment. Liver tests still matter, and prolonged high exposures produced hepatic foci and tumors in rats; human relevance is uncertain, so the label advises weighing benefit and hepatic risk rather than calling the drug liver safe.

Adverse Effects

  • Fever, headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, phlebitis, rash, pruritus, hypokalemia, and liver-test elevation.

  • Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, hemolysis, hemoglobinuria, clinically significant hepatic injury, renal dysfunction, and resistant breakthrough infection.
