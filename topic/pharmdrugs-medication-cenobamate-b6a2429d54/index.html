Class

Voltage-gated sodium-current inhibitor and GABA-A positive allosteric modulator antiseizure drug

Used To Treat

Focal-onset seizures in adults, usually as carefully titrated adjunctive therapy for incompletely controlled epilepsy.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for cenobamate in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Cenobamate inhibits voltage-gated sodium currents, including persistent sodium current, and positively modulates GABA-A ion channels at a site distinct from benzodiazepines. The exact contribution of each action to clinical seizure control is not fully established.

Nursing Essentials

  • Follow the starter titration exactly; fever, lymphadenopathy, facial swelling, rash, eosinophilia, hepatitis, nephritis, or myocarditis may represent DRESS even before rash is obvious.

  • Monitor sedation, dizziness, gait/falls, suicidal thinking, liver injury, seizure change, QT-shortening drugs, and interactions that raise phenytoin/phenobarbital/clobazam metabolites or reduce oral-contraceptive exposure.

Key Labs

  • Liver tests before/when indicated, ECG if short-QT risk exists, antiseizure drug levels when interactions require, and pregnancy/contraception review.

Nclex Traps

  • Most rhythm warnings emphasize long QT; cenobamate is the high-yield antiseizure drug that shortens QT and is contraindicated in familial short-QT syndrome.

Description

Cenobamate suppresses focal seizures through two complementary actions: it reduces repetitive firing through voltage-gated sodium currents and strengthens inhibitory GABA-A channel activity. Its slow starter titration is a safety mechanism, because faster titration was linked to DRESS; it also shortens QT and has unusually important CYP2C19/CYP3A/CYP2B6 interactions.
