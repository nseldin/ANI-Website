Definition

Strawberry cervix is an observed cervical sign. It is a clue to Trichomonas vaginalis infection. A positive finding means punctate erythema and hemorrhage create a strawberry appearance.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Strawberry cervix means punctate erythema and hemorrhage create a strawberry appearance.

Why It Matters

The observed Strawberry cervix finding can signal Trichomonas vaginalis infection. Protozoal inflammation makes superficial cervical capillaries friable and punctately hemorrhagic. Strawberry cervix cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • Trichomonas vaginalis infection

Assessment Method

Inspect the cervix with speculum and adequate light.

Positive Finding

Punctate erythema and hemorrhage create a strawberry appearance.

Mechanism

Protozoal inflammation makes superficial cervical capillaries friable and punctately hemorrhagic.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Strawberry cervix finding raises concern for Trichomonas vaginalis infection because protozoal inflammation makes superficial cervical capillaries friable and punctately hemorrhagic. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Strawberry cervix does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Strawberry cervix to focus evaluation of Trichomonas vaginalis infection and the reviewed alternatives: gonococcal cervicitis, ectropion, recent intercourse. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the cervix with speculum and adequate light. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Strawberry cervix as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Usually absent to the naked eye and not specific. Therefore, Strawberry cervix cannot confirm or exclude Trichomonas vaginalis infection by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as gonococcal cervicitis, ectropion, recent intercourse.

False Negative Situations

Strawberry cervix may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of Trichomonas vaginalis infection.
