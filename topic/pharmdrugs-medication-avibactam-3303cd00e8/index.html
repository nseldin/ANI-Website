Class

Non-beta-lactam diazabicyclooctane inhibitor of selected class A, class C, and class D serine beta-lactamases

Used To Treat

Used with ceftazidime for labeled susceptible Gram-negative infections, including organisms producing selected ESBL, AmpC, KPC, and OXA-48-like enzymes; also used mechanistically with aztreonam in selected MBL-producing Enterobacterales strategies.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Avibactam does not inhibit metallo-beta-lactamases. Treating an NDM, VIM, or IMP producer with ceftazidime/avibactam alone can fail even when the word carbapenemase is the only result noticed.

Mechanism

Avibactam opens its diazabicyclooctane ring and covalently carbamylates the active-site serine of susceptible beta-lactamases. Unlike classical suicide inhibitors, it can recyclize and regenerate, permitting reversible repeated inhibition. Ceftazidime then reaches PBPs and inhibits wall synthesis. Resistance can still emerge through KPC or AmpC mutations, increased enzyme expression, altered porins, efflux, or PBP change. Coordinated renal adjustment matters because both ceftazidime and avibactam are cleared renally; underdosing either can create functional monotherapy and select resistance.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the enzyme or susceptibility profile and renal function, then administer the fixed combination over the specified infusion because exposure of both components determines success.

  • Repeat cultures for persistent bacteremia and escalate clinical nonresponse because new resistance, absent source control, or an unrecognized MBL can defeat the regimen.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to ceftazidime, avibactam, or related beta-lactams is a major contraindication under the product label.

  • Do not call avibactam an antibiotic or substitute it independently for the fixed ceftazidime combination.

Interactions

  • Probenecid and other OAT inhibitors may alter avibactam renal transport; use the complete product interaction guidance.

  • Nephrotoxic co-therapy can reduce clearance and raise ceftazidime neurotoxicity risk.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, carbapenemase gene or phenotype, creatinine/eGFR, CBC, liver tests, mental status, and serial cultures when clearance matters.

  • For suspected MBL infection, identify co-produced serine enzymes and obtain susceptibility for the actual aztreonam-plus-avibactam strategy rather than extrapolating.

Nclex Traps

  • Avibactam covers KPC and some OXA-48-like enzymes, not NDM/VIM/IMP.

  • Aztreonam supplies MBL stability; avibactam protects aztreonam from co-produced serine beta-lactamases.

Description

Avibactam is a non-beta-lactam diazabicyclooctane inhibitor that restores ceftazidime activity against many class A enzymes including KPC and ESBLs, class C AmpC enzymes, and some class D OXA-48-like enzymes. It does not inhibit class B metallo-beta-lactamases such as NDM, VIM, or IMP because those enzymes use zinc rather than a serine acyl intermediate. Avibactam alone does not kill bacteria. Its special role with aztreonam follows complementary chemistry: aztreonam resists MBL hydrolysis, while avibactam blocks co-produced serine enzymes that would otherwise destroy aztreonam.

Display Name

Avibactam

Adverse Effects

  • Adverse effects arise from the complete combination and include hypersensitivity, diarrhea/CDI, nausea, liver-test abnormalities, and ceftazidime-related neurologic toxicity when renal clearance falls.

  • Resistance can emerge during therapy, especially in high-burden or inadequately controlled infection, so clinical and microbiologic response must be trended.
