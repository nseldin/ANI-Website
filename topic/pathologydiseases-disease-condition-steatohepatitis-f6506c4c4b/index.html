Plain Meaning

Steatohepatitis is fatty liver disease with steatosis plus hepatocyte inflammation and ballooning injury, creating a scar-forming pathway toward fibrosis, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, and liver failure.

Pathology

Steatohepatitis is the injury phase of fatty liver disease: triglyceride fat is present in hepatocytes and the liver also shows inflammation, ballooning hepatocyte injury, and risk for scar formation. Simple steatosis is fat storage; steatohepatitis means the fat is associated with active liver-cell damage.

Pathophysiology

Insulin resistance, lipotoxic free fatty acids, mitochondrial stress, oxidative injury, gut-derived inflammatory signals, and cytokine activation injure hepatocytes. Stellate cells respond to repeated injury by laying down collagen, so steatohepatitis can progress to fibrosis, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, and hepatocellular carcinoma.

Etiology

Metabolic syndrome, obesity, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, alcohol-associated injury, rapid weight change, malnutrition/refeeding contexts, chronic viral or autoimmune overlap, and medications such as amiodarone, methotrexate, valproate, tamoxifen, glucocorticoids, and some antiretrovirals.

Signs Symptoms

  • Often silent; may have fatigue, vague right upper quadrant discomfort, hepatomegaly, or mild transaminase elevation

  • Advanced disease clues include thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, low albumin, prolonged INR/PT, rising bilirubin, ascites, varices, jaundice, encephalopathy, or sarcopenia

Diagnostics

  • ALT/AST can be normal or mildly elevated and do not reliably stage fibrosis

  • Assess metabolic risks: A1c/glucose, lipids, BMI/waist trend, alcohol history, medication/supplement review, hepatitis testing when indicated, and autoimmune/metabolic workup when the story does not fit

  • Ultrasound or MRI-PDFF can identify fat; elastography or fibrosis scores such as FIB-4 help estimate fibrosis risk; biopsy is selective but can confirm steatohepatitis and stage fibrosis

Treatments

  • Treat the driver: weight/metabolic management, diabetes and lipid optimization, alcohol cessation when relevant, medication review, nutrition plan, and exercise plan when safe

  • Escalate hepatology evaluation for significant fibrosis risk, decompensation signs, unclear cause, or worsening synthetic function

Nursing Priorities

  • Teach the distinction: steatosis is fat; steatohepatitis is fat plus inflammation and hepatocyte injury.

  • Do not reassure from AST/ALT alone; fibrosis risk can exist with modest enzymes.

  • Track platelets, INR/PT, albumin, bilirubin, glucose/A1c, lipids, weight trend, alcohol use, medication risks, and decompensation cues.

Complications

  • Fibrosis

  • Cirrhosis

  • Portal hypertension

  • Ascites

  • Variceal bleeding

  • Hepatic encephalopathy

  • Hepatocellular carcinoma

Patient Education

  • The key is controlling the injury driver, not just watching liver enzymes.

  • Report jaundice, increasing abdominal swelling, black stools, vomiting blood, confusion, severe fatigue, fever, or reduced urination.

Nclex Traps

  • Steatohepatitis is not simply a synonym for fatty liver; it adds inflammation and cell injury.

  • Normal or mild AST/ALT does not rule out fibrosis.

  • The nursing priority is risk modification plus surveillance for fibrosis/decompensation cues.
