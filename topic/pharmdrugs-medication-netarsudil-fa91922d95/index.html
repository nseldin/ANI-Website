Class

Ophthalmic Rho-associated-protein-kinase inhibitor trabecular outflow enhancer

Used To Treat

Once-daily reduction of elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Epithelial corneal edema, described as honeycomb or bullous change, can occur especially with pre-existing stromal edema or compromised endothelial function after ocular procedures. Conjunctival hyperemia, corneal verticillata, instillation pain and conjunctival hemorrhage are common. Contaminated multidose bottles can cause bacterial keratitis.

Mechanism

ROCK signaling maintains actomyosin contraction, focal adhesions and extracellular-matrix stiffness in conventional outflow tissue. Inhibition relaxes trabecular and Schlemm-canal cells, widens fluid pathways and reduces resistance; decreased episcleral venous pressure improves the downstream pressure gradient. Norepinephrine-transporter inhibition may contribute reduced aqueous production.

Nursing Essentials

  • Inspect for new corneal haze, honeycomb/bullous epithelial change or vision reduction, especially after corneal procedures or with existing edema. Explain that verticillata is a whorl-like deposit pattern and often does not reduce vision.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed contraindication beyond clinical hypersensitivity concerns, but clarify compromised corneal endothelium/stromal edema, recent corneal procedure and unexplained visual decline.

Interactions

  • Separate other topical eye drugs by at least 5 minutes. Combined netarsudil/latanoprost adds both drugs' ocular adverse profiles; do not duplicate the single agents unintentionally.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic nerve and visual fields

  • Slit-lamp/corneal examination when pain, haze or reduced vision develops

  • Conjunctival hyperemia and adherence assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Netarsudil acts mainly on trabecular/conventional outflow, unlike prostaglandins that emphasize uveoscleral flow.

  • Corneal verticillata is a whorl pattern; honeycomb epithelial edema with vision change is a different warning finding.

  • A red eye is common, but pain and vision loss still require evaluation.

Description

Netarsudil is a once-nightly Rho-kinase inhibitor that lowers pressure mainly by relaxing trabecular-meshwork and Schlemm-canal cytoskeleton, improving the conventional outflow route; it also lowers episcleral venous pressure and may reduce aqueous production. Conjunctival hyperemia is very common. Corneal verticillata and honeycomb/bullous epithelial corneal edema are distinctive recognition findings.

Adverse Effects

  • Conjunctival hyperemia, corneal verticillata, instillation pain, conjunctival hemorrhage, blurred/reduced vision, lacrimation, eyelid erythema and epithelial corneal edema.
