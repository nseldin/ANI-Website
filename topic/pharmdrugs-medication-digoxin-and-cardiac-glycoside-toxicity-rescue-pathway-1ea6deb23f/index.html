Used To Treat

Emergency decision support for acute or chronic digoxin toxicity and related foxglove, oleander, or other cardiac-glycoside poisoning.

Description

This pathway separates acute ingestion from chronic accumulation because potassium patterns, serum interpretation, and Fab calculations differ. Suspect toxicity with gastrointestinal symptoms, confusion, color or visual change, bradycardia, atrioventricular block, atrial tachycardia with block, ventricular dysrhythmia, shock, or hyperkalemia in a plausible exposure. Stop digoxin, obtain ECG and serial electrolytes, contact Poison Help, and give digoxin immune Fab for life-threatening features. Calculate from known burden when reliable. Otherwise use the current label and toxicologist-directed empiric dosing rather than extrapolating a post-Fab total level.

Mechanism

Cardiac glycosides inhibit Na/K-ATPase, raising intracellular sodium and indirectly calcium, while increasing vagal tone. Therapeutic inotropy becomes conduction block, triggered activity, and unstable automaticity when exposure is excessive. Acute pump inhibition releases potassium extracellularly, so hyperkalemia can signal severe poisoning; chronic patients may instead be potassium depleted from diuretics. Fab binds free glycoside and pulls it away from tissue, allowing pump recovery and rapid potassium entry into cells, which explains why potassium can fall after successful treatment.

Administration Timing

  • Immediately stop exposure, stabilize airway and circulation, obtain ECG and potassium, and call Poison Help; do not delay Fab for an unstable rhythm, shock, or severe toxic hyperkalemia.

  • Use known acute burden or chronic steady-state concentration to estimate Fab when reliable, and use label or AHA toxicologist-directed empiric dosing when unknown.

  • Continue prolonged monitoring after improvement, especially in renal failure or sustained plant exposure, because Fab complexes clear slowly and recurrence can occur.

Nursing Essentials

  • Distinguish acute ingestion, chronic accumulation, and non-digoxin plant or animal glycoside because serum assays and calculated dose behave differently.

  • Trend rhythm, perfusion, potassium, magnesium, renal function, and free digoxin when available because total digoxin after Fab is misleading.

  • Avoid reflexive cardioversion or calcium decisions without toxicology input because the glycoside-poisoned myocardium is highly irritable and evidence is context dependent.

Key Labs

  • Pre-Fab serum digoxin, serial potassium and magnesium, creatinine, glucose, blood gas, ECG, and post-Fab free or unbound digoxin when recurrence is suspected; plant glycosides may cross-react unpredictably.

Adverse Effects

  • Toxicity causes nausea, vomiting, confusion, visual disturbance, bradyarrhythmia, ventricular dysrhythmia, hyperkalemia, and shock; Fab can produce hypokalemia, allergy, or worsening heart failure.

Contraindications

  • Do not wait for a classic serum level or every symptom before treating a life-threatening presentation; therapeutic digoxin education without suspected toxicity should remain in medication guidance because Fab indications, concentration interpretation, and emergency escalation in this pathway apply only to poisoning.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Shock, high-grade block, ventricular dysrhythmia, severe hyperkalemia, recurrent toxicity, or failed initial Fab requires immediate toxicology, critical-care, and repeat-Fab assessment.

Interactions

  • Amiodarone, verapamil, macrolides, quinidine, and renal decline can increase digoxin exposure; loop or thiazide diuretics can increase sensitivity through hypokalemia.

  • Fab removes therapeutic effect, while renal failure delays complex clearance and can produce recurrence.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Older adults

    Note

    Renal decline, low lean body mass, interacting drugs, and baseline conduction disease make chronic toxicity possible at apparently modest serum concentrations.

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    Weight and ingested burden determine urgency; plant exposures may be uncertain and warrant immediate Poison Help calculation.

  • Population

    Renal failure

    Note

    Both digoxin elimination and Fab-complex clearance slow because renal clearance is reduced, so recurrent free glycoside and delayed rhythm or potassium changes require longer surveillance.

Evidence Limitations

  • Serum concentrations correlate imperfectly with clinical toxicity, especially when drawn before distribution equilibrium or after Fab, and plant glycosides are less predictable.

Nclex Traps

  • Acute toxicity often raises potassium; chronic toxicity can coexist with low potassium.

  • Total digoxin after Fab is not a redosing target.

  • A therapeutic medication search should remain in routine medication guidance because this pathway's Fab dosing and emergency escalation apply only when toxicity is suspected.

Regulatory Status

This is an educational emergency pathway anchored to FDA-approved DigiFab and AHA toxicology guidance; management of non-digoxin glycosides is specialist-guided and may be off-label.

Boxed Warning

CARDIAC-GLYCOSIDE EMERGENCY: unstable rhythm, shock, severe hyperkalemia, or a large exposure requires immediate 911, Poison Help, continuous ECG and hemodynamic monitoring, and frequent potassium checks. Give digoxin immune Fab promptly for life-threatening toxicity because binding free glycoside permits sodium-potassium pump recovery; potassium can then fall rapidly. Calculate Fab from a reliable pre-treatment burden or use the toxicologist-directed empiric protocol, and never redose from a routine total digoxin level after Fab because common assays also count bound, inactive drug.
