Used To Treat

Use this comparison to choose the correct mechanism and safety frame when a learner encounters hypertension, albuminuric CKD, HFrEF, post-MI care, ACE-inhibitor cough, angioedema history, or a proposed switch to sacubitril/valsartan.

Description

ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin-II formation and bradykinin breakdown. ARBs block the AT1 receptor while leaving ACE and bradykinin metabolism largely intact. ARNIs pair an ARB, valsartan, with neprilysin inhibition, which preserves natriuretic peptides but also raises bradykinin-related concern. All three lower AT1 signaling and therefore share hypotension, hyperkalemia, efferent-arteriole GFR effects, and fetal toxicity. ACE inhibitors stand out for dry cough and angioedema because ACE normally clears bradykinin. ARBs have much less cough and are often selected for cough intolerance, but rare angioedema still occurs. Sacubitril/valsartan is not simply a stronger ARB: sacubitril preserves vasoactive peptides while valsartan prevents neprilysin-related angiotensin-II accumulation from driving AT1. That dual mechanism improves HFrEF outcomes in appropriate patients but requires a 36-hour ACE-inhibitor washout and avoidance in a history of angioedema.

Mechanism

ACE inhibition lowers angiotensin II upstream and increases bradykinin. AT1 blockade prevents angiotensin II from activating vasoconstriction and aldosterone even when angiotensin II concentration rises. Neprilysin inhibition raises natriuretic peptides, bradykinin, adrenomedullin, and other vasoactive peptides; natriuretic-peptide receptor signaling increases cyclic GMP, promoting natriuresis, vasodilation, and antifibrotic effects. Neprilysin also degrades angiotensin II, so blocking neprilysin alone could raise a harmful counter-signal; valsartan is included to block AT1. Combining sacubitril with an ACE inhibitor would impair bradykinin breakdown through two pathways, explaining the angioedema danger and washout. Aliskiren is a fourth route: it blocks renin itself and lowers downstream angiotensin I, angiotensin II, and aldosterone, but it has not replaced outcome-proven ACE inhibitor, ARB, or ARNI roles.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNIs, and aliskiren all directly disrupt fetal RAAS signaling. The comparison is about target and indication, not a pregnancy-safe option.

Adverse Effects

  • Shared: hypotension, dizziness, hyperkalemia, creatinine change, and AKI in RAAS-dependent perfusion. ACE inhibitor: cough and more bradykinin angioedema. ARNI: hypotension and angioedema concern plus combination-specific interactions.

  • Severe airway swelling, renal failure, dangerous potassium elevation, and fetal injury require rapid recognition.

Contraindications

  • Do not combine ACE inhibitor with ARNI or start one within 36 hours of the other. Avoid ARNI and ACE inhibitors in any relevant angioedema history according to guideline and labeling.

  • Do not stack ACE inhibitor plus ARB or add aliskiren in diabetes. Pregnancy is a contraindication to direct RAAS blockade.

Nursing Essentials

  • When switching, identify the previous drug and last dose. The 36-hour rule applies specifically between ACE inhibitor and sacubitril/valsartan because of overlapping peptide metabolism; it is not an arbitrary scheduling preference.

  • Trend pressure, potassium, creatinine, volume, cough, swelling, and clinical outcome. Check whether the card's indication is evidence-based for that exact drug and condition.

Interactions

  • Separate ACE inhibitor and ARNI by 36 hours. Sacubitril/valsartan already supplies an ARB, so a separate ARB is duplicate therapy.

  • NSAID plus diuretic plus any RAAS blocker magnifies AKI physiology; potassium-raising drugs and lithium require deliberate review.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure/orthostasis, potassium, creatinine/eGFR, volume status, urine albumin when relevant, and BNP versus NT-proBNP context during neprilysin inhibition.

  • BNP can rise because neprilysin helps degrade it, whereas NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate and more directly reflects changing wall stress during ARNI therapy.

Nclex Traps

  • ACE inhibitor cough may justify an ARB; ACE-inhibitor angioedema is a different and more dangerous history and does not make ARNI an automatic substitute.

  • Entresto is sacubitril plus valsartan. Adding losartan or valsartan does not create better pathway coverage; it duplicates ARB exposure.
