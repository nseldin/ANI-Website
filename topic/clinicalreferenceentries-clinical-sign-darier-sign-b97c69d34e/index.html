Definition

Darier sign is a bedside lesion-rubbing sign. It is a clue to cutaneous mast-cell degranulation. A positive finding means the lesion becomes raised, erythematous, pruritic, or wheal-like.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Darier sign means the lesion becomes raised, erythematous, pruritic, or wheal-like.

Why It Matters

The observed Darier sign finding can signal cutaneous mast-cell degranulation. Mechanical stimulation triggers mast cells in the lesion to release histamine and other mediators. Darier sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cutaneous mast-cell degranulation

Assessment Method

Gently stroke a suspected mastocytosis lesion once and observe for several minutes.

Positive Finding

The lesion becomes raised, erythematous, pruritic, or wheal-like.

Mechanism

Mechanical stimulation triggers mast cells in the lesion to release histamine and other mediators.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Darier sign finding raises concern for cutaneous mast-cell degranulation because mechanical stimulation triggers mast cells in the lesion to release histamine and other mediators. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Darier sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Darier sign to focus evaluation of cutaneous mast-cell degranulation and the reviewed alternatives: dermatographism, urticaria, insect bite. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently stroke a suspected mastocytosis lesion once and observe for several minutes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Darier sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Antihistamines, age, and lesion type can blunt response; dermatographism can mimic it. Therefore, Darier sign cannot confirm or exclude cutaneous mast-cell degranulation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • dermatographism

  • urticaria

  • insect bite

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as dermatographism, urticaria, insect bite.

False Negative Situations

Darier sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of cutaneous mast-cell degranulation.

Modern Status

Current characteristic sign; systemic symptoms are a safety concern. Interpret Darier sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Antihistamines, age, and lesion type can blunt response; dermatographism can mimic it.

Safety Precautions

Observe Darier sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Gently stroke a suspected mastocytosis lesion once and observe for several minutes. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not rub extensively because mediator release can provoke flushing, hypotension, or anaphylaxis.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Darier sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The lesion becomes raised, erythematous, pruritic, or wheal-like. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Gently stroke a suspected mastocytosis lesion once and observe for several minutes. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, cutaneous mast-cell degranulation, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Darier sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not rub extensively because mediator release can provoke flushing, hypotension, or anaphylaxis. Base escalation for Darier sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
