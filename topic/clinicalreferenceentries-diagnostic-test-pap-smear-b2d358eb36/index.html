Summary

Pap smear is a high-yield Cancer Screening diagnostic reference. Cervical cytology screen for precancer/cancer changes.

Quick Answer

Pap smear: Cervical cytology screen for precancer/cancer changes. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Normal / negative cytology

    Meaning

    No precancerous or malignant cervical cells are reported in the sample.

  • Label

    Abnormal cytology

    Meaning

    ASC-US, LSIL, HSIL, AGC, or malignant cells require risk-based follow-up such as HPV testing, repeat cytology, colposcopy, or biopsy.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Pap results screen cells; colposcopy/biopsy confirms tissue diagnosis when needed.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Cervical cytology screen for precancer/cancer changes.

    • Before / preparation

    • Confirm the purpose of screening, age/risk eligibility, prep requirements, and whether abnormal results require diagnostic confirmation.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Teach that screening estimates risk; it does not always diagnose. Promote follow-up and reduce fear with clear next steps.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Positive screening with symptoms, high-risk history, severe abnormality, or failure to follow up on a positive result.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Do not treat a screen as a final diagnosis unless the test is designed and interpreted that way.
