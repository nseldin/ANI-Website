Definition

Microbiota means the microorganisms that occupy a particular environment, such as the skin, mouth, vagina, or colon. The term identifies the living community itself. It is related to, but narrower than, microbiome, which may also include the community's genes, functions, products, and environmental context.

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    A microbial community forms through repeated exposure, competition for nutrients and attachment sites, local oxygen and pH conditions, host secretions, immune pressure, diet, medications, age, and illness. Community members can exclude incoming pathogens, transform nutrients, and signal to host tissues; they can also cause opportunistic infection when barriers fail or organisms reach a normally sterile site.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Examples include skin microbiota adapted to dry or oily sites, oral communities organized in dental biofilms, and dense intestinal communities that metabolize compounds the host cannot process alone. The organisms and proportions differ by body site, so one sample cannot represent the entire person.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Understanding microbiota prevents two common errors: treating every detected organism as infection and assuming every usual resident is harmless in every location. Culture or molecular detection from a nonsterile site must be interpreted with symptoms, inflammation, specimen quality, and the anatomic source. A usual colonizer in blood, cerebrospinal fluid, or deep tissue may be clinically important.

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    There is no single universal 'normal' list. Communities vary among healthy people and over time, and many sequencing studies detect DNA without proving that organisms are alive or causing disease. Microbiota findings should therefore support clinical reasoning rather than replace it.
