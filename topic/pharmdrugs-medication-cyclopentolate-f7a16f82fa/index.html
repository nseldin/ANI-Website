Class

Intermediate-duration ophthalmic antimuscarinic cycloplegic and mydriatic

Used To Treat

Cycloplegic refraction and pupil dilation for ophthalmic examination or selected procedures, especially when stronger accommodation paralysis than tropicamide is needed.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Systemic anticholinergic CNS toxicity can be severe in children. Use the lowest effective concentration, apply nasolacrimal pressure for 2 to 3 minutes and monitor behavior, temperature, pulse and breathing when risk is high. It can precipitate acute angle closure in a narrow anterior chamber.

Mechanism

Competitive blockade of muscarinic M3 signaling prevents iris-sphincter contraction and ciliary-muscle accommodation. The radial dilator becomes unopposed, the pupil enlarges and the lens can no longer increase curvature for near focus. Nasolacrimal absorption creates a peripheral and central anticholinergic exposure; punctal pressure reduces it.

Nursing Essentials

  • Warn that near vision and light sensitivity may remain impaired beyond the exam. In children, use the prescribed concentration only, wipe excess immediately and observe for unusual agitation, sleepiness, fever, ataxia or breathing change.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity and known/suspected angle-closure glaucoma or narrow angle unless the ophthalmic plan specifically addresses it. Clarify Down syndrome, spastic paralysis, brain injury and anticholinergic sensitivity because exaggerated responses have been reported.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics add delirium, tachycardia, urinary retention and heat risk. Pilocarpine opposes the ocular effect. Adrenergic mydriatics can add pupil dilation and systemic cardiovascular effects.

Key Labs

  • Pupil size/reactivity and accommodative recovery

  • Intraocular pressure/angle context

  • Pulse, temperature, mental and respiratory status if systemic exposure is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • Cyclopentolate causes both mydriasis and cycloplegia.

  • A restless or hallucinating child after dilation may have systemic anticholinergic toxicity.

  • Punctal pressure is a dose-limiting safety step in children.

Description

Cyclopentolate is an ophthalmic antimuscarinic that blocks the iris sphincter and ciliary muscle, producing mydriasis plus cycloplegia. By preventing accommodation, it reveals the eye's true refractive error, which is especially useful in children who can otherwise focus through hyperopia. Systemic absorption can cause flushing, fever, tachycardia, agitation, hallucinations, ataxia or cardiorespiratory collapse, with infants and children at greatest risk.

Adverse Effects

  • Stinging, photophobia, blurred near vision, prolonged cycloplegia, increased IOP/acute angle closure, dry mouth, flushing, fever, tachycardia, urinary retention, agitation, confusion, hallucinations, ataxia and rare cardiorespiratory collapse.
