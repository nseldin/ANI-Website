Summary

Ova & parasites (ova and parasites) is a high-yield GI diagnostic reference. Examines stool for parasites/ova when exposure, travel, or persistent diarrhea suggests parasitic infection.

Quick Answer

Ova & parasites (ova and parasites): Examines stool for parasites/ova when exposure, travel, or persistent diarrhea suggests parasitic infection. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Negative

    Meaning

    No ova or parasites are seen in the submitted specimen; intermittent shedding can require repeat specimens.

  • Label

    Positive

    Meaning

    Parasite, ova, cysts, or trophozoites are identified and therapy depends on the organism.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Travel, exposure, eosinophilia, persistent diarrhea, weight loss, or immunosuppression changes urgency.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Examines stool for parasites/ova when exposure, travel, or persistent diarrhea suggests parasitic infection.

    • Before / preparation

    • Collect the correct specimen from the correct source, use sterile technique when required, label site/time, and obtain cultures before antimicrobials when it will not dangerously delay treatment.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Prevent contamination, send the specimen promptly, and connect results to isolation, antibiotic stewardship, and clinical deterioration.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Positive blood or CSF culture, sepsis cues, worsening fever, hypotension, mental status change, or a contaminated specimen that could mislead therapy.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • Do not delay life-saving antibiotics too long for a perfect specimen in an unstable client.
