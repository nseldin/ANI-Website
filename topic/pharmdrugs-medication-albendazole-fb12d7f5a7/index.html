Used To Treat

Neurocysticercosis and hydatid disease under the U.S. label, with guideline-supported treatment of many intestinal or tissue helminths. Organism, tissue location, life-cycle stage, meal fat content, treatment duration, and need for corticosteroids or antiepileptics determine how it is used.

Mechanism

Albendazole binds selectively to the colchicine-sensitive site of parasite beta-tubulin and blocks alpha-beta tubulin polymerization. Microtubules support nutrient uptake, vesicle movement, mitotic spindle formation, and intestinal-cell integrity. As they disappear, glucose transport falls, glycogen is depleted, fumarate-reductase and ATP-generating pathways fail, and the parasite loses motility and dies. Hepatic oxidation forms albendazole sulfoxide, the main circulating active metabolite; further oxidation produces inactive sulfone. A fatty meal greatly increases systemic absorption and is useful for tissue infection, whereas low systemic exposure may be intentionally adequate for some luminal infections. Antigen release from dying cysticerci activates host cytokines and edema, explaining the need for corticosteroids and seizure planning.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to benzimidazoles. Avoid pregnancy during treatment and for the label-specified interval afterward.

  • Ocular cysticercosis, uncontrolled intracranial pressure, and lesion-specific risks require specialist evaluation before killing parasites.

Nursing Essentials

  • For neurocysticercosis, verify brain and eye imaging, corticosteroid timing, antiepileptic plan, lesion count/location, and inpatient monitoring needs before the first dose.

  • For prolonged courses, trend CBC and liver tests at least at the protocol interval. Stop and evaluate fever, sore throat, bruising, jaundice, or marked transaminase rise.

Interactions

  • Dexamethasone, praziquantel, and cimetidine can raise albendazole-sulfoxide concentrations; enzyme inducers can lower exposure.

  • The interaction may be deliberate in neurocysticercosis but still changes toxicity and requires protocol-based interpretation.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, liver tests, pregnancy test, renal function, and drug-specific levels only in specialist contexts

  • Brain/eye imaging, seizure control, intracranial-pressure signs, stool/serology or cyst response, adherence, meal composition, and inflammatory worsening

Nclex Traps

  • Neurologic worsening after albendazole can reflect inflammatory killing of CNS cysts, not treatment failure.

  • Fat increases systemic albendazole-sulfoxide exposure, which is desirable for tissue disease but changes toxicity.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Bone-marrow suppression, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anemia, and hepatotoxicity can occur, especially during prolonged high-dose or repeated cycles and in liver disease. Monitor CBC and liver tests before and during systemic treatment. Before treating suspected neurocysticercosis, evaluate for ocular cysts and intracranial lesion burden because parasite death can provoke vision-threatening inflammation, cerebral edema, raised intracranial pressure, or seizures. The drug can cause fetal harm.

Description

Albendazole is a benzimidazole anthelmintic converted in the liver to active albendazole sulfoxide, which reaches blood, cerebrospinal fluid, and tissue cysts. It binds helminth beta-tubulin, prevents microtubule polymerization, and disrupts glucose uptake, secretory transport, cell division, and ATP production until susceptible worms immobilize and die. Systemic exposure distinguishes it from the more lumen-focused mebendazole and makes it useful for neurocysticercosis and echinococcal hydatid disease. Killing tissue larvae can abruptly expose parasite antigens, so inflammation may worsen seizures, cerebral edema, ocular damage, or mass effect even while treatment is working. Imaging and anti-inflammatory planning are therefore part of safe pharmacology.

Adverse Effects

  • Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, headache, dizziness, reversible alopecia, fever, and elevated transaminases

  • Leukopenia/pancytopenia, severe liver injury, hypersensitivity, acute kidney injury, intracranial hypertension or seizures from inflammatory cyst death
