Class

Peripheral quaternary muscarinic antagonist antisecretory and vagolytic

Used To Treat

Reduction of salivary/airway secretions and vagal reflexes during anesthesia, protection from muscarinic effects when neostigmine or pyridostigmine reverses nondepolarizing blockade, and formulation-specific treatment of sialorrhea, COPD or other indications.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Tachycardia, acute angle closure, heat illness from impaired sweating, urinary retention, ileus and thickened respiratory secretions can be serious. Pairing with a cholinesterase inhibitor must use the correct ratio and timing; glycopyrrolate treats muscarinic effects but does not restore neuromuscular transmission.

Mechanism

Competitive antagonism at M1-M5 muscarinic receptors reduces glandular secretion, slows GI/bladder smooth-muscle activity, inhibits vagal M2 effects on the heart and causes mydriatic/ocular effects if exposure reaches the eye. The permanently charged quaternary ammonium structure limits passive CNS diffusion. It does not block nicotinic receptors, so it cannot reverse paralysis by itself.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify indication and formulation. During reversal, administer with the ordered neostigmine or pyridostigmine ratio and continue train-of-four, airway, heart-rate and secretion monitoring.

  • Assess urinary output/retention, bowel sounds/distention, glaucoma symptoms, temperature and ability to clear thick secretions. Provide oral care and avoid overheating.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity; product/indication-specific contraindications include glaucoma, obstructive uropathy, GI obstruction or paralytic ileus, unstable acute hemorrhage, severe ulcerative colitis/toxic megacolon and myasthenia gravis unless used specifically to control muscarinic effects during reversal.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, antihistamines, tricyclics, antipsychotics and antiparkinsonian agents add urinary retention, ileus, tachycardia, blurred vision and heat risk. Potassium chloride solid oral dosage forms can worsen GI injury when motility is reduced.

Key Labs

  • Heart rate/rhythm, blood pressure, respiratory secretion/airway assessment

  • Urine output and bladder/bowel assessment

  • Train-of-four and ventilatory recovery when paired with reversal therapy

Nclex Traps

  • Glycopyrrolate blocks muscarinic side effects; it does not reverse nicotinic skeletal paralysis.

  • Limited CNS penetration does not mean no anticholinergic toxicity.

  • Drying secretions can make mucus harder to clear.

Description

Glycopyrrolate is a quaternary antimuscarinic that blocks acetylcholine at peripheral muscarinic receptors with little blood-brain-barrier penetration. In anesthesia it dries secretions, counters vagal bradycardia and is paired with neostigmine so increased acetylcholine can reverse skeletal blockade without causing dangerous bradycardia, bronchial secretions and bowel activity. Its limited CNS entry distinguishes it from atropine, but peripheral anticholinergic toxicity remains substantial.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, thick secretions, blurred vision/mydriasis, tachycardia, urinary retention, constipation/ileus, reduced sweating and heat intolerance, confusion rarely, and injection-site reactions.
