Quick Answer

SAMPLE captures decision-critical background efficiently, but it is not a replacement for the primary survey, medication reconciliation, or a complete health history.

Sections

  • Label

    Order and flexibility

    Text

    The letters provide a recall checklist, not a reason to interrogate a critically ill patient in a rigid order. Ask the highest-impact question first and use family, records, medical identification, and emergency systems when the patient cannot respond.

  • Label

    Medication and allergy precision

    Text

    Clarify drug, dose, route, schedule, last dose, indication, adherence, over-the-counter products, substances, and the actual reaction to an allergy. A label without a reaction can cause both undertreatment and unsafe exposure.

  • Label

    Last oral intake

    Text

    Record what and when the patient last ate or drank because aspiration risk, anesthesia, procedures, glucose, and poisoning decisions may depend on it. It does not mean only the last oral medication.

  • Label

    Reassessment

    Text

    History can change as the patient stabilizes or collateral information arrives. Update the record and communicate new high-risk information.

Source Note

Original ANI synthesis uses the conventional SAMPLE framework and explicitly corrects the common last-oral-intake ambiguity.

Summary

SAMPLE is a focused history used after immediate threats are addressed: signs and symptoms, allergies, medications, pertinent past history, last oral intake, and events leading to the illness or injury.
