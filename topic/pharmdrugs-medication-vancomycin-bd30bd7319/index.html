Used To Treat

Susceptible infections based on organism, infection site, cultures, allergies, renal function, and local resistance patterns.

Mechanism

Vancomycin works by binding D-Ala-D-Ala cell-wall precursors so gram-positive bacteria cannot polymerize and cross-link peptidoglycan properly. IV therapy targets serious gram-positive infections such as MRSA; oral vancomycin stays in the gut for C. difficile. Infusion reaction, kidney injury, levels/AUC, and culture fit matter.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning commonly emphasized for IV use; nephrotoxicity, infusion reactions, and ototoxicity risk are major safety concerns.

Contraindications

  • Severe hypersensitivity.

  • Renal impairment is not an absolute contraindication but requires dosing/level monitoring.

  • Clarify serious allergy or prior severe reaction before giving.

  • Clarify pregnancy, lactation, renal/hepatic impairment, unstable vital signs, and high-risk interactions when clinically relevant.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor renal function and ordered levels.

  • Infuse slowly to reduce infusion reaction risk.

  • Assess for hearing changes and kidney injury.

  • Verify the exact medication, dose, route, allergies, indication, baseline assessment, and hold parameters.

  • Trend the client's response rather than treating one isolated number.

  • Question orders when the medication effect could worsen the client's current priority problem.

  • Subclass specificity: Vancomycin is categorized here as Glycopeptide cell-wall inhibitor antibiotic. Pathway: Anti-infective medication > Cell-wall inhibitor > Glycopeptide antibiotic. This subtype shapes the expected mechanism, contraindications, boxed/high-alert warnings, monitoring, interactions, and patient teaching.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, amphotericin B, NSAIDs may increase nephrotoxicity/ototoxicity risk.

  • Review duplicate therapy, additive sedation, additive bleeding risk, QT-prolonging combinations, renal/hepatic clearance, and CYP/P-gp interactions when relevant.

Nclex Traps

  • Flushing/hypotension during infusion suggests infusion reaction; slow/stop and follow protocol.

  • Do not delay urgent antibiotics for sepsis, but obtain cultures first if ordered and it does not delay care.

Description

Vancomycin is a glycopeptide cell-wall inhibitor antibiotic: it works by binding D-Ala-D-Ala cell-wall precursors so gram-positive bacteria cannot polymerize and cross-link peptidoglycan properly. It is used for serious gram-positive infections including MRSA; oral vancomycin is used for C. difficile because it stays in the gut lumen.
