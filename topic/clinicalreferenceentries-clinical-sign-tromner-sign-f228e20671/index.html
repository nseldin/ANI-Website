Definition

Trömner sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability. A positive finding means the thumb and index finger flex.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Trömner sign means the thumb and index finger flex.

Why It Matters

A positive Trömner sign response can signal cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability. Finger-flexor afferents trigger an exaggerated reflex when cervical descending inhibition is impaired. Trömner sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability

Assessment Method

Tap the volar surface of the distal middle finger upward.

Positive Finding

The thumb and index finger flex.

Mechanism

Finger-flexor afferents trigger an exaggerated reflex when cervical descending inhibition is impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Trömner sign response raises concern for cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability because finger-flexor afferents trigger an exaggerated reflex when cervical descending inhibition is impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Trömner sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Trömner sign to focus evaluation of cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability and the reviewed alternatives: brisk normal reflexes, anxiety, hyperthyroidism. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Tap the volar surface of the distal middle finger upward. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Trömner sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Normal prevalence varies and no single cutoff reliably diagnoses myelopathy. Therefore, Trömner sign cannot confirm or exclude cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • brisk normal reflexes

  • anxiety

  • hyperthyroidism

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from brisk normal reflexes, anxiety, hyperthyroidism, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Trömner sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; interpretation parallels Hoffmann sign. When performed as described, Trömner sign can adjust concern for cervical corticospinal tract hyperexcitability, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Trömner sign: Normal prevalence varies and no single cutoff reliably diagnoses myelopathy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Trömner sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Tap the volar surface of the distal middle finger upward. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Compare sides and the rest of the reflex examination rather than labeling any finger movement positive.
