Class

Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody against the shared p40 subunit of interleukin-12 and interleukin-23

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, active psoriatic arthritis, and moderate-to-severe Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis in label-defined patients. In inflammatory bowel disease the induction dose is intravenous and maintenance is subcutaneous, whereas psoriasis and psoriatic-arthritis regimens are subcutaneous; the route sequence is therefore part of the indication, not a cosmetic product difference.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious bacterial, fungal, and mycobacterial infections can occur; evaluate for tuberculosis before treatment, do not start during a clinically important active infection, and avoid live vaccines. Malignancies have occurred because IL-12/23 participate in tumor immune surveillance. Stop and evaluate anaphylaxis or serious hypersensitivity. Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome, noninfectious pneumonia, organizing pneumonia, and eosinophilic pneumonia are rare but important explanations for new neurologic or respiratory symptoms.

Mechanism

IL-12 is a p35/p40 heterodimer that binds IL-12R beta1/beta2 and activates TYK2/JAK2-STAT4, driving Th1 differentiation and interferon-gamma production. IL-23 is a p19/p40 heterodimer that binds IL-23R/IL-12R beta1 and activates JAK2/TYK2-STAT3, supporting Th17-cell survival and secretion of IL-17, IL-22, and other barrier-inflammatory mediators. Ustekinumab binds soluble p40 before either cytokine can engage IL-12R beta1; it does not bind receptor-bound cytokine or directly deplete lymphocytes. Blocking the shared subunit reduces both Th1 and IL-23-dependent Th17 signaling. Because it leaves many other immune pathways intact, the effect is selective, but TB and serious infection screening still matter. Its long IgG half-life explains widely spaced maintenance doses and why adverse immune effects do not disappear immediately after the last injection.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen TB and vaccination status before therapy; teach patients to report infection, persistent cough, confusion, seizures, visual change, severe headache, or allergic symptoms promptly.

  • Verify indication-specific route and schedule: IV induction followed by subcutaneous maintenance for bowel disease versus subcutaneous regimens for psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis.

Contraindications

  • Clinically significant hypersensitivity to ustekinumab or excipients; defer treatment during an important active infection.

  • Clarify TB exposure, recurrent infection, malignancy and skin-cancer history, unexplained neurologic symptoms, chronic cough or dyspnea, live vaccines, and concomitant immunosuppression.

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines and generally avoid combining with another biologic or JAK inhibitor because additive immunosuppression has not been established as safe.

  • Improvement of intense inflammation can restore cytokine-suppressed CYP activity; review narrow-therapeutic-index drugs even though ustekinumab does not directly inhibit or induce CYP enzymes.

Key Labs

  • TB and infection evaluation, vaccination review, CBC and liver context when clinically indicated, and objective skin/joint/bowel response

  • Skin-cancer surveillance, neurologic assessment for possible PRES, and respiratory evaluation for noninfectious pneumonia

Nclex Traps

  • Ustekinumab blocks shared p40, so it affects both IL-12 and IL-23; it is not a selective IL-23 p19 inhibitor.

  • The bowel-disease regimen begins IV and continues subcutaneously; confusing it with an all-subcutaneous psoriasis schedule can produce a major dosing error.

Description

Ustekinumab is a targeted biologic that binds p40, a protein subunit shared by the cytokines IL-12 and IL-23. IL-12 promotes Th1 and natural-killer-cell interferon-gamma responses; IL-23 stabilizes and expands pathogenic Th17-lineage responses that sustain psoriasis, enthesitis, arthritis, and bowel inflammation. One antibody therefore dampens two related immune circuits upstream of their effector cytokines. The p40 target distinguishes ustekinumab from guselkumab, which blocks only IL-23 p19, and from secukinumab, which neutralizes downstream IL-17A. That distinction helps explain why ustekinumab treats inflammatory bowel disease while direct IL-17 blockade may worsen it. It is not a broad cytotoxic drug, but suppressing these circuits still reduces host defense and immune surveillance.

Adverse Effects

  • Nasopharyngitis, upper-respiratory infection, headache, fatigue, injection-site erythema, abdominal pain, nausea, fever, and bronchitis

  • Serious infection or TB, anaphylaxis, malignancy including nonmelanoma skin cancer, PRES, and noninfectious or eosinophilic pneumonia
