Definition

Matles sign is an observed Achilles-integrity sign. It is a clue to Achilles tendon rupture. A positive finding means the affected foot rests more dorsiflexed.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Matles sign means the affected foot rests more dorsiflexed.

Why It Matters

The observed Matles sign finding can signal Achilles tendon rupture. Loss of Achilles tension removes passive plantarflexion from the gastrocnemius-soleus unit. Matles sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • Achilles tendon rupture

Assessment Method

With the patient prone and knees flexed to 90 degrees, compare resting ankle position.

Positive Finding

The affected foot rests more dorsiflexed.

Mechanism

Loss of Achilles tension removes passive plantarflexion from the gastrocnemius-soleus unit.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Matles sign finding raises concern for Achilles tendon rupture because loss of Achilles tension removes passive plantarflexion from the gastrocnemius-soleus unit. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Matles sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Matles sign to focus evaluation of Achilles tendon rupture and the reviewed alternatives: normal asymmetry, calf atrophy, prior surgery. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the patient prone and knees flexed to 90 degrees, compare resting ankle position. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Matles sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Bilateral injury and ankle stiffness limit comparison. Therefore, Matles sign cannot confirm or exclude Achilles tendon rupture by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal asymmetry

  • calf atrophy

  • prior surgery

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal asymmetry, calf atrophy, prior surgery.

False Negative Situations

Matles sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of Achilles tendon rupture.

Modern Status

Current adjunct. When performed as described, Matles sign can adjust concern for Achilles tendon rupture, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Matles sign: Bilateral injury and ankle stiffness limit comparison.

Safety Precautions

Observe Matles sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: With the patient prone and knees flexed to 90 degrees, compare resting ankle position. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Support the legs and compare resting angle without forcing movement.
