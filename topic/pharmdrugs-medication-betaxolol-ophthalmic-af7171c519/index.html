Relatively beta-1-selective ophthalmic adrenergic blocker aqueous suppressant

Used To Treat

Elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension when an ophthalmic beta blocker is appropriate.

Description

Betaxolol ophthalmic reduces aqueous production through relatively beta-1-selective blockade. Compared with timolol, less beta-2 bronchial blockade can make it a considered option when pulmonary risk matters, but selectivity is incomplete and dose dependent. Systemic bradycardia, heart block, hypotension and bronchospasm remain possible, so it is not a free pass for asthma or severe COPD.

Mechanism

Beta blockade on ciliary epithelium reduces cAMP-driven aqueous secretion. Betaxolol prefers beta-1 over beta-2 receptors, limiting but not eliminating bronchial beta-2 antagonism. Nasolacrimal absorption can still deliver clinically active drug systemically; punctal occlusion reduces exposure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Topical betaxolol can still cause systemic beta-blocker effects. Avoid in sinus bradycardia, advanced AV block, cardiogenic shock or overt heart failure, and use great caution with reactive airway disease despite relative beta-1 selectivity.

Adverse Effects

  • Ocular stinging/tearing, blurred vision, bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, heart block, heart-failure worsening and possible bronchospasm.

Contraindications

  • Sinus bradycardia, second- or third-degree AV block without pacing, cardiogenic shock, overt cardiac failure and hypersensitivity. Clarify asthma/COPD and concurrent AV-node-slowing therapy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check pulse, rhythm, blood pressure and pulmonary history. Explain that beta-1 selectivity lowers but does not remove beta-2 bronchospasm risk.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Interactions

  • Oral beta blockers and other AV-node-slowing agents add bradycardia/hypotension. Drugs that inhibit metabolism can increase systemic exposure. Beta blockade can mask hypoglycemia adrenergic symptoms.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic nerve and visual fields

  • Pulse, blood pressure, ECG or respiratory assessment when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Beta-1 selective does not mean lung risk is zero.

  • The ophthalmic route still needs pulse and respiratory screening.

  • Punctal occlusion remains useful even with a selective agent.
