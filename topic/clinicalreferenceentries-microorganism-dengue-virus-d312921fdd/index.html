Definition

Dengue virus is a clinically important virus associated with dengue fever, dengue with warning signs, and severe dengue and shock. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

Envelope status helps explain environmental stability and susceptibility to routine controls, while genome type shapes replication and diagnostic targets. Capsid symmetry is left explicitly unknown in this bounded record rather than inferred without a reviewed taxonomic source.

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Mosquito-bite prevention, vector control, and vaccination only under current jurisdiction- and patient-specific guidance reduce dengue risk. The reviewed route description is arthropod borne transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 4 to 10 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Dengue infects immune and endothelial-associated cells; a dysregulated host response and capillary leak can cause hemoconcentration, bleeding, and shock. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Dengue virus is associated with dengue fever, dengue with warning signs, and severe dengue and shock. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
