Definition

Opening snap is an auscultatory cardiac sign. It is a clue to mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis. A positive finding means a sharp high-frequency early-diastolic sound precedes a rumble.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Opening snap means a sharp high-frequency early-diastolic sound precedes a rumble.

Why It Matters

A positive Opening snap response can signal mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis. High left-atrial pressure abruptly domes and tenses a still-mobile stenotic mitral valve. Opening snap cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis

Assessment Method

Listen after S2 at the apex and lower left sternal border.

Positive Finding

A sharp high-frequency early-diastolic sound precedes a rumble.

Mechanism

High left-atrial pressure abruptly domes and tenses a still-mobile stenotic mitral valve.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Opening snap response raises concern for mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis because high left-atrial pressure abruptly domes and tenses a still-mobile stenotic mitral valve. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Opening snap does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Opening snap to focus evaluation of mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis and the reviewed alternatives: S3, split S2, pericardial knock. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Listen after S2 at the apex and lower left sternal border. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Opening snap as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

It may be absent in severe calcification and can be confused with S3. Therefore, Opening snap cannot confirm or exclude mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • S3

  • split S2

  • pericardial knock

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from S3, split S2, pericardial knock, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Opening snap may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current classic clue; may disappear with calcification. Interpret Opening snap in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: It may be absent in severe calcification and can be confused with S3.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Opening snap, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Listen after S2 at the apex and lower left sternal border. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Use timing and pitch, then confirm valve anatomy with echocardiography.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Opening snap observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: A sharp high-frequency early-diastolic sound precedes a rumble. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Listen after S2 at the apex and lower left sternal border. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, mobile stenotic atrioventricular valve, usually rheumatic mitral stenosis, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Opening snap on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
