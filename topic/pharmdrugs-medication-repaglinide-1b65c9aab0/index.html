Contraindications

  • Concomitant gemfibrozil

  • Type 1 diabetes, DKA, active hypoglycemia, or product hypersensitivity

  • Avoid automatic administration when a meal is absent; use conservative dosing in severe renal or hepatic impairment

Nursing Essentials

  • Administer within 30 minutes before a meal and skip that dose if the meal is skipped; add a dose only when an additional meal is actually eaten under the prescribed plan.

  • Reconcile exact meal pattern, glucose trend, renal/hepatic function, and CYP2C8 interactions before titration.

Interactions

  • Gemfibrozil is contraindicated; clopidogrel also raises exposure and requires strict labeled dose limits if unavoidable.

  • Rifampin can unpredictably induce/inhibit pathways over time; trimethoprim, cyclosporine, azoles, and macrolides may raise exposure.

  • Insulin and other glucose-lowering agents add hypoglycemia; beta blockers can mask warning symptoms.

Key Labs

  • Premeal/postmeal glucose and A1c

  • Renal/hepatic function

  • Meal adherence, hypoglycemia pattern, and full interaction review

Nclex Traps

  • Repaglinide is not a once-daily background medicine; it follows meals.

  • Short acting does not mean incapable of severe hypoglycemia.

  • Do not combine repaglinide with gemfibrozil or NPH insulin.

Class

Short-acting meglitinide insulin secretagogue; meal-linked pancreatic SUR1/KATP-channel blocker

Used To Treat

Adjunct to nutrition and activity for adults with type 2 diabetes and functioning beta cells. It is taken within 30 minutes before each meal; if a meal is skipped, that meal's dose is skipped. It does not treat type 1 diabetes or DKA.

Mechanism

Repaglinide binds the SUR1 regulatory component of the beta-cell ATP-sensitive potassium channel at a site overlapping but not identical to sulfonylureas. Channel closure depolarizes the beta cell, opens voltage-gated calcium channels, and triggers calcium-dependent insulin-granule exocytosis. Rapid absorption and relatively short elimination align secretion with postmeal glucose, but the pathway still bypasses normal glucose sensing and can lower glucose excessively. CYP2C8 is the major metabolic enzyme, CYP3A4 contributes, and hepatic uptake involves OATP1B1; simultaneous inhibition by gemfibrozil and its metabolite can multiply and prolong exposure.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Severe hypoglycemia can occur when a dose is not matched to food, during renal/hepatic impairment, or with interacting drugs. Gemfibrozil is contraindicated because CYP2C8/OATP-related inhibition markedly increases repaglinide exposure. Repaglinide is not indicated with NPH insulin because serious cardiovascular events were observed in combination trials.

Description

Repaglinide closes pancreatic beta-cell SUR1/KATP channels to trigger a short, meal-linked burst of endogenous insulin; it is a short-acting meglitinide secretagogue. It targets the same beta-cell KATP control system as sulfonylureas but binds with faster on/off kinetics, so dosing follows eating rather than a fixed once-daily schedule. The rule 'no meal, no dose' is mechanistic: giving it without incoming carbohydrate can produce hypoglycemia. Its defining interaction is gemfibrozil, which markedly raises repaglinide exposure and is contraindicated.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypoglycemia, weight gain, headache, dizziness, nausea, arthralgia, and respiratory symptoms

  • Rare hepatic injury, hypersensitivity, or visual change during rapid glucose shifts

  • Serious exposure increase with CYP2C8/OATP interactions
