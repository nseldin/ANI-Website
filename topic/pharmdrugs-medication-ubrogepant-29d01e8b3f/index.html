Class

Oral CGRP receptor antagonist gepant for acute migraine

Used To Treat

Acute migraine with or without aura in adults; it is not indicated for preventive treatment.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current UBRELVY labeling has no boxed warning. It is contraindicated with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors because exposure rises markedly and in patients with serious ubrogepant hypersensitivity. Anaphylaxis can occur within minutes to days; monitor new/worsening hypertension and Raynaud phenomenon and avoid strong CYP3A inducers that can eliminate efficacy.

Mechanism

Ubrogepant occupies the CLR/RAMP1 CGRP receptor complex and prevents CGRP from activating Gs-linked adenylyl cyclase and cAMP signaling at trigeminal ganglion, brainstem, and meningeal neurovascular targets. This lowers an important migraine pain amplifier without activating serotonin-receptor vasoconstriction. Receptor antagonism does not imply that every migraine symptom has one CGRP-only cause.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify attack onset, 50- or 100-mg dose, whether a second dose is permitted at least 2 hours later, 200-mg daily maximum, interacting CYP3A/P-gp/BCRP drugs, renal/hepatic adjustment and pregnancy context.

  • Assess headache red flags and medication-overuse pattern; monitor nausea, somnolence, blood pressure, painful pale/blue digits and delayed hypersensitivity.

Contraindications

  • Contraindicated with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors because greatly increased ubrogepant exposure can increase adverse effects.

  • Contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity to ubrogepant or an excipient. Avoid end-stage renal disease; dose adjustment is required in severe renal or hepatic impairment.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors are contraindicated; moderate or weak inhibitors require a lower regimen. Strong CYP3A inducers should be avoided because reduced exposure is expected to cause loss of efficacy.

  • P-gp or BCRP-only inhibitors can raise exposure and may require dose modification. Ask specifically about antimicrobials, anticonvulsants, transplant drugs, and supplements rather than relying on the patient to recognize enzyme names.

Key Labs

  • No routine level. Monitor attack response, repeat-dose frequency, blood pressure, Raynaud symptoms and renal/hepatic suitability; perform a complete interaction review.

Nclex Traps

  • Ubrogepant is acute therapy only; do not schedule it as daily prevention.

  • A second dose is separated by at least 2 hours so exposure from the first dose can be assessed and the 200-mg daily ceiling preserved.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibition is a contraindication, not merely a suggestion to watch for nausea.

Description

Ubrogepant is an oral small-molecule CGRP receptor antagonist that blocks CGRP signaling to treat an adult migraine attack after it begins. A second dose may be given at least 2 hours later when the product-specific regimen allows, but the total must not exceed 200 mg in 24 hours; it is not a scheduled preventive drug. Blocking CGRP reduces trigeminovascular pain signaling without serotonin-receptor vasoconstriction, while CYP3A exposure and postmarketing vascular effects remain clinically important.

Adverse Effects

  • Nausea and somnolence are the most common effects; dry mouth can occur.

  • Serious immediate or delayed hypersensitivity, postmarketing hypertension, and Raynaud phenomenon are the high-consequence concerns.
