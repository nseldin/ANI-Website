Class

Reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor used for nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade reversal

Used To Treat

Reversal of residual nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade after surgery when spontaneous recovery is already present. Other uses such as myasthenia gravis must remain separate from the anesthesia-reversal decision.

Boxed Warning

RESIDUAL-PARALYSIS WARNING: do not give neostigmine by the clock or without evidence of spontaneous recovery. Pair single-ingredient neostigmine with an antimuscarinic, but do not automatically add atropine or glycopyrrolate when fixed-combination PREVDUO already supplies glycopyrrolate. Use objective neuromuscular monitoring and maintain airway support because under-reversal, cholinergic toxicity, or paradoxical weakness can threaten ventilation.

Mechanism

Neostigmine reversibly inhibits acetylcholinesterase, allowing acetylcholine to accumulate and compete with a nondepolarizing blocker at skeletal-muscle nicotinic receptors. The same acetylcholine also stimulates muscarinic receptors, which explains why an antimuscarinic is required. The effect has a ceiling: once acetylcholinesterase is maximally inhibited, more neostigmine adds adverse effects rather than overcoming profound block. Giving a large dose when little blockade remains can itself impair neuromuscular transmission, so objective recovery and careful timing are mechanistically essential.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify the blocker, time, depth, weight, antimuscarinic order, and fixed-combination status because neostigmine has a ceiling and PREVDUO already contains glycopyrrolate; this formulation check prevents an unintended duplicate antimuscarinic dose.

  • Assess ventilation, head lift or grip only as adjuncts, secretion burden, heart rate, bronchospasm, and quantitative train-of-four because clinical movement can coexist with unsafe pharyngeal weakness.

  • Keep airway equipment and treatment for bradycardia or bronchospasm available because muscarinic toxicity can emerge rapidly.

Contraindications

  • Mechanical gastrointestinal or urinary obstruction and known hypersensitivity are contraindications; anesthesia reversal also requires evidence that the block is pharmacologically reversible and already recovering.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides, magnesium, lithium, local anesthetics, and some antiarrhythmics can intensify or prolong neuromuscular blockade, making apparent reversal less reliable.

  • Atropine or glycopyrrolate counteracts muscarinic effects but does not replace objective confirmation that skeletal-muscle recovery is adequate.

Key Labs

  • Quantitative train-of-four ratio is the principal response measure; use ECG, oxygenation, capnography, and renal function to evaluate safety because renal impairment can prolong both blocker and neostigmine exposure.

Nclex Traps

  • Single-ingredient neostigmine needs an antimuscarinic because acetylcholine rises at muscarinic as well as nicotinic receptors, but PREVDUO already contains glycopyrrolate and should not receive an automatic duplicate antimuscarinic dose.

  • More drug cannot reliably reverse profound block and can worsen weakness when blockade is minimal.

  • PREVDUO contains both neostigmine and glycopyrrolate.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Renal impairment

    Note

    Renal clearance is reduced and the original blocker may also persist, so recurrence and prolonged effects require extended quantitative monitoring.

  • Population

    Asthma, bradyarrhythmia, or coronary disease

    Note

    Muscarinic bronchospasm, bradycardia, and hemodynamic stress can be poorly tolerated even when an antimuscarinic is used.

  • Population

    Myasthenia gravis

    Note

    Chronic disease treatment and cholinergic-crisis questions are different from postoperative reversal and should not inherit this dosing pathway because their indications, dosing, and safety decisions are not interchangeable.

Description

Neostigmine increases acetylcholine at nicotinic and muscarinic synapses. For anesthesia reversal, the current injection label uses 0.03 mg/kg IV when blockade is shorter acting or more recovered and 0.07 mg/kg IV for longer-acting agents or deeper residual block, with a maximum of 0.07 mg/kg or 5 mg, whichever is less. Single-ingredient neostigmine must be paired with an antimuscarinic such as atropine about 15 micrograms/kg or glycopyrrolate about 10 micrograms/kg before or concomitantly because the desired nicotinic effect otherwise arrives with bradycardia, secretions, bronchospasm, and gastrointestinal activity. PREVDUO is a fixed neostigmine-glycopyrrolate product, so verify the formulation and do not automatically add another atropine or glycopyrrolate dose that would duplicate its built-in antimuscarinic component.

Administration Timing

  • Confirm spontaneous recovery with a peripheral nerve stimulator before use; 0.03 mg/kg IV is used for shorter-acting or more recovered block and 0.07 mg/kg for deeper or longer-acting residual block, without exceeding 0.07 mg/kg or 5 mg.

  • With single-ingredient neostigmine, give atropine or glycopyrrolate before or concomitantly and ensure the antimuscarinic effect is established as neostigmine takes effect. PREVDUO already contains glycopyrrolate, so do not automatically duplicate it with a separate atropine or glycopyrrolate dose.

  • Continue quantitative train-of-four and airway monitoring until sustained adequate recovery because time-based or visual assessment alone can miss residual weakness.

Adverse Effects

  • Bradycardia, dysrhythmia, hypotension, increased secretions, bronchospasm, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramping, diarrhea, and paradoxical neuromuscular weakness can occur.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Residual weakness, falling oxygenation, hypoventilation, severe bradycardia, bronchospasm, or cholinergic symptoms requires immediate airway support and anesthesia reassessment rather than repeated empiric neostigmine.

Evidence Limitations

  • Train-of-four count without a quantitative ratio and bedside strength tests alone can overestimate safe upper-airway recovery.

Regulatory Status

Neostigmine injection and the PREVDUO neostigmine-glycopyrrolate combination are FDA-approved prescription products for specified reversal of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade. PREVDUO is labeled for adults and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older.
