Definition

Pleural friction rub is a localized coarse grating or creaking sound produced when inflamed pleural layers rub against each other as the lungs move during breathing.

Plain Language

A pleural friction rub sounds like two rough surfaces rubbing together with each breath.

Why It Matters

The sound indicates pleural inflammation and can occur with pneumonia, pulmonary embolism or infarction, autoimmune disease, infection, malignancy, or another pleural disorder. Sudden pleuritic pain, shortness of breath, low oxygen, coughing blood, or instability requires urgent evaluation.

Associated Conditions

  • inflammation or roughening of the visceral and parietal pleural surfaces from infection, pulmonary infarction or embolism, autoimmune disease, malignancy, uremia, trauma, or another pleural process

Assessment Method

Auscultate the painful or suspected region and matching opposite site during quiet and deeper breathing; document location, respiratory-phase timing, tenderness, whether coughing changes the sound, associated pleuritic pain, oxygenation, fever, dyspnea, and whether the sound stops during a comfortable brief breath hold.

Positive Finding

A localized short coarse grating, creaking, or leather-like sound is heard during inspiration, expiration, or both and usually does not clear with coughing.

Mechanism

Inflamed roughened visceral and parietal pleural layers generate friction as they slide against each other during breathing.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Pleural friction rub response raises concern for inflammation or roughening of the visceral and parietal pleural surfaces from infection, pulmonary infarction or embolism, autoimmune disease, malignancy, uremia, trauma, or another pleural process because inflamed roughened visceral and parietal pleural layers generate friction as they slide against each other during breathing. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pleural friction rub does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Evidence Limitations

The rub may be brief or confined to a small area, can resemble coarse crackles or rhonchi, and its absence does not exclude pleurisy, pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, or malignancy. Therefore, Pleural friction rub cannot confirm or exclude inflammation or roughening of the visceral and parietal pleural surfaces from infection, pulmonary infarction or embolism, autoimmune disease, malignancy, uremia, trauma, or another pleural process by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • coarse crackles

  • rhonchi

  • pericardial friction rub

  • stethoscope or clothing artifact

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from coarse crackles, rhonchi, pericardial friction rub, stethoscope or clothing artifact, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Pleural friction rub may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current clue to pleural inflammation, not a diagnosis of its cause; the sound may be transient and should be integrated with history, imaging, and cardiopulmonary assessment. Safety Precautions

Before assessing Pleural friction rub, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate the painful or suspected region and matching opposite site during quiet and deeper breathing; document location, respiratory-phase timing, tenderness, whether coughing changes the sound, associated pleuritic pain, oxygenation, fever, dyspnea, and whether the sound stops during a comfortable brief breath hold.
