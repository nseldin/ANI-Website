Class

Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23

Used To Treat

Adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis or active psoriatic arthritis and label-defined adults with moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease. Formulation, induction route, and maintenance interval differ by disease and product; verify the exact current regimen rather than transferring a psoriasis schedule to bowel disease.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious infections can occur; evaluate for tuberculosis before treatment, avoid initiation during active important infection, and avoid live vaccines. Serious hypersensitivity may occur. For inflammatory-bowel-disease treatment, assess liver enzymes and bilirubin before treatment and during induction, with ongoing monitoring according to clinical context; interrupt and investigate suspected drug-induced liver injury. Verify current indication-specific route and formulation.

Mechanism

IL-23 is a p19/p40 heterodimer released by activated dendritic cells and macrophages. It binds the IL-23R/IL-12R beta1 complex on lymphocytes, activating JAK2 and TYK2 with downstream STAT3-dominant transcription. That program maintains pathogenic Th17 and type-3 immune responses, increasing IL-17A/F, IL-22, and tissue-recruiting mediators. Guselkumab binds p19 and prevents IL-23 from engaging its receptor, reducing keratinocyte activation, neutrophilic tissue recruitment, enthesitis, synovitis, and bowel inflammation. Because p19 is absent from IL-12, IL-12R beta2-STAT4-interferon-gamma signaling remains available. Its long antibody half-life permits infrequent maintenance dosing; the persistence also means infection or hepatic monitoring must continue between doses rather than only on injection day.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen TB and vaccines and teach infection precautions; for bowel-disease use, obtain baseline and induction-phase liver tests because symptoms alone may lag behind hepatocellular injury.

  • Verify product, route, loading schedule, and disease indication. Track skin, joint, enthesis, and bowel response with the objective measure relevant to the treated disease.

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to guselkumab or excipients; delay during clinically important active infection.

  • Clarify TB, recurrent infection, chronic or acute liver disease, alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs, live vaccines, and other immunosuppressive therapy.

Interactions

  • Avoid live vaccines and unstudied combinations with other biologic or targeted synthetic DMARDs.

  • Review other hepatotoxic agents in bowel-disease patients; inflammation improvement may normalize CYP activity even though guselkumab is not a direct CYP inhibitor.

Key Labs

  • TB and infection evaluation, vaccination review, AST/ALT, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin for bowel-disease regimens, and indication-specific response

  • Skin examination, injection reaction, hypersensitivity, and symptoms of hepatitis such as dark urine, jaundice, pruritus, nausea, or right-upper-quadrant pain

Nclex Traps

  • Guselkumab blocks IL-23 p19 only; it does not block shared p40 and therefore does not directly inhibit IL-12.

  • A psoriasis maintenance schedule cannot be assumed for ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease; route and induction differ.

Description

Guselkumab is a selective IL-23 p19 inhibitor: it binds the cytokine's unique p19 subunit and leaves IL-12 signaling intact. IL-23 is not the molecule that initially creates every Th17 cell; it is the survival and pathogenicity signal that maintains IL-17-, IL-22-, and GM-CSF-producing lymphocytes in psoriatic plaques, entheses, joints, and inflamed bowel. Blocking p19 therefore turns down a disease-sustaining circuit while preserving the IL-12-Th1 axis that ustekinumab also suppresses. This molecular distinction is clinically useful when comparing biologics, but it does not eliminate infection risk. Bowel-disease regimens also require liver monitoring because serious drug-induced liver injury has been reported and current labeling contains indication-specific hepatic precautions.

Adverse Effects

  • Upper-respiratory infection, headache, injection-site reaction, arthralgia, diarrhea, gastroenteritis, herpes simplex, and tinea infection

  • Serious infection or TB, anaphylaxis, liver-enzyme elevation or drug-induced liver injury, and severe hypersensitivity
