Definition

Diabetic retinopathy develops when chronic hyperglycemia damages retinal microvessels, causing leakage, ischemia, pathologic new vessels, and macular edema. The characteristic pattern is often no early symptoms, later blurred vision, floaters, field loss, or sudden visual loss from hemorrhage or detachment. Confirm or stage it with scheduled dilated retinal examination or validated retinal photography; distinguish it from macular degeneration, retinal vein occlusion, hypertensive retinopathy, cataract, and retinal detachment by fundus pattern and imaging. The priority is to optimize glucose, blood pressure, kidney, and lipid risk without abrupt unsafe changes because vitreous hemorrhage can complicate Diabetic retinopathy. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Weakness, sweating, tremor, palpitations, mental-status change, weight change, thirst/polyuria, muscle cramps/spasm, paresthesias, fatigue, temperature intolerance, or abnormal growth/nutrition cues can reveal metabolic instability.

  • Escalate severe hypo/hyperglycemia, seizure, dysrhythmia, tetany/laryngospasm, adrenal crisis, thyroid storm, refeeding electrolyte collapse, or DKA/HHS signs.

Diagnostics

  • scheduled dilated retinal examination or validated retinal photography

  • optical coherence tomography for macular edema

  • fluorescein angiography and specialist grading when treatment planning requires it

  • Distinguish Diabetic retinopathy from macular degeneration, retinal vein occlusion, hypertensive retinopathy, cataract, and retinal detachment by fundus pattern and imaging.

Labs

  • glucose may be high or low depending on condition

  • sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphate changes can become life-threatening

  • anion gap and ketones matter in DKA

Treatments

  • optimize glucose, blood pressure, kidney, and lipid risk without abrupt unsafe changes

  • use intraocular anti-VEGF therapy or focal treatment for indicated macular edema

  • use laser or vitreoretinal surgery for proliferative complications

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate the same day for sudden floaters or flashes, curtain-like shadow, abrupt vision loss, severe eye pain or redness after a procedure, or new visual symptoms in pregnancy because vitreous hemorrhage, retinal detachment, endophthalmitis, or rapidly changing retinopathy can permanently impair sight.

  • Ask about blurred or fluctuating vision, floaters, dark curtain, visual-field loss, pregnancy, kidney disease, and prior retinal treatment, and document visual acuity in each eye because macular edema and vitreous or retinal complications can progress independently and may be painless.

  • Coordinate dilated retinal examination or validated retinal photography at the diabetes-specific interval and shorten follow-up for existing disease or pregnancy because screening detects treatable microvascular damage before the patient perceives vision loss.

  • Trend A1C, blood pressure, lipids, kidney status, and smoking and support gradual individualized risk-factor improvement because chronic hyperglycemia and vascular stress drive capillary leakage, ischemia, and neovascularization.

  • After intravitreal injection, laser, or vitreoretinal surgery, monitor pain, redness, discharge, acuity, pressure symptoms, and positioning instructions because endophthalmitis, pressure rise, hemorrhage, or retinal detachment requires rapid recognition.

Complications

  • vitreous hemorrhage can complicate Diabetic retinopathy.

  • tractional retinal detachment can complicate Diabetic retinopathy.

  • macular edema can complicate Diabetic retinopathy.

  • glaucoma can complicate Diabetic retinopathy.

  • permanent blindness can complicate Diabetic retinopathy.

Red Flags

  • Sudden shower of floaters, flashing lights, curtain or shadow, or abrupt visual-field loss

  • Sudden severe reduction in vision or new dense visual haze

  • Severe eye pain, redness, photophobia, or discharge after injection or surgery

  • New or rapidly worsening visual symptoms during pregnancy or severe hypertension

Patient Education

  • Keep dilated-eye appointments even when vision is sharp because retinal damage can be advanced before central vision changes.

  • Call the eye team urgently for flashes, many new floaters, a curtain, sudden blur, or post-procedure pain and redness because early retinal or infection treatment can preserve vision.

Nclex Traps

  • Good visual acuity does not exclude sight-threatening early disease

  • Sudden floaters, curtain-like loss, or major blur requires urgent eye evaluation
