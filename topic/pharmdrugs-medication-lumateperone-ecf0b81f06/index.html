Class

Second-generation serotonin-dopamine modulating antipsychotic

Used To Treat

Schizophrenia in adults, bipolar I or II depressive episodes as monotherapy or adjunct to lithium/valproate, and adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder in adults under current labeling.

Boxed Warning

Two boxed warnings apply. First, elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis who receive antipsychotic drugs have an increased risk of death; lumateperone is not approved to treat dementia-related psychosis. Second, antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young adult patients; Caplyta is not approved for pediatric use. Monitor every antidepressant-treated client closely for clinical worsening, agitation, unusual behavior change, or emergent suicidality, especially during the first months and after dose changes, and escalate an immediate safety concern without delay.

Mechanism

The clinical mechanism is not fully established. Lumateperone antagonizes 5-HT2A receptors and partially agonizes central D2 receptors; it also interacts with D1, alpha-1, serotonin transporters, and downstream glutamate signaling, creating combined serotonin-dopamine modulation.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor mental status, suicidality, sedation, orthostasis/falls, temperature/rigidity for NMS, involuntary movements, weight, glucose, lipids, CBC when leukopenia risk exists, and infection.

  • Screen strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, alcohol/CNS depressants, pregnancy, and dementia-related psychosis.

Key Labs

  • Weight/BMI, glucose/A1c, lipids, CBC when indicated, BP/orthostasis, and movement-disorder assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Lumateperone's newer receptor profile does not erase antipsychotic class emergencies such as NMS, tardive dyskinesia, falls, or dementia-related mortality.

Description

Lumateperone is an atypical antipsychotic with potent 5-HT2A antagonism and lower-affinity D2 partial agonist activity, plus serotonergic and glutamatergic network effects. It can treat psychosis and depressive syndromes, but sedation, orthostasis, falls, metabolic change, leukopenia, tardive dyskinesia, NMS, and CYP3A interaction risks remain despite a comparatively distinct receptor profile.
