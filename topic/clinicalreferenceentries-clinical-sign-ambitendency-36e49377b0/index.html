Definition

Ambitendency is an observed motor sign. It is a clue to catatonia. A positive finding means the patient alternates between approaching and withdrawing, unable to complete the act.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ambitendency means the patient alternates between approaching and withdrawing, unable to complete the act.

Why It Matters

The observed Ambitendency finding can signal catatonia. Competing motor programs activate without effective selection or termination. Ambitendency cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonia

Assessment Method

Offer a simple action such as a handshake and observe initiation.

Positive Finding

The patient alternates between approaching and withdrawing, unable to complete the act.

Mechanism

Competing motor programs activate without effective selection or termination.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Ambitendency finding raises concern for catatonia because competing motor programs activate without effective selection or termination. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ambitendency does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ambitendency to focus evaluation of catatonia and the reviewed alternatives: apraxia, fear, functional movement. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Offer a simple action such as a handshake and observe initiation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ambitendency as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Ordinary hesitation and anxiety can look similar. Therefore, Ambitendency cannot confirm or exclude catatonia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • apraxia

  • fear

  • functional movement

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as apraxia, fear, functional movement.

False Negative Situations

Ambitendency may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonia.
