Definition

Litten sign is an observed thoracic sign. It is a clue to diaphragmatic excursion. A positive finding means a moving shadow descends along the lower interspaces during inspiration; asymmetry or absence is abnormal.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Litten sign means a moving shadow descends along the lower interspaces during inspiration; asymmetry or absence is abnormal.

Why It Matters

The observed Litten sign finding can signal diaphragmatic excursion. The contracting diaphragm and moving lung edge alter the contour and transmitted light-shadow line. Litten sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • diaphragmatic excursion

Assessment Method

Use tangential light across the lower chest while the patient breathes deeply.

Positive Finding

A moving shadow descends along the lower interspaces during inspiration; asymmetry or absence is abnormal.

Mechanism

The contracting diaphragm and moving lung edge alter the contour and transmitted light-shadow line.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Litten sign finding raises concern for diaphragmatic excursion because the contracting diaphragm and moving lung edge alter the contour and transmitted light-shadow line. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Litten sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Litten sign to focus evaluation of diaphragmatic excursion and the reviewed alternatives: normal asymmetry, obesity, shallow breathing. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Use tangential light across the lower chest while the patient breathes deeply. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Litten sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus, lighting, and chest wall shape make it unreliable. Therefore, Litten sign cannot confirm or exclude diaphragmatic excursion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal asymmetry

  • obesity

  • shallow breathing

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as normal asymmetry, obesity, shallow breathing.

False Negative Situations

Litten sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of diaphragmatic excursion.

Modern Status

Historical and examiner dependent. Litten sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude diaphragmatic excursion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Litten sign: Body habitus, lighting, and chest wall shape make it unreliable.

Safety Precautions

Observe Litten sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Use tangential light across the lower chest while the patient breathes deeply. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not substitute a shadow observation for ultrasound when diaphragmatic dysfunction matters.
