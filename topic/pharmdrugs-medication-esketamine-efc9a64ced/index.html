Class

Intranasal NMDA receptor antagonist antidepressant

Used To Treat

Treatment-resistant depression with an oral antidepressant and depressive symptoms in adults with major depressive disorder plus acute suicidal ideation or behavior, under monitored restricted-program administration.

Boxed Warning

Sedation, dissociation, respiratory depression, abuse/misuse, and suicidal thoughts/behaviors: administer only in a certified healthcare setting, monitor for at least two hours, and do not dispense for unsupervised home use. It has not been shown to prevent suicide; emergency safety care remains necessary.

Mechanism

Esketamine noncompetitively blocks NMDA glutamate receptors. The downstream antidepressant model involves a glutamate surge through AMPA signaling, BDNF release, and mTOR-linked synaptic plasticity; the exact clinical antidepressant mechanism is not fully resolved.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check BP before dosing and reassess after; monitor consciousness, dissociation, breathing/oxygenation, nausea/vomiting, anxiety, perceptual change, and readiness for safe discharge.

  • Clients must arrange transport and avoid driving or hazardous activity until the next day after restorative sleep.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, respiratory/mental status, substance-use risk, pregnancy context, and urinary symptoms with repeated exposure.

Nclex Traps

  • Esketamine may reduce depressive symptoms quickly, but it does not replace suicide precautions or emergency evaluation.

Description

Esketamine is the S-enantiomer of ketamine and a monitored intranasal antidepressant that noncompetitively blocks NMDA glutamate receptors, triggering rapid downstream AMPA/BDNF-linked synaptic effects rather than classic monoamine reuptake blockade. Transient dissociation, sedation, respiratory depression, blood-pressure elevation, impaired judgment, and abuse potential require observation after every dose.
