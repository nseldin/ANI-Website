Definition

Mulberry molars is an observed dental sign. It is a clue to late congenital syphilis. A positive finding means crowns are small with many poorly developed cusps resembling a mulberry.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Mulberry molars means crowns are small with many poorly developed cusps resembling a mulberry.

Why It Matters

The observed Mulberry molars finding can signal late congenital syphilis. Infection during tooth development disrupts normal cusp and enamel formation. Mulberry molars cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • late congenital syphilis

Assessment Method

Inspect first permanent molars.

Positive Finding

Crowns are small with many poorly developed cusps resembling a mulberry.

Mechanism

Infection during tooth development disrupts normal cusp and enamel formation.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Mulberry molars finding raises concern for late congenital syphilis because infection during tooth development disrupts normal cusp and enamel formation. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Mulberry molars does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Dental morphology is not diagnostic alone. Therefore, Mulberry molars cannot confirm or exclude late congenital syphilis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.
