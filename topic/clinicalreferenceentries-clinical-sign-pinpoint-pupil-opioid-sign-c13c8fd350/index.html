Definition

Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign is an observed pupillary sign. It is a clue to opioid toxidrome. A positive finding means both pupils are markedly constricted.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign means both pupils are markedly constricted.

Why It Matters

The observed Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign finding can signal opioid toxidrome. Mu-opioid receptor activation in the Edinger-Westphal pathway increases parasympathetic pupillary tone. Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • opioid toxidrome

Assessment Method

Assess pupils with respiratory rate, ventilation, consciousness, and exposure context.

Positive Finding

Both pupils are markedly constricted.

Mechanism

Mu-opioid receptor activation in the Edinger-Westphal pathway increases parasympathetic pupillary tone.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign finding raises concern for opioid toxidrome because mu-opioid receptor activation in the Edinger-Westphal pathway increases parasympathetic pupillary tone. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign to focus evaluation of opioid toxidrome and the reviewed alternatives: clonidine toxicity, Horner syndrome, miotic eye drops. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Assess pupils with respiratory rate, ventilation, consciousness, and exposure context. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Hypoxia, mixed ingestion, or certain opioids can produce normal or large pupils. Therefore, Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign cannot confirm or exclude opioid toxidrome by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • clonidine toxicity

  • Horner syndrome

  • miotic eye drops

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as clonidine toxicity, Horner syndrome, miotic eye drops.

False Negative Situations

Pinpoint-pupil opioid sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of opioid toxidrome.

The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Treat respiratory depression with ventilation and naloxone rather than waiting for miosis.
