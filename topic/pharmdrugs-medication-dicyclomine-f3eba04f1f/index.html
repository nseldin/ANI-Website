Used To Treat

Short-term treatment of functional bowel/irritable bowel syndrome spasm in adults after exclusion of obstruction and other acute pathology.

Mechanism

Competitive muscarinic antagonism reduces parasympathetic smooth-muscle contraction and glandular secretion. Dicyclomine also antagonizes bradykinin- and histamine-induced smooth-muscle spasm through a direct musculotropic effect. As a tertiary amine it can enter the CNS, explaining drowsiness, agitation, hallucinations and delirium in susceptible patients.

Contraindications

  • Infants younger than 6 months and nursing mothers

  • Glaucoma, myasthenia gravis, obstructive uropathy, obstructive GI disease, severe ulcerative colitis and reflux esophagitis

  • Unstable cardiovascular status in acute hemorrhage

Nursing Essentials

  • Assess for acute abdomen, distention, vomiting, stool/gas passage, bowel sounds, urinary retention, glaucoma symptoms, myasthenia and tachyarrhythmia before giving.

  • Teach heat precautions, hydration, oral care, vision/driving safety and urgent reporting of eye pain, inability to void, severe constipation, fever or confusion.

Interactions

  • Other anticholinergics, first-generation antihistamines, tricyclics, antipsychotics and antiparkinsonian drugs add toxicity.

  • Antacids can interfere with absorption; follow formulation-specific timing.

  • Opposes prokinetics and cholinesterase inhibitors and can worsen opioid-related constipation/retention.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum concentration

  • Heart rate, temperature, mental status and bowel/bladder function

  • Renal/hepatic function when vulnerability or accumulation is plausible; diagnostic evaluation for obstruction or acute pathology

Nclex Traps

  • An infant with dicyclomine exposure is an emergency-risk situation, not routine colic treatment.

  • Hot dry skin, fever, tachycardia, dilated pupils and confusion suggest anticholinergic toxicity.

  • Pain relief does not prove IBS or rule out obstruction.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Contraindicated in infants younger than 6 months because severe respiratory/CNS events and deaths have been reported, and contraindicated in nursing mothers. Also avoid glaucoma, myasthenia gravis, obstructive GI/urinary disease, severe ulcerative colitis, reflux esophagitis and unstable cardiovascular status. Reduced sweating can cause heat prostration.

Description

Dicyclomine is an antispasmodic that both blocks muscarinic acetylcholine receptors and directly relaxes GI smooth muscle. Reduced cholinergic contraction can ease functional cramping, but the same mechanism reduces sweating, bowel/bladder tone and accommodation and can accelerate heart rate or impair cognition. It is symptom therapy, not treatment for inflammation, infection, ischemia or obstruction.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, blurred vision, mydriasis, dizziness, somnolence, nausea, weakness and nervousness

  • Tachycardia, palpitations, urinary retention, constipation and reduced sweating/heat stroke

  • Confusion, agitation, hallucinations or anticholinergic delirium
