Definition

Schaefer sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to corticospinal tract dysfunction. A positive finding means the great toe extends.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Schaefer sign means the great toe extends.

Why It Matters

A positive Schaefer sign response can signal corticospinal tract dysfunction. Achilles compression can activate afferent pathways that reveal loss of corticospinal suppression of the extensor plantar pattern. Schaefer sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • corticospinal tract dysfunction

Assessment Method

Gently squeeze the Achilles tendon while observing the toes.

Positive Finding

The great toe extends.

Mechanism

Achilles compression can activate afferent pathways that reveal loss of corticospinal suppression of the extensor plantar pattern.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Schaefer sign response raises concern for corticospinal tract dysfunction because achilles compression can activate afferent pathways that reveal loss of corticospinal suppression of the extensor plantar pattern. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Schaefer sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Schaefer sign to focus evaluation of corticospinal tract dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: Achilles pain, ankle guarding, voluntary toe extension. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently squeeze the Achilles tendon while observing the toes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Schaefer sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The maneuver is poorly standardized and lacks dependable standalone diagnostic performance. Therefore, Schaefer sign cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • Achilles pain

  • ankle guarding

  • voluntary toe extension

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from Achilles pain, ankle guarding, voluntary toe extension, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Schaefer sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; rarely needed. Schaefer sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction. Apply the reviewed limitation for Schaefer sign: The maneuver is poorly standardized and lacks dependable standalone diagnostic performance.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Schaefer sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Gently squeeze the Achilles tendon while observing the toes. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not squeeze an injured or recently repaired Achilles tendon.

Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Gently squeeze the Achilles tendon while observing the toes. Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Base escalation for Schaefer sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
