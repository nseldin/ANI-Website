Class

Dual type 1 and type 2 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor for BPH

Used To Treat

Symptomatic BPH in men with an enlarged prostate to improve symptoms and reduce acute urinary retention or BPH surgery risk; may be combined with tamsulosin. It is not approved to prevent prostate cancer.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Observed high-grade prostate-cancer diagnoses were increased in prevention trials; dutasteride is not approved for prevention. Pregnant people must not handle leaking capsules because skin absorption can disrupt external genital development in a male fetus. Any confirmed PSA rise from the on-treatment nadir requires evaluation.

Mechanism

Competitive, slowly dissociating inhibition of both 5-alpha-reductase isoenzymes suppresses DHT formation in prostate and other tissues. Lower androgen-receptor stimulation reduces epithelial/stromal growth and prostate volume over time. The terminal half-life is about 5 weeks and drug remains detectable for months, explaining the 6-month post-dose blood-donation restriction and prolonged pregnancy-handling concern.

Nursing Essentials

  • Establish baseline PSA/prostate assessment and teach that symptom improvement is measured over months, not days.

  • Do not let pregnant staff/family handle leaking capsules; no blood donation until 6 months after the final dose.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy and clinically significant hypersensitivity to dutasteride or other 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors

  • Not indicated in pediatric patients

  • Evaluate prostate cancer and other obstructive causes before treatment

Interactions

  • Chronic potent CYP3A inhibitors can increase dutasteride exposure.

  • Tamsulosin combination adds alpha-blocker orthostatic effects but provides faster dynamic-outlet relief.

  • Avoid duplicate 5-alpha-reductase products.

Key Labs

  • PSA baseline, new nadir after 3 to 6 months and periodic trend

  • BPH symptom score, prostate size and post-void residual

  • Liver function when clinically indicated

Nclex Traps

  • Double an isolated PSA after at least 3 months for comparison with untreated reference ranges, but evaluate any confirmed rise from the nadir directly.

  • Dutasteride shrinks the gland; it does not relax smooth muscle immediately.

  • Its long half-life explains the unusually long blood-donation and exposure precautions.

Description

Dutasteride blocks both type 1 and type 2 5-alpha-reductase, reducing conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone and gradually shrinking androgen-dependent prostate tissue. Benefit takes months because tissue must involute. It lowers PSA about 50 percent within 3 to 6 months, so cancer surveillance requires a new baseline and attention to any confirmed rise from the nadir.

Adverse Effects

  • Erectile dysfunction, decreased libido, ejaculatory disorder and reduced semen volume

  • Breast enlargement or tenderness

  • Depressed mood and hypersensitivity/angioedema reports

  • PSA suppression that can mask cancer if interpreted against an untreated baseline
