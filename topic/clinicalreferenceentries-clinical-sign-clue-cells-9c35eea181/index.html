Definition

Clue cells is a microscopic laboratory sign. It is a clue to bacterial vaginosis. A positive finding means epithelial borders are obscured by adherent coccobacilli and look stippled.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Clue cells means epithelial borders are obscured by adherent coccobacilli and look stippled.

Why It Matters

The Clue cells laboratory finding can signal bacterial vaginosis. Dense bacterial adherence coats squamous cells after loss of lactobacillus-dominant flora. Clue cells cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • bacterial vaginosis

Assessment Method

Examine saline wet mount of vaginal epithelial cells.

Positive Finding

Epithelial borders are obscured by adherent coccobacilli and look stippled.

Mechanism

Dense bacterial adherence coats squamous cells after loss of lactobacillus-dominant flora.

Clinical Interpretation

The Clue cells laboratory finding raises concern for bacterial vaginosis because dense bacterial adherence coats squamous cells after loss of lactobacillus-dominant flora. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Clue cells does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Clue cells to focus evaluation of bacterial vaginosis and the reviewed alternatives: cytolytic vaginosis, yeast debris, trichomoniasis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine saline wet mount of vaginal epithelial cells. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Clue cells as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Reader skill and specimen quality affect detection. Therefore, Clue cells cannot confirm or exclude bacterial vaginosis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cytolytic vaginosis

  • yeast debris

  • trichomoniasis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with cytolytic vaginosis, yeast debris, trichomoniasis.

False Negative Situations

Clue cells may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
