Used To Treat

Improvement of survival in stable patients with symptomatic heart failure and left-ventricular systolic dysfunction after acute myocardial infarction, and treatment of adult hypertension under current U.S. labeling. It is also used in broader heart-failure practice under guideline-directed criteria.

Mechanism

Eplerenone competitively blocks the mineralocorticoid receptor, reducing aldosterone-dependent transcription of SGK1, ENaC stabilization, and sodium-potassium-ATPase. Distal sodium reabsorption and the lumen-negative voltage fall, reducing potassium and hydrogen secretion. In heart and vasculature, receptor blockade limits collagen deposition, inflammation, hypertrophy, endothelial dysfunction, and electrical remodeling after myocardial injury. Greater selectivity for the mineralocorticoid receptor means little meaningful antagonism at androgen and progesterone receptors, explaining fewer endocrine adverse effects than spironolactone. CYP3A converts eplerenone to inactive metabolites, so clarithromycin, azole antifungals, protease inhibitors, and other strong inhibitors raise exposure and hyperkalemia risk. The short plasma half-life does not mean kidney and potassium effects can be ignored between doses because receptor occupancy, physiology, and accumulating potassium evolve over time.

Contraindications

  • Hyperkalemia, severe renal impairment or anuria, adrenal insufficiency, and duplicate potassium-sparing therapy are major drug-dependent barriers.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated for eplerenone and finerenone; exact potassium and eGFR start thresholds depend on the agent and indication.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors and elevated baseline potassium are contraindications. For hypertension, current labeling adds stricter creatinine-clearance, diabetes-with-microalbuminuria, potassium-supplement, and duplicate potassium-sparing restrictions.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check potassium and eGFR before initiation and at the label- or protocol-defined early interval after a start or dose change, then periodically and during acute illness.

  • Reconcile ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI, potassium chloride, salt substitutes, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, tacrolimus, cyclosporine, and another MRA.

  • Document whether the use is post-MI heart failure, broader guideline-directed HF, or hypertension because label cutoffs and evidence are not identical. Review every CYP3A inhibitor before the first dose.

Interactions

  • RAAS blockers, potassium products, trimethoprim, NSAIDs, heparin, tacrolimus, and cyclosporine converge on hyperkalemia.

  • CYP3A inhibitors increase exposure to eplerenone and finerenone; inducers can reduce efficacy.

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, and selected antivirals are contraindicated; moderate inhibitors may require dose adjustment or avoidance under labeling.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, bicarbonate, sodium, pressure, volume status, and indication-specific albuminuria or heart-failure trajectory.

  • Early potassium surveillance matters because toxicity can appear before disease-modifying benefit is clinically visible.

Nclex Traps

  • MRA benefit in heart failure or CKD is not simply extra urine; receptor blockade reduces maladaptive inflammation, fibrosis, and remodeling.

  • Potassium-sparing means reduced potassium excretion, not freedom from potassium monitoring.

  • Fewer endocrine effects do not mean less need for potassium monitoring. Selectivity changes off-target effects, not the intended renal mechanism.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hyperkalemia is the major life-threatening risk. Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated because increased eplerenone exposure magnifies potassium retention. Obtain potassium before therapy, within the early label-defined interval after initiation or dose change, and periodically; exact renal and interaction restrictions differ between hypertension and post-MI heart-failure indications.

Class Pathway

  • Intracellular mineralocorticoid receptor

  • Selective aldosterone antagonism

  • Reduced remodeling and distal potassium secretion

Description

Eplerenone, branded Inspra, is a steroidal MRA engineered to bind the mineralocorticoid receptor more selectively than spironolactone. It therefore causes much less gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction, and menstrual disturbance, but it still reduces distal potassium excretion and can cause fatal hyperkalemia. Eplerenone has no clinically important active metabolites, a parent half-life of roughly three to six hours, and substantial CYP3A metabolism. Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated because they markedly raise exposure. Its labeled post-MI use targets stable patients with left-ventricular systolic dysfunction and heart-failure symptoms, where benefit comes from limiting aldosterone-mediated remodeling and fibrosis, not from strong acute diuresis. Hypertension dosing has stricter renal and interacting-drug contraindications than post-MI use, so indication must be known before applying a cutoff.

Adverse Effects

  • Hyperkalemia, kidney-function change, hypotension, dizziness, and hyponatremia can occur because mineralocorticoid-dependent sodium uptake and potassium secretion are suppressed.

  • Severe hyperkalemia can cause weakness, conduction block, ventricular arrhythmia, or cardiac arrest; risk rises with CKD, diabetes, older age, RAAS blockade, and potassium products.

  • Endocrine effects are much less frequent than with spironolactone because androgen and progesterone receptor binding is low.
