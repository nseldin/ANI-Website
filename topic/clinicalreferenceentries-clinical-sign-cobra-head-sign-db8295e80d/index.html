Definition

Cobra-head sign is an imaging sign. It is a clue to intravesical ureterocele. A positive finding means a rounded contrast-filled structure with a thin surrounding halo resembles a cobra head.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Cobra-head sign means a rounded contrast-filled structure with a thin surrounding halo resembles a cobra head.

Why It Matters

The Cobra-head sign imaging pattern can signal intravesical ureterocele. Contrast enters a dilated submucosal distal ureter outlined by bladder wall and edema. Cobra-head sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • intravesical ureterocele

Assessment Method

Review contrast imaging of the distal ureter and bladder.

Positive Finding

A rounded contrast-filled structure with a thin surrounding halo resembles a cobra head.

Mechanism

Contrast enters a dilated submucosal distal ureter outlined by bladder wall and edema.

Clinical Interpretation

The Cobra-head sign imaging pattern raises concern for intravesical ureterocele because contrast enters a dilated submucosal distal ureter outlined by bladder wall and edema. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Cobra-head sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Cobra-head sign to focus evaluation of intravesical ureterocele and the reviewed alternatives: bladder diverticulum, UVJ mass, impacted stone. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review contrast imaging of the distal ureter and bladder. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Cobra-head sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pseudo-ureteroceles from tumor or edema can mimic it. Therefore, Cobra-head sign cannot confirm or exclude intravesical ureterocele by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from acquisition artifact, an inappropriate imaging phase or plane, or overlap with bladder diverticulum, UVJ mass, impacted stone.

False Negative Situations

Cobra-head sign may be missed when the associated process is early or subtle, the relevant region is not included, or acquisition timing and resolution are inadequate.
