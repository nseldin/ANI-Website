Definition

Rosenbach sign is an observed eyelid sign. It is a clue to Graves disease. A positive finding means fine eyelid tremor is visible.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Rosenbach sign means fine eyelid tremor is visible.

Why It Matters

The observed Rosenbach sign finding can signal Graves disease. Adrenergic excess and enhanced physiologic tremor affect the thin eyelid muscles. Rosenbach sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • Graves disease

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to close the eyes gently.

Positive Finding

Fine eyelid tremor is visible.

Mechanism

Adrenergic excess and enhanced physiologic tremor affect the thin eyelid muscles.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Rosenbach sign finding raises concern for Graves disease because adrenergic excess and enhanced physiologic tremor affect the thin eyelid muscles. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Rosenbach sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Rosenbach sign to focus evaluation of Graves disease and the reviewed alternatives: essential tremor, caffeine, fatigue. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to close the eyes gently. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Rosenbach sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Fine tremor is common and lighting affects detection. Therefore, Rosenbach sign cannot confirm or exclude Graves disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • essential tremor

  • caffeine

  • fatigue

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as essential tremor, caffeine, fatigue.

False Negative Situations

Rosenbach sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of Graves disease.

Modern Status

Historical nonspecific adjunct. Rosenbach sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude Graves disease. Apply the reviewed limitation for Rosenbach sign: Fine tremor is common and lighting affects detection.

Safety Precautions

Observe Rosenbach sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Ask the patient to close the eyes gently. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not infer hyperthyroidism from eyelid flutter alone.
