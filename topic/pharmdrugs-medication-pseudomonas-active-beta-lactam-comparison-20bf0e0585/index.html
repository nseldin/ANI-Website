Used To Treat

Susceptible Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections with the narrowest active drug that reaches the site, plus newer agents for difficult-to-treat resistance when susceptibility supports them.

Description

Pseudomonas is difficult because its outer membrane admits drugs poorly, OprD loss can block carbapenem entry, Mex efflux pumps remove drug, chromosomal Pseudomonas-derived cephalosporinase (AmpC/PDC) hydrolyzes beta-lactams, and PBP mutations can reduce target affinity. Traditional active beta-lactams include piperacillin/tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, aztreonam, imipenem/cilastatin, and meropenem when the isolate is susceptible. Ertapenem is not dependable. Ceftolozane has unusually strong intrinsic antipseudomonal PBP activity and stability against several non-carbapenemase mechanisms; ceftazidime/avibactam can overcome selected AmpC and serine beta-lactamases; imipenem/relebactam can restore activity when class A/C enzymes and permeability contribute; cefiderocol exploits iron transport. None is universally active.

Mechanism

The susceptibility result integrates several barriers at once. A carbapenem-resistant isolate may remain cefepime- or piperacillin/tazobactam-susceptible when OprD loss selectively impairs carbapenem entry, while combined AmpC upregulation, efflux, and PBP changes can defeat multiple classes without a named carbapenemase. Extended infusion can improve fT>MIC for a susceptible MIC but cannot rescue true resistance. Newer drugs should be matched to the likely mechanism: ceftolozane/tazobactam is often valuable for non-carbapenemase DTR Pseudomonas, while ceftazidime/avibactam or imipenem/relebactam fits selected serine-enzyme profiles. MBL-producing Pseudomonas remains especially difficult and requires specialist interpretation.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: A broad empiric antipseudomonal drug is not permission to continue two active Gram-negative agents after susceptibilities return. Unnecessary combination exposure increases kidney, ear, neurologic, and microbiome harm without automatically preventing resistance.

Adverse Effects

  • Toxicity follows the selected agent: piperacillin/tazobactam adds sodium and renal concerns, cefepime can cause neurotoxicity, carbapenems interact with valproate, and salvage combinations still cause allergy and CDI.

  • Clinical failure can arise from abscess, empyema, device biofilm, obstruction, ischemic tissue, or underexposure even when the laboratory calls the isolate susceptible.

Contraindications

  • Do not call ertapenem antipseudomonal because it belongs to the carbapenem class; its organism profile has a deliberate nonfermenter gap.

  • Do not treat respiratory colonization as pneumonia without syndrome evidence because Pseudomonas commonly colonizes damaged airways and devices.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain blood and site cultures before therapy when safe, then track source, oxygen/hemodynamics, renal clearance, infusion completion, and culture response because each explains whether the chosen exposure is working.

  • At 24 to 72 hours, stop duplicate Gram-negative coverage and narrow to the most focused active agent when the patient, source, and susceptibility data allow.

Interactions

  • Aminoglycosides may be used initially in selected severe settings but add nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity; compatibility must be verified because beta-lactams can inactivate aminoglycosides in solution.

  • Renal recovery can create augmented clearance and underexposure, while renal failure creates neurotoxicity; the same dose can therefore fail in opposite directions across a septic course.

Key Labs

  • Culture/MIC, prior susceptibility history, carbapenemase testing, creatinine/eGFR, urine output, CBC, and source-specific imaging or respiratory assessment.

  • DTR Pseudomonas is defined by nonsusceptibility to a set of traditional antipseudomonal drugs, not by resistance to one carbapenem alone.

Nclex Traps

  • Ceftriaxone and ertapenem do not provide dependable Pseudomonas coverage.

  • Ceftolozane/tazobactam's Pseudomonas value comes largely from ceftolozane's intrinsic stability and PBP activity, not from tazobactam inhibiting every pseudomonal enzyme.

Population Risks

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric caution

    Note

    Pediatric indication, meningitis role, formulation, dose, interval, renal maturation, and maximum dose differ by agent and age. Use the exact label or pediatric infectious-disease protocol because beta-lactams are not safely scaled from an adult regimen by intuition.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adult caution

    Note

    Older adults can have sharply reduced renal clearance despite an unremarkable serum creatinine. Estimate kidney function, reassess it during acute illness, and connect new confusion, myoclonus, or seizure to possible beta-lactam accumulation rather than assuming delirium is unrelated.

  • Type

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    Many beta-lactams have substantial pregnancy and lactation experience, but infection severity, allergy history, maternal organ function, placental exposure, neonatal bilirubin handling, and the exact agent still matter. Treat serious infection promptly with an evidence-supported regimen rather than applying one class-wide pregnancy rule.
