    Identity and classification

  • Content

    Hepatitis A virus is an acellular, obligately intracellular virus with a positive sense ssrna genome and a non enveloped particle. Envelope status helps explain environmental stability and susceptibility to routine controls, while genome type shapes replication and diagnostic targets. Capsid symmetry is left explicitly unknown in this bounded record rather than inferred without a reviewed taxonomic source.

    Id

    structure-traits

    Title

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Vaccination, hand hygiene, safe food and water, and timely exposure management under current guidance prevent most hepatitis A. The reviewed route description is fecal oral transmission. The reviewed incubation estimate is 15 to 50 days; individual timing can still vary with inoculum, route, host factors, and the syndrome being measured. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    Id

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    HAV reaches the liver after enteric acquisition; immune-mediated hepatocyte injury causes acute hepatitis but does not produce chronic infection. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Id

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Hepatitis A virus is associated with acute hepatitis A, cholestatic hepatitis, and fulminant hepatitis. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Serum IgM anti-HAV supports acute infection, while total antibody reflects current or past immunity and must not be used alone to diagnose acute disease. A positive result can represent infection, colonization, past exposure, residual material, or contamination depending on the method and source; a negative result can reflect timing, specimen quality, prior therapy, or an untested target.

Add transmission-based, public-health, pregnancy, vector, food, water, or occupational measures only when current organism- and setting-specific guidance supports them.
