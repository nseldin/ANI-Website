Class

Oral HIV-1 gp120-directed pre-attachment inhibitor prodrug

Used To Treat

With other active antiretrovirals, multidrug-resistant HIV-1 in heavily treatment-experienced adults whose current regimen is failing because of resistance, intolerance, or safety considerations.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context: current RUKOBIA labeling has no boxed warning. Strong CYP3A inducers are contraindicated because low temsavir exposure can cause virologic failure and resistance. Use caution with long-QT history, cardiac disease or torsades-risk drugs because supratherapeutic exposure prolongs QTc. Monitor immune reconstitution and aminotransferases, especially with hepatitis B or C coinfection.

Mechanism

After phosphatases remove the prodrug group, temsavir occupies a conserved pocket on gp120 near the CD4-binding region, stabilizing envelope in a conformation that cannot productively attach to CD4. Blocking the first receptor-engagement step prevents downstream coreceptor binding and fusion. Resistance substitutions in gp120 can reduce temsavir susceptibility; the drug does not inhibit reverse transcriptase, integrase or protease.

Nursing Essentials

  • Build and document an active companion regimen from resistance history; trend HIV RNA early and regularly rather than assuming a novel mechanism guarantees suppression.

  • Give the 600-mg extended-release tablet twice daily, swallow whole, and review strong inducers, QT drugs, hepatitis status, oral-estrogen dose and adherence barriers.

Key Labs

  • HIV RNA, CD4 count, resistance testing, AST/ALT/bilirubin, HBV/HCV status and viral markers, potassium/magnesium and ECG when QT risk is present.

Nclex Traps

  • Fostemsavir is the prodrug; temsavir is the active gp120 binder.

  • It prevents attachment before CD4 engagement and is not the same as ibalizumab's CD4-directed post-attachment blockade.

Description

Fostemsavir is an oral phosphate prodrug rapidly converted to temsavir, a first-in-class HIV-1 gp120 attachment inhibitor for salvage therapy. Temsavir binds viral envelope gp120 and prevents the conformational interaction needed for gp120 to attach to host CD4. It must be combined with an optimized background regimen and is not routine initial HIV therapy.
