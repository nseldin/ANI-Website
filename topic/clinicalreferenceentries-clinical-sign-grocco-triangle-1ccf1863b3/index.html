Definition

Grocco triangle is a percussion sign. It is a clue to large unilateral pleural effusion. A positive finding means a small triangular area of dullness appears contralaterally.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Grocco triangle means a small triangular area of dullness appears contralaterally.

Why It Matters

A positive Grocco triangle response can signal large unilateral pleural effusion. Mediastinal displacement and transmitted fluid pressure compress the opposite basal lung. Grocco triangle cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large unilateral pleural effusion

Assessment Method

Percuss the paraspinal lower chest on the side opposite a known effusion.

Positive Finding

A small triangular area of dullness appears contralaterally.

Mechanism

Mediastinal displacement and transmitted fluid pressure compress the opposite basal lung.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Grocco triangle response raises concern for large unilateral pleural effusion because mediastinal displacement and transmitted fluid pressure compress the opposite basal lung. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Grocco triangle does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Grocco triangle to focus evaluation of large unilateral pleural effusion and the reviewed alternatives: obesity, paraspinal muscle dullness, bilateral effusions. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Percuss the paraspinal lower chest on the side opposite a known effusion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Grocco triangle as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

The sign is subtle, inconsistently reproduced, and obsolete with imaging. Therefore, Grocco triangle cannot confirm or exclude large unilateral pleural effusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • obesity

  • paraspinal muscle dullness

  • bilateral effusions

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from obesity, paraspinal muscle dullness, bilateral effusions, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Grocco triangle may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical and unreliable. Grocco triangle remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude large unilateral pleural effusion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Grocco triangle: The sign is subtle, inconsistently reproduced, and obsolete with imaging.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Grocco triangle, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Percuss the paraspinal lower chest on the side opposite a known effusion. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not infer effusion volume or side from this sign.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Grocco triangle observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: A small triangular area of dullness appears contralaterally. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Percuss the paraspinal lower chest on the side opposite a known effusion.
