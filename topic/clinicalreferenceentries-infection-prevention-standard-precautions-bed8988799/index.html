Definition

Standard Precautions are the baseline infection-prevention practices used for the care of every patient, regardless of whether infection is known or suspected. They address blood; body fluids, secretions, and excretions except sweat; nonintact skin; mucous membranes; contaminated equipment; and respiratory hygiene risks.

  • Content

    Use them during every patient encounter and choose specific components from the anticipated exposure. Gloves, gowns, masks, eye protection, safe injection technique, sharps safety, respiratory hygiene, equipment reprocessing, and environmental controls are risk-based parts of one baseline system.

    Id

    indications

    Title

    Indications

  • Content

    The approach interrupts transmission before a diagnosis is available. Hand hygiene removes or kills transient organisms; barriers reduce transfer to skin, clothing, eyes, nose, and mouth; aseptic technique prevents introducing organisms into vulnerable sites; and cleaning or reprocessing removes contamination from shared equipment.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Assess the task before contact, perform hand hygiene at the required moments, select personal protective equipment for the expected exposure, use aseptic and injection-safety technique, handle sharps safely, contain contaminated materials, clean shared equipment, and remove PPE without contaminating yourself before performing hand hygiene again.

    Title

    Procedure

  • Content

    Standard Precautions do not mean identical PPE for every task, and they may be insufficient for pathogens requiring Contact, Droplet, Airborne, or other enhanced controls. The patient's syndrome, the care setting, current public-health guidance, and facility policy determine added measures.

    Do not let a negative or pending diagnosis replace exposure assessment. Use the least restrictive measures that safely control transmission, preserve dignity and communication, and never allow isolation equipment to delay urgent stabilization.
