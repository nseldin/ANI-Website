Definition

Frank sign is an observed external-ear sign. It is a clue to long-term cardiovascular risk association. A positive finding means a diagonal crease traverses the earlobe.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Frank sign means a diagonal crease traverses the earlobe.

Why It Matters

The observed Frank sign finding can signal long-term cardiovascular risk association. Shared age-related connective-tissue and microvascular changes may correlate with atherosclerotic burden, but causality is unproven. Frank sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • long-term cardiovascular risk association

Assessment Method

Inspect the earlobes bilaterally.

Positive Finding

A diagonal crease traverses the earlobe.

Mechanism

Shared age-related connective-tissue and microvascular changes may correlate with atherosclerotic burden, but causality is unproven.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Frank sign finding raises concern for long-term cardiovascular risk association because shared age-related connective-tissue and microvascular changes may correlate with atherosclerotic burden, but causality is unproven. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Frank sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Frank sign to focus evaluation of long-term cardiovascular risk association and the reviewed alternatives: sleep crease, scar, normal fold. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect the earlobes bilaterally. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Frank sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, ethnicity, piercing, and normal anatomy confound the association. Therefore, Frank sign cannot confirm or exclude long-term cardiovascular risk association by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • sleep crease

  • scar

  • normal fold

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as sleep crease, scar, normal fold.

False Negative Situations

Frank sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of long-term cardiovascular risk association.

Modern Status

Epidemiologic association; not a diagnostic sign of coronary disease. Interpret Frank sign in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Age, ethnicity, piercing, and normal anatomy confound the association.

Safety Precautions

Observe Frank sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect the earlobes bilaterally. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not alarm a patient or order cardiac testing solely because of an earlobe crease.
