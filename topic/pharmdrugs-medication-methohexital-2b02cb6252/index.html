Class

Ultrashort-acting IV barbiturate GABA-A general anesthetic; Schedule IV

Used To Treat

Induction of anesthesia, brief monitored procedures with minimal painful stimulation, and induction for electroconvulsive therapy when its rapid recovery and relatively limited seizure shortening are useful.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: use only in hospital or ambulatory settings with continuous respiratory and cardiac monitoring, immediate resuscitative drugs, age/size-appropriate bag-mask and intubation equipment, and personnel skilled in airway management. A dedicated observer must continuously monitor deeply sedated patients. Apnea, hypoventilation, laryngospasm and cardiorespiratory arrest can occur.

Mechanism

Methohexital binds barbiturate sites on GABA-A receptors and prolongs chloride-channel opening, hyperpolarizing neurons; at higher concentrations it can directly activate GABA-A current and suppress excitatory transmission. Rapid lipid-soluble brain entry causes fast unconsciousness, while redistribution ends the initial effect. Barbiturate induction can increase porphyrin synthesis by inducing hepatic ALA synthase, explaining the porphyria contraindication.

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm fasting/aspiration plan, porphyria history, airway equipment, continuous ECG/oxygenation/ventilation and a separate analgesia plan before use.

  • For outpatient procedures, ensure recovery criteria and an escort; patients should not drive or perform hazardous tasks for the label-specified interval. For ECT, document seizure quality and hemodynamic recovery.

Contraindications

  • Latent or manifest porphyria, barbiturate hypersensitivity, or a situation in which general anesthesia is contraindicated. Use extreme caution with severe respiratory, cardiovascular, hepatic, renal or endocrine impairment and status asthmaticus.

Interactions

  • Opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol and other CNS depressants amplify apnea and hypotension. Chronic enzyme inducers or tolerance can alter dose response. Other seizure-threshold-changing drugs affect ECT interpretation.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG, blood pressure, oxygenation and ventilation

  • Neurologic and seizure-quality assessment for ECT

  • Hepatic/renal context and pregnancy testing when clinically relevant

Nclex Traps

  • Methohexital is hypnosis, not analgesia.

  • Its rapid first recovery reflects redistribution and does not erase respiratory risk.

  • Porphyria is a true contraindication, not a minor precaution.

Description

Methohexital is an ultrashort barbiturate anesthetic that rapidly produces hypnosis by enhancing GABA-A inhibition. Brain effect ends mainly through redistribution, so recovery can be quick after a bolus, but respiratory depression, hypotension, laryngospasm and repeated-dose accumulation remain possible. Unlike most anesthetics it can facilitate excitatory movement and is often selected for electroconvulsive therapy because it generally preserves seizure duration better than many alternatives.

Adverse Effects

  • Apnea, hypoventilation, laryngospasm, hypotension, tachycardia, injection injury, hiccups, cough, skeletal movement/tremor, seizure-like activity, postoperative drowsiness, nausea/vomiting and shivering.
