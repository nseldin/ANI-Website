Used To Treat

Mechanism-based selection for depression, anxiety, sleep/appetite problems, smoking cessation and avoidance of selected SSRI adverse effects.

Description

These drugs are not interchangeable atypical antidepressants. Bupropion inhibits norepinephrine/dopamine reuptake and nicotinic signaling, making it activating with less sexual dysfunction but dose-related seizure risk. Mirtazapine blocks presynaptic alpha-2, 5-HT2, 5-HT3 and H1 receptors, increasing norepinephrine/serotonin while causing appetite and sedation. Trazodone blocks 5-HT2A, alpha-1 and H1 receptors and weakly inhibits SERT, linking sleepiness and orthostasis to priapism/QT risk. Buspirone is a delayed-onset 5-HT1A partial-agonist anxiolytic without GABA-A reinforcement or reliable immediate panic relief.

Mechanism

Bupropion raises catecholamine signaling through NET/DAT inhibition and reduces nicotine reinforcement through nicotinic acetylcholine-receptor antagonism. Mirtazapine removes alpha-2 inhibitory feedback from norepinephrine and serotonin neurons; 5-HT2/3 blockade channels serotonergic signaling toward 5-HT1A while H1 blockade sedates. Trazodone's 5-HT2A antagonism plus weak SERT inhibition is antidepressant at adequate exposure, while alpha-1/H1 blockade dominates at common low bedtime doses. Buspirone initially activates presynaptic 5-HT1A autoreceptors and, with repeated dosing, changes serotonergic firing; it does not directly enhance GABA like benzodiazepines.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Antidepressants increase suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young-adult patients in short-term studies. Monitor every age group for clinical worsening, emerging suicidality, agitation, or abrupt behavioral change, especially after starting or changing the dose. The suicidality warning applies to bupropion, mirtazapine and trazodone as antidepressants. Buspirone has no boxed warning, but MAOI-related hypertension/serotonin toxicity and delayed onset remain important.

Adverse Effects

  • Bupropion: insomnia, hypertension, tremor and seizures

  • Mirtazapine: somnolence, appetite increase, weight gain and rare neutropenia

  • Trazodone: sedation, orthostasis, QT prolongation and priapism

  • Buspirone: dizziness, nausea, headache and restlessness

Contraindications

  • Bupropion: seizure disorder, eating disorder and abrupt alcohol/sedative withdrawal

  • All relevant agents: prohibited MAOI intervals

  • Trazodone QT/priapism risk and mirtazapine metabolic burden require patient-specific assessment

Nursing Essentials

  • Match the desired effect to the receptor map instead of calling every bedtime medication a sleep drug.

  • Teach that buspirone must be taken consistently for delayed benefit and cannot be used like an as-needed benzodiazepine.

Interactions

  • Bupropion strongly inhibits CYP2D6 and lowers seizure threshold with other drugs.

  • Trazodone and buspirone are CYP3A4 sensitive; serotonergic combinations add toxicity.

  • Alcohol and sedatives magnify mirtazapine/trazodone impairment.

Key Labs

  • Blood pressure, weight/appetite and seizure risk

  • ECG/electrolytes when trazodone QT risk exists

  • CBC if infection symptoms occur with mirtazapine

Nclex Traps

  • Bupropion is not serotonergic and is contraindicated in bulimia/anorexia because seizure risk is amplified.

  • Low-dose trazodone sedation does not mean the same exposure is treating major depression.

  • Buspirone does not terminate an acute panic attack.
