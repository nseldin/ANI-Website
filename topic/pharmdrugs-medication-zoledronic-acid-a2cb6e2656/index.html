Contraindications

  • Reclast: hypocalcemia, CrCl below 35 mL/min, acute renal impairment and serious hypersensitivity

  • Correct vitamin-D/mineral abnormalities and dehydration first

  • Avoid duplicate zoledronic products and use oncology-specific renal adjustment when applicable

Nursing Essentials

  • Before each dose verify product, indication, calcium/vitamin D, creatinine/CrCl, hydration, dental status and recent invasive dental plans.

  • Teach expected brief acute-phase symptoms versus urgent oliguria, jaw nonhealing, hypocalcemic spasm or persistent thigh/groin pain.

Interactions

  • Nephrotoxins and renally cleared drugs increase AKI risk.

  • Aminoglycosides and loop diuretics can compound hypocalcemia.

  • Other antiresorptives add jaw, femur and mineral toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Serum creatinine and Cockcroft-Gault CrCl before each dose

  • Calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and 25-OH vitamin D

  • Dental exam and thigh/groin symptom assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Reclast and Zometa are the same molecule, not interchangeable dosing schedules.

  • Infusion duration and hydration are renal-safety interventions.

  • Atypical femur fracture can announce itself as weeks of thigh or groin pain before complete fracture.

Used To Treat

Reclast regimens treat/prevent selected osteoporosis and treat Paget disease; Zometa/oncology regimens treat hypercalcemia of malignancy and skeletal complications of selected cancers. Products contain the same active molecule but use different doses, intervals and renal rules.

Mechanism

The P-C-P bisphosphonate backbone chelates bone mineral and concentrates drug beneath osteoclasts. Inhibiting farnesyl-pyrophosphate synthase in the mevalonate pathway prevents prenylation of Ras/Rho/Rac-family small GTPases needed for cytoskeletal attachment, vesicle trafficking and acid secretion. The osteoclast detaches or undergoes apoptosis. Long skeletal retention explains durable effect and why toxicity/benefit do not end when plasma infusion clears.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: For Reclast, hypocalcemia, CrCl below 35 mL/min or acute renal impairment are contraindications; calculate Cockcroft-Gault CrCl before every dose, hydrate appropriately and infuse over at least 15 minutes. Do not give Reclast with Zometa because both are zoledronic acid. Complete an oral exam and investigate thigh/groin pain.

Description

Zoledronic acid is a potent IV nitrogen bisphosphonate that binds hydroxyapatite at active bone-remodeling surfaces. Osteoclasts ingest it while resorbing bone; intracellular inhibition of farnesyl-pyrophosphate synthase then disables protein prenylation, ruffled-border function and survival. Resorption falls for months to years, but hypocalcemia, renal injury, jaw osteonecrosis and atypical femur fracture require precise screening.

Adverse Effects

  • Acute-phase fever, myalgia, arthralgia, headache and influenza-like symptoms after infusion

  • Hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia and hypomagnesemia

  • Acute kidney injury or renal failure

  • Osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femoral fracture and severe musculoskeletal pain
