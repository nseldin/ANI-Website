Definition

Winterbottom sign is a palpated lymph-node sign. It is a clue to West African trypanosomiasis. A positive finding means painless rubbery posterior cervical nodes are enlarged.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Winterbottom sign means painless rubbery posterior cervical nodes are enlarged.

Why It Matters

The observed Winterbottom sign finding can signal West African trypanosomiasis. Trypanosome proliferation and immune activation enlarge lymphoid tissue in the posterior neck. Winterbottom sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • West African trypanosomiasis

Assessment Method

Palpate posterior cervical lymph nodes.

Positive Finding

Painless rubbery posterior cervical nodes are enlarged.

Mechanism

Trypanosome proliferation and immune activation enlarge lymphoid tissue in the posterior neck.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Winterbottom sign finding raises concern for West African trypanosomiasis because trypanosome proliferation and immune activation enlarge lymphoid tissue in the posterior neck. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Winterbottom sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Winterbottom sign to focus evaluation of West African trypanosomiasis and the reviewed alternatives: EBV infection, HIV, reactive adenopathy. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate posterior cervical lymph nodes. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Winterbottom sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Nodes are nonspecific and may be absent. Therefore, Winterbottom sign cannot confirm or exclude West African trypanosomiasis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • EBV infection

  • HIV

  • reactive adenopathy

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as EBV infection, HIV, reactive adenopathy.

False Negative Situations

Winterbottom sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of West African trypanosomiasis.
