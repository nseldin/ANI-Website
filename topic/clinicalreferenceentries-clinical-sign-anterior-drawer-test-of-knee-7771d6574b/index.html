Definition

Anterior drawer test of knee is a bedside knee-stability maneuver. It is a clue to anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. A positive finding means the tibia translates excessively or has a soft endpoint.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Anterior drawer test of knee means the tibia translates excessively or has a soft endpoint.

Why It Matters

A positive Anterior drawer test of knee response can signal anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. The ACL normally limits forward tibial movement, especially in flexion. Anterior drawer test of knee cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency

Assessment Method

Flex the knee to 90 degrees, stabilize the foot, and draw the proximal tibia forward after checking starting position.

Positive Finding

The tibia translates excessively or has a soft endpoint.

Mechanism

The ACL normally limits forward tibial movement, especially in flexion.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Anterior drawer test of knee response raises concern for anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency because the ACL normally limits forward tibial movement, especially in flexion. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Anterior drawer test of knee does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Anterior drawer test of knee to focus evaluation of anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: PCL tear, generalized laxity, meniscal pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex the knee to 90 degrees, stabilize the foot, and draw the proximal tibia forward after checking starting position. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Anterior drawer test of knee as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Hamstring guarding and an unrecognized posteriorly sagged tibia can mislead. Therefore, Anterior drawer test of knee cannot confirm or exclude anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • PCL tear

  • generalized laxity

  • meniscal pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from PCL tear, generalized laxity, meniscal pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Anterior drawer test of knee may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; less sensitive acutely than Lachman. When performed as described, Anterior drawer test of knee can adjust concern for anterior cruciate ligament insufficiency, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Anterior drawer test of knee: Hamstring guarding and an unrecognized posteriorly sagged tibia can mislead.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Anterior drawer test of knee, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Flex the knee to 90 degrees, stabilize the foot, and draw the proximal tibia forward after checking starting position. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Correct posterior sag before interpreting anterior movement.
