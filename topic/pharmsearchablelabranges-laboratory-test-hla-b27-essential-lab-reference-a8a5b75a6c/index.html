Range

HLA-B27 is usually reported as present/positive or absent/negative rather than with a numeric reference interval. Use the performing laboratory's method and report; the background frequency of HLA-B27 differs among populations, so one result does not provide a universal disease probability.

Why

A positive HLA-B27 result is associated with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA, inflammatory arthritis affecting the sacroiliac joints and spine) and can support evaluation when the history and examination fit; many healthy people are positive, and some people with axSpA are negative. Assess age at symptom onset, back or buttock pain and stiffness that worsen with rest and improve with movement, peripheral-joint or heel symptoms, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, recent infection, uveitis, and family history. Evaluation can include CRP/ESR and sacroiliac radiographs or MRI with rheumatology review. Escalate urgently for new eye pain, red eye, photophobia, blurred vision, or vision loss because uveitis can threaten vision. A positive HLA-B27 result does not diagnose axSpA by itself, and a negative result does not rule it out.

High Causes

  • Axial spondyloarthritis, including ankylosing spondylitis, when the clinical picture fits

  • Reactive arthritis

  • Psoriatic arthritis with spondyloarthritis features

  • Arthritis associated with Crohn disease or ulcerative colitis

  • Acute anterior uveitis association

  • Healthy carrier state; many positive people never develop disease

Low Causes

  • HLA-B27 absent; this lowers support for some spondyloarthritis patterns but does not exclude axial spondyloarthritis.

Critical Concerns

  • New eye pain, red eye, photophobia, blurred vision, or vision loss requires prompt eye assessment because untreated uveitis can damage vision.

  • Severe back pain with fever, a major neurologic change, or rapid deterioration requires urgent evaluation for causes beyond a spondyloarthritis marker.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess age at symptom onset, pain pattern, morning stiffness, function, peripheral-joint or heel symptoms, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, recent infection, uveitis, family history, and current anti-inflammatory or immune-modifying therapy.

  • Review CRP/ESR and sacroiliac radiograph or MRI findings with the examination and rheumatology plan; do not interpret HLA-B27 in isolation.

Nclex Traps

  • A positive HLA-B27 result supports a compatible clinical evaluation but is not a diagnosis; many healthy people are positive.

  • A negative HLA-B27 result does not exclude axial spondyloarthritis.

  • Eye pain, redness, photophobia, or vision change is a uveitis priority, not a routine arthritis symptom.
