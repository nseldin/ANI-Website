Definition

Muscle spasticity is a velocity-dependent increase in muscle tone: a relaxed limb resists passive stretch more strongly when it is moved faster. It is an upper-motor-neuron sign and may include a catch, clasp-knife release, brisk reflexes, or clonus.

Plain Language

Muscle spasticity means a muscle becomes abnormally tight during movement, especially when the limb is moved quickly.

Why It Matters

Muscle spasticity can interfere with walking, transfers, hygiene, sleep, comfort, skin protection, and joint movement and may contribute to contracture. A new focal pattern can signal acute brain or spinal-cord disease, while sudden worsening can be triggered by pain, infection, urinary retention, constipation, or medication change and needs cause-directed assessment.

Associated Conditions

Assessment Method

Position the patient safely and ask them to relax, then move each joint gently through passive range at slow and faster speeds while comparing sides; document the point of catch, degree of resistance, pattern, pain, clonus, reflexes, strength, function, skin risk, medications, and whether fixed contracture is present.

Positive Finding

Passive movement produces abnormally increased resistance that becomes greater at faster stretch speeds, sometimes with a catch or clasp-knife release.

Mechanism

Loss of descending inhibitory control makes spinal stretch-reflex circuits overresponsive, so faster muscle lengthening triggers greater involuntary contraction.

Evidence Limitations

Anxiety, pain, guarding, improper speed, joint disease, rigidity, and contracture can alter resistance; a scale score does not by itself identify the lesion or measure function. Therefore, Muscle spasticity cannot confirm or exclude upper-motor-neuron pathway dysfunction from stroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, traumatic brain injury, or other central nervous system disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as contracture, rigidity, paratonia, pain guarding.

False Negative Situations

Muscle spasticity may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of upper-motor-neuron pathway dysfunction from stroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, traumatic brain injury, or other central nervous system disease.

Modern Status

Current upper-motor-neuron sign; severity varies with position, speed, pain, infection, bladder or bowel problems, medication timing, and fatigue, and it must be distinguished from rigidity and fixed contracture.
