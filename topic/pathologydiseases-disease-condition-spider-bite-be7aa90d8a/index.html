Definition

Spider bite develops when venom or local tissue injury produces a spectrum from minor pain and inflammation to necrosis or systemic neurotoxic effects depending on species and exposure. The characteristic pattern is a witnessed bite or compatible local lesion, with severe cramping and autonomic findings suggesting widow venom and progressive necrosis suggesting recluse injury. Confirm or stage it with clinical identification from exposure, geography, lesion evolution, pain, and systemic findings; distinguish it from community-acquired MRSA, cellulitis, contact reaction, vasculitis, tick bite, and pressure injury because many presumed spider bites are not witnessed. Signs Symptoms

  • Timeline and trigger matter: exposure, ingestion, submersion, temperature stress, anesthesia, procedure, bite, or postoperative immobility plus vital-sign, neurologic, respiratory, skin, pain, and perfusion changes.

  • Escalate altered mental status, airway trouble, shock, dysrhythmia, severe temperature abnormality, rapidly spreading tissue injury, respiratory distress, or worsening pain/swelling.

Diagnostics

  • clinical identification from exposure, geography, lesion evolution, pain, and systemic findings

  • CBC, hemolysis, kidney, coagulation, or muscle testing only when systemic toxicity is suspected

  • culture only when purulence or another infection is present

  • Distinguish Spider bite from community-acquired MRSA, cellulitis, contact reaction, vasculitis, tick bite, and pressure injury because many presumed spider bites are not witnessed.

Labs

  • lactate may rise with shock

  • glucose can be immediately reversible

  • potassium and acid-base shifts can kill quickly

  • drug levels matter when available

Treatments

  • clean the wound and provide analgesia and tetanus care

  • treat muscle spasm or systemic toxicity supportively and seek poison-center guidance

  • manage necrotic injury with serial assessment rather than early aggressive excision

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate immediately for dyspnea, facial or tongue swelling, severe generalized cramps, chest pain, shock, rapidly expanding necrosis, fever, jaundice, dark urine, falling hemoglobin, or oliguria because anaphylaxis, severe envenomation, sepsis, hemolysis, or kidney injury may be present.

  • Record bite time and setting, safely obtained spider photograph, local pain and skin change, allergies, medicines, tetanus status, and competing diagnoses because many presumed spider bites are bacterial infections or other lesions rather than confirmed envenomation.

  • Wash with soap and water, apply wrapped cool packs intermittently, elevate the limb, remove constrictive jewelry, and mark erythema borders because simple wound care limits swelling and makes progression measurable without damaging tissue.

  • Trend vital signs, pain spread, muscle cramping, diaphoresis, abdominal rigidity, wound color, blistering, urine color, hemoglobin, and kidney function as indicated because widow neurotoxicity and recluse-associated necrosis or hemolysis follow different time courses.

  • Contact poison control or a toxicology service for systemic symptoms and provide prescribed analgesia, tetanus prophylaxis, wound care, or antivenom while monitoring reactions because expert species- and severity-based management prevents unnecessary cutting, suction, antibiotics, or surgery.

Red Flags

  • Dyspnea, wheeze, facial swelling, hypotension, or other anaphylaxis findings

  • Severe generalized muscle cramping, abdominal rigidity, chest pain, or autonomic instability

  • Rapidly expanding blister, dusky skin, necrosis, spreading erythema, or fever

  • Jaundice, dark urine, falling hemoglobin, reduced urine output, or acute kidney injury

Patient Education

  • Tell the patient not to cut, squeeze, suction, burn, or apply an unwrapped ice pack because those measures add tissue injury without reliably removing venom.

  • Teach daily wound photographs and prompt reassessment for expanding pain, discoloration, fever, jaundice, or dark urine because serious effects can appear after the initial bite.

Nclex Traps

  • Most unexplained necrotic skin lesions are not proven spider bites

  • Do not incise, suction, or apply destructive chemicals to the wound
