Used To Treat

Reduction of sickle-cell complications through long-term disease modification. Hydroxyurea reduces crises and related complications through several effects including higher fetal hemoglobin. Crizanlizumab reduces vaso-occlusive-crisis frequency by blocking P-selectin-mediated cellular adhesion. Neither is an immediate analgesic or a substitute for acute chest syndrome and vaso-occlusive crisis care.

Description

Sickle cell vaso-occlusion is not only a misshapen-red-cell problem. Deoxygenated HbS polymerizes, cells dehydrate and stiffen, leukocytes and platelets activate, endothelium expresses adhesion molecules, and microvascular flow slows until ischemia and inflammation reinforce one another. Hydroxyurea raises fetal hemoglobin, which does not participate in HbS polymer formation, and also lowers neutrophil burden, improves red-cell hydration and deformability, and reduces adhesion. Crizanlizumab blocks P-selectin, limiting the tethering interactions among activated endothelium, leukocytes, platelets, and sickled cells. The drugs target different points in the same pathologic loop.

Mechanism

Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase by quenching the catalytic tyrosyl radical in its RRM2 subunit, reducing deoxyribonucleotide production and DNA synthesis. In sickle cell disease, the complete HbF-induction mechanism is not fully defined, but higher HbF dilutes HbS within red cells and interrupts polymer growth; lower neutrophil counts, increased red-cell water, better deformability, nitric-oxide-related signaling, and reduced adhesion also contribute. Crizanlizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody against P-selectin. By blocking P-selectin binding to PSGL-1 and related ligands, it reduces rolling and adhesion of leukocytes and platelets on activated endothelium. Less cellular traffic and sticking reduces the chance that sluggish sickled blood becomes a vaso-occlusive plug.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Hydroxyurea can cause severe myelosuppression, embryo-fetal harm, and long-term malignancy risk; CBC-based holding and reproductive precautions are central. Crizanlizumab can cause serious infusion-related reactions that may resemble or trigger severe pain, and it can interfere with automated platelet counts through in-vitro platelet clumping.

Adverse Effects

  • Myelosuppression, macrocytosis, GI/skin effects, leg ulcers, and reproductive or malignancy concerns with hydroxyurea

  • Infusion reaction, fever, nausea, arthralgia, back pain, or severe pain during crizanlizumab infusion

  • Breakthrough vaso-occlusion because neither therapy eliminates HbS or all inflammatory triggers

Contraindications

  • Do not use either as sole treatment for an acute vaso-occlusive crisis, stroke, splenic sequestration, or acute chest syndrome.

  • Hydroxyurea requires CBC recovery, renal dose awareness, and pregnancy/reproductive assessment.

  • Crizanlizumab infusion reactions require immediate severity-based management rather than assuming every new pain episode is baseline disease.

Nursing Essentials

  • For hydroxyurea, trend CBC/reticulocytes, HbF/MCV adherence clues, renal function, and crisis frequency; macrocytosis is expected but does not replace adherence assessment.

  • For crizanlizumab, monitor through and after infusion, distinguish infusion reaction from evolving crisis, and send a promptly analyzed citrate or heparin specimen if EDTA platelet clumping produces a false low count.

Interactions

  • Other marrow suppressants add cytopenia to hydroxyurea; live vaccines and infection risk require individualized review.

  • Crizanlizumab can be used with hydroxyurea under a specialist plan because the mechanisms differ, but infusion and laboratory-interference risks remain.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential, reticulocytes, hemoglobin fraction/HbF, renal/liver function, pregnancy status, and crisis/transfusion history

  • Accurate platelet count method during crizanlizumab, oxygenation, chest symptoms, neurologic findings, pain pattern, and organ-damage surveillance

Nclex Traps

  • Hydroxyurea is not only a chemotherapy label; in sickle cell disease its clinically useful effects include HbF increase and reduced inflammatory cellular burden.

  • Crizanlizumab blocks adhesion, not HbS polymerization, and it does not replace acute crisis treatment.
