Definition

Shiga toxins are related bacterial protein toxins produced by Shigella dysenteriae type 1 and by Shiga toxin-producing E. coli. The abbreviations Stx1 and Stx2 identify major toxin families; production is a pathotype property, not a feature of every E. coli strain.

    Shiga toxins are related bacterial protein toxins produced by Shigella dysenteriae type 1 and by Shiga toxin-producing E. coli. The abbreviations Stx1 and Stx2 identify major toxin families; production is a pathotype property, not a feature of every E. coli strain.

    definition

    Definition

  • Content

    The binding component recognizes susceptible cell-surface glycolipids such as Gb3, allowing the enzymatic component to enter cells and damage 28S ribosomal RNA. Protein synthesis stops, and endothelial injury is especially important in small vessels. In the kidney, microvascular injury, platelet activation, and red-cell fragmentation help produce the anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury of hemolytic uremic syndrome.

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Bloody diarrhea followed by pallor, reduced urine output, bruising, or fatigue raises concern for STEC-associated HUS. Stool evaluation should include Shiga toxin or toxin-gene testing in addition to culture according to current laboratory guidance.

  • Content

    The mechanism creates an important treatment consequence: in suspected STEC infection, antibiotics may increase HUS risk and should not be given reflexively. Hydration and close monitoring for renal and hematologic complications are central while diagnosis and clinical severity are evaluated.

    Shiga toxin is not an identity alias for E. coli, and not all STEC infections progress to HUS. Toxin detection must be interpreted with the clinical syndrome and specimen timing; this card does not substitute for pathogen-specific testing or emergency management.
