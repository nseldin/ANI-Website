Used To Treat

Under the current U.S. MIFEPREX regimen, medication termination of an intrauterine pregnancy through 70 days gestation using mifepristone followed 24 to 48 hours later by buccal misoprostol, with access to follow-up and emergency care. Other evidence-based obstetric uses follow separate protocols and product contexts.

Description

The two drugs perform complementary biological steps. Progesterone maintains decidual tissue, keeps the cervix relatively closed, and reduces myometrial sensitivity. Mifepristone competitively blocks progesterone receptors, destabilizing the decidua, separating the gestational sac, softening the cervix, and increasing uterine responsiveness to prostaglandins. Misoprostol is then converted to a prostaglandin E1 analogue that ripens the cervix and produces uterine contractions that expel tissue. The sequence is more effective than either mechanism alone. It does not treat ectopic pregnancy because there is no intrauterine gestation to evacuate, and expected bleeding must be distinguished from hemorrhage or incomplete abortion.

Mechanism

Mifepristone binds progesterone receptors with high affinity and prevents progesterone-dependent transcription. Decidual support and capillary integrity break down, endogenous prostaglandin release rises, cervical collagen remodels, and myometrial gap-junction and prostaglandin responsiveness increase. Mifepristone also antagonizes glucocorticoid receptors at different exposures, which explains the separate high-dose Korlym indication and adrenal-risk profile; formulations and regimens are not interchangeable. Misoprostol is de-esterified to misoprostol acid, which activates EP prostanoid receptors. In the cervix it promotes collagen degradation and water uptake; in myometrium it increases contractile calcium signaling. Coordinated contraction plus cervical opening produces evacuation.

Boxed Warning

Mifepristone 200 mg labeling carries a boxed warning for rare serious and sometimes fatal infection or bleeding and is distributed through the Mifepristone REMS Program. Serious infection can present atypically without fever or striking pelvic findings; persistent severe pain, malaise more than 24 hours after misoprostol, syncope, sustained fever, or heavy prolonged bleeding requires urgent assessment. Follow-up must confirm completion. Misoprostol carries a boxed warning that pregnancy exposure can cause abortion, premature birth, congenital injury, or uterine rupture; rupture risk rises with later gestation and prior uterine surgery. Neither drug treats ectopic pregnancy.

Adverse Effects

  • Cramping, bleeding, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, dizziness, fever, and chills

  • Hemorrhage, incomplete abortion, ongoing pregnancy, serious infection or sepsis, uterine rupture in higher-risk contexts, and delayed diagnosis of ectopic pregnancy

Contraindications

  • Suspected or confirmed ectopic pregnancy, chronic adrenal failure or long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy for the MIFEPREX regimen, bleeding disorder or anticoagulant therapy when prohibited, inherited porphyria, IUD still in place, or inability to access emergency care.

  • Confirm gestational age, intrauterine location when clinically indicated, medication list, hemoglobin/bleeding risk, and the exact product/regimen rather than treating all mifepristone use as one indication.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give precise expectations for timing, pain and bleeding, emergency thresholds, contact pathway, and follow-up confirmation. Vague advice such as expect bleeding is unsafe.

  • Assess emotional and physical needs without judgment, preserve privacy, and use trauma-informed communication while maintaining rigorous ectopic, hemorrhage, and infection surveillance.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A modifiers can alter mifepristone exposure; chronic corticosteroids may be antagonized because mifepristone also blocks glucocorticoid receptors.

  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs can magnify bleeding; other uterotonics or prostaglandins can add contraction effects under specialist protocols.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy dating and location assessment as indicated, baseline hemoglobin when bleeding risk warrants it, Rh testing and prophylaxis according to current protocol, and follow-up confirmation of completion

  • Bleeding quantity and duration, pain trajectory, vital signs, syncope, fever, malaise, abdominal tenderness, and pregnancy symptoms that persist

Nclex Traps

  • Mifepristone weakens progesterone support; misoprostol supplies the cervical-ripening and contraction signal. The order reflects the biology.

  • Minimal bleeding can signal failure or ectopic pregnancy, while heavy bleeding can signal hemorrhage or incomplete abortion; neither extreme should be dismissed.
