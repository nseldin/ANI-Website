Used To Treat

Manifestations of psychotic disorders; decanoate provides long-acting maintenance after response and tolerability are established. It is not proven useful for nonpsychotic anxiety or routine behavior control.

Mechanism

Mesolimbic D2 antagonism reduces positive psychotic symptoms. Nigrostriatal blockade shifts striatal dopamine-acetylcholine balance toward cholinergic activity, causing dystonia, akathisia and parkinsonism; chronic adaptation can produce tardive dyskinesia. Tuberoinfundibular D2 blockade raises prolactin. Esterification of fluphenazine with decanoic acid increases lipid solubility, creating an intramuscular depot that hydrolyzes gradually to active fluphenazine; this changes pharmacokinetics, not the receptor mechanism. Some alpha-1 and other phenothiazine effects still cause hypotension, sedation, photosensitivity and anticholinergic symptoms.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Fluphenazine is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. High EPS/tardive-dyskinesia burden, NMS and prolonged depot exposure require structured movement surveillance and oral tolerability assessment before long-acting therapy.

Contraindications

  • Severe CNS depression/coma, suspected subcortical brain damage, blood dyscrasia or significant liver damage under labeling

  • Phenothiazine hypersensitivity

  • Do not initiate depot therapy before assessing response, tolerability and ability to follow up

Nursing Essentials

  • Document oral dose/response, last injection, injection interval and emerging adverse effects; depot symptoms can reflect accumulation.

  • Perform AIMS and acute EPS checks, and distinguish akathisia from psychiatric agitation before escalating the dose.

Interactions

  • Other D2 blockers add EPS/NMS risk.

  • Anticholinergics can mask worsening tardive movements and add cognitive/GI toxicity.

  • CNS depressants, antihypertensives and QT-prolonging agents add sedation, hypotension or dysrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • AIMS and EPS exam

  • CBC/liver tests when indicated

  • ECG/electrolytes and prolactin assessment based on risk/symptoms

Nclex Traps

  • Long acting does not mean rapidly acting.

  • Once injected, depot exposure cannot be taken back; early intolerance matters.

  • High potency predicts EPS, not stronger ultimate antipsychotic benefit.

Description

Fluphenazine is a high-potency phenothiazine antipsychotic whose strong D2 blockade produces antipsychotic efficacy with less alpha-1, H1 and muscarinic burden than chlorpromazine but more prominent EPS, akathisia and prolactin effects. Oral drug is used to establish response; the decanoate ester releases slowly from muscle for maintenance. A long-acting injection improves exposure consistency but cannot be removed once given, so a wrong dose or unrecognized intolerance can persist for weeks.

Adverse Effects

  • Dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism and tardive dyskinesia

  • Hyperprolactinemia and sexual/reproductive effects

  • NMS, seizures, sedation, orthostasis and QT risk

  • Phenothiazine photosensitivity, liver injury or blood dyscrasias
