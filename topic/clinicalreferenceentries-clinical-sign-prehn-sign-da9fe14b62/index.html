Definition

Prehn sign is a bedside scrotal-pain maneuver. It is a clue to historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion. A positive finding means pain relief was historically considered supportive of epididymitis.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Prehn sign means pain relief was historically considered supportive of epididymitis.

Why It Matters

A positive Prehn sign response can signal historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion. Elevation may reduce traction on an inflamed epididymis, but the mechanisms overlap. Prehn sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion

Assessment Method

Gently support and elevate the painful testis only as part of examination.

Positive Finding

Pain relief was historically considered supportive of epididymitis.

Mechanism

Elevation may reduce traction on an inflamed epididymis, but the mechanisms overlap.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Prehn sign response raises concern for historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion because elevation may reduce traction on an inflamed epididymis, but the mechanisms overlap. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Prehn sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Prehn sign to focus evaluation of historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion and the reviewed alternatives: torsion of appendage, hernia, renal colic. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently support and elevate the painful testis only as part of examination. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Prehn sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Both torsion and infection can improve or worsen with elevation. Therefore, Prehn sign cannot confirm or exclude historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • torsion of appendage

  • hernia

  • renal colic

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from torsion of appendage, hernia, renal colic, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Prehn sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Outdated and unreliable; cannot rule torsion in or out. Prehn sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude historical differentiation of epididymitis from testicular torsion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Prehn sign: Both torsion and infection can improve or worsen with elevation.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Prehn sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Gently support and elevate the painful testis only as part of examination. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never delay emergency torsion management based on pain response.
