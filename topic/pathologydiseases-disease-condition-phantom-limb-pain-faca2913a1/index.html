Definition

Phantom limb pain is a neuropathic pain syndrome in which maladaptive peripheral nerve, spinal, and cortical signaling generates real pain perceived in an amputated body part. The characteristic pattern is burning, shooting, cramping, twisting, or electric pain in the absent limb, distinguished from nonpainful phantom sensation and residual-limb pain. Confirm or stage it with clinical pain characterization with residual-limb skin, socket, neuroma, vascular, infection, and musculoskeletal examination; distinguish it from stump infection, ischemia, neuroma, prosthetic pressure injury, radiculopathy, complex regional pain, and nonpainful phantom sensation by examination and distribution. Signs Symptoms

  • Pain, swelling, deformity, wound depth, drainage, odor, tissue color, blistering, numbness/tingling, pulses, movement, sensation, capillary refill, fever, or mechanism of injury shapes tissue-risk assessment.

  • Escalate absent pulses, compartment syndrome signs, evisceration, circumferential burn compromise, spreading infection, necrosis, or pain out of proportion.

Diagnostics

  • clinical pain characterization with residual-limb skin, socket, neuroma, vascular, infection, and musculoskeletal examination

  • functional, sleep, mood, trauma, and medication assessment

  • imaging or electrodiagnostics only when a residual structural cause is suspected

  • Distinguish Phantom limb pain from stump infection, ischemia, neuroma, prosthetic pressure injury, radiculopathy, complex regional pain, and nonpainful phantom sensation by examination and distribution.

Labs

  • CK and potassium may rise with muscle breakdown

  • WBC may rise with infection

  • lactate may rise with shock

  • albumin/prealbumin may reflect nutrition risk

Treatments

  • use mirror or graded motor therapy and rehabilitation

  • apply individualized neuropathic and nonopioid pain treatment

  • correct socket, neuroma, infection, vascular, mood, and sleep contributors

Nursing Priorities

  • Ask the patient to locate and describe phantom pain, nonpainful phantom sensation, residual-limb pain, back or opposite-limb pain, triggers, sleep, mood, and function because these distinct pain generators require different treatment and all are real experiences after amputation.

  • Inspect the residual limb and prosthesis interface for pressure, erythema, blister, ulcer, infection, edema, neuroma tenderness, ischemia, contracture, and socket fit because a treatable local problem can amplify pain that the patient perceives in the missing limb.

  • Use a consistent phantom-limb pain and functional scale before and after prescribed multimodal amputation-pain therapy and monitor sedation, constipation, falls, mood, and medication misuse because meaningful treatment should improve sleep and participation rather than only lower a number.

  • Coordinate mirror therapy or graded motor imagery, desensitization, positioning, range-of-motion work, prosthetic adjustment, and psychological support because recalibrating sensorimotor input and restoring function can reduce pain without relying solely on medicines.

  • Escalate after amputation for suicidal thinking, uncontrolled phantom-limb pain with autonomic distress, rapidly spreading residual-limb infection, black or cool tissue, new severe swelling, or sudden dyspnea because psychiatric crisis, sepsis, ischemia, thrombosis, or embolism must not be mislabeled as phantom pain.

Complications

  • chronic disability can complicate Phantom limb pain.

  • depression can complicate Phantom limb pain.

  • sleep loss can complicate Phantom limb pain.

  • falls can complicate Phantom limb pain.

  • medication toxicity can complicate Phantom limb pain.

  • prosthesis abandonment can complicate Phantom limb pain.

Red Flags

  • Suicidal thinking, inability to remain safe, or severe uncontrolled pain crisis

  • Spreading redness, purulent drainage, fever, wound breakdown, or exposed tissue

  • Cool pale or black residual limb, absent pulse, or rapidly worsening ischemic pain

  • Acute limb swelling with chest pain, sudden dyspnea, or hemoptysis

Patient Education

  • Phantom pain is generated by the nervous system and is not imaginary; describe its pattern separately from socket or wound pain because each type has different useful treatments.

  • Check the residual limb with a mirror every day and stop using a prosthesis that causes persistent redness, blistering, or skin breakdown because pressure injury can progress without normal sensation.

Nclex Traps

  • The pain is real even though the limb is absent

  • Escalating opioids alone does not address the reorganized sensorimotor mechanism
