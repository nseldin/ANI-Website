Definition

Wheal means a smooth, raised, temporary area of skin swelling caused by fluid in the superficial dermis (the supportive skin layer beneath the surface). It is commonly called a hive.

Plain Language

A wheal is a temporary swollen hive that usually fades or moves within hours.

Why It Matters

Wheals commonly indicate urticaria, but airway swelling, breathing difficulty, low blood pressure, or rapidly progressive symptoms can signal anaphylaxis and require emergency treatment. A painful lesion lasting longer than 24 hours or leaving bruising is atypical and needs another explanation.

Associated Conditions

  • superficial dermal edema, usually from mast-cell mediator release in urticaria, allergic reactions, physical triggers, medications, infection, or other inflammatory causes

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate lesions, mark or photograph their borders when appropriate, document itch or burning, color, central pallor, size, distribution, migration, and duration of each lesion, and immediately assess the lips, tongue, voice, swallowing, breathing, blood pressure, and other signs of angioedema or anaphylaxis.

Positive Finding

A smooth, raised, transient area of skin edema appears, often with central pallor and a red rim; an individual uncomplicated urticarial wheal usually fades within 24 hours without scale or residual bruising.

Mechanism

Histamine and other mediators increase permeability in superficial dermal vessels, allowing fluid to collect temporarily in the dermis and lift the skin.
