Used To Treat

Lower elevated intraocular pressure in open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension; acute angle closure uses an urgent multimodal sequence and definitive angle treatment rather than routine outpatient-drop logic alone.

Description

Glaucoma drugs protect the optic nerve by changing aqueous-humor flow. Prostaglandin analogs increase mainly uveoscleral outflow; Rho-kinase inhibitors and pilocarpine improve trabecular drainage by different mechanisms; beta blockers, alpha-2 agonists and carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors reduce ciliary-body aqueous production. Matching the pathway makes combinations understandable and exposes duplicate mechanisms.

Mechanism

Aqueous humor is secreted by ciliary epithelium, enters the posterior chamber, passes through the pupil and exits mostly through trabecular meshwork/Schlemm canal or the uveoscleral route. Intraocular pressure reflects production minus outflow. Lowering pressure slows risk; it does not restore optic-nerve fibers already lost, so adherence and visual-field/optic-nerve follow-up matter even when the eye feels normal.

Boxed Warning

Eye drops can produce systemic toxicity through nasolacrimal absorption. Timolol can cause fatal bronchospasm or cardiac decompensation; brimonidine can cause profound CNS depression in infants; carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors can cause sulfonamide reactions; miotics can impair dark vision and rarely precede retinal tear; prostaglandins can permanently darken the iris. Choose by comorbidity, not pressure alone.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify open-angle versus acute angle-closure physiology, baseline pressure, optic-nerve/field damage, asthma/COPD, pulse/heart block, renal function, corneal disease, uveitis, lens status and all current eye drops before administration.

  • Verify the exact eye, formulation, concentration, frequency, contact-lens instructions, and whether other drops must be separated by at least 5 minutes. Never let the bottle tip touch the eye, lashes, fingers, or another surface.

  • After the drop, gently close the eyelid and press the nasolacrimal duct for 1 to 2 minutes when appropriate. This reduces drainage into the nose and systemic exposure; wiping excess from the skin also limits local irritation.

Key Labs

  • Intraocular pressure, optic-nerve examination, visual fields and adherence; pulse/blood pressure and respiratory status with beta or alpha agonists; renal/acid-base context with carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors; retinal/corneal assessment when symptoms arise.

Nclex Traps

  • Open-angle glaucoma is often painless; absence of symptoms does not mean pressure control is unimportant.

  • Topical timolol is still a systemic beta-blocker exposure.

  • Punctal occlusion is a safety intervention, not merely a comfort tip.
