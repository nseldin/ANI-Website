Definition

Tachypnea is an observed respiratory-rate sign in which breathing is faster than the expected resting range for the patient's age and situation. In a typical resting adult, it commonly means more than 20 breaths per minute.

Plain Language

Tachypnea means breathing faster than expected.

Why It Matters

A rising respiratory rate can be an early warning of hypoxemia, infection, sepsis, pulmonary embolism, acidosis, heart or lung disease, pain, or other deterioration. It can also occur normally with exercise, pregnancy, fever, or anxiety, so the rate must be interpreted with effort, depth, oxygenation, mental status, and the patient's trend.

Assessment Method

Count respirations for a full minute when possible without coaching the pattern; document breaths per minute, age, rest or activity, temperature, depth, rhythm, work of breathing, ability to speak, oxygenation, mental status, pain, medication exposure, and change from baseline.

Positive Finding

The measured resting respiratory rate is above the expected range for the patient's age and situation; in a typical resting adult this is commonly more than 20 breaths per minute.

Mechanism

Chemical, neural, thermal, emotional, or mechanical signals increase respiratory drive, and some disorders raise rate to improve ventilation or compensate for smaller tidal breaths, hypoxemia, carbon dioxide change, or metabolic acidosis.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Tachypnea finding raises concern for a respiratory rate above the age- and context-appropriate resting range from exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, pregnancy, hypoxemia, acidosis, infection, sepsis, pulmonary or cardiac disease, medication, or early clinical deterioration because chemical, neural, thermal, emotional, or mechanical signals increase respiratory drive, and some disorders raise rate to improve ventilation or compensate for smaller tidal breaths, hypoxemia, carbon dioxide change, or metabolic acidosis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Tachypnea does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Tachypnea to focus evaluation of a respiratory rate above the age- and context-appropriate resting range from exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, pregnancy, hypoxemia, acidosis, infection, sepsis, pulmonary or cardiac disease, medication, or early clinical deterioration and the reviewed alternatives: exercise-related rapid breathing, hyperventilation, Kussmaul breathing, measurement artifact. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Count respirations for a full minute when possible without coaching the pattern; document breaths per minute, age, rest or activity, temperature, depth, rhythm, work of breathing, ability to speak, oxygenation, mental status, pain, medication exposure, and change from baseline. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Tachypnea as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, pregnancy, activity, fever, pain, anxiety, talking, and inaccurate short counting intervals affect interpretation; tachypnea describes rate and is not synonymous with hyperventilation, which depends on ventilation relative to carbon dioxide production. Therefore, Tachypnea cannot confirm or exclude a respiratory rate above the age- and context-appropriate resting range from exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, pregnancy, hypoxemia, acidosis, infection, sepsis, pulmonary or cardiac disease, medication, or early clinical deterioration by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as exercise-related rapid breathing, hyperventilation, Kussmaul breathing, measurement artifact.

False Negative Situations

Tachypnea may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of a respiratory rate above the age- and context-appropriate resting range from exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, pregnancy, hypoxemia, acidosis, infection, sepsis, pulmonary or cardiac disease, medication, or early clinical deterioration.

Modern Status

Current vital-sign abnormality and often an early deterioration clue, but it is nonspecific and can be physiologic.
