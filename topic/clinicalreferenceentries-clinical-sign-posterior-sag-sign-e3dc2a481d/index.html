Definition

Posterior sag sign is an observed knee-alignment sign. It is a clue to posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. A positive finding means the affected proximal tibia sags posteriorly.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Posterior sag sign means the affected proximal tibia sags posteriorly.

Why It Matters

The observed Posterior sag sign finding can signal posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. Gravity displaces the tibia backward when the PCL cannot support it. Posterior sag sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency

Assessment Method

Flex both hips and knees to 90 degrees and view tibial tuberosities from the side.

Positive Finding

The affected proximal tibia sags posteriorly.

Mechanism

Gravity displaces the tibia backward when the PCL cannot support it.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Posterior sag sign finding raises concern for posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency because gravity displaces the tibia backward when the PCL cannot support it. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Posterior sag sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Posterior sag sign to focus evaluation of posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: tibial deformity, hamstring tightness, positioning error. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Flex both hips and knees to 90 degrees and view tibial tuberosities from the side. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Posterior sag sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Body habitus and bilateral injury reduce comparison. Therefore, Posterior sag sign cannot confirm or exclude posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tibial deformity

  • hamstring tightness

  • positioning error

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tibial deformity, hamstring tightness, positioning error.

False Negative Situations

Posterior sag sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency.
