Class

First-line antitubercular medication

Used To Treat

Active tuberculosis as part of combination therapy and latent tuberculosis infection when ordered by the provider.

Mechanism

Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis in the Mycobacterium tuberculosis cell wall. The nursing link is killing TB organisms while preventing liver injury and peripheral neuropathy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Monitor liver injury symptoms: fatigue, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, dark urine, jaundice, or right upper quadrant pain.

  • Give pyridoxine, vitamin B6, when ordered to reduce peripheral neuropathy risk.

Contraindications

  • Clarify active liver disease, prior severe isoniazid liver injury, heavy alcohol use, or current symptoms of hepatitis before giving.

Interactions

  • Alcohol increases hepatotoxicity risk.

  • Can increase levels or toxicity risk of selected anticonvulsants and other hepatically metabolized drugs.

Key Labs

  • Baseline and follow-up liver function tests when clinically indicated.

  • Assess neuropathy symptoms, adherence, and TB symptom response.

Nclex Traps

  • Peripheral tingling with isoniazid points to neuropathy risk and vitamin B6 teaching.

  • TB therapy is not judged only by symptom improvement. Adherence and public health follow-up matter.

Description

Isoniazid is a first-line antitubercular medication: it inhibits mycolic acid synthesis in the Mycobacterium tuberculosis cell wall. It is used for active tuberculosis as part of combination therapy and latent tuberculosis infection when ordered by the provider.
