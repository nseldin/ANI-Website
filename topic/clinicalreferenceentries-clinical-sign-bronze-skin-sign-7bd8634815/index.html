Definition

Bronze skin sign is an observed pigmentation sign. It is a clue to primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload. A positive finding means diffuse hyperpigmentation is accentuated in creases or mucosa.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bronze skin sign means diffuse hyperpigmentation is accentuated in creases or mucosa.

Why It Matters

The observed Bronze skin sign finding can signal primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload. High ACTH-related melanocortin activity stimulates melanin in adrenal failure; iron and melanin contribute in hemochromatosis. Bronze skin sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload

Assessment Method

Inspect palmar creases, scars, pressure areas, mucosa, and generalized skin tone.

Positive Finding

Diffuse hyperpigmentation is accentuated in creases or mucosa.

Mechanism

High ACTH-related melanocortin activity stimulates melanin in adrenal failure; iron and melanin contribute in hemochromatosis.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Bronze skin sign finding raises concern for primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload because high ACTH-related melanocortin activity stimulates melanin in adrenal failure; iron and melanin contribute in hemochromatosis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bronze skin sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bronze skin sign to focus evaluation of primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload and the reviewed alternatives: melasma, drug pigmentation, chronic liver disease. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect palmar creases, scars, pressure areas, mucosa, and generalized skin tone. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bronze skin sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Lighting, baseline skin tone, sun exposure, and medicines confound recognition. Therefore, Bronze skin sign cannot confirm or exclude primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • melasma

  • drug pigmentation

  • chronic liver disease

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as melasma, drug pigmentation, chronic liver disease.

False Negative Situations

Bronze skin sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of primary adrenal insufficiency or iron overload.

Safety Precautions

Observe Bronze skin sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Inspect palmar creases, scars, pressure areas, mucosa, and generalized skin tone. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: With hypotension, vomiting, or electrolyte disturbance, evaluate adrenal crisis urgently.
