Definition

Posterior drawer test is a bedside knee-stability maneuver. It is a clue to posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. A positive finding means posterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Posterior drawer test means posterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft.

Why It Matters

A positive Posterior drawer test response can signal posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency. The posterior cruciate ligament is the primary restraint to posterior tibial translation in a flexed knee, so disruption removes the firm endpoint under backward force. Posterior drawer test cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency

Assessment Method

With hip and knee flexed to 90 degrees, push the proximal tibia posteriorly.

Positive Finding

Posterior translation is increased or the endpoint is soft.

Mechanism

The posterior cruciate ligament is the primary restraint to posterior tibial translation in a flexed knee, so disruption removes the firm endpoint under backward force.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Posterior drawer test response raises concern for posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency because the posterior cruciate ligament is the primary restraint to posterior tibial translation in a flexed knee, so disruption removes the firm endpoint under backward force. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Posterior drawer test does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Posterior drawer test to focus evaluation of posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency and the reviewed alternatives: tibial plateau injury, ACL tear, normal laxity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With hip and knee flexed to 90 degrees, push the proximal tibia posteriorly. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Posterior drawer test as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain, guarding, and starting sag affect comparison. Therefore, Posterior drawer test cannot confirm or exclude posterior cruciate ligament insufficiency by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tibial plateau injury

  • ACL tear

  • normal laxity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from tibial plateau injury, ACL tear, normal laxity, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Posterior drawer test may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current primary bedside PCL test. Interpret Posterior drawer test in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Pain, guarding, and starting sag affect comparison.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Posterior drawer test, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With hip and knee flexed to 90 degrees, push the proximal tibia posteriorly. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Check pulses and nerves after high-energy injury before repeated testing.
