Used To Treat

Romiplostim, eltrombopag, and avatrombopag have product-specific roles in persistent or chronic immune thrombocytopenia. Avatrombopag and lusutrombopag also have procedure-timed indications in chronic liver disease. Eltrombopag additionally has selected severe aplastic anemia indications. The goal is a platelet count sufficient to reduce bleeding risk, not normalization.

Description

Thrombopoietin receptor agonists tell megakaryocyte precursors to make more platelets, but their structures and practical rules differ. Romiplostim is a weekly injected peptibody. Eltrombopag is an oral small molecule with major liver warnings and chelates calcium, iron, magnesium, and other polyvalent cations, so food and supplement timing controls absorption. Avatrombopag is oral and is taken with food; it does not share eltrombopag's cation-separation rule. Lusutrombopag is a fixed seven-day course scheduled before a procedure in adults with chronic liver disease. All can overshoot the clinical need and increase thrombosis, so treatment follows indication and platelet trajectory rather than a desire for a normal number.

Mechanism

Endogenous thrombopoietin binds MPL, a cytokine receptor on megakaryocyte progenitors, activating JAK2/STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling to promote survival, proliferation, differentiation, and platelet production. Romiplostim is an Fc-peptide fusion that binds and activates MPL without sharing thrombopoietin's amino-acid sequence. Eltrombopag, avatrombopag, and lusutrombopag bind transmembrane-domain sites and stimulate signaling in a noncompetitive manner with endogenous thrombopoietin. Platelet rise is delayed because marrow precursors must mature, and counts can fall after discontinuation. The same increased production explains why portal, venous, arterial, or microvascular thrombosis is a central safety concern.

Boxed Warning

Eltrombopag carries boxed warnings for hepatic decompensation with interferon/ribavirin in chronic hepatitis C and severe potentially life-threatening hepatotoxicity. The other linked products do not carry that same boxed warning, but all require thrombosis surveillance and indication-specific platelet monitoring. Do not use these agents merely to normalize a laboratory count.

Adverse Effects

  • Thrombotic or thromboembolic events, especially with additional risk factors or excessive platelet response

  • Hepatotoxicity, cataract concerns, and major cation-food interactions with eltrombopag

  • Recurrent thrombocytopenia after discontinuation and possible marrow reticulin change with chronic stimulation

Contraindications

  • Do not substitute an ITP regimen for a chronic-liver-disease procedure regimen.

  • Do not use to normalize platelets or treat every cause of thrombocytopenia without diagnosis.

  • Review thrombosis history, liver function, interacting food/minerals, and marrow disease before selecting a product.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend platelet count at the exact product interval and interpret the trajectory relative to dose timing and procedure date.

  • Teach eltrombopag cation separation precisely; a calcium-rich meal can reduce absorption enough to look like treatment failure.

Interactions

  • Eltrombopag chelates polyvalent cations and affects OATP1B1/BCRP substrates; separation and dose review are essential.

  • CYP2C9/CYP3A modulators can alter avatrombopag exposure, while additive prothrombotic therapies increase harm across the class.

Key Labs

  • Platelet count trend, CBC/smear, liver tests, and diagnosis-specific marrow evaluation

  • Thrombotic symptoms, procedure timing, bleeding frequency, cataract/eye assessment where relevant, and adherence to food rules

Nclex Traps

  • These drugs increase platelet production; they do not work immediately like transfused platelets.

  • Eltrombopag must be separated from calcium and minerals, while avatrombopag is taken with food.
