Sections

  • Content

    Microbial genetics describes inherited and acquired changes in microbial genetic material. Horizontal gene transfer moves genetic information between organisms rather than only from parent to offspring.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    Mutation, recombination, plasmids, bacteriophages, and mobile genetic elements can change virulence, antigen expression, and drug susceptibility. Selection favors variants that survive a particular immune or antimicrobial pressure.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Bacteria may acquire genes through transformation, transduction, or conjugation. Viruses may accumulate mutations or exchange genome segments, and resistance determinants can spread across related or sometimes unrelated bacteria.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    The concept explains why resistance can emerge during therapy, why local susceptibility patterns change, and why genomic epidemiology can link outbreaks. Stewardship and infection prevention reduce opportunities for selection and spread.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Detection of a gene does not always prove that it is expressed or clinically dominant. Phenotypic susceptibility and patient context remain important.

    Id

    limitations

    Title

    Limitations

  • Content

    NCBI Medical Microbiology and WHO antimicrobial-resistance guidance describe genetic change, selection, and spread as central mechanisms of microbial adaptation.
