Used To Treat

Reversible bronchoconstriction in asthma or COPD, with the exact role determined by onset, duration, diagnosis, and whether an inhaled corticosteroid is present. Albuterol and levalbuterol are rapid short-acting bronchodilators. Salmeterol is a slower-onset maintenance LABA. Formoterol is a rapid-onset LABA; only an appropriate inhaled corticosteroid-formoterol product under a prescribed anti-inflammatory reliever or MART plan can serve as an asthma reliever.

Description

All beta2 agonists relax airway smooth muscle, but duration alone does not determine whether an inhaler is a rescue medicine. Albuterol and levalbuterol reach bronchodilation quickly and wear off within hours, so they can relieve acute bronchospasm but do not treat the inflammation causing asthma attacks. Salmeterol lasts about 12 hours but begins too slowly for rescue. Formoterol lasts about 12 hours and begins quickly; when paired with an inhaled corticosteroid in a validated regimen, each symptom-driven dose treats both bronchospasm and inflammation. LABA monotherapy is unsafe in asthma because bronchodilation can hide worsening inflammation without treating it. LABA monotherapy may be used in COPD because the disease and evidence base differ.

Mechanism

Beta2 receptors couple to Gs, which activates adenylyl cyclase, raises cyclic AMP, and activates protein kinase A. PKA lowers contractile signaling by reducing myosin light-chain kinase activity, limiting calcium-dependent contraction, and opening potassium channels, so airway smooth muscle relaxes. Systemic beta2 stimulation explains tremor, tachycardia, hyperglycemia, lactate generation, and intracellular potassium shift. Albuterol is racemic; levalbuterol is its active R-enantiomer. Salmeterol's long lipophilic side chain anchors near the receptor and contributes to prolonged action but slower onset. Formoterol has enough water solubility to reach the receptor rapidly and enough lipophilicity to remain in airway tissue. The receptor pathway is shared, but molecular access and retention explain the clinically crucial timing differences.

Boxed Warning

Asthma safety rule: never use salmeterol, formoterol, or another LABA without inhaled corticosteroid treatment. A fixed ICS-LABA product avoids LABA monotherapy, but not every ICS-LABA can be used for symptom relief. Increasing reliever need, nighttime symptoms, falling peak flow, or relief that lasts less than expected indicates worsening disease and requires reassessment rather than repeated unsupervised dosing.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor, nervousness, palpitations, tachycardia, headache, hyperglycemia, and transient hypokalemia from systemic beta2 stimulation

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, excessive use with arrhythmia or ischemia, and delayed care when temporary bronchodilation masks worsening inflammation

Contraindications

  • Do not use a LABA alone in asthma or use a maintenance-only LABA as rescue therapy.

  • Avoid duplicate beta agonists and reassess marked cardiovascular disease, tachyarrhythmia, hypokalemia, hyperthyroidism, or repeated rescue use.

Nursing Essentials

  • Identify the exact inhaler, active ingredients, device, prescribed role, and maximum daily plan; color and brand familiarity are not enough.

  • After a rescue dose, reassess work of breathing, speech, air movement, oxygenation, heart rate, and response. A quieter chest can mean less airflow, not improvement.

Interactions

  • Nonselective beta blockers can blunt bronchodilation and provoke bronchospasm; MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, stimulants, and other sympathomimetics can amplify cardiovascular effects.

  • Loop or thiazide diuretics can magnify hypokalemia; other QT-prolonging or arrhythmogenic drugs increase concern during high beta-agonist exposure.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, reliever frequency, peak flow or spirometry, heart rate/rhythm, and inhaler technique

  • Potassium, glucose, and lactate when repeated high-dose therapy or toxicity is suspected

Nclex Traps

  • A long-acting drug is not automatically slow: formoterol is rapid-onset, while salmeterol is not.

  • Bronchodilation treats smooth-muscle constriction; it does not by itself suppress the airway inflammation that drives asthma risk.
