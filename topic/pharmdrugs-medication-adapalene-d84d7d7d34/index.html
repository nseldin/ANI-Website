Class

Third-generation topical retinoid; RAR-beta/gamma-selective comedolytic

Used To Treat

Acne vulgaris; OTC 0.1% gel and prescription strengths/formulations have age- and product-specific labeling.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Do not apply to eczema, abrasions, sunburn or mucosa. Limit UV exposure, use sunscreen, avoid waxing treated skin, and reduce frequency if erythema, scaling, burning or stinging becomes significant. Pregnancy and breastfeeding require product-specific clinician review even though systemic absorption is low.

Mechanism

After entering keratinocytes, adapalene binds RAR-beta/gamma, which heterodimerize with retinoid X receptors and bind retinoic-acid response elements in DNA. Changed transcription normalizes differentiation in the follicular infundibulum, decreases corneocyte cohesion and microcomedone formation, and modulates AP-1/Toll-like-receptor-related inflammation. The drug treats the acne-prone field rather than killing bacteria directly; improvement follows epithelial turnover over 8-12 weeks.

Nursing Essentials

  • Apply a thin film to the entire acne-prone area once daily as labeled, not repeatedly to individual lesions.

  • Use gentle cleanser and moisturizer; judge response after weeks, while distinguishing tolerable dryness from severe dermatitis.

Contraindications

  • Known adapalene or vehicle hypersensitivity

  • Avoid damaged, eczematous, abraded or sunburned skin

  • Clarify pregnancy, breastfeeding and concurrent retinoid or highly irritating skin therapy

Interactions

  • Other retinoids, salicylic acid, sulfur/resorcinol, astringents, abrasive cleansers and high-alcohol products add irritation.

  • Benzoyl peroxide is chemically compatible with adapalene and is often paired for complementary antimicrobial action.

  • Waxing over treated skin increases epidermal stripping.

Key Labs

  • No routine serum level or laboratory monitoring

  • Pregnancy assessment when clinically relevant

  • Serial lesion type/count, skin-barrier reaction, adherence and sun exposure

Nclex Traps

  • Adapalene is a comedolytic transcriptional retinoid, not an antibiotic.

  • More frequent application does not work faster; it increases barrier injury.

  • The first weeks may look worse, but blistering, edema or severe pain is not a normal therapeutic endpoint.

Description

Adapalene is a third-generation topical retinoid that prevents and clears microcomedones, the microscopic keratin plugs that begin acne lesions. It is not an antibiotic. It binds nuclear retinoic acid receptors, especially RAR-beta and RAR-gamma, and reprograms follicular keratinocyte differentiation so cells shed without sealing the pilosebaceous duct. It also reduces inflammatory signaling and is more photochemically stable than tretinoin, but still causes dose-limiting dryness and irritation.

Adverse Effects

  • Erythema, scaling, dryness, burning, stinging and pruritus

  • Early apparent acne worsening as pre-existing microcomedones surface

  • Photosensitivity, sunburn and irritant or allergic contact dermatitis
