  • Content

    Fecal-oral transmission occurs when an infectious agent shed in feces reaches another person's mouth, usually through contaminated hands, food, water, equipment, or environmental surfaces.

    Id

    definition

    Title

    Definition

  • Content

    The route links intestinal shedding to ingestion. Poor hand hygiene, unsafe sanitation, cross-contamination during food preparation, and environmentally persistent forms such as spores can keep that chain intact. Hand hygiene, safe water, food controls, toilet sanitation, and environmental cleaning interrupt different steps.

    Id

    mechanism

    Title

    Mechanism

  • Content

    Enteric pathogenic E. coli may spread in contaminated food or water, while C. difficile spores may move from fecally contaminated healthcare surfaces or hands. These examples share a route but differ in ecology, persistence, testing, and prevention details.

    Id

    examples

    Title

    Examples

  • Content

    Recognizing the route helps clinicians choose exposure questions, specimen strategies, patient education, outbreak controls, and precautions. It also explains why protecting only the symptomatic patient is insufficient when contaminated food, water, or shared environments remain part of the chain.

    Id

    clinical-significance

    Title

    Clinical significance

  • Content

    Fecal-oral is a transmission route, not proof that every intestinal organism is pathogenic. Normal gut organisms can be present without disease, and some species also cause endogenous extraintestinal infection through mechanisms that are not person-to-person fecal-oral spread.
