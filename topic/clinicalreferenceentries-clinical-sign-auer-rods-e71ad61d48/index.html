Definition

Auer rods is a microscopic marrow or blood sign. It is a clue to myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia. A positive finding means needle-like azurophilic cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Auer rods means needle-like azurophilic cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Why It Matters

The Auer rods laboratory finding can signal myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia. Abnormal fusion of primary granules forms crystalline peroxidase-positive rods in myeloid blasts. Auer rods cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia

Assessment Method

Review blasts in blood or marrow.

Positive Finding

Needle-like azurophilic cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Mechanism

Abnormal fusion of primary granules forms crystalline peroxidase-positive rods in myeloid blasts.

Clinical Interpretation

The Auer rods laboratory finding raises concern for myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia because abnormal fusion of primary granules forms crystalline peroxidase-positive rods in myeloid blasts. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Auer rods does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Auer rods to focus evaluation of myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia and the reviewed alternatives: stain precipitate, phagocytosed material, toxic granules. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Review blasts in blood or marrow. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Auer rods as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Absence does not exclude AML and rare mimics require expert review. Therefore, Auer rods cannot confirm or exclude myeloid blasts, especially acute myeloid leukemia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • stain precipitate

  • phagocytosed material

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with stain precipitate, phagocytosed material, toxic granules.

False Negative Situations

Auer rods may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
