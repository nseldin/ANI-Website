Definition

Duroziez sign is an auscultatory vascular maneuver. It is a clue to severe aortic regurgitation. A positive finding means a systolic murmur with proximal compression and diastolic murmur with distal compression is heard.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Duroziez sign means a systolic murmur with proximal compression and diastolic murmur with distal compression is heard.

Why It Matters

A positive Duroziez sign response can signal severe aortic regurgitation. Reversed diastolic flow and high forward systolic flow create direction-dependent turbulence. Duroziez sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • severe aortic regurgitation

Assessment Method

Auscultate the femoral artery while applying graded distal then proximal compression.

Positive Finding

A systolic murmur with proximal compression and diastolic murmur with distal compression is heard.

Mechanism

Reversed diastolic flow and high forward systolic flow create direction-dependent turbulence.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Duroziez sign response raises concern for severe aortic regurgitation because reversed diastolic flow and high forward systolic flow create direction-dependent turbulence. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Duroziez sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Duroziez sign to focus evaluation of severe aortic regurgitation and the reviewed alternatives: femoral stenosis, arteriovenous fistula, transmitted murmur. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Auscultate the femoral artery while applying graded distal then proximal compression. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Duroziez sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Compression degree is poorly standardized and arterial disease can confound it. Therefore, Duroziez sign cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • femoral stenosis

  • arteriovenous fistula

  • transmitted murmur

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from femoral stenosis, arteriovenous fistula, transmitted murmur, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Duroziez sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; operator dependent. Duroziez sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude severe aortic regurgitation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Duroziez sign: Compression degree is poorly standardized and arterial disease can confound it.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Duroziez sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Auscultate the femoral artery while applying graded distal then proximal compression. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid forceful compression in a painful, ischemic, grafted, or aneurysmal groin.

Base escalation for Duroziez sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
