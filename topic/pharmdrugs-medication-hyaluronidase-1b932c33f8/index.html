Class

Hyaluronic-acid depolymerizing dispersion enzyme used off-label for selected antineoplastic extravasations

Used To Treat

Selected chemotherapy extravasation, particularly vinca alkaloids and selected taxanes under the 2025 Oncology Nursing Society pathway, as an OFF-LABEL local antidote paired with drug-specific warm-compress and aspiration measures. It is not the antidote for anthracycline or vasopressor extravasation.

Boxed Warning

High-yield warning context (not necessarily an FDA boxed warning): Known hypersensitivity is a contraindication. Product labeling advises against injection into or around infected or acutely inflamed areas because spreading permeability can disseminate infection. Increasing pain, blistering, ulceration, sensory change, impaired movement, delayed capillary refill, compartment findings, or expanding injury requires urgent oncology, plastic/hand surgery, wound, and pharmacy evaluation.

Mechanism

Hyaluronidase hydrolyzes glycosidic bonds in hyaluronic acid, reducing the viscosity of the interstitial ground substance and temporarily increasing tissue permeability. A vesicant trapped in a small compartment exposes the same cells to a high concentration for longer and can cause progressive injury. Dispersing that depot lowers local concentration and increases the surface area available for vascular and lymphatic removal, which explains the warm-compress pairing for agents whose clearance is helped by local vasodilation. This logic is agent-specific: anthracyclines bind tissue and generate free-radical injury, so dexrazoxane/cold-compress pathways are used instead, while vasopressor ischemia requires alpha blockade with phentolamine rather than nonspecific dispersion alone.

Nursing Essentials

  • Never flush the infiltrated line because flushing pushes more vesicant into tissue. Preserve the catheter long enough to aspirate and, when the protocol specifies, to deliver part of the antidote.

  • Confirm drug, concentration, estimated volume, site, time, symptoms, blood return, and temperature strategy with pharmacy because warm versus cold and antidote choice reverse for different antineoplastics.

  • Map and photograph erythema, swelling, pain, blistering, capillary refill, sensation, pulses, and range of motion; elevate when directed and repeat neurovascular checks because deeper injury may progress beyond visible borders.

  • Do not automatically use hyaluronidase for norepinephrine or other vasopressor extravasation because alpha-mediated ischemia requires the agent-specific phentolamine pathway. Do not use it for anthracyclines because their tissue binding and free-radical injury require dexrazoxane and cold-compress guidance instead.

Contraindications

  • Known hypersensitivity is a contraindication. Product labeling advises against injection into or around infected or acutely inflamed areas because spreading permeability can disseminate infection.

  • Do not apply a chemotherapy-extravasation technique to dermal-filler complications, ophthalmic uses, or routine subcutaneous-fluid facilitation. Do not substitute hyaluronidase for phentolamine in vasopressor ischemia or dexrazoxane in anthracycline extravasation.

Key Labs

  • No serum hyaluronidase test guides therapy. Monitoring is primarily serial local examination, photographs with scale, circumference, pain score, distal neurovascular status, and functional assessment.

  • Obtain CBC, renal/hepatic studies, or drug-specific monitoring when the chemotherapy exposure or underlying treatment requires it to detect systemic treatment toxicity, and document dose, lot, injection sites, compress schedule, consultations, and follow-up to preserve treatment traceability.

Nclex Traps

  • Stop and aspirate; do not flush an extravasated vesicant line.

  • Hyaluronidase disperses selected vinca/taxane deposits and is paired with warm compresses; it does not neutralize the chemotherapy molecule.

  • Antidotes are agent-specific: phentolamine for vasopressors and dexrazoxane for anthracyclines are different pathways.

Description

Hyaluronidase is an enzyme that cleaves hyaluronic acid in the extracellular matrix, increasing tissue permeability so small local injections can disperse a selected extravasated vinca or taxane depot, lower its local concentration, and promote vascular and lymphatic removal. The enzyme does not chemically inactivate vincristine, vinblastine, vinorelbine, paclitaxel, or docetaxel; benefit depends on early recognition, stopping the infusion, aspirating residual drug, using the correct temperature strategy, and following an antineoplastic-specific protocol. The ONS 2025 guidance pairs hyaluronidase with warm compresses for specified vinca and taxane extravasations. This is an OFF-LABEL use of products whose labeled roles are chiefly dispersion and absorption of other injected fluids or drugs.

Administration Timing

  • At the first sign of antineoplastic extravasation, stop the infusion immediately, leave the catheter or needle in place, disconnect tubing, and aspirate as much residual drug as possible without flushing. Notify the chemotherapy-extravasation team and identify the exact drug before choosing an antidote.

  • For a protocol-confirmed vinca alkaloid or qualifying taxane extravasation, administer the institution's product-specific hyaluronidase dose by small injections around/through the affected area and apply the recommended warm compress. Use the 2025 ONS timing and technique rather than a generic subcutaneous-fluid label dose.

  • Remove the device only after aspiration and any protocol-directed access use are complete. Photograph and measure the area, arrange serial reassessment, and provide written return instructions because necrosis can evolve after an initially mild appearance.

Adverse Effects

  • Local burning, pain, erythema, edema, pruritus, and injection-site bleeding can occur. Urticaria, angioedema, or anaphylaxis is uncommon but possible.

  • Dispersion into infected or inflamed tissue may spread local disease, and incorrect use for the wrong vesicant can delay the agent-specific antidote and worsen injury.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Increasing pain, blistering, ulceration, sensory change, impaired movement, delayed capillary refill, compartment findings, or expanding injury requires urgent oncology, plastic/hand surgery, wound, and pharmacy evaluation.

  • Continue follow-up until the site clearly resolves. A benign first-hour appearance does not rule out delayed vesicant necrosis.

Evidence Limitations

  • Use for antineoplastic extravasation is OFF-LABEL and evidence relies heavily on mechanistic rationale, case series, and consensus. The exact agents included in the 2025 ONS pathway and the stocked product concentration control bedside practice.

  • Taxane guidance has evolved; institutions should align their protocol with the current ONS document rather than extrapolating from older cold-compress-only practices.

Regulatory Status

OFF-LABEL for chemotherapy extravasation. Hyaluronidase products are FDA approved for specified dispersion/absorption indications; antineoplastic use follows the 2025 ONS and institutional protocol.
