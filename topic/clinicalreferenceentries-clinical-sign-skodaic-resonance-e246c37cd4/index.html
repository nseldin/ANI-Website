Definition

Skodaic resonance is a percussion sign. It is a clue to lung compressed above a pleural effusion. A positive finding means a hyperresonant or tympanitic band is heard just above the effusion.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Skodaic resonance means a hyperresonant or tympanitic band is heard just above the effusion.

Why It Matters

A positive Skodaic resonance response can signal lung compressed above a pleural effusion. Compressed partially aerated lung overlying fluid changes regional acoustic impedance. Skodaic resonance cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • lung compressed above a pleural effusion

Assessment Method

Percuss from the dull fluid level upward.

Positive Finding

A hyperresonant or tympanitic band is heard just above the effusion.

Mechanism

Compressed partially aerated lung overlying fluid changes regional acoustic impedance.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Skodaic resonance response raises concern for lung compressed above a pleural effusion because compressed partially aerated lung overlying fluid changes regional acoustic impedance. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Skodaic resonance does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Skodaic resonance to focus evaluation of lung compressed above a pleural effusion and the reviewed alternatives: normal upper-lung resonance, loculated effusion, cavity. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Percuss from the dull fluid level upward. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Skodaic resonance as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Percussion boundaries are imprecise and body habitus reduces reliability. Therefore, Skodaic resonance cannot confirm or exclude lung compressed above a pleural effusion by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal upper-lung resonance

  • loculated effusion

  • cavity

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal upper-lung resonance, loculated effusion, cavity, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Skodaic resonance may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical percussion adjunct. Skodaic resonance remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude lung compressed above a pleural effusion. Apply the reviewed limitation for Skodaic resonance: Percussion boundaries are imprecise and body habitus reduces reliability.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Skodaic resonance, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Percuss from the dull fluid level upward. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Confirm suspected fluid with ultrasound rather than mapping by percussion alone.
