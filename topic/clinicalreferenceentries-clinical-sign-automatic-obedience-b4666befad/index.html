Definition

Automatic obedience is an observed behavioral sign. It is a clue to catatonia. A positive finding means the patient follows commands automatically even when told not to.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Automatic obedience means the patient follows commands automatically even when told not to.

Why It Matters

The observed Automatic obedience finding can signal catatonia. Catatonic disruption of frontal-volitional control makes an external command trigger motor output automatically before reflective inhibition or goal-directed choice can intervene. Automatic obedience cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • catatonia

Assessment Method

Give a simple safe command and observe disproportionately immediate compliance.

Positive Finding

The patient follows commands automatically even when told not to.

Mechanism

Catatonic disruption of frontal-volitional control makes an external command trigger motor output automatically before reflective inhibition or goal-directed choice can intervene.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Automatic obedience finding raises concern for catatonia because catatonic disruption of frontal-volitional control makes an external command trigger motor output automatically before reflective inhibition or goal-directed choice can intervene. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Automatic obedience does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Automatic obedience to focus evaluation of catatonia and the reviewed alternatives: mania, developmental condition, trauma response. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Give a simple safe command and observe disproportionately immediate compliance. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Automatic obedience as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Cooperation, cultural deference, and misunderstanding are not pathology. Therefore, Automatic obedience cannot confirm or exclude catatonia by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • mania

  • developmental condition

  • trauma response

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as mania, developmental condition, trauma response.

False Negative Situations

Automatic obedience may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of catatonia.

Modern Status

Safety Precautions

Observe Automatic obedience with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Give a simple safe command and observe disproportionately immediate compliance. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Never issue unsafe or degrading commands to test this sign.

Nursing Relevance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."
