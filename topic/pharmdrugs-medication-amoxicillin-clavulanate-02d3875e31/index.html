Used To Treat

Susceptible beta-lactamase-producing lower respiratory infection, acute bacterial otitis media, bacterial sinusitis, skin or skin-structure infection, and urinary tract infection. Use depends on organism, site, local resistance, allergy, kidney function, and formulation. When testing shows the organism is susceptible to plain amoxicillin without relevant beta-lactamase production, adding clavulanate offers little benefit and adds adverse-effect burden.

Mechanism

Amoxicillin enters susceptible bacteria and covalently acylates penicillin-binding proteins, especially transpeptidases that cross-link peptidoglycan. Wall synthesis stops while autolysins continue remodeling, producing osmotic instability and bactericidal lysis in actively dividing organisms. Clavulanate resembles a beta-lactam substrate and reacts with the active-site serine of selected beta-lactamases, forming progressively stable inactive enzyme complexes. By consuming those enzymes before they hydrolyze amoxicillin, clavulanate restores exposure at the PBP target for susceptible beta-lactamase producers. The inhibitor cannot repair an altered PBP or reverse every resistance mechanism, so culture and susceptibility still outrank the word 'combination.'

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Serious or fatal beta-lactam hypersensitivity and severe cutaneous reactions can occur. A previous amoxicillin-clavulanate-associated cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction is a contraindication because re-exposure can produce recurrent severe injury. Drug-induced enterocolitis syndrome can cause delayed repetitive vomiting, pallor, lethargy, hypotension or shock without the hives or wheeze expected in IgE anaphylaxis, especially in children. Clostridioides difficile diarrhea and liver injury may begin during therapy or after it ends, so a delayed presentation still requires medication linkage and evaluation.

Contraindications

  • Previous serious hypersensitivity to amoxicillin, clavulanate, penicillins, cephalosporins, or another relevant beta-lactam reaction such as anaphylaxis or SJS/TEN.

  • Previous cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction caused by amoxicillin-clavulanate; clarify severe renal impairment and avoid the 875-mg tablet when product labeling excludes it at low GFR.

Nursing Essentials

  • Give at the start of a meal because food improves clavulanate absorption and reduces GI intolerance. Verify the exact amoxicillin-to-clavulanate ratio: two 250/125-mg tablets are not equivalent to one 500/125-mg tablet because they double the clavulanate dose.

  • Obtain cultures when indicated, calculate renal function, document the beta-lactam reaction phenotype, and monitor infection response, stool pattern, rash or mucosal lesions, delayed vomiting, jaundice, dark urine, pruritus, and right-upper-quadrant symptoms.

Interactions

  • Probenecid reduces renal tubular secretion of amoxicillin and prolongs exposure; allopurinol increases rash frequency with ampicillin-class drugs, and oral anticoagulant response can shift during illness or therapy, so monitor the relevant clinical context.

  • Do not assume every tablet, chewable, suspension, or extended-release product is substitutable milligram for milligram. The amoxicillin/clavulanate ratio and release design determine exposure and tolerability.

Key Labs

  • Cultures and susceptibility, serum creatinine/eGFR, CBC and liver tests when prolonged or high risk, and PT/INR when clinically indicated with oral anticoagulants.

  • For toxicity or new symptoms, assess bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, AST/ALT, creatinine, hydration, stool testing, eosinophilia or systemic hypersensitivity findings, and timing relative to the last dose.

Nclex Traps

  • Clavulanate protects amoxicillin from selected beta-lactamases; it is not the main cell-wall killer and does not guarantee MRSA, Pseudomonas, atypical, or ESBL coverage.

  • Take it at the start of a meal, and never substitute tablet counts by amoxicillin milligrams alone because the clavulanate amount can change.

Description

Amoxicillin clavulanate is an oral two-part beta-lactam antibiotic in which amoxicillin binds PBPs and blocks bacterial cell-wall cross-linking while clavulanate inhibits selected beta-lactamases that would otherwise destroy amoxicillin. The partner does not make amoxicillin universally broad: altered PBPs, porin loss, efflux, AmpC enzymes, metallo-beta-lactamases, and many carbapenemases can still defeat the regimen. Clavulanate also adds diarrhea and a distinctive risk of delayed cholestatic or mixed liver injury. This is why the combination is chosen for a plausible beta-lactamase problem rather than automatically replacing plain amoxicillin for every infection.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea or loose stool, nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, rash, urticaria, headache, vaginitis, and candidal overgrowth; GI effects rise as clavulanate exposure increases.

  • Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN/DRESS/AGEP, DIES, C. difficile colitis, cholestatic or mixed hepatic injury, cytopenias, interstitial nephritis, crystalluria, and seizures with excessive beta-lactam exposure or severe renal impairment.
