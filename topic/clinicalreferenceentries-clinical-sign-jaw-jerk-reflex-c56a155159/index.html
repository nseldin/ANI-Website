Definition

Jaw jerk reflex is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons. A positive finding means a brisk jaw-closing response is disproportionate to other reflexes.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Jaw jerk reflex means a brisk jaw-closing response is disproportionate to other reflexes.

Why It Matters

A positive Jaw jerk reflex response can signal bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons. Trigeminal motor reflex circuits become hyperexcitable when bilateral corticobulbar inhibition is lost. Jaw jerk reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons

Assessment Method

Place a finger on the relaxed chin with the mouth slightly open and tap the finger.

Positive Finding

A brisk jaw-closing response is disproportionate to other reflexes.

Mechanism

Trigeminal motor reflex circuits become hyperexcitable when bilateral corticobulbar inhibition is lost.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Jaw jerk reflex response raises concern for bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons because trigeminal motor reflex circuits become hyperexcitable when bilateral corticobulbar inhibition is lost. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Jaw jerk reflex does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Jaw jerk reflex to focus evaluation of bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons and the reviewed alternatives: anxiety, clenching, normal brisk reflex. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Place a finger on the relaxed chin with the mouth slightly open and tap the finger. Evidence Limitations

Baseline response varies with age and tension; it is not disease-specific. Therefore, Jaw jerk reflex cannot confirm or exclude bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • anxiety

  • clenching

  • normal brisk reflex

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from anxiety, clenching, normal brisk reflex, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Jaw jerk reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current localization adjunct; a slight response may be normal. When performed as described, Jaw jerk reflex can adjust concern for bilateral corticobulbar tract hyperexcitability above the pons, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Jaw jerk reflex: Baseline response varies with age and tension; it is not disease-specific.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Jaw jerk reflex, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Place a finger on the relaxed chin with the mouth slightly open and tap the finger. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Ensure the jaw is relaxed and compare with bulbar and limb findings.
