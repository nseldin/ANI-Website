Class

Neonatal Fc receptor antagonist that lowers circulating IgG

Description

Efgartigimod is an engineered IgG1 Fc fragment that lowers pathogenic IgG autoantibodies. In acetylcholine-receptor-positive generalized myasthenia gravis, fewer circulating receptor antibodies means less complement-mediated damage and less blockade or internalization of acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction. It does not create acetylcholine or directly strengthen muscle; it changes the antibody burden over a treatment cycle, so response and infection risk must be assessed over time.

Mechanism

The neonatal Fc receptor, FcRn, normally rescues IgG from lysosomal degradation by binding it in acidic endosomes and recycling it to plasma. Efgartigimod binds FcRn and competes with endogenous IgG, so more IgG is degraded. Total IgG and disease-causing anti-AChR IgG fall while albumin, which uses a different FcRn binding site, is relatively spared. As the drug effect wanes, IgG recovers; this explains cyclic dosing and why vaccination and infection timing matter.

Used To Treat

Generalized myasthenia gravis in adults who are anti-acetylcholine receptor antibody positive. The intravenous Vyvgart product must not be confused with the subcutaneous efgartigimod alfa and hyaluronidase combination, which has separate route and indication details.

Boxed Warning

No boxed warning. Serious infection, hypersensitivity, and infusion-related reactions are the central hazards because lowering IgG can weaken humoral protection and biologic infusion can trigger acute reactions.

Contraindications

  • The current label lists no formal contraindication; do not infuse through an uncontrolled serious infection or after a serious product hypersensitivity without specialist review.

  • Do not interchange intravenous Vyvgart and subcutaneous Vyvgart Hytrulo by volume or schedule.

Adverse Effects

  • Respiratory tract infection, headache, urinary tract infection, and infusion-related reaction.

  • Serious infection, anaphylaxis, hypotension, chills, dyspnea, rash, or other hypersensitivity.

Interactions

  • Because FcRn blockade lowers IgG, it may reduce exposure or effectiveness of other Fc-containing or IgG-based biologics; monitor the companion therapy and avoid assuming stable concentrations.

  • Evaluate age-appropriate vaccination before a cycle. Live vaccines are generally avoided during treatment because safety during IgG reduction has not been established.

Nursing Essentials

  • Document baseline myasthenia symptoms, respiratory and bulbar function, infection screen, vaccination status, venous access, and the exact cycle week.

  • Monitor during and after infusion for dyspnea, chest discomfort, blood-pressure change, chills, rash, or anaphylaxis; stop and treat according to severity.

  • Escalate increasing dysphagia, weak cough, inability to handle secretions, falling vital capacity, or respiratory distress as possible myasthenic crisis rather than waiting for the next dose.

Key Labs

  • No routine therapeutic drug level

  • Clinical myasthenia score and respiratory or bulbar assessment

  • IgG level and infection testing when clinically indicated

Administration

  • Administer by IV infusion in label-defined weekly doses within a treatment cycle. Inspect, dilute, infuse, and observe according to the current product instructions.

  • Begin another cycle from clinical evaluation and label timing; do not schedule continuously without reassessing response and infection.

Patient Education

  • Report fever, urinary symptoms, persistent cough, painful rash, or infusion symptoms promptly.

  • Explain that benefit comes from lowering IgG autoantibodies and may vary by cycle; new swallowing or breathing difficulty is an emergency.

Nclex Traps

  • Lowering IgG can improve antibody-mediated weakness while increasing infection risk; assess both response and infection before the next cycle.

  • New dysphagia, weak cough, or dyspnea is possible myasthenic crisis and needs emergency respiratory assessment, not routine infusion follow-up.

Routes

  • Intravenous infusion in treatment cycles; do not interchange with the subcutaneous efgartigimod alfa and hyaluronidase product.

Serious Adverse Reactions

  • Serious infection during IgG reduction.

  • Anaphylaxis, severe hypersensitivity, or clinically important infusion-related reaction.

Toxicity Management

  • No specific antidote exists. Stop the infusion for a serious reaction, support airway and circulation, evaluate for infection, and involve neurology or emergency care; new respiratory or bulbar weakness must also be treated as possible myasthenic crisis.

Special Populations

  • Pregnancy and lactation data are insufficient, and lowering maternal IgG could affect passive antibody transfer; coordinate treatment and infant-vaccine planning with specialists.

  • Pediatric safety and effectiveness are not established. Older adults may have greater infection vulnerability.

  • Mild renal impairment can increase exposure; data are limited in moderate or severe renal impairment. Hepatic metabolism is not expected to be a major clearance route, but population-specific decisions follow current labeling.
