Used To Treat

Long-term maintenance bronchodilation and exacerbation reduction in COPD and product-specific add-on maintenance treatment of asthma with Respimat. Tiotropium prevents symptoms over time; it does not act quickly enough to treat acute bronchospasm.

Mechanism

Acetylcholine activates airway M3-Gq-phospholipase C-IP3-calcium signaling, leading to myosin light-chain kinase activation and bronchial smooth-muscle contraction. Tiotropium competitively blocks M1 through M5 receptors, but its slow dissociation from M1 and M3 and more rapid dissociation from M2 create kinetic selectivity that preserves bronchodilation for about 24 hours. The quaternary ammonium structure limits CNS penetration. It does not activate beta2 receptors, suppress eosinophilic gene transcription, or immediately reverse a severe bronchospasm. Systemically absorbed drug is largely eliminated in urine, which explains why impaired renal clearance can increase anticholinergic effects even though the medicine is inhaled.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Tiotropium is not a rescue medication and must not be used as initial treatment of an acute episode. Immediate hypersensitivity, paradoxical bronchospasm, acute narrow-angle glaucoma, and urinary retention require prompt action. HandiHaler capsules must never be swallowed, and Respimat and HandiHaler strengths are not dose-for-dose interchangeable.

Contraindications

  • Hypersensitivity to tiotropium, ipratropium, atropine derivatives, or product components

  • Use caution with narrow-angle glaucoma, prostatic hyperplasia, bladder-neck obstruction, and moderate to severe renal impairment

Nursing Essentials

  • Confirm HandiHaler versus Respimat, prescribed strength, loading technique, inspiratory ability, and that the patient has a separate rescue inhaler.

  • Teach glaucoma and urinary-retention red flags and assess dry mouth, bowel function, urine flow, symptoms, and exacerbations.

Interactions

  • Avoid routine combination with another anticholinergic because effects are additive.

  • Combination with LABA or ICS/LABA may be intended; reconcile every active ingredient to avoid duplicate LAMA exposure.

Key Labs

  • Symptoms, COPD exacerbations, spirometry, inspiratory technique, ocular symptoms, and bladder emptying

  • Renal function when impairment or systemic anticholinergic effects are present

Nclex Traps

  • The HandiHaler capsule is inhaled through the device, not swallowed.

  • Once-daily maintenance does not mean immediate rescue, even if the patient feels chronically less short of breath.

Description

Tiotropium is a once-daily inhaled LAMA that prevents acetylcholine from tightening airway smooth muscle. It binds muscarinic receptors with similar affinity but dissociates slowly from M1 and M3 and faster from M2, producing prolonged functional blockade of the bronchoconstrictor M3 pathway. In COPD it improves airflow and reduces exacerbations; a specific soft-mist formulation also has an asthma maintenance indication. The HandiHaler capsule is not an oral drug: it is pierced inside the device and its powder is inhaled in two breaths. Swallowing the capsule delivers little useful lung effect. Because renal excretion contributes to exposure, anticholinergic toxicity deserves closer attention in moderate or severe renal impairment.

Adverse Effects

  • Dry mouth, constipation, cough, pharyngitis, sinusitis, dyspepsia, urinary symptoms, or tachycardia

  • Paradoxical bronchospasm, hypersensitivity or angioedema, acute angle-closure glaucoma, urinary retention, or anticholinergic toxicity
