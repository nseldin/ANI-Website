Used To Treat

Postpartum depression with either a monitored 60-hour IV brexanolone infusion or a 14-day oral evening zuranolone course.

Description

These postpartum-depression therapies positively modulate GABA-A receptors at a site distinct from GABA, increasing inhibitory chloride-channel responses when GABA binds. Brexanolone is chemically identical to allopregnanolone, a progesterone metabolite, while zuranolone is an oral synthetic neuroactive steroid. The therapeutic PPD mechanism is not fully established, but both amplify synaptic and extrasynaptic inhibitory signaling rather than acting like serotonin reuptake inhibitors.

Mechanism

Positive allosteric modulation increases the probability or effectiveness of GABA-A channel opening without directly replacing GABA. Extrasynaptic delta-containing receptors support tonic inhibition and synaptic gamma-containing receptors support phasic inhibition; neurosteroids can modulate both. This broad network suppression explains potential rapid symptom improvement as well as sedation, slowed cognition, falls, respiratory depression with other depressants, and driving impairment.

Boxed Warning

The boxed warnings differ. Brexanolone can cause excessive sedation and sudden loss of consciousness during infusion and is restricted by the ZULRESSO REMS with continuous pulse oximetry and direct observation. Zuranolone causes driving impairment; patients must avoid driving or hazardous activity until at least 12 hours after every dose throughout the 14-day course and may not accurately judge their own impairment.

Nursing Essentials

  • Screen suicidality, bipolar/psychosis symptoms, respiratory risk, renal/hepatic status, falls, alcohol/opioid/benzodiazepine use, infant-care support, and exact REMS or driving plan.

  • Differentiate ordinary sleep from progressive unresponsiveness or hypoventilation, and ensure another responsible adult handles infant interactions whenever sedation could make care unsafe.

Key Labs

  • No serum concentration guides therapy. Use serial depression/suicide assessment, sedation and respiratory monitoring, pregnancy/contraception counseling for zuranolone, and product-specific renal/hepatic review.

Nclex Traps

  • Brexanolone's REMS is about monitored infusion-associated loss of consciousness; zuranolone's boxed warning is specifically next-day driving/hazard impairment.

  • A 14-day zuranolone course is not a PRN sedative and should not be extended casually.
