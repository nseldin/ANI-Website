Definition

Psoas sign is a bedside abdominal maneuver. It is a clue to retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation. A positive finding means right lower-quadrant or flank pain is reproduced.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Psoas sign means right lower-quadrant or flank pain is reproduced.

Why It Matters

A positive Psoas sign response can signal retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation. Movement stretches or contracts the psoas beside an inflamed retrocecal appendix or adjacent process. Psoas sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation

Assessment Method

Passively extend the right hip in lateral position or ask for resisted hip flexion.

Positive Finding

Right lower-quadrant or flank pain is reproduced.

Mechanism

Movement stretches or contracts the psoas beside an inflamed retrocecal appendix or adjacent process.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Psoas sign response raises concern for retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation because movement stretches or contracts the psoas beside an inflamed retrocecal appendix or adjacent process. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Psoas sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Psoas sign to focus evaluation of retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation and the reviewed alternatives: septic hip, lumbar radiculopathy, renal inflammation. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Passively extend the right hip in lateral position or ask for resisted hip flexion. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Psoas sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A negative test does not exclude appendicitis and hip disease causes false positives. Therefore, Psoas sign cannot confirm or exclude retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • septic hip

  • lumbar radiculopathy

  • renal inflammation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from septic hip, lumbar radiculopathy, renal inflammation, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Psoas sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct with limited sensitivity. When performed as described, Psoas sign can adjust concern for retrocecal appendicitis or psoas irritation, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Psoas sign: A negative test does not exclude appendicitis and hip disease causes false positives.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Psoas sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Passively extend the right hip in lateral position or ask for resisted hip flexion. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Move gently and avoid force when hip fracture or severe pain is possible.
