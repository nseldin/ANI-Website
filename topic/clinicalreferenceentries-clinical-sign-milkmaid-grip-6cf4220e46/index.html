Definition

Milkmaid grip is an observed motor sign. It is a clue to chorea. A positive finding means grip pressure repeatedly tightens and relaxes instead of remaining sustained.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Milkmaid grip means grip pressure repeatedly tightens and relaxes instead of remaining sustained.

Why It Matters

The observed Milkmaid grip finding can signal chorea. Irregular involuntary basal-ganglia output interrupts tonic hand contraction. Milkmaid grip cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • chorea

Assessment Method

Ask the patient to squeeze two fingers steadily.

Positive Finding

Grip pressure repeatedly tightens and relaxes instead of remaining sustained.

Mechanism

Irregular involuntary basal-ganglia output interrupts tonic hand contraction.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Milkmaid grip finding raises concern for chorea because irregular involuntary basal-ganglia output interrupts tonic hand contraction. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Milkmaid grip does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Milkmaid grip to focus evaluation of chorea and the reviewed alternatives: tremor, myoclonus, functional movement disorder. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Ask the patient to squeeze two fingers steadily. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Milkmaid grip as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Weakness, pain, and poor understanding can also make grip inconsistent. Therefore, Milkmaid grip cannot confirm or exclude chorea by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tremor

  • myoclonus

  • functional movement disorder

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as tremor, myoclonus, functional movement disorder.

False Negative Situations

Milkmaid grip may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of chorea.
