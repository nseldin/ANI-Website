Used To Treat

Non-emergent hyperkalemia management when GI potassium removal is appropriate and bowel risk has been considered.

Mechanism

Sodium polystyrene sulfonate is a nonabsorbed cation-exchange resin that exchanges sodium for potassium in the GI tract, especially the colon. Bound potassium leaves in stool. It does not stabilize the myocardium or rapidly shift potassium into cells, so ECG-change hyperkalemia still needs emergency protocol therapy.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for Sodium polystyrene sulfonate in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Description

Sodium polystyrene sulfonate is a nonabsorbed cation-exchange potassium binder used to remove excess potassium through the GI tract in selected non-emergent hyperkalemia plans. It trades sodium for potassium in the intestine so potassium leaves in stool; it does not rapidly protect the myocardium when ECG changes signal life-threatening hyperkalemia.
