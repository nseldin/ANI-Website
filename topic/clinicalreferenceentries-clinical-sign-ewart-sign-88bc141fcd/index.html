Definition

Ewart sign is a percussion and auscultatory sign. It is a clue to large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base. A positive finding means dullness with bronchial breathing or egophony appears at the left infrascapular region.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Ewart sign means dullness with bronchial breathing or egophony appears at the left infrascapular region.

Why It Matters

A positive Ewart sign response can signal large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base. An enlarged fluid-filled pericardial sac compresses adjacent lung and produces local atelectatic consolidation signs. Ewart sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base

Assessment Method

Examine below the left scapular angle with percussion, breath sounds, and voice transmission.

Positive Finding

Dullness with bronchial breathing or egophony appears at the left infrascapular region.

Mechanism

An enlarged fluid-filled pericardial sac compresses adjacent lung and produces local atelectatic consolidation signs.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Ewart sign response raises concern for large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base because an enlarged fluid-filled pericardial sac compresses adjacent lung and produces local atelectatic consolidation signs. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Ewart sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Ewart sign to focus evaluation of large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base and the reviewed alternatives: left lower-lobe collapse, pleural fluid, splenomegaly. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Examine below the left scapular angle with percussion, breath sounds, and voice transmission. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Ewart sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Obesity, small effusions, and coexisting lung disease limit accuracy. Therefore, Ewart sign cannot confirm or exclude large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • left lower-lobe collapse

  • pleural fluid

  • splenomegaly

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from left lower-lobe collapse, pleural fluid, splenomegaly, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Ewart sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; imaging is preferred. Ewart sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude large pericardial effusion compressing the left lung base. Apply the reviewed limitation for Ewart sign: Obesity, small effusions, and coexisting lung disease limit accuracy.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Ewart sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Examine below the left scapular angle with percussion, breath sounds, and voice transmission. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not infer tamponade severity from this regional chest finding.
