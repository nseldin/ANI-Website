  • Content

    Hantavirus is an acellular, obligately intracellular virus with a negative sense ssrna genome and a enveloped particle. Envelope status helps explain environmental stability and susceptibility to routine controls, while genome type shapes replication and diagnostic targets. Capsid symmetry is left explicitly unknown in this bounded record rather than inferred without a reviewed taxonomic source.

    Structure and traits

  • Content

    Rodent control and ventilated, low-dust cleanup methods that avoid aerosolizing rodent waste reduce exposure; no routine U.S. vaccine is available. The reviewed route description is zoonotic exposure transmission. This authority entry represents multiple hantaviruses and two major syndrome families with different reviewed intervals. Transmission risk therefore depends on the actual route, exposure, host, setting, and stage rather than the organism name alone.

    ecology-transmission

    Title

    Ecology and transmission

  • Content

    Hantaviruses acquired from infected rodents can injure vascular endothelium; capillary leak predominantly affects lungs in HPS and kidneys in HFRS. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    pathogenesis

    Title

    Pathogenesis

  • Content

    Hantavirus is associated with hantavirus pulmonary syndrome and hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
