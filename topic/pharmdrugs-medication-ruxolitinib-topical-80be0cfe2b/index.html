Class

Topical JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor for atopic dermatitis and nonsegmental vitiligo

Used To Treat

Short-term/non-continuous mild-to-moderate atopic dermatitis in non-immunocompromised patients age 2 years and older and nonsegmental vitiligo in patients age 12 years and older, within strict BSA and quantity limits.

Description

Ruxolitinib cream is a topical Janus kinase inhibitor, not the oral myelofibrosis drug. It inhibits JAK1 and JAK2 inside skin cells, blocking STAT phosphorylation downstream of multiple cytokines that drive dermatitis and melanocyte-directed inflammation. It is used on up to 20% BSA for atopic dermatitis and up to 10% BSA for vitiligo. Despite topical delivery, it carries the full JAK-class boxed warning and requires risk-based infection, malignancy, cardiovascular, thrombosis and blood-count assessment. Why is ruxolitinib cream short-term or non-continuous and restricted by body-surface area and weekly quantity? JAK1/JAK2 inhibition is intended to stay predominantly local, but systemic exposure rises as treated area, amount, frequency, barrier disruption, or occlusion increases. Therefore the label limits application and requires reassessment instead of indefinite whole-body use, while infection, blood-count, malignancy, cardiovascular, and thrombosis risks remain clinically relevant. The boxed warning reflects serious events seen with systemic JAK inhibition; it does not mean every topical user has the same exposure, but it makes dose-area discipline meaningful.

Mechanism

Many cytokine receptors lack intrinsic kinase activity and recruit JAKs. Cytokine binding activates JAK1/JAK2, which phosphorylate receptor tails and STAT proteins; STAT dimers then enter the nucleus and alter inflammatory gene transcription. Ruxolitinib interrupts this relay. In AD, reduced cytokine signaling decreases inflammation and itch. In vitiligo, blocking interferon-gamma-related JAK signaling can reduce cytotoxic T-cell pressure and permit repigmentation, which may take many months.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events and thrombosis have occurred with JAK inhibitors; these risks are included in OPZELURA labeling. Avoid active serious infection and patients at high thrombosis risk, weigh risk carefully in current/past smokers or cardiovascular-risk patients, examine for skin cancer, and discontinue after MI or stroke as labeled.

Adverse Effects

  • Application-site acne/pruritus/erythema, folliculitis and respiratory infections

  • Herpes zoster or other serious/opportunistic infection

  • Anemia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, leukopenia or thrombocytopenia

  • Nonmelanoma skin cancer, MACE and thrombosis risk

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication; avoid active serious infection

  • Combination with biologics, other JAK inhibitors or potent immunosuppressants is not recommended

  • Clarify thrombosis, malignancy, cardiovascular risk, smoking history, cytopenia, pregnancy/lactation and BSA/quantity limits

Nursing Essentials

  • Document disease, age, BSA and tube use: AD up to 20% BSA; vitiligo up to 10% BSA; no occlusion.

  • Screen TB/infection risk, skin cancer, CBC symptoms, smoking/CV history and thrombosis warning signs before and during use.

Interactions

  • Avoid combination with therapeutic biologics, other JAK inhibitors, azathioprine or cyclosporine.

  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase systemic exposure; avoid when possible or assess carefully.

  • Live-vaccine planning and immune status require clinician review.

Key Labs

  • CBC as clinically indicated

  • TB/hepatitis/infection screening based on risk and local practice

  • Skin examination, BSA/quantity tracking, lipids when systemic exposure/risk warrants and pregnancy assessment

Nclex Traps

  • Topical ruxolitinib still carries the JAK boxed warning.

  • AD and vitiligo use different age and BSA limits.

  • Repigmentation in vitiligo is slow; absence of early color return does not justify exceeding tube limits.
