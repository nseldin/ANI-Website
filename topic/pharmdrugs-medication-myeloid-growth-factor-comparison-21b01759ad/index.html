Used To Treat

Product-specific prevention or shortening of severe neutropenia, stem-cell mobilization, transplant-related marrow recovery, and hematopoietic acute-radiation syndrome. These drugs reduce selected infection risks by accelerating cell recovery; they do not treat an existing bacterial infection directly.

Description

Filgrastim and pegfilgrastim stimulate the G-CSF receptor and mainly expand and mature neutrophil precursors. Pegfilgrastim is filgrastim with polyethylene glycol attached, reducing renal clearance and allowing neutrophil-mediated self-clearance after marrow recovery, which supports once-per-cycle dosing. Sargramostim is GM-CSF and acts more broadly on granulocyte, monocyte, macrophage, and related progenitors. Because progenitor cells are dividing rapidly, giving a growth factor too close to cytotoxic chemotherapy can expose the very cells being stimulated to greater injury. The timing rule follows the biology, not convenience.

Mechanism

G-CSF receptor activation on committed myeloid progenitors triggers JAK/STAT, Ras/MAPK, and PI3K/Akt signaling, promoting neutrophil precursor survival, proliferation, differentiation, and release while enhancing selected mature neutrophil functions. Filgrastim is cleared through renal and neutrophil-receptor pathways and is often dosed daily. PEGylation enlarges pegfilgrastim and reduces renal filtration; as neutrophils recover, receptor-mediated clearance rises, creating self-regulating exposure. Sargramostim binds the GM-CSF receptor alpha chain and shared beta chain, activating JAK2/STAT5 and other cascades across a broader myeloid range. That broader action helps explain leukocytosis, fluid leak, effusions, and arrhythmia concerns.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: All three can cause severe hypersensitivity and excessive leukocytosis. G-CSF products can cause splenic rupture, ARDS, sickle-cell crisis, glomerulonephritis, capillary leak, and aortitis. Sargramostim can cause infusion reactions, capillary leak with pleural/pericardial effusions, and supraventricular arrhythmias. Keep product-specific chemotherapy timing and on-body-injector instructions exact.

Adverse Effects

  • Bone or musculoskeletal pain and leukocytosis

  • Splenic rupture, ARDS, capillary leak, aortitis, glomerulonephritis, or sickle-cell crisis with G-CSF products

  • Effusions, edema, infusion reaction, or supraventricular dysrhythmia with sargramostim

Contraindications

  • Serious hypersensitivity to the product or related proteins; filgrastim and pegfilgrastim cross-reactivity matters.

  • Do not give within the product's prohibited interval around myelosuppressive chemotherapy or radiation.

  • Use heightened caution with sickle-cell disease, pulmonary infiltrates, fluid retention, splenomegaly, or myeloid malignancy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Trend ANC and total WBC against the chemotherapy nadir and stop criteria; a high WBC does not prove infection is treated.

  • Escalate left upper quadrant or shoulder pain, new hypoxemia, edema/hypotension, fever with back or abdominal pain, hematuria, or a new inflammatory syndrome.

Interactions

  • Lithium and corticosteroids can add myeloproliferative effects, especially with GM-CSF.

  • Cytotoxic chemotherapy timing is an interaction with marrow biology even when no metabolic interaction exists.

Key Labs

  • CBC with differential/ANC and trend through nadir and recovery

  • Spleen symptoms/exam, oxygenation, renal findings, inflammatory symptoms, weight/fluid balance, and infusion reaction surveillance

Nclex Traps

  • Filgrastim raises neutrophil production; it is not an antibiotic.

  • Pegfilgrastim is not simply a larger daily dose of filgrastim, and sargramostim is GM-CSF rather than G-CSF.
