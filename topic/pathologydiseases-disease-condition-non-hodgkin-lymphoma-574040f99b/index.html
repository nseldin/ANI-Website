Definition

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma develops when diverse clonal B-, T-, or NK-cell neoplasms arise in nodes or extranodal tissue and range from indolent to rapidly proliferative. The characteristic pattern is persistent painless nodes, extranodal mass symptoms, marrow cytopenias, splenomegaly, or constitutional symptoms with behavior determined by subtype. Confirm or stage it with excisional or adequate core biopsy with morphology, immunophenotype, cytogenetics, and molecular studies; distinguish it from infection, autoimmune adenopathy, Hodgkin lymphoma, leukemia, and metastatic cancer by complete tissue classification. The priority is to match observation, antibody, chemotherapy, targeted, cellular, or radiation therapy to exact subtype and stage because tumor lysis can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma. can follow if the process advances.

Pathophysiology

Malignant cells grow abnormally, invade local tissue, and may spread through lymphatic or bloodstream routes.

Signs Symptoms

  • Fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, bruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, fever, bone pain, lymphadenopathy, weight loss, neurologic change, or transfusion symptoms guide hematology/oncology priority.

  • Escalate neutropenic fever, active bleeding, severe anemia symptoms, tumor lysis signs, hypercalcemia crisis, or transfusion reaction.

Diagnostics

  • excisional or adequate core biopsy with morphology, immunophenotype, cytogenetics, and molecular studies

  • PET-CT or subtype-appropriate imaging plus marrow assessment when indicated

  • LDH, viral, organ, cardiac, and tumor-lysis risk evaluation

  • Distinguish Non-Hodgkin lymphoma from infection, autoimmune adenopathy, Hodgkin lymphoma, leukemia, and metastatic cancer by complete tissue classification.

Labs

  • hemoglobin/hematocrit may fall with anemia or bleeding

  • platelets may fall with bleeding risk

  • ANC below 500 means severe neutropenia risk

  • calcium, potassium, phosphate, and uric acid matter in tumor lysis

  • CBC may show anemia, leukopenia/leukocytosis, or thrombocytopenia depending on disease and treatment

  • calcium may rise in selected malignancies

Treatments

  • match observation, antibody, chemotherapy, targeted, cellular, or radiation therapy to exact subtype and stage

  • prevent tumor lysis and infection in high-burden aggressive disease

  • use transplant or cellular therapy for selected relapsed disease

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate for neutropenic fever, airway or vena-cava compression, spinal or bowel symptoms, severe tumor lysis, cytokine-release findings, neurologic decline, or shock because infection, compression, and treatment emergencies require immediate action.

  • Assess node growth, fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue, abdominal fullness, neurologic symptoms, infection risk, and performance status because diverse lymphoma subtypes behave from indolent to immediately aggressive.

  • Coordinate adequate tissue biopsy, immunophenotyping, molecular studies, PET or CT staging, marrow assessment when indicated, and baseline organ testing because histology and biology determine treatment more than the word lymphoma alone.

  • Administer prescribed immunochemotherapy, targeted therapy, cellular therapy, prophylaxis, and supportive products with independent verification because regimens carry subtype-specific infusion, infection, marrow, cytokine, neurologic, and tumor-lysis risks.

  • Monitor blood counts, electrolytes, uric acid, kidney and liver function, fever, infusion reactions, cognition, oxygenation, pain, and response because toxicity and rapidly changing tumor burden may threaten several organs.

Complications

  • tumor lysis can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • infection can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • marrow failure can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • organ compression can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • central nervous system spread

  • treatment toxicity can complicate Non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

Red Flags

  • Fever during neutropenia, rigors, hypotension, confusion, or suspected sepsis

  • Stridor, facial swelling, venous distention, bowel obstruction, or new cord-compression findings

  • Rising potassium, phosphate or uric acid with oliguria, weakness, or dysrhythmia

  • Fever with hypotension, hypoxemia, severe agitation, seizure, or acute neurologic decline after therapy

Patient Education

  • Know the exact lymphoma subtype and treatment because different diseases sharing this name can have very different urgency, medicines, and follow-up.

  • Call immediately for fever, breathing difficulty, facial swelling, weakness, bowel obstruction symptoms, or confusion because infection or compression can progress rapidly.

Nclex Traps

  • Non-Hodgkin lymphoma is not one treatment entity

  • Steroids before biopsy can obscure diagnostic tissue in some presentations
