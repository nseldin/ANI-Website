Summary

Urinalysis is a high-yield Renal/Urologic diagnostic reference. Screens urine for infection, hydration, glucose/ketones, blood, protein, and renal clues.

Quick Answer

Urinalysis: Screens urine for infection, hydration, glucose/ketones, blood, protein, and renal clues. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Infection pattern

    Meaning

    Leukocyte esterase, nitrites, WBCs, bacteria, and symptoms support UTI; culture clarifies organism and susceptibility when needed.

  • Label

    Kidney/glomerular pattern

    Meaning

    Protein, blood, RBC casts, or dysmorphic RBCs suggest kidney or glomerular disease rather than simple contamination.

  • Label

    Metabolic/hydration pattern

    Meaning

    Glucose, ketones, specific gravity, pH, and bilirubin/urobilinogen can point toward diabetes, dehydration, acid-base, liver, or medication contexts.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Screens urine for infection, hydration, glucose/ketones, blood, protein, and renal clues.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
