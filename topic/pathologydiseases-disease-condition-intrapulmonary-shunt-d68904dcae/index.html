Plain Meaning

Blood reaches the left side of the heart without being adequately oxygenated in ventilated alveoli.

Word Origin

A shunt is a bypass. Intrapulmonary shunt means blood bypasses effective gas exchange inside the lung.

Pathology

In shunt physiology, perfusion continues through lung regions that receive little or no ventilation. Because no fresh alveolar gas reaches those capillaries, raising FiO2 has limited effect until the alveoli are reopened, fluid is treated, or the shunt source is corrected.

Etiology

ARDS, pneumonia, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, aspiration, alveolar hemorrhage, and congenital or acquired right-to-left shunt physiology can produce shunt-like hypoxemia.

Signs Symptoms

  • Hypoxemia out of proportion to oxygen delivery

  • Crackles, decreased breath sounds, respiratory distress, cyanosis, or altered mental status depending on cause

  • Low PaO2/FiO2 ratio in ARDS contexts

Diagnostics

  • ABG response to oxygen

  • Chest x-ray/CT for alveolar filling or collapse

  • Echocardiography/bubble study if intracardiac shunt is suspected

  • Ventilator/PEEP response assessment

Treatments

  • Recruit alveoli with PEEP/CPAP when indicated

  • Treat pneumonia, edema, atelectasis, aspiration, or hemorrhage

  • Prone positioning or advanced ventilator strategies in severe ARDS

  • Correct structural shunts when applicable

Nursing Priorities

  • Recognize that shunt oxygenation may not normalize with FiO2 alone

  • Track oxygenation response after recruitment maneuvers, PEEP changes, suctioning, positioning, and diuresis/antibiotics when ordered

  • Protect hemodynamics because aggressive pressure support can lower preload

Complications

  • Refractory hypoxemia

  • Respiratory failure

  • Pulmonary hypertension/right-heart strain

  • Cardiac arrest

Patient Education

  • Explain that oxygen may need pressure support because some air sacs are not open enough to exchange gas.

Nclex Traps

  • Shunt is perfusion without ventilation; dead space is ventilation without perfusion.

  • High FiO2 alone is weak against true shunt if the alveoli remain collapsed or flooded.
