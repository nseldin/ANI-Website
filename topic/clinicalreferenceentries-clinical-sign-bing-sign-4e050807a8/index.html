Definition

Bing sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to corticospinal tract dysfunction. A positive finding means the great toe extends rather than flexes away.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Bing sign means the great toe extends rather than flexes away.

Why It Matters

A positive Bing sign response can signal corticospinal tract dysfunction. A noxious distal stimulus may recruit the pathologic extensor plantar pattern after corticospinal disinhibition. Bing sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • corticospinal tract dysfunction

Assessment Method

Use a sterile blunt pin stimulus on the dorsum of the great toe while avoiding skin injury.

Positive Finding

The great toe extends rather than flexes away.

Mechanism

A noxious distal stimulus may recruit the pathologic extensor plantar pattern after corticospinal disinhibition.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Bing sign response raises concern for corticospinal tract dysfunction because a noxious distal stimulus may recruit the pathologic extensor plantar pattern after corticospinal disinhibition. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Bing sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Bing sign to focus evaluation of corticospinal tract dysfunction and the reviewed alternatives: normal nociceptive withdrawal, neuropathy, fear response. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Use a sterile blunt pin stimulus on the dorsum of the great toe while avoiding skin injury. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Bing sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Pain response and sensory loss make the sign unreliable; no stable accuracy estimate is available. Therefore, Bing sign cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • normal nociceptive withdrawal

  • neuropathy

  • fear response

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from normal nociceptive withdrawal, neuropathy, fear response, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Bing sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct; potentially uncomfortable and seldom required. Bing sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude corticospinal tract dysfunction. Apply the reviewed limitation for Bing sign: Pain response and sensory loss make the sign unreliable; no stable accuracy estimate is available.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Bing sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Use a sterile blunt pin stimulus on the dorsum of the great toe while avoiding skin injury. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Never pierce the skin or use the maneuver when sensation, perfusion, or infection risk is abnormal.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Bing sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The great toe extends rather than flexes away. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Use a sterile blunt pin stimulus on the dorsum of the great toe while avoiding skin injury. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, corticospinal tract dysfunction, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Bing sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Never pierce the skin or use the maneuver when sensation, perfusion, or infection risk is abnormal. Base escalation for Bing sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
