Used To Treat

Life-threatening or potentially life-threatening digoxin toxicity and selected severe non-digoxin cardiac-glycoside poisonings under toxicology guidance.

Mechanism

Fab fragments bind free extracellular digoxin with high affinity, lowering the active concentration. That creates a gradient that pulls digoxin away from tissue Na/K-ATPase, restoring pump function and reducing vagal and direct conduction toxicity. Potassium can fall rapidly as pump activity returns, which is a desired mechanistic sign but can overshoot into hypokalemia. The Fab-digoxin complex is cleared mainly by the kidneys; renal failure can prolong complex residence and allow recurrent free drug, so response and unbound levels matter more than a high post-treatment total level.

Contraindications

  • There is no absolute contraindication when life-threatening toxicity is present, but prior Fab allergy and papaya, papain, chymopapain, or bromelain sensitivity may increase hypersensitivity risk.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain the pre-Fab digoxin level when it will not delay rescue, exact weight, renal function, potassium, ECG, exposure amount and time because these determine dose and recurrence risk.

  • Use continuous ECG, hemodynamic, and oxygenation monitoring; recheck potassium and magnesium frequently and renal function serially because reversal can rapidly lower potassium, unmask underlying atrial fibrillation or heart failure, and recur when Fab-glycoside complexes clear slowly.

  • After Fab, label total digoxin results as uninterpretable for toxicity and use clinical response or a validated free/unbound assay if available.

Interactions

  • Fab neutralizes therapeutic digoxin as well as toxic digoxin, so heart failure or ventricular rate control may worsen after reversal.

  • Renal failure delays Fab-complex clearance; calcium, antiarrhythmics, pacing, and electrolyte treatment require toxicology guidance because cardiac-glycoside myocardium is unstable.

Key Labs

  • Pre-treatment serum digoxin, serial potassium and magnesium, creatinine, ECG, blood gas, glucose, and free or unbound digoxin after Fab when recurrence is suspected; total post-Fab digoxin commonly rises and is not a toxicity endpoint.

Nclex Traps

  • A high total digoxin concentration after Fab usually reflects bound drug and should not trigger automatic redosing because routine assays commonly measure Fab-bound as well as unbound digoxin.

  • Potassium can fall quickly after successful reversal.

  • Dose calculations differ for known acute ingestion and chronic steady-state concentration.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Renal failure

    Note

    The Fab-glycoside complex clears slowly, making recurrent free toxicity possible for days and extending monitoring requirements.

  • Population

    Infants and children

    Note

    Dose calculations are weight- and burden-based; a small absolute ingestion can be large per kilogram, so poison-center calculation is essential.

  • Population

    Chronic digoxin users

    Note

    Toxicity may occur at a modest serum level because age, renal decline, hypokalemia, and interacting drugs change tissue sensitivity.

Boxed Warning

CARDIAC-GLYCOSIDE EMERGENCY: unstable rhythm, shock, or severe hyperkalemia requires immediate Poison Help/toxicology and resuscitation. Dose DigiFab from a validated burden estimate or protocol, use continuous ECG and hemodynamic monitoring, and check potassium frequently because successful binding can rapidly lower serum potassium; never interpret a routine total digoxin level after Fab as free toxic drug because routine assays commonly include Fab-bound digoxin.

Description

Digoxin immune Fab consists of antibody fragments that bind digoxin and related cardiac glycosides. Each DigiFab vial contains 40 mg Fab and binds approximately 0.5 mg digoxin. For a known acute digoxin ingestion, estimated vials equal ingested milligrams divided by 0.5; for chronic toxicity with a known steady-state concentration, estimated vials equal serum digoxin in ng/mL times weight in kg divided by 100. The label offers empiric approaches when the amount is unknown, while AHA toxicology guidance uses larger empiric rescue for a critically ill unknown acute ingestion. Because assay antibodies often measure both bound and unbound drug after Fab, a total digoxin level becomes misleading.

Administration Timing

  • Give promptly for life-threatening dysrhythmia, hemodynamic instability, severe hyperkalemia attributable to acute cardiac-glycoside poisoning, or another specialist-defined severe indication; do not wait for a concentration when the patient is unstable.

  • Known acute digoxin dose: vials = ingested mg / 0.5. Known chronic steady-state concentration: vials = serum ng/mL x weight kg / 100; round and dose through a poison center or toxicologist.

  • When dose or level is unknown, use the current label and toxicology protocol rather than guessing; AHA describes 10 to 20 vials for critically ill unknown acute digoxin poisoning.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypokalemia, worsening heart failure, rapid ventricular response in atrial fibrillation, hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis, fever, and recurrence of toxicity can occur.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Persistent or recurrent dysrhythmia, hyperkalemia, shock, or rising free digoxin requires poison-center consultation, reassessment of dose, repeat Fab consideration, and advanced support.

Evidence Limitations

  • Non-digoxin plant and animal glycosides may cross-react variably with Fab and laboratory assays, so dose-response is less predictable.
