Class Example Names

  • Raloxifene

  • Calcitonin

Used To Treat

Selected postmenopausal osteoporosis contexts; raloxifene also reduces risk of invasive breast cancer in defined postmenopausal groups, while calcitonin has limited alternative-use roles in Paget disease and hypercalcemia.

Description

Raloxifene and calcitonin both reduce bone resorption, but they act through different receptors and are not interchangeable general bone drugs. Raloxifene activates estrogen signaling in bone while antagonizing it in breast, improving vertebral-fracture outcomes while carrying thromboembolic and fatal-stroke risk. Calcitonin directly suppresses osteoclast activity but develops tachyphylaxis and has weak osteoporosis fracture evidence plus a malignancy signal, so continued use requires periodic justification.

Mechanism

Raloxifene-bound estrogen receptors recruit tissue-specific coactivators/corepressors: bone turnover falls without uterine estrogen stimulation, while breast estrogen signaling is antagonized. Calcitonin binds a Gs-coupled receptor on osteoclasts, raises cyclic AMP and rapidly reduces ruffled-border resorption; receptor downregulation and signaling desensitization explain loss of effect over time.

Boxed Warning

Raloxifene carries a BOXED WARNING for venous thromboembolism and increased death from stroke in postmenopausal women with coronary disease or high major-coronary-event risk. Calcitonin has no boxed warning but carries a malignancy signal and requires periodic benefit-risk reassessment.

Adverse Effects

  • Raloxifene: hot flashes, leg cramps, edema and VTE

  • Calcitonin: nausea, flushing, hypocalcemia, hypersensitivity and nasal irritation by route

  • Neither provides the broad fracture profile of every first-line osteoporosis therapy

Contraindications

  • Raloxifene: active/past VTE and pregnancy

  • Calcitonin: salmon-calcitonin hypersensitivity and uncorrected hypocalcemia

  • Match drug to fracture site, cancer/stroke/VTE profile and alternative availability

Nursing Essentials

  • Stop raloxifene at least 72 hours before and during prolonged immobilization when labeling directs because stasis and estrogenic coagulation effects compound VTE risk.

  • Reassess calcitonin benefit periodically and monitor nasal mucosa or injection hypersensitivity by route.

Interactions

  • Cholestyramine markedly reduces raloxifene absorption; highly protein-bound drugs require review.

  • Calcitonin can increase lithium clearance and lower lithium concentrations.

  • Calcium/vitamin-D strategy must match hypocalcemia risk without causing excess.

Key Labs

  • Calcium and vitamin D

  • VTE/stroke risk and bone density for raloxifene

  • Calcium, alkaline phosphatase/clinical response and route-specific nasal exam for calcitonin

Nclex Traps

  • Raloxifene reduces invasive breast-cancer risk but does not treat existing cancer or hot flashes.

  • Calcitonin can lower calcium and then lose effect through tachyphylaxis.

  • Vertebral benefit does not automatically mean proven hip-fracture benefit.
