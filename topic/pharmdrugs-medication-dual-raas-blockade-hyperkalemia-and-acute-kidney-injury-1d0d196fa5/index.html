Used To Treat

This card explains why routine ACE-inhibitor plus ARB therapy is avoided, why aliskiren with ACE inhibitor or ARB is contraindicated in diabetes, and why Entresto must not be combined with a separate ACE inhibitor or ARB.

Description

Blocking more than one point in the RAAS can lower pressure or albuminuria further, but it also removes redundant physiologic supports for filtration and potassium excretion. ACE inhibitor plus ARB, ACE inhibitor or ARB plus aliskiren, and accidental ARB duplication with sacubitril/valsartan increase hypotension, hyperkalemia, and acute kidney injury without routine outcome benefit sufficient to justify the added harm. The risk is greatest when CKD, diabetes, older age, dehydration, heart failure, NSAID use, diuretics, potassium supplements, trimethoprim, or mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists are present. Sacubitril/valsartan creates an additional rule: it already contains valsartan, so a separate ARB is duplicate therapy; an ACE inhibitor is contraindicated because combined ACE and neprilysin inhibition markedly increases angioedema risk. Medication reconciliation must recognize ingredients and pathways, not just brand names.

Mechanism

Each RAAS blocker reduces AT1 and aldosterone signaling by a different route. Stacking them produces greater efferent-arteriole dilation, lower glomerular capillary pressure, less aldosterone-mediated potassium secretion, and more systemic pressure reduction. When renal inflow falls, the kidney has less ability to compensate, so GFR can collapse. Hyperkalemia rises because distal nephron sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion lose aldosterone support while CKD limits potassium clearance. Aliskiren combination trials in diabetes showed increased renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and hypotension, supporting the contraindication. ACE inhibitor plus sacubitril also blocks two peptide-degradation pathways, allowing bradykinin-related mediators to accumulate and increasing angioedema risk beyond ordinary duplicate RAAS effects.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING - FETAL TOXICITY: When pregnancy is detected, discontinue as soon as possible and arrange an appropriate alternative. Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can injure or kill the developing fetus. During the second and third trimesters, fetal renal perfusion and filtration depend on angiotensin II signaling; blocking that pathway can cause fetal anuria and oligohydramnios. Because later amniotic fluid is largely fetal urine, prolonged oligohydramnios can produce pulmonary hypoplasia and skeletal or skull deformation. Neonatal hypotension, anuria, renal failure, and death can follow. Every drug in a dual-RAAS combination also carries fetal toxicity. Duplicate therapy compounds maternal renal and pressure risk without creating a fetal-safe exception.

Adverse Effects

  • Hypotension, dizziness, syncope, creatinine rise, acute kidney injury, hyperkalemia, and reduced urine output.

  • Airway angioedema with ACE inhibitor plus ARNI, severe dysrhythmia from potassium elevation, shock, dialysis-requiring AKI, and fetal injury.

Contraindications

  • Do not combine aliskiren with an ACE inhibitor or ARB in patients with diabetes. Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with sacubitril/valsartan or administer within 36 hours.

  • Routine ACE inhibitor plus ARB and ARB plus ARNI duplication should be questioned. Any rare specialist exception requires explicit indication and close monitoring.

Nursing Essentials

  • Expand brand names into ingredients: Entresto contains valsartan; Lotrel contains benazepril; Exforge contains valsartan; Tekturna is aliskiren. This prevents hidden duplicate pathways.

  • If duplicate blockade is discovered, assess pressure, orthostasis, creatinine/eGFR, potassium, urine output, hydration, NSAID/diuretic use, pregnancy, and swelling before escalating.

Interactions

  • NSAIDs, diuretics, potassium products, MRAs, trimethoprim, heparin, and CKD amplify the same renal or potassium hazards.

  • Lithium concentration can rise as renal sodium handling and GFR change; neurologic or GI symptoms require prompt assessment.

Key Labs

  • Potassium, creatinine/eGFR, BUN, bicarbonate, sodium, urine output, pressure, ECG when potassium is high, and volume/perfusion assessment.

  • Trend values after every initiation or titration and sooner during acute illness. A normal old result does not protect a newly dehydrated patient.

Nclex Traps

  • More complete pathway blockade is not automatically better. Surrogate improvements such as lower albuminuria do not outweigh increased AKI, hyperkalemia, and hypotension when patient outcomes do not improve.

  • Entresto is already an ARNI containing valsartan. Adding an ARB is duplication; adding an ACE inhibitor also violates the bradykinin washout rule.
