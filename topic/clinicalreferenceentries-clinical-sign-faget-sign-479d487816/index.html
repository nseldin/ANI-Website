Definition

Faget sign is a pulse-temperature relationship sign. It is a clue to relative bradycardia during selected infections. A positive finding means pulse is slower than expected for the degree of fever.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Faget sign means pulse is slower than expected for the degree of fever.

Why It Matters

The observed Faget sign finding can signal relative bradycardia during selected infections. Pathogen, cytokine, medication, or conduction effects blunt the usual fever-related chronotropic response. Faget sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • relative bradycardia during selected infections

Assessment Method

Compare heart rate with measured fever over time.

Positive Finding

Pulse is slower than expected for the degree of fever.

Mechanism

Pathogen, cytokine, medication, or conduction effects blunt the usual fever-related chronotropic response.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Faget sign finding raises concern for relative bradycardia during selected infections because pathogen, cytokine, medication, or conduction effects blunt the usual fever-related chronotropic response. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Faget sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Faget sign to focus evaluation of relative bradycardia during selected infections and the reviewed alternatives: drug effect, athletic bradycardia, myocarditis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Compare heart rate with measured fever over time. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Faget sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Beta-blockers, age, conduction disease, and arbitrary thresholds strongly confound it. Therefore, Faget sign cannot confirm or exclude relative bradycardia during selected infections by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • athletic bradycardia

  • myocarditis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as drug effect, athletic bradycardia, myocarditis.

False Negative Situations

Faget sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of relative bradycardia during selected infections.

Modern Status

Historical clue with inconsistent definitions. Faget sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude relative bradycardia during selected infections. Apply the reviewed limitation for Faget sign: Beta-blockers, age, conduction disease, and arbitrary thresholds strongly confound it.
