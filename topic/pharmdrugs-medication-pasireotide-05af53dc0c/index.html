Class

Used To Treat

Pituitary Cushing disease when surgery is not an option or not curative; long-acting formulation also treats selected inadequately controlled acromegaly.

Boxed Warning

No drug-specific boxed warning is listed for pasireotide in this nursing reference. Major safety review centers on hypersensitivity, organ toxicity, route-specific hazards, high-alert dosing, population risk, and serious class warnings when present.

Mechanism

Pasireotide activates somatostatin receptors, especially SSTR5, reducing adenylate-cyclase signaling and secretion of ACTH or GH from susceptible pituitary tumors. In pancreatic beta and enteroendocrine cells it reduces insulin, GLP-1, and GIP secretion, explaining disproportionate hyperglycemia.

Nursing Essentials

  • Check fasting glucose/A1c before treatment and monitor intensively during initiation; reassess diabetes drugs after dose changes or discontinuation to avoid hyper- or hypoglycemia.

  • Monitor cortisol/hypocortisolism, ECG/heart rate, potassium/magnesium, liver tests, gallbladder disease, stool/fat malabsorption, thyroid/pituitary axes, and tumor response.

Key Labs

  • Glucose/A1c, cortisol or IGF-1 by indication, liver tests, potassium/magnesium, ECG/QTc, thyroid/pituitary hormones, and gallbladder assessment.

Nclex Traps

  • Pasireotide can control Cushing disease while causing diabetes because SSTR5 suppresses insulin and incretins.

Description

Pasireotide is a somatostatin analog with strong SSTR5 activity, allowing it to suppress ACTH from many corticotroph tumors and growth hormone from somatotroph tumors. SSTR5 signaling also suppresses pancreatic insulin and incretin release, so severe hyperglycemia or ketoacidosis can appear quickly even in clients without known diabetes.
