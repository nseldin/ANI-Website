Used To Treat

Product-specific rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, hidradenitis suppurativa, juvenile arthritis, and uveitis. Indications are molecule-specific: a shared TNF target does not mean every agent has the same evidence, route, schedule, pediatric use, or bowel-disease effectiveness.

Description

TNF-alpha is a master inflammatory cytokine that activates endothelial adhesion, leukocyte recruitment, fever, acute-phase signaling, synovial destruction, and granuloma maintenance. Adalimumab and golimumab are fully human monoclonal antibodies; infliximab is a chimeric monoclonal antibody given intravenously; etanercept is a soluble TNF-receptor-2 Fc fusion that also traps lymphotoxin-alpha; certolizumab is a PEGylated antigen-binding fragment without an Fc region. The structural differences matter: because infliximab contains murine variable-region sequences and is infused, antidrug antibodies and infusion reactions deserve special attention. Because etanercept is a receptor fusion rather than an interchangeable monoclonal antibody, its bowel-disease evidence differs. Because certolizumab lacks an Fc region, it lacks FcRn-mediated active placental transport and Fc-dependent effector activity.

Mechanism

Soluble and transmembrane TNF-alpha normally bind TNFR1 and TNFR2, recruiting adaptor proteins that activate NF-kappaB, MAP kinase, cell-survival, inflammatory, and context-dependent death pathways. Monoclonal antibodies neutralize TNF and can engage transmembrane TNF; intact IgG1 Fc regions may recruit complement or Fc-receptor effects. Etanercept presents two extracellular TNFR2 domains fused to human IgG1 Fc, functioning as a decoy receptor for TNF-alpha and lymphotoxin-alpha. Certolizumab is a PEGylated Fab prime fragment: it neutralizes TNF but lacks Fc-mediated complement, antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, and active FcRn placental transport. Blocking TNF reduces inflammation but also weakens granuloma integrity, which explains tuberculosis and endemic-fungal reactivation risk. Therefore, a shared target predicts a shared infection warning but does not erase molecule-specific route, Fc, pregnancy-transfer, immunogenicity, or indication differences.

Boxed Warning

All five represented TNF blockers carry boxed warnings for serious infections and malignancy. Test for latent tuberculosis and assess hepatitis B before treatment, treat latent TB when indicated, avoid starting during active infection, and stop or interrupt for serious infection or sepsis according to the exact label. The warning includes bacterial sepsis, disseminated or extrapulmonary TB, invasive fungal disease, opportunistic infection, and lymphoma or other malignancy concerns, especially in younger patients and with combined immunosuppression.

Adverse Effects

  • Injection or infusion reactions, serious infection, TB or HBV reactivation, invasive fungal infection, cytopenias, and malignancy

  • Heart-failure worsening, demyelinating disease, lupus-like autoimmunity, paradoxical psoriasis, liver injury, and product-specific immunogenicity

Contraindications

  • Do not combine TNF blockers with each other, anakinra, abatacept, another biologic DMARD, or a JAK inhibitor unless an exceptional specialist protocol explicitly justifies it.

  • Infliximab doses above 5 mg/kg are contraindicated in moderate or severe heart failure; all TNF blockers require careful heart-failure and demyelination review.

Nursing Essentials

  • Before first dose, document TB risk/testing, hepatitis B status, vaccines, infection, malignancy/skin history, heart failure, neurologic disease, CBC/liver context, and pregnancy plan.

  • During therapy, treat fever, cough, weight loss, night sweats, dyspnea, painful rash, neurologic change, jaundice, or infusion reaction as a target-linked safety signal rather than a routine side effect.

Interactions

  • Other biologic or targeted immunosuppressants markedly increase infection risk; live vaccines are avoided during clinically important immunosuppression.

  • Methotrexate may reduce infliximab antidrug antibodies in selected regimens, while immunogenicity and dosing gaps can increase infusion or loss-of-response problems.

Key Labs

  • TB assessment, hepatitis B serology, CBC, liver tests, infection evaluation, and disease-activity response

  • Heart-failure symptoms, neurologic findings, skin examination, infusion/injection reactions, and age-appropriate malignancy surveillance

Nclex Traps

  • A negative baseline TB test does not end monitoring; new exposure or symptoms still matter because TNF blockade can produce disseminated disease.

  • Certolizumab's low placental transfer is a molecular distinction, not proof that every pregnancy question is risk-free or that live infant vaccines never need review.
