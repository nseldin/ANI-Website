Used To Treat

WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension to improve exercise capacity and reduce clinical worsening, progression, or hospitalization, alone or in evidence-based combination with another PAH pathway.

Description

Endothelin-receptor antagonists block endothelin-1, a potent pulmonary vasoconstrictor and vascular-growth signal. Ambrisentan is relatively ETA-selective; bosentan and macitentan block ETA and ETB. ETA selectivity preserves more endothelial ETB-mediated endothelin clearance and nitric-oxide/prostacyclin release, while dual antagonism also blocks smooth-muscle ETB constriction. Selectivity is a mechanistic distinction, not proof that one drug is universally superior.

Mechanism

ETA receptors on pulmonary vascular smooth muscle couple mainly to Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, and intracellular calcium, causing vasoconstriction, hypertrophy, fibrosis, and proliferation. ETB receptors are context dependent: endothelial ETB helps clear endothelin and release nitric oxide and prostacyclin, whereas smooth-muscle ETB can constrict. Blocking pathologic endothelin signaling lowers pulmonary vascular resistance and right-ventricular afterload over time; it does not directly remove fluid or repair an acutely failing right ventricle.

Boxed Warning

All three have boxed embryo-fetal toxicity warnings and are contraindicated during pregnancy; exclude pregnancy and maintain effective contraception during therapy and for one month after stopping. Bosentan additionally has boxed hepatotoxicity, monthly aminotransferase monitoring, and a restricted REMS. Ambrisentan and macitentan do not inherit bosentan's exact monthly-liver schedule, but liver injury remains a reason for symptom-driven testing and reassessment.

Adverse Effects

  • Peripheral edema, nasal congestion, headache, flushing, and anemia or hemoglobin decline are common class problems.

  • Hepatotoxicity is most operationally prominent with bosentan. Pulmonary edema can reveal pulmonary veno-occlusive disease, and decreased sperm counts may occur.

Contraindications

  • Pregnancy is contraindicated for all three. Ambrisentan is contraindicated in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis; macitentan is contraindicated after serious hypersensitivity; bosentan has additional contraindicated interactions such as cyclosporine or glyburide under current labeling.

  • Do not assume edema is benign drug fluid retention; worsening right-heart failure and pulmonary veno-occlusive disease must remain in the differential.

Interactions

  • Bosentan induces CYP enzymes and can reduce hormonal-contraceptive exposure; hormonal contraception alone may therefore be unreliable. Bosentan interactions with cyclosporine and glyburide are particularly important.

  • Cyclosporine raises ambrisentan exposure and limits its dose. Strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors and combined CYP3A4/CYP2C9 inhibition can make macitentan exposure ineffective or excessive.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify pregnancy testing and contraception, baseline hemoglobin, edema/weight, blood pressure, liver history, pulmonary edema or PVOD risk, reproductive counseling, and all interactions before therapy.

  • Follow the exact product: monthly ALT/AST for bosentan; hemoglobin at initiation, one month, and periodically for ambrisentan; hemoglobin and clinical liver assessment for macitentan according to label and context.

  • Escalate acute pulmonary edema, rapid weight gain, syncope, worsening oxygenation, jaundice, dark urine, severe fatigue, or symptomatic anemia.

Key Labs

  • Pregnancy testing, hemoglobin/hematocrit, bosentan monthly ALT/AST with bilirubin when indicated, product-specific liver assessment, and PAH functional/right-heart response.

Nclex Traps

  • Bosentan is dual ETA/ETB blockade with monthly liver tests and CYP induction; do not transfer that exact monitoring and interaction profile to every ERA.

  • Ambrisentan is ETA-selective, but it still causes edema, anemia, and embryo-fetal harm.

  • Macitentan has an active metabolite and major CYP3A4/CYP2C9 interaction rules.

  • Pulmonary edema after starting an ERA can indicate PVOD rather than ordinary fluid retention.

Population Risks

  • Type

    pregnancy

    Label

    Pregnancy and lactation

    Note

    All three are teratogenic in animal data and contraindicated during pregnancy. Product-specific pregnancy testing, effective contraception through one month after discontinuation, and lactation avoidance are central safety requirements.

  • Type

    pediatric

    Label

    Pediatric use

    Note

    Bosentan has selected labeling from age 3 years for idiopathic or congenital PAH. Ambrisentan and macitentan remain adult indications in current U.S. labeling.

  • Type

    geriatric

    Label

    Older adults

    Note

    Older adults may have mixed PH mechanisms, anemia, edema, hepatic disease, and interacting drugs; confirm WHO Group 1 disease and monitor perfusion and volume closely.
