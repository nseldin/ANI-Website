Used To Treat

Selected shock or severe hypotension states when ordered, especially when bradycardia or low cardiac output is part of the protocol. It is less favored than norepinephrine for many septic shock clients because tachydysrhythmias are more likely.

Mechanism

Stimulates adrenergic receptors in a dose-related pattern: beta-1 effects can increase heart rate and contractility, and higher-dose alpha-1 effects increase vasoconstriction. Low-dose renal-dose dopamine is not a kidney-protection strategy.

Boxed Warning

High-alert safety focus: tachydysrhythmias, myocardial ischemia, hypertension, tissue ischemia, extravasation injury, and using it before correcting severe hypovolemia.

Contraindications

  • Clarify uncorrected tachydysrhythmias or ventricular fibrillation.

  • Question use in pheochromocytoma or severe uncorrected hypovolemia unless the prescriber is managing an emergency exception.

Nursing Essentials

  • Use an infusion pump and monitor ECG continuously for tachycardia, ectopy, and dysrhythmias.

  • Trend MAP, urine output, mentation, peripheral perfusion, IV site, lactate, and chest pain.

  • Do not document low-dose dopamine as renal protection; assess actual urine output and kidney function.

Interactions

  • MAO inhibitors, tricyclic antidepressants, other vasopressors, and sympathomimetics can increase pressor or dysrhythmia effects.

  • Phenytoin with dopamine has been associated with hypotension and bradycardia in some references; clarify before coadministration.

Key Labs

  • Continuous ECG and blood pressure.

  • Urine output and creatinine/eGFR.

  • Lactate and perfusion markers in shock.

  • Potassium and acid-base status as ordered.

Nclex Traps

  • Dopamine in the ICU is not the same teaching anchor as dopamine agonists for Parkinson disease.

  • If the client develops tachydysrhythmias or chest pain, reassess and notify the provider; more pressure can come at the cost of ischemia.
