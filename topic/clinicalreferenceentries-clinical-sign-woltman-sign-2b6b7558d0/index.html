Definition

Woltman sign is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to hypothyroidism. A positive finding means the Achilles reflex contracts normally but relaxes slowly.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Woltman sign means the Achilles reflex contracts normally but relaxes slowly.

Why It Matters

A positive Woltman sign response can signal hypothyroidism. Reduced thyroid hormone slows calcium reuptake and muscle relaxation kinetics. Woltman sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • hypothyroidism

Assessment Method

Elicit the ankle jerk with the muscle relaxed and observe relaxation.

Positive Finding

The Achilles reflex contracts normally but relaxes slowly.

Mechanism

Reduced thyroid hormone slows calcium reuptake and muscle relaxation kinetics.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Woltman sign response raises concern for hypothyroidism because reduced thyroid hormone slows calcium reuptake and muscle relaxation kinetics. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Woltman sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Woltman sign to focus evaluation of hypothyroidism and the reviewed alternatives: peripheral neuropathy, myxedema, medication effect. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Elicit the ankle jerk with the muscle relaxed and observe relaxation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Woltman sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Age, neuropathy, and technique alter the reflex; absence does not exclude hypothyroidism. Therefore, Woltman sign cannot confirm or exclude hypothyroidism by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • peripheral neuropathy

  • myxedema

  • medication effect

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from peripheral neuropathy, myxedema, medication effect, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Woltman sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Woltman sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Elicit the ankle jerk with the muscle relaxed and observe relaxation. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Time relaxation rather than merely calling the reflex weak.
