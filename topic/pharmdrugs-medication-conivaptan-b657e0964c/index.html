Class

Intravenous dual vasopressin V1a- and V2-receptor antagonist

Used To Treat

Euvolemic and hypervolemic hyponatremia in hospitalized patients under FDA labeling. Treatment is a short continuous-infusion course, generally two to four days, when parenteral aquaresis and close sodium monitoring are appropriate; current availability and formulary access vary.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. FDA boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Conivaptan can overcorrect serum sodium and cause osmotic demyelination, especially in malnutrition, alcoholism, or advanced liver disease. It is a potent CYP3A inhibitor with major contraindicated interactions, and infusion-site phlebitis or tissue injury is common. Use only in a monitored hospital setting with frequent sodium, neurologic, hemodynamic, urine-output, medication, and IV-site assessment.

Mechanism

V2 blockade prevents vasopressin-triggered Gs-cAMP-protein-kinase-A signaling and aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting-duct principal cells. Water permeability falls, dilute urine increases, and serum sodium rises because free water is removed more than electrolyte. V1a receptors normally couple through Gq, phospholipase C, IP3, and intracellular calcium to constrict vascular smooth muscle; blocking them can reduce vascular tone and contribute to hypotension. Conivaptan is extensively metabolized by CYP3A and inhibits the same pathway, raising exposure to susceptible co-medications. Continuous infusion sustains receptor blockade but creates local venous irritation; a large vein, site checks, and site rotation reduce injury. Sodium can overcorrect when aquaresis is brisk, oral water cannot match loss, another corrective therapy is added, or the underlying vasopressin stimulus ends.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain baseline sodium, osmolality, urine studies, volume state, neurologic findings, pressure, potassium, kidney and liver function, and a complete interaction review. Use a large vein and inspect the site frequently.

  • Measure sodium and urine response at protocol-defined intervals, maintain access to water when safe, rotate or change the infusion site as directed, and stop or actively manage therapy if correction exceeds the target.

Contraindications

  • Hypovolemic hyponatremia, anuria, inability to respond to thirst, urgent need for immediate sodium correction, and use with strong CYP3A inhibitors are central contraindication patterns.

  • Because conivaptan inhibits CYP3A, review all narrow-therapeutic-index substrates and label-specific contraindicated medications before the loading dose.

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A inhibitors are contraindicated, while conivaptan itself increases exposure to CYP3A substrates such as selected statins, immunosuppressants, sedatives, and antiarrhythmics.

  • Other sodium-raising therapy, diuretics, or fluid restriction can accelerate correction; vasodilators add hypotension.

Key Labs

  • Serum sodium at frequent intervals, serum and urine osmolality, urine output, fluid balance, potassium, BUN, creatinine/eGFR, liver context, pressure, neurologic examination, and IV-site findings.

  • Continue interaction awareness after the infusion because CYP3A inhibition does not disappear instantly when the pump stops.

Nclex Traps

  • Conivaptan blocks V1a and V2; tolvaptan is V2 selective. The added V1a effect helps explain hypotension and a different interaction and infusion profile.

  • The usual two- to four-day course does not define a magic toxicity deadline; it reflects the labeled inpatient infusion strategy and cumulative safety burden.

Description

Conivaptan, branded Vaprisol, is an IV vaptan that blocks both V2 receptors in the kidney and V1a receptors on vascular smooth muscle. V2 blockade produces electrolyte-sparing water excretion and raises serum sodium; V1a blockade can add vasodilation and hypotension. Unlike oral selective tolvaptan, conivaptan requires a loading dose followed by continuous infusion and commonly causes infusion-site phlebitis. It is both a CYP3A substrate and a clinically important CYP3A inhibitor, so interactions can persist beyond the infusion and limit use. It is designed for closely monitored hospitalized euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia, not hypovolemic hyponatremia and not emergency replacement for hypertonic saline when immediate sodium correction is needed. A short course reflects the infusion and interaction design, not a harmless endpoint once four days pass.

Adverse Effects

  • Infusion-site reaction or phlebitis, thirst, headache, hypokalemia, orthostatic hypotension, fever, and polyuria are common or important effects.

  • Overly rapid sodium correction can cause osmotic demyelination; severe hypotension, dehydration, hypernatremia, and interaction-related toxicity can occur.
