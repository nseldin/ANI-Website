Definition

Asymmetric tonic neck reflex is an infant primitive-reflex sign. It is a clue to normal early brainstem motor pattern. A positive finding means face-side limbs extend while skull-side limbs flex in a fencer posture.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Asymmetric tonic neck reflex means face-side limbs extend while skull-side limbs flex in a fencer posture.

Why It Matters

A positive Asymmetric tonic neck reflex response can signal normal early brainstem motor pattern. Neck proprioceptive input organizes primitive ipsilateral extension and contralateral flexion. Asymmetric tonic neck reflex cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • normal early brainstem motor pattern

Assessment Method

Turn the supine infant's head gently to one side.

Positive Finding

Face-side limbs extend while skull-side limbs flex in a fencer posture.

Mechanism

Neck proprioceptive input organizes primitive ipsilateral extension and contralateral flexion.

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from voluntary posture, dystonia, birth injury, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Asymmetric tonic neck reflex may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.
