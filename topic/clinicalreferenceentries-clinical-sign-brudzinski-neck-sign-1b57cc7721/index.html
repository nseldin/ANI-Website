Definition

Brudzinski neck sign is a bedside meningeal maneuver. It is a clue to meningeal irritation. A positive finding means involuntary hip and knee flexion occurs.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Brudzinski neck sign means involuntary hip and knee flexion occurs.

Why It Matters

A positive Brudzinski neck sign response can signal meningeal irritation. Neck flexion stretches inflamed meninges, prompting protective flexion that reduces tension along the neuraxis. Brudzinski neck sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • meningeal irritation

Assessment Method

Gently flex the supine patient's neck while watching the hips and knees.

Positive Finding

Involuntary hip and knee flexion occurs.

Mechanism

Neck flexion stretches inflamed meninges, prompting protective flexion that reduces tension along the neuraxis.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Brudzinski neck sign response raises concern for meningeal irritation because neck flexion stretches inflamed meninges, prompting protective flexion that reduces tension along the neuraxis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Brudzinski neck sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Brudzinski neck sign to focus evaluation of meningeal irritation and the reviewed alternatives: paratonia, neck pain, voluntary guarding. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Gently flex the supine patient's neck while watching the hips and knees. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Brudzinski neck sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

A negative sign does not exclude meningitis and positive responses are not etiologically specific. Therefore, Brudzinski neck sign cannot confirm or exclude meningeal irritation by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • paratonia

  • neck pain

  • voluntary guarding

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from paratonia, neck pain, voluntary guarding, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Brudzinski neck sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Historical adjunct with poor sensitivity. Brudzinski neck sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude meningeal irritation. Apply the reviewed limitation for Brudzinski neck sign: A negative sign does not exclude meningitis and positive responses are not etiologically specific.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Brudzinski neck sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Gently flex the supine patient's neck while watching the hips and knees. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid neck flexion with suspected cervical injury or raised intracranial-pressure instability.
