Nonhormonal neurokinin-3 receptor antagonist thermoregulatory therapy

Used To Treat

Moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms caused by menopause.

Boxed Warning

Hepatotoxicity has occurred. Before starting, obtain ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and total/direct bilirubin; do not start if ALT, AST or total bilirubin is at least 2 times ULN. Repeat hepatic tests monthly for the first 3 months, then at months 6 and 9. Stop immediately and obtain tests for fatigue, anorexia, nausea/vomiting, pruritus, jaundice, pale stool, dark urine or abdominal pain; discontinue at labeled enzyme/bilirubin thresholds.

Mechanism

Estrogen withdrawal increases neurokinin B activity in kisspeptin/neurokinin B/dynorphin neurons, narrowing the thermoneutral zone and triggering heat-dissipation responses to small temperature shifts. Fezolinetant selectively blocks NK3 receptors on these neurons, rebalances thermoregulatory firing and reduces vasomotor episodes without showing a clear sustained effect on measured estrogen, FSH or testosterone.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify baseline hepatic eligibility and every scheduled result before refills or continuation. Contraindications include cirrhosis, severe renal impairment/end-stage renal disease, and concomitant CYP1A2 inhibitors, which markedly raise exposure.

  • Teach the symptom-triggered stop rule in plain language; do not wait for the next scheduled laboratory date when liver-injury symptoms occur. Track hot-flash frequency/severity and sleep impact without assuming response proves safety.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, ALP and total/direct bilirubin at baseline, monthly for months 1-3, then months 6 and 9; more frequent tests for elevations and renal/hepatic assessment as indicated.

Nclex Traps

  • Fezolinetant is nonhormonal but not monitoring-free; liver surveillance is mandatory.

  • Do not start when either aminotransferase or total bilirubin is at least 2 times ULN, even if the patient feels well.

Description

Fezolinetant is a nonhormonal NK3 receptor antagonist that reduces menopausal hot flashes by blocking neurokinin B signaling on hypothalamic KNDy neurons and stabilizing the thermoregulatory center. It does not replace estrogen or broadly suppress ovarian hormones. Its educational priority is equally mechanistic and safety-critical: rare serious drug-induced liver injury now carries a boxed warning and a fixed laboratory schedule.
