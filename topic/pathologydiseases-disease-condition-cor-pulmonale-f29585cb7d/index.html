Definition

Cor pulmonale develops when pulmonary vascular or parenchymal disease raises right-ventricular afterload and causes right-heart structural and functional failure. The characteristic pattern is jugular venous distention, edema, hepatomegaly, loud pulmonic closure, exertional syncope, and chronic lung or pulmonary vascular disease. Confirm or stage it with echocardiography for right-heart size, function, valves, and pressure estimate; distinguish it from left-heart failure, liver disease, renal edema, constrictive pericarditis, and isolated venous disease by echo, lungs, hemodynamics, and organ studies. The priority is to treat hypoxemia, sleep-disordered breathing, thromboembolism, or lung disease because right-heart shock can complicate Cor pulmonale. can follow if the process advances.

Signs Symptoms

  • Dyspnea, tachypnea, accessory-muscle use, abnormal breath sounds, cough/sputum, chest pain, hypoxemia, cyanosis, fatigue, or confusion.

  • Silent chest, stridor, rising CO2 signs, or exhaustion is more dangerous than a noisy but moving airway.

Diagnostics

  • echocardiography for right-heart size, function, valves, and pressure estimate

  • pulmonary function, imaging, oxygenation, sleep, and thromboembolic evaluation

  • right-heart catheterization when classification or advanced therapy depends on hemodynamics

  • Distinguish Cor pulmonale from left-heart failure, liver disease, renal edema, constrictive pericarditis, and isolated venous disease by echo, lungs, hemodynamics, and organ studies.

Labs

  • PaO2 or SpO2 may fall with gas-exchange failure

  • PaCO2 may rise with ventilatory failure

  • WBC may rise with infection

  • lactate may rise with severe hypoxia or sepsis

Treatments

  • treat hypoxemia, sleep-disordered breathing, thromboembolism, or lung disease

  • use careful diuresis for venous congestion

  • apply pulmonary-hypertension therapy only for an appropriately classified indication

Nursing Priorities

  • Escalate for new syncope, chest pain, severe hypoxemia, rapidly rising oxygen need, hypotension, cool extremities, confusion, oliguria, sustained rhythm change, or abrupt edema and abdominal distention because acute right-ventricular failure can progress to shock.

  • Assess the underlying lung or hypoxic disorder, dyspnea, oxygen use, sleep symptoms, chest pain, syncope, jugular venous distention, edema, hepatomegaly, weight, and functional decline because chronic pulmonary pressure overload eventually weakens the right ventricle.

  • Trend oxygen saturation at rest and activity, respiratory effort, rhythm, blood pressure, daily weight, edema, intake and output, renal and liver function, and ordered echocardiographic or pulmonary-pressure findings because hypoxemia, congestion, and falling output interact.

  • Administer prescribed oxygen to the individualized target, diuretics, inhaled therapy, and treatment of sleep-disordered breathing or thromboembolic disease while monitoring carbon dioxide risk, pressure, electrolytes, and kidney function because both untreated hypoxia and excessive volume removal can worsen right-heart performance.

  • Use paced activity, pulmonary rehabilitation, sodium and fluid guidance, skin protection, vaccination, and smoking cessation because reducing pulmonary stress and preventing respiratory infection preserves limited cardiopulmonary reserve.

Complications

  • right-heart shock can complicate Cor pulmonale.

  • arrhythmia can complicate Cor pulmonale.

  • liver and kidney congestion

  • thrombosis can complicate Cor pulmonale.

  • syncope can complicate Cor pulmonale.

  • sudden death can complicate Cor pulmonale.

Red Flags

  • Syncope or chest pain during minimal exertion

  • Severe hypoxemia or a rapid increase above baseline oxygen requirement

  • Hypotension, cool skin, confusion, oliguria, or narrow pulse pressure

  • Rapidly worsening edema, ascites, jugular venous distention, or sustained dysrhythmia

Patient Education

  • Teach the patient not to change oxygen or diuretic doses independently and to report a rapid weight or oxygen-requirement increase before severe right-heart congestion develops.

  • Explain that protecting the lungs with tobacco avoidance, vaccines, prescribed ventilation, and rehabilitation also protects the right side of the heart.

Nclex Traps

  • Cor pulmonale excludes right-heart failure caused primarily by left-heart disease

  • Excessive diuresis can reduce the preload a failing right ventricle still needs
