Used To Treat

Selected spontaneous preterm labor when a brief delay is likely to benefit the fetus and no maternal or fetal indication requires delivery. Choice is individualized; these drugs are not routine maintenance therapy and do not treat infection, abruption, cervical insufficiency, or membrane rupture itself.

Description

Nifedipine, indomethacin, and terbutaline all reduce uterine contraction through different points in the pathway. Nifedipine removes the calcium signal needed for actin-myosin cycling. Indomethacin lowers prostaglandins that stimulate contraction and cervical remodeling. Terbutaline raises cAMP, which suppresses myosin-light-chain kinase. Their hazards reveal their targets: nifedipine can lower maternal vascular tone and blood pressure; indomethacin can reduce fetal renal perfusion and constrict the prostaglandin-dependent ductus arteriosus; terbutaline can drive maternal beta-adrenergic tachycardia, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia, myocardial ischemia, and pulmonary edema. Selection therefore depends on gestational age and patient physiology, not which drug sounds most obstetric.

Mechanism

Myometrial contraction requires cytosolic calcium to bind calmodulin and activate myosin-light-chain kinase. Nifedipine blocks Cav1.2 L-type channels and decreases that calcium influx. Prostaglandins PGE2 and PGF2-alpha increase calcium signaling, gap-junction coordination, and cervical remodeling; indomethacin inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 and lowers their synthesis. Beta-2 receptors couple to Gs and adenylyl cyclase; terbutaline raises cAMP and protein kinase A activity, which phosphorylates targets that reduce myosin-light-chain kinase activity and calcium sensitivity. Because the same targets exist outside the uterus, adverse effects are predictable rather than random. A drug can reduce contractions while still making the overall maternal-fetal situation less safe.

Boxed Warning

Terbutaline must not be used for prolonged tocolysis beyond 48 to 72 hours or outpatient maintenance; its boxed warning describes maternal deaths and severe cardiopulmonary and metabolic reactions. Indomethacin carries the NSAID cardiovascular and GI boxed warning and pregnancy-specific fetal renal, oligohydramnios, and ductal risks that increase later in gestation. Nifedipine has no U.S. boxed warning for tocolysis, which is an off-label use, but excessive hypotension can reduce maternal and uteroplacental perfusion. No tocolytic should delay delivery when infection, hemorrhage, severe maternal disease, or fetal compromise makes birth the safer treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • Nifedipine: flushing, headache, dizziness, peripheral edema and hypotension; indomethacin: GI, renal, platelet and fetal effects; terbutaline: tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia and hyperglycemia

  • Maternal collapse or pulmonary edema, fetal ductal constriction or oligohydramnios, fetal tachycardia and neonatal hypoglycemia, or progression of the underlying obstetric emergency

Contraindications

  • Contraindications to delaying delivery override the desire to stop contractions. Diagnose the obstetric problem before treating the contraction monitor.

  • Avoid or modify by agent in maternal hypotension/cardiac disease, renal disease, asthma-sensitive NSAID reactions, gestational-age-related ductal risk, bleeding risk, and concurrent vasoactive therapy.

Nursing Essentials

  • Record baseline blood pressure, pulse, respiratory status, lung sounds, glucose/electrolytes when relevant, contraction pattern, fetal tracing, gestational age, and cervical findings.

  • Reassess the intended endpoint and stop time. Continuing because contractions return can convert a short bridge into unproven maintenance exposure.

Interactions

  • Nifedipine plus other antihypertensives or magnesium can magnify hypotension; terbutaline plus sympathomimetics can magnify tachyarrhythmia and ischemia.

  • Indomethacin combines poorly with nephrotoxins, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, and other NSAIDs and can blunt antihypertensive or diuretic effects.

Key Labs

  • Maternal vital signs, renal function, glucose, potassium, fluid balance, and indication-specific infection or preeclampsia studies

  • Fetal heart rate, contractions, cervical change, amniotic fluid, and ductal assessment if indomethacin duration or gestational context warrants it

Nclex Traps

  • Magnesium sulfate may be present in the same patient for fetal neuroprotection or eclampsia prevention, but that does not make it the preferred maintenance tocolytic.

  • A quieter contraction monitor is not automatically clinical success if perfusion falls, infection progresses, or delivery was indicated.
