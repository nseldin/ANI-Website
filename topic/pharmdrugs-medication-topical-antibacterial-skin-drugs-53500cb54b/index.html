Used To Treat

Topical clindamycin for acne vulgaris and topical mupirocin ointment for susceptible staphylococcal or streptococcal impetigo; they are not interchangeable wound creams.

Description

Topical clindamycin and mupirocin inhibit bacterial protein synthesis at entirely different targets. Clindamycin binds 23S rRNA in the 50S ribosome and is used across an acne field, ideally with benzoyl peroxide to limit resistance. Mupirocin competitively inhibits bacterial isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase and is used for short-course impetigo treatment. Neither substitutes for drainage, debridement, systemic therapy, or culture when infection is deep, spreading, purulent, recurrent, or systemic.

Mechanism

Clindamycin prevents peptide-chain elongation at the 50S ribosomal subunit; methylation of 23S rRNA creates macrolide-lincosamide-streptogramin B cross-resistance. Mupirocin resembles isoleucyl-adenylate and blocks charging of tRNA with isoleucine, starving bacterial protein synthesis at low concentrations and becoming bactericidal at higher local concentrations. The unique enzyme target means no routine cross-resistance with common antibiotic classes.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Topical clindamycin can be absorbed enough to cause C. difficile colitis and is contraindicated after antibiotic-associated colitis or with inflammatory bowel disease histories listed in labeling. Mupirocin overuse selects resistance; ointment is not ophthalmic or intranasal, and its polyethylene-glycol vehicle should not be used over large open wounds in significant renal impairment.

Adverse Effects

  • Local burning, pruritus, dryness, erythema and contact dermatitis

  • Clindamycin: diarrhea, bloody diarrhea, C. difficile colitis and gram-negative folliculitis

  • Mupirocin: hypersensitivity and resistant-organism overgrowth

Contraindications

  • Clindamycin/lincomycin hypersensitivity, regional enteritis, ulcerative colitis or prior antibiotic-associated colitis

  • Mupirocin or vehicle hypersensitivity

  • Clarify deep infection, abscess, systemic symptoms, mucosal use, renal impairment with large open wounds and treatment failure

Nursing Essentials

  • Match drug to diagnosis: acne-field suppression is not impetigo therapy, and impetigo ointment is not cellulitis treatment.

  • Reassess mupirocin by 3-5 days and culture/escalate nonresponse rather than extending indefinitely.

Interactions

  • Clindamycin and erythromycin can antagonize each other at overlapping ribosomal binding sites.

  • Do not mix mupirocin ointment with other topical products because dilution can reduce activity and alter stability.

  • Benzoyl peroxide is paired with topical clindamycin to lower resistance pressure.

Key Labs

  • Culture and susceptibility for recurrent, outbreak, MRSA-risk or nonresponding infection

  • C. difficile testing when significant diarrhea occurs

  • Renal function when polyethylene-glycol exposure from large damaged areas is plausible

Nclex Traps

  • Topical route does not eliminate clindamycin colitis risk.

  • Mupirocin ointment is not the same product as intranasal mupirocin.

  • A fluctuant abscess needs source control; adding ointment is not drainage.
