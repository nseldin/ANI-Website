Mechanism

Lithium enters neurons through sodium-related pathways and alters intracellular signaling rather than binding one surface receptor. Its likely therapeutic actions include inhibiting inositol monophosphatase and glycogen-synthase-kinase-3, changing phosphatidylinositol second-messenger recycling, gene transcription, circadian signaling, neurotransmitter release, and neuroplasticity; no single mechanism fully explains its clinical effect. Lithium is filtered at the glomerulus and largely reabsorbed in the proximal tubule with sodium. When the kidney senses low effective volume or sodium, it reabsorbs more sodium and lithium, which explains toxicity after vomiting, diarrhea, fever, heavy sweating, low-sodium intake, diuretics, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, or ARBs.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Lithium toxicity is closely related to serum concentration and may occur near therapeutic levels. Safe use requires available rapid serum-lithium measurement plus clinical surveillance; neurologic toxicity can persist despite a falling number, so treat the patient and exposure pattern, not a level in isolation.

Contraindications

  • Avoid or obtain specialist risk planning in severe renal or cardiovascular disease, marked dehydration, sodium depletion, or inability to monitor levels

  • Pregnancy requires individualized maternal-fetal counseling; first-trimester exposure carries cardiac-malformation risk, while abrupt discontinuation can trigger severe relapse

  • Do not combine casually with interacting diuretics, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or other drugs that impair renal clearance

Nursing Essentials

  • Draw levels at the prescribed post-dose time, commonly a 12-hour trough, because a random early level can look falsely high and cannot be compared reliably with a trough target.

  • Teach stable, not excessive, salt and fluid intake and an illness plan: vomiting, diarrhea, fever, reduced intake, or heavy sweating should trigger prompt advice because volume loss increases renal lithium retention.

Interactions

  • Thiazides, ACE inhibitors, ARBs and many NSAIDs can raise lithium by reducing renal clearance; risk may emerge over days rather than immediately.

  • Loop/potassium-sparing diuretics and sodium-changing illness can also destabilize levels even when the interaction is less predictable.

  • Antipsychotics, serotonergic drugs, calcium-channel blockers and neuromuscular blockers can add neurologic, serotonin, cardiac, or procedural toxicity.

Key Labs

  • Timed serum lithium after initiation, dose/formulation change, interaction, illness and periodically when stable

  • Creatinine/eGFR, sodium, urinalysis or concentrating symptoms, TSH/free T4 and calcium

  • Pregnancy testing/counseling when relevant; ECG in older adults or cardiac risk

Nclex Traps

  • A normal-looking lithium level does not overrule new ataxia, confusion, coarse tremor or dysarthria.

  • Fine stable tremor can be expected; coarse worsening tremor with GI or gait change suggests toxicity.

  • Do not teach a low-sodium diet: sudden sodium restriction can increase lithium retention.

Used To Treat

Acute manic episodes and maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder; specialist-directed use can also augment treatment-resistant depression and reduce recurrent mood episodes or suicide risk in selected patients.

Description

Lithium is an elemental-ion mood stabilizer, not a sedative, antipsychotic, or anticonvulsant repurposed by name. It is one of the most effective drugs for classic bipolar mania and relapse prevention, but the concentration that treats illness lies close to the concentration that causes neurologic, renal, and cardiac toxicity. The kidney handles lithium similarly to sodium, so dehydration, sodium depletion, declining renal filtration, and drugs that increase proximal sodium reabsorption can make a previously safe dose toxic.

Adverse Effects

  • Fine tremor, nausea/diarrhea, thirst, polyuria, cognitive slowing, acne and weight gain

  • Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and chronic tubulointerstitial renal injury

  • Hypothyroidism/goiter and hyperparathyroidism with hypercalcemia

  • Toxicity with coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, confusion, seizures, coma, dysrhythmia and possible persistent neurologic injury
