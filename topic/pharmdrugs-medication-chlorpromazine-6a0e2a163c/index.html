Used To Treat

Schizophrenia/psychotic disorders, severe agitation or mania, severe nausea/vomiting, intractable hiccups, preoperative apprehension and selected pediatric behavioral indications under product labeling.

Mechanism

D2 antagonism in mesolimbic and chemoreceptor-trigger-zone pathways reduces psychosis and emesis. Nigrostriatal and tuberoinfundibular D2 blockade cause EPS and prolactin elevation. Alpha-1 blockade prevents normal vasoconstriction on standing, so pressure falls and reflex tachycardia may occur. H1 blockade promotes sedation and appetite. M1 blockade reduces salivation, bowel/bladder contraction, sweating and accommodation, creating anticholinergic toxicity and heat risk. Phenothiazine tissue deposition and photoreactivity help explain photosensitivity and chronic corneal/lens changes; hepatic hypersensitivity can produce cholestatic jaundice.

Boxed Warning

BOXED WARNING: Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs have increased mortality, commonly from cardiovascular or infectious causes; Chlorpromazine is not approved for dementia-related psychosis. Profound hypotension, anticholinergic ileus/retention, heat illness, cholestatic jaundice, blood dyscrasias and ocular/skin deposition require drug-specific surveillance in addition to EPS, tardive dyskinesia and NMS.

Contraindications

  • Coma or major CNS-depressant states and phenothiazine hypersensitivity

  • Use extreme caution with severe hypotension, marrow suppression, liver disease, narrow-angle glaucoma, ileus or urinary retention

  • Avoid unprotected sun/heat exposure and uncritical use in fall-prone older adults

Nursing Essentials

  • Check orthostatic vitals and implement fall precautions before focusing only on movement effects.

  • Teach gradual position change, hydration as appropriate, sun protection, oral care, constipation prevention and urgent reporting of fever/sore throat, jaundice, severe abdominal distention or inability to void.

Interactions

  • Alcohol, opioids, sedatives and antihypertensives magnify CNS depression and hypotension.

  • Other anticholinergics add delirium, ileus, retention and heat injury.

  • Other D2 or QT-prolonging drugs add EPS/NMS or dysrhythmia risk.

Key Labs

  • Orthostatic blood pressure and ECG risk

  • CBC and liver tests when baseline risk or symptoms warrant

  • AIMS/EPS, bowel/bladder function, eye/skin changes and metabolic monitoring

Nclex Traps

  • Low potency predicts autonomic and sedative burden, not weak antipsychotic efficacy.

  • Absent severe EPS does not make chlorpromazine safe when the client is hypotensive, constipated or overheated.

  • Dark urine plus jaundice is not a benign medication color change.

Description

Chlorpromazine blocks dopamine D2 receptors to reduce psychosis, severe agitation, nausea/vomiting and intractable hiccups, while strong alpha-1, H1 and muscarinic blockade defines its low-potency first-generation profile. Those off-target actions produce orthostatic hypotension, sedation, weight gain, dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision and impaired sweating. It generally causes less EPS per clinically effective dose than haloperidol or fluphenazine, but EPS, prolactin elevation, tardive dyskinesia and NMS remain possible.

Adverse Effects

  • Sedation, orthostatic hypotension, reflex tachycardia and falls

  • Dry mouth, constipation/ileus, urinary retention, blurred vision and heat intolerance

  • Photosensitivity, skin pigmentation, corneal/lens deposits and cholestatic jaundice

  • EPS, prolactin elevation, tardive dyskinesia, NMS, seizures and blood dyscrasias
