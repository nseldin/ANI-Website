Used To Treat

Hyperphosphatemia in CKD, especially dialysis, by preventing absorption of phosphate eaten with meals; ferric citrate also has a separate iron-deficiency-anemia indication in nondialysis CKD.

Description

Phosphate binders work inside the gut, not in the bloodstream. Sevelamer's protonated amines ionically bind phosphate; ferric citrate and sucroferric oxyhydroxide exchange ligands with dietary phosphate to form poorly absorbed complexes. All must meet food in the GI tract, so taking them away from meals misses the target. Their tradeoffs are GI obstruction/binding interactions, iron absorption/overload, pill burden and stool changes, not a shared systemic receptor effect.

Mechanism

Sevelamer is a cross-linked nonabsorbed polymer whose amines bind anionic phosphate through ionic and hydrogen bonds. Ferric iron forms insoluble ferric-phosphate complexes; some ferric-citrate iron is absorbed and can replete or overload stores. Sucroferric oxyhydroxide is largely insoluble polynuclear iron oxyhydroxide; hydroxyl/water ligands exchange with phosphate across physiologic pH and most bound phosphate leaves in stool. Lower absorption reduces serum phosphorus and calcium-phosphate product.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Bowel obstruction is a sevelamer contraindication, and severe GI ulceration, necrosis or perforation has occurred. Ferric citrate is contraindicated in iron-overload syndromes. Iron-based binders require iron-store surveillance; all binders can reduce absorption of important oral drugs.

Adverse Effects

  • Sevelamer: nausea, vomiting, constipation/diarrhea, dysphagia, obstruction and GI injury

  • Ferric citrate: dark stool, diarrhea/constipation, nausea and iron overload

  • Sucroferric oxyhydroxide: dark stool and diarrhea; chewing burden and selected binding interactions

Contraindications

  • Sevelamer: bowel obstruction and serious hypersensitivity

  • Ferric citrate: iron-overload syndromes

  • Assess severe GI disease, dysphagia, recent surgery, peritonitis and iron-accumulation disorders

Nursing Essentials

  • Give with meals and distribute dose according to meal phosphate load; adherence cannot be judged from a medication list alone.

  • Review every oral drug for product-specific spacing and monitor diet, dialysis adequacy and PTH rather than escalating binder blindly.

Interactions

  • Sevelamer lowers absorption of ciprofloxacin, mycophenolate and selected vitamins/drugs.

  • Iron binders interact with levothyroxine, doxycycline, cephalexin and other chelating/binding targets with product-specific spacing.

  • Ferric citrate plus IV/oral iron can cause overload.

Key Labs

  • Serum phosphorus, calcium and intact PTH

  • Bicarbonate/chloride and fat-soluble-vitamin context with sevelamer

  • Ferritin, transferrin saturation and hemoglobin with iron-based products

Nclex Traps

  • A phosphate binder taken without food has little dietary phosphate to bind.

  • Ferric citrate is both a binder and an absorbable iron source; sucroferric oxyhydroxide is much less absorbed.

  • Dark stool from iron can obscure recognition of GI bleeding, so assess symptoms and hemoglobin.
