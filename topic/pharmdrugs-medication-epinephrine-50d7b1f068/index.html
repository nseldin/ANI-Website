Mechanism

Alpha-1 vasoconstriction raises systemic vascular resistance and reduces mucosal edema; beta-1 stimulation increases cardiac rate and contractility; beta-2 stimulation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle and reduces further mediator release from mast cells and basophils. These linked effects explain why one drug addresses the major fatal mechanisms of anaphylaxis. Intramuscular thigh injection provides rapid, safer absorption, whereas an IV bolus produces an abrupt peak that can cause severe hypertension, ischemia, or dysrhythmia. In cardiac arrest, the goal and dosing are different because coronary perfusion during CPR, not allergic mediator blockade, is the immediate target.

Boxed Warning

MEDICATION-ERROR HAZARD: epinephrine route and concentration errors can be fatal. For anaphylaxis, give the appropriate dose IM in the mid-outer thigh without delay because airway edema, bronchospasm, vasodilation, and capillary leak can progress rapidly. Verify the device, dose, concentration, and route; the 1 mg/mL IM product and diluted IV preparations are not interchangeable. Monitor airway, oxygenation, blood pressure, rhythm, response, and recurrence. Antihistamines and corticosteroids are adjuncts, not substitutes for IM epinephrine. Reserve IV infusion for refractory shock in a continuously monitored setting.

Contraindications

  • There is no absolute contraindication to IM epinephrine for anaphylaxis because untreated airway obstruction or shock is more dangerous than expected adrenergic effects.

  • Do not use racemic epinephrine, an inhaler, antihistamine, or corticosteroid as a substitute for IM epinephrine in systemic anaphylaxis because those options do not reliably reverse the combined airway edema, bronchospasm, vasodilation, and capillary leak.

Nursing Essentials

  • Verify concentration, route, device dose, and indication aloud because confusing 1 mg/mL IM product with a diluted IV preparation can be fatal.

  • Remove the trigger when safe, place a hypotensive patient supine with legs elevated unless breathing or pregnancy requires modification, and avoid sudden standing because empty ventricular filling can precipitate collapse.

  • Monitor airway, voice, stridor, wheeze, blood pressure, pulse, oxygenation, mental status, skin, and recurrence because improvement can be temporary.

Interactions

  • Nonselective beta blockers can blunt beta effects and leave unopposed vasoconstriction; refractory shock may require specialist-directed glucagon because glucagon increases intracellular cyclic AMP independently of the beta receptor.

  • Tricyclic antidepressants, monoamine oxidase inhibitors, stimulants, and some anesthetics can amplify pressor or dysrhythmic effects, but these interactions do not justify withholding IM epinephrine in anaphylaxis.

Key Labs

  • Anaphylaxis is a clinical diagnosis, and epinephrine must not wait for laboratory confirmation because airway edema and shock can progress while tests are pending; serum tryptase can support later evaluation, but a normal value does not exclude the event.

  • ECG, electrolytes, glucose, lactate, renal function, and blood gas are guided by severity, prolonged shock, infusion use, or suspected ischemia rather than required before the first IM dose.

Nclex Traps

  • Epinephrine comes before diphenhydramine and corticosteroids.

  • IM mid-outer thigh is the routine first route for anaphylaxis; IV bolus dosing is not interchangeable.

  • Improvement does not end monitoring because symptoms can recur.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Infants and children

    Note

    Dose by weight and use a device that matches the child's weight because underdosing can fail while excessive dosing increases cardiovascular toxicity.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Treat maternal anaphylaxis promptly and use left uterine displacement after midpregnancy because maternal oxygenation and perfusion determine fetal survival.

  • Population

    Older adults or cardiovascular disease

    Note

    Ischemic and dysrhythmic risk is higher, but this is not an absolute contraindication; careful monitoring accompanies lifesaving treatment.

Patient Education

  • For auto-injectors, teach immediate use for severe allergic reaction, call emergency services, and carry two devices when prescribed.

Used To Treat

Immediate first-line treatment of anaphylaxis and selected resuscitation, vasopressor, and airway indications under indication-specific protocols. Route and concentration are not interchangeable: intramuscular thigh dosing for anaphylaxis is fundamentally different from intravenous cardiac-arrest or titrated infusion use.

Description

Epinephrine is the first medicine for anaphylaxis because it stimulates alpha-1, beta-1, and beta-2 receptors to constrict dilated vessels, support cardiac output, relax bronchial smooth muscle, and limit further mediator release. It should be given intramuscularly in the mid-outer thigh at the first clinical suspicion; there is no absolute contraindication when anaphylaxis is present. Antihistamines may ease itching or hives but do not reliably reverse shock or airway obstruction, so giving them first creates dangerous delay. A dose can be repeated every 5 to 15 minutes while emergency support is activated. Refractory shock requires expert airway, fluid, infusion, and trigger-specific care rather than repeated unsupervised IV boluses.

Administration Timing

  • Give age- and weight-appropriate epinephrine intramuscularly in the mid-outer thigh immediately when anaphylaxis is suspected; do not wait for hypotension, a rash, or complete diagnostic certainty.

  • Repeat every 5 to 15 minutes when symptoms persist or recur while calling emergency services, positioning safely, supporting airway and oxygenation, and giving rapid isotonic fluid for shock.

  • Use an IV infusion only in a monitored setting for refractory anaphylaxis under an experienced protocol; never substitute the cardiac-arrest concentration or route for routine IM treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • Tremor, anxiety, palpitations, headache, pallor, nausea, and transient hypertension reflect adrenergic activation.

  • Dose, route, or concentration errors can cause ventricular dysrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, severe hypertension, pulmonary edema, cerebral hemorrhage, or local ischemic injury.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Persistent hypotension, hypoxemia, airway swelling, altered consciousness, chest pain, or dysrhythmia after IM doses requires immediate advanced resuscitation and a monitored infusion pathway.

  • Observe according to reaction severity and risk because biphasic recurrence can occur after initial improvement; prescribe and teach auto-injector use and trigger follow-up when appropriate.

Evidence Limitations

  • Ethical constraints limit randomized placebo trials in fatal anaphylaxis; physiologic evidence, observational outcomes, and consensus consistently support prompt IM use.

  • Observation duration and adjunct choices depend on severity and context; no adjunct reliably prevents every biphasic reaction.

Regulatory Status

FDA-labeled epinephrine products cover emergency allergic reactions and other product-specific indications; exact concentration, route, device, and label must be verified.
