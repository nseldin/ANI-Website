Definition

Clarke sign is a patellofemoral compression maneuver. It is a clue to patellofemoral pain. A positive finding means retropatellar pain or inability to sustain contraction occurs.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Clarke sign means retropatellar pain or inability to sustain contraction occurs.

Why It Matters

A positive Clarke sign response can signal patellofemoral pain. Quadriceps contraction presses the patella against the trochlea under examiner resistance. Clarke sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • patellofemoral pain

Assessment Method

Apply gentle pressure just above the patella while the patient contracts the quadriceps.

Positive Finding

Retropatellar pain or inability to sustain contraction occurs.

Mechanism

Quadriceps contraction presses the patella against the trochlea under examiner resistance.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Clarke sign response raises concern for patellofemoral pain because quadriceps contraction presses the patella against the trochlea under examiner resistance. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Clarke sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Clarke sign to focus evaluation of patellofemoral pain and the reviewed alternatives: quadriceps inhibition, fat pad pain, anxiety. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Apply gentle pressure just above the patella while the patient contracts the quadriceps. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Clarke sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

False positives are common even in asymptomatic knees. Therefore, Clarke sign cannot confirm or exclude patellofemoral pain by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • quadriceps inhibition

  • fat pad pain

  • anxiety

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from quadriceps inhibition, fat pad pain, anxiety, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Clarke sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Outdated or unreliable as a standalone test; can be painful. Clarke sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude patellofemoral pain. Apply the reviewed limitation for Clarke sign: False positives are common even in asymptomatic knees.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Clarke sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Apply gentle pressure just above the patella while the patient contracts the quadriceps. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Do not grind the patella or repeat a painful low-value maneuver.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Clarke sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: Retropatellar pain or inability to sustain contraction occurs. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Apply gentle pressure just above the patella while the patient contracts the quadriceps. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, patellofemoral pain, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Clarke sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Do not grind the patella or repeat a painful low-value maneuver. Base escalation for Clarke sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
