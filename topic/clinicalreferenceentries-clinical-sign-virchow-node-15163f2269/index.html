Definition

Virchow node is a palpated lymph-node sign. It is a clue to thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer. A positive finding means a hard enlarged left supraclavicular node is present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Virchow node means a hard enlarged left supraclavicular node is present.

Why It Matters

The observed Virchow node finding can signal thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer. Lymph from the thoracic duct drains near the left venous angle, carrying malignant cells from abdominal or thoracic sites. Virchow node cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer

Assessment Method

Palpate the left supraclavicular fossa.

Positive Finding

A hard enlarged left supraclavicular node is present.

Mechanism

Lymph from the thoracic duct drains near the left venous angle, carrying malignant cells from abdominal or thoracic sites.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Virchow node finding raises concern for thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer because lymph from the thoracic duct drains near the left venous angle, carrying malignant cells from abdominal or thoracic sites. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Virchow node does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Virchow node to focus evaluation of thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer and the reviewed alternatives: tuberculosis, lymphoma, reactive adenopathy. Evidence Limitations

It is not specific for gastric cancer and may be absent in advanced disease. Therefore, Virchow node cannot confirm or exclude thoracic or abdominal malignancy, classically gastric cancer by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

False Positive Situations

False Negative Situations
