Used To Treat

Long-term gout with tophi, radiographic damage, frequent flares, or other guideline-supported indications. Allopurinol is preferred first-line, including in chronic kidney disease when started low and titrated. Febuxostat is generally reserved for inadequate allopurinol response, intolerance, or when allopurinol is not advisable. Probenecid increases renal urate excretion and requires suitable kidney function and stone-risk planning.

Description

The goal of urate-lowering therapy is to keep serum urate below its saturation threshold long enough that tissue crystals dissolve. Allopurinol and febuxostat inhibit xanthine oxidase, reducing new uric-acid production. Probenecid blocks proximal-tubule urate reabsorption, increasing urinary excretion. As serum urate falls, old deposits remodel and crystals can transiently shed into the joint, provoking early flares even though treatment is working. That is why low starting doses, gradual titration, serial serum urate, and months of anti-inflammatory prophylaxis are paired with long-term therapy.

Mechanism

Purine degradation produces hypoxanthine, then xanthine, then uric acid through xanthine oxidoreductase. Allopurinol is converted to oxypurinol; both inhibit the enzyme, with oxypurinol providing sustained effect. Febuxostat is a non-purine inhibitor of oxidized and reduced xanthine oxidase. Probenecid inhibits apical URAT1-mediated urate reabsorption and other renal organic-anion transport, increasing urine uric acid while also reducing tubular secretion of drugs such as penicillin and methotrexate. A treat-to-target strategy titrates against serial serum urate, commonly below 6 mg/dL, because a fixed low dose may leave crystals thermodynamically favored.

Boxed Warning

Febuxostat carries a boxed warning for increased cardiovascular death in gout patients with established cardiovascular disease compared with allopurinol and should be reserved according to its label. Allopurinol can cause fatal SJS, TEN, or DRESS; HLA-B*58:01 markedly raises risk and targeted pre-treatment testing is recommended for higher-prevalence ancestry groups. Probenecid can cause uric-acid stones and serious hypersensitivity and can dangerously alter renal elimination of other drugs.

Adverse Effects

  • Allopurinol: rash, severe cutaneous reaction, DRESS, liver or kidney injury, cytopenias; febuxostat: cardiovascular death warning, liver abnormalities, rash

  • Probenecid: nephrolithiasis, renal colic, hematuria, GI effects, hypersensitivity, and clinically important organic-anion transport interactions

Contraindications

  • Never ignore a new allopurinol rash. Clarify HLA-B*58:01 risk, renal function, cardiovascular disease, stones, urine flow, liver tests, pregnancy, and azathioprine/mercaptopurine use.

  • Allopurinol and febuxostat can dangerously raise thiopurine exposure because xanthine oxidase participates in mercaptopurine metabolism; the combination requires specialist dose redesign or avoidance.

Nursing Essentials

  • Explain that early flares do not mean treatment failed. Continue the preventive agent during a flare unless a true toxicity requires stopping, and treat the inflammation separately.

  • Trend serum urate and titrate to target while monitoring adherence, kidney/liver safety, flare frequency, tophi, and prophylaxis duration.

Interactions

  • Allopurinol or febuxostat with azathioprine/mercaptopurine can cause profound myelosuppression; febuxostat labeling contraindicates the combination.

  • Probenecid raises concentrations of penicillins, methotrexate, and other organic acids; salicylates antagonize its uricosuric effect.

Key Labs

  • Serial serum urate, renal and liver function, CBC when toxicity or thiopurine interaction is relevant, and HLA-B*58:01 before allopurinol in recommended populations

  • Flare count, tophus burden, cardiovascular risk, hydration, stone symptoms, urine findings, and medication adherence

Nclex Traps

  • A flare after starting urate lowering can reflect crystal mobilization, not rising urate or treatment failure.

  • Allopurinol treats chronic urate burden; it does not directly turn off an established acute inflammatory flare.
