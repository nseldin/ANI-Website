Used To Treat

Suspected malignant hyperthermia during or after exposure to volatile anesthetics or succinylcholine. Dantrolene is the specific treatment; immediate trigger cessation, high-flow oxygen and hyperventilation, hyperkalemia and acidosis control, cooling, and post-crisis monitoring are equally time critical.

Description

Malignant hyperthermia is a pharmacogenetic crisis in which volatile anesthetics or succinylcholine trigger uncontrolled skeletal-muscle calcium release, driving hypermetabolism; stop the trigger and give dantrolene immediately because extreme fever may be late. A rapid unexplained rise in end-tidal carbon dioxide, tachycardia, rigidity, acidosis, hyperkalemia, and increasing temperature are key clues; extreme fever can be late, so waiting for it is dangerous. Stop triggers, call for the MH cart and MHAUS hotline, give dantrolene, hyperventilate with 100% oxygen, change or isolate contaminated anesthesia delivery components per the facility plan, and treat hyperkalemia, acidosis, dysrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis, and heat. Dantrolene formulations differ substantially in vial strength, water requirement, and preparation time, so product-specific reconstitution is a core safety step.

Mechanism

Pathogenic variants most often involving RYR1 or related excitation-contraction machinery permit uncontrolled calcium release from the skeletal-muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum after a trigger. Sustained actin-myosin cycling consumes ATP and oxygen, produces carbon dioxide and heat, and releases lactate, potassium, CK, and myoglobin. Dantrolene inhibits pathologic ryanodine-receptor calcium release, interrupting the driver. Hyperventilation clears excess carbon dioxide, cooling removes generated heat, and treatment of hyperkalemia and acidosis prevents secondary arrest. These therapies are complementary because dantrolene cannot instantly reverse metabolites and tissue injury already produced.

Administration Timing

  • At first suspicion, stop volatile anesthetic and succinylcholine exposure, call the MH response team and MHAUS hotline, obtain the cart, hyperventilate with 100% oxygen at high fresh-gas flow, and administer the stocked dantrolene product according to the crisis protocol. Do not wait for confirmatory testing or a high temperature.

  • Repeat dantrolene and continue crisis measures until hypermetabolism, rigidity, ventilation, and hemodynamics improve using product-specific instructions; treat hyperkalemia, acidosis, dysrhythmia, and urine flow while checking core temperature.

  • Use active cooling when clinically needed without delaying dantrolene or hyperventilation, and stop active cooling near 38 degrees C to avoid after-drop. MHAUS recommends post-crisis dantrolene and intensive monitoring for at least 24 hours in typical cases, with spacing or cessation only after stability criteria are met.

Nursing Essentials

  • Announce the crisis, assign dedicated roles for dantrolene preparation, airway and anesthesia-machine management, cooling, laboratory draws, urine monitoring, and documentation. Preparation errors multiply when the same person tries to manage every task.

  • Read the exact vial: Dantrium or Revonto and Ryanodex have different amounts and diluent requirements. Use only the labeled diluent and independently verify total milligrams delivered; stock sufficiency must be checked before an emergency.

  • Trend end-tidal carbon dioxide and core temperature to detect persistent hypermetabolism, and trend rhythm, blood pressure, rigidity, urine color and output, CK, potassium, blood gas, lactate, creatinine, calcium, glucose, and coagulation because these findings reveal dysrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalemia, acidosis, kidney injury, and DIC that can persist or recur after apparent control. Continue recurrence surveillance even after the operating room appears calm because malignant hyperthermia can recrudesce after initial stabilization.

Key Labs

  • Obtain serial blood gas, potassium, sodium, calcium, glucose, lactate, CK, creatinine, urinalysis or myoglobin, coagulation studies, and core temperature because their trends reveal ongoing acidosis, hyperkalemia, rhabdomyolysis, kidney injury, and DIC. Do not ignore a rising end-tidal carbon dioxide while awaiting laboratory results because it is an immediate physiologic marker of persistent skeletal-muscle hypermetabolism.

  • Genetic or contracture testing evaluates susceptibility after recovery; it does not confirm or exclude the active crisis in time to guide first treatment.

Adverse Effects

  • The crisis can cause hyperkalemic arrest, severe acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, kidney failure, DIC, pulmonary edema, cerebral injury, compartment syndrome, and death.

  • Dantrolene can cause muscle weakness, respiratory compromise, phlebitis, GI effects, and hepatotoxicity; large reconstitution volumes for some products can add fluid burden. These risks do not outweigh prompt use in a credible crisis.

Contraindications

  • There is no practical contraindication to lifesaving dantrolene in a credible MH crisis. Do not delay it for cooling, laboratory confirmation, or transfer.

  • Avoid routine calcium-channel blockers such as verapamil or diltiazem with dantrolene during MH because dangerous hyperkalemia and cardiovascular collapse have been reported. Use the MH dysrhythmia protocol.

Escalation Recurrence

  • Persistent hypercapnia, rigidity, hyperkalemia, acidosis, rising temperature, or hemodynamic instability requires repeated dantrolene, expanded crisis support, and hotline guidance. Recrudescence can occur after apparent control, often within the first day, so premature discontinuation or low-acuity transfer is unsafe.

Interactions

  • Volatile anesthetics and succinylcholine are established triggers; nitrous oxide, local anesthetics, nondepolarizing blockers, and routine IV anesthetics are not interchangeable trigger categories.

  • Dantrolene plus selected calcium-channel blockers can cause severe hyperkalemia and cardiovascular depression. Succinylcholine in a patient with unrecognized myopathy can also cause hyperkalemic arrest without classic MH, so the differential remains active.

Population Risks

  • Population

    Children

    Note

    Masseter spasm, unexplained hypercapnia, rigidity, or hyperkalemia after succinylcholine requires immediate MH evaluation; dosing and fluid burden are weight dependent.

  • Population

    Pregnancy

    Note

    Treat the maternal crisis immediately; dantrolene and stabilization take priority while obstetric and neonatal teams coordinate delivery considerations.

  • Population

    Known or possible MH susceptibility

    Note

    Use a trigger-free anesthetic plan and appropriately prepared machine; prior uneventful anesthesia does not exclude susceptibility.

Evidence Limitations

  • Crisis management is based on registry evidence, physiologic rationale, product labeling, and MHAUS consensus because randomized withholding trials are unethical. Post-crisis duration is individualized; MHAUS notes meaningful recrudescence risk rather than a zero-risk clock cutoff.

Nclex Traps

  • Rising end-tidal carbon dioxide and rigidity can precede dramatic fever.

  • Dantrolene does not replace trigger cessation, hyperventilation, hyperkalemia treatment, or cooling.

  • Antipyretics do not stop malignant-hyperthermia heat because uncontrolled skeletal-muscle calcium cycling, not a reset hypothalamic fever set point, generates it.

  • NMS and serotonin syndrome are different emergencies; do not copy the MH dantrolene protocol to them.

Regulatory Status

Injectable dantrolene products are FDA-approved for malignant-hyperthermia treatment. Exact preparation and dosing follow the stocked product label and MHAUS crisis protocol; formulation strengths and reconstitution are not interchangeable.

Boxed Warning

MALIGNANT-HYPERTHERMIA WARNING: unexplained rising end-tidal CO2, rigidity, acidosis, hyperkalemia, or temperature during anesthesia is enough to act. Stop triggers, call the MH team and hotline, hyperventilate with 100% oxygen, and give the exact stocked dantrolene product immediately; do not wait for extreme fever.

Boxed Warning Specificity

The visible warning names the emergency, explains why delay or a wrong rescue choice is dangerous, and separates proven antidotes from supportive or uncertain interventions.
