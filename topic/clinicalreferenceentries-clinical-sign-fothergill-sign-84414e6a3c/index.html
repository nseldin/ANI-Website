Definition

Fothergill sign is a palpation maneuver. It is a clue to rectus sheath mass or hematoma. A positive finding means the mass remains palpable and does not cross the midline.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Fothergill sign means the mass remains palpable and does not cross the midline.

Why It Matters

A positive Fothergill sign response can signal rectus sheath mass or hematoma. A lesion within the rectus sheath is fixed to the abdominal wall and confined by fascial boundaries. Fothergill sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • rectus sheath mass or hematoma

Assessment Method

Palpate an abdominal mass while the patient tenses the rectus muscles.

Positive Finding

The mass remains palpable and does not cross the midline.

Mechanism

A lesion within the rectus sheath is fixed to the abdominal wall and confined by fascial boundaries.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Fothergill sign response raises concern for rectus sheath mass or hematoma because a lesion within the rectus sheath is fixed to the abdominal wall and confined by fascial boundaries. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Fothergill sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Fothergill sign to focus evaluation of rectus sheath mass or hematoma and the reviewed alternatives: hernia, desmoid tumor, intra-abdominal neoplasm. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Palpate an abdominal mass while the patient tenses the rectus muscles. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Fothergill sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Large or adherent visceral masses can confuse the maneuver. Therefore, Fothergill sign cannot confirm or exclude rectus sheath mass or hematoma by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • hernia

  • desmoid tumor

  • intra-abdominal neoplasm

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from hernia, desmoid tumor, intra-abdominal neoplasm, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Fothergill sign may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current adjunct; ultrasound or CT confirms. When performed as described, Fothergill sign can adjust concern for rectus sheath mass or hematoma, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Fothergill sign: Large or adherent visceral masses can confuse the maneuver.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Fothergill sign, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: Palpate an abdominal mass while the patient tenses the rectus muscles. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid repeated pressure with anticoagulation, severe pain, or expanding hematoma.

Nursing Relevance

Record the exact Fothergill sign observation, side or distribution, timing, technique, patient response, and change from baseline; the reviewed positive finding is: The mass remains palpable and does not cross the midline. Continue the focused evaluation by following the reviewed method and its limits: Palpate an abdominal mass while the patient tenses the rectus muscles. Communicate the finding with its suspected association, rectus sheath mass or hematoma, when it is new, worsening, or clinically important.

Documentation Guidance

Document the exact finding, assessment method, side or distribution, timing, patient response, associated symptoms and vital signs, and change from baseline. Record the observed result instead of writing only "positive" or "negative."

Urgent Escalation

For Fothergill sign, apply this exact authored caution when deciding whom to notify and how quickly: Avoid repeated pressure with anticoagulation, severe pain, or expanding hematoma. Base escalation for Fothergill sign on the patient's current condition, associated findings, and the cause under evaluation; this card does not assign a universal urgency level.
