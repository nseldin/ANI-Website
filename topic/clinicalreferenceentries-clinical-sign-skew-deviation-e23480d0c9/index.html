Definition

Skew deviation is an observed ocular alignment sign. It is a clue to central otolith pathway imbalance. A positive finding means one eye makes a vertical corrective movement when uncovered.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Skew deviation means one eye makes a vertical corrective movement when uncovered.

Why It Matters

The observed Skew deviation finding can signal central otolith pathway imbalance. Asymmetric central otolith tone produces vertical ocular misalignment. Skew deviation cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • central otolith pathway imbalance

Assessment Method

Perform alternate cover testing in an appropriate patient with vertical diplopia or acute vestibular syndrome.

Positive Finding

One eye makes a vertical corrective movement when uncovered.

Mechanism

Asymmetric central otolith tone produces vertical ocular misalignment.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Skew deviation finding raises concern for central otolith pathway imbalance because asymmetric central otolith tone produces vertical ocular misalignment. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Skew deviation does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Skew deviation to focus evaluation of central otolith pathway imbalance and the reviewed alternatives: trochlear palsy, thyroid eye disease, decompensated phoria. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Perform alternate cover testing in an appropriate patient with vertical diplopia or acute vestibular syndrome. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Skew deviation as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Small skews can be hard to detect and peripheral causes occasionally coexist. Therefore, Skew deviation cannot confirm or exclude central otolith pathway imbalance by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • trochlear palsy

  • thyroid eye disease

  • decompensated phoria

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as trochlear palsy, thyroid eye disease, decompensated phoria.

False Negative Situations

Skew deviation may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of central otolith pathway imbalance.

Modern Status

Current central-localization adjunct. When performed as described, Skew deviation can adjust concern for central otolith pathway imbalance, but it is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Apply the reviewed limitation for Skew deviation: Small skews can be hard to detect and peripheral causes occasionally coexist.

Safety Precautions

Observe Skew deviation with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Perform alternate cover testing in an appropriate patient with vertical diplopia or acute vestibular syndrome. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not interpret skew outside the correct dizziness syndrome or without checking baseline strabismus.
