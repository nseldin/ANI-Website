Class

Oral ATP-competitive multikinase antifibrotic inhibiting PDGFR, FGFR, VEGFR, and related signaling

Used To Treat

Treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, chronic fibrosing interstitial lung diseases with a progressive phenotype, and slowing pulmonary-function decline in systemic-sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease in adults. It slows decline; it does not dissolve existing scar or rescue acute hypoxemia.

Boxed Warning

No current U.S. boxed warning. High-yield warning context: Drug-induced liver injury, sometimes severe or fatal, requires baseline and scheduled ALT, AST, and bilirubin monitoring. Severe diarrhea can cause dehydration and renal compromise. Evaluate bleeding, arterial thromboembolism, GI perforation, recent abdominal surgery, wound healing, nephrotic-range proteinuria, and embryo-fetal toxicity. Capsules must be swallowed whole with food; acute ILD worsening or hypoxemia still requires urgent diagnostic evaluation.

Mechanism

Nintedanib competes with ATP at PDGFR-alpha/beta, FGFR1-3, VEGFR1-3, CSF1R, FLT3, and selected Src-family kinase pockets. Blocking autophosphorylation reduces downstream Ras-MAPK, PI3K-AKT, and other signaling involved in fibroblast migration, proliferation, differentiation, vascular remodeling, and extracellular-matrix deposition. It therefore slows ongoing remodeling rather than enzymatically removing mature collagen. Oral absorption increases with food, and the drug is mainly a P-glycoprotein substrate with minor CYP3A4 metabolism. Hepatic exposure rises with impairment. Enterocyte and vascular effects help explain diarrhea, liver injury, bleeding, arterial events, GI perforation, and proteinuria rather than these being unrelated memorized warnings.

Nursing Essentials

  • Obtain liver tests and pregnancy status before treatment; administer with food about twelve hours apart and start antidiarrheal/hydration management early under the plan.

  • Trend stool count, weight, intake/output, liver injury symptoms, bleeding, chest or neurologic ischemic symptoms, abdominal pain, wound healing, urine protein, oxygenation, and FVC.

Contraindications

  • No label-listed absolute contraindication, but moderate or severe hepatic impairment is not recommended and mild impairment requires a lower dose

  • Use heightened caution with active bleeding, full anticoagulation, recent abdominal surgery, prior GI perforation, high cardiovascular risk, pregnancy, or inability to manage diarrhea

Interactions

  • P-gp and CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ketoconazole can raise exposure; rifampin and other inducers lower exposure.

  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets increase bleeding concern; smoking reduces exposure and should be stopped for disease and pharmacokinetic reasons.

Key Labs

  • ALT, AST, bilirubin before and during treatment, pregnancy testing, renal function, urinalysis/protein, weight, and hydration

  • FVC, symptoms, exertional oxygenation, acute exacerbations, bleeding, ischemia, and abdominal findings

Nclex Traps

  • Nintedanib slows fibrosis progression; it does not reverse established scar.

  • Its kinase targets explain both antifibrotic benefit and vascular/GI toxicity, so bleeding or severe abdominal pain is not routine diarrhea.

Description

Nintedanib is an oral antifibrotic multikinase inhibitor that slows the rate at which lung function declines in several progressive fibrosing interstitial lung diseases. It blocks ATP-binding sites on PDGF, FGF, and VEGF receptors and selected related kinases, reducing signals that recruit and proliferate fibroblasts and promote myofibroblast activity and matrix deposition. The clinical win is often a less steep fall in forced vital capacity, not dramatic symptomatic improvement or scar reversal. Diarrhea is common and can produce dehydration, renal injury, and discontinuation if treated late. The same VEGF-pathway inhibition that contributes to antifibrotic signaling also helps explain bleeding, arterial thromboembolism, impaired healing, and GI-perforation concerns.

Adverse Effects

  • Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, decreased appetite, weight loss, headache, or elevated liver enzymes

  • Drug-induced liver injury, bleeding, arterial thromboembolism, GI perforation, impaired wound healing, proteinuria or thrombotic microangiopathy, and embryo-fetal harm
