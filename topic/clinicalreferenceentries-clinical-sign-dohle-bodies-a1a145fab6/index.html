Definition

Döhle bodies is a microscopic neutrophil sign. It is a clue to accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis. A positive finding means pale blue peripheral cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Döhle bodies means pale blue peripheral cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Why It Matters

The Döhle bodies laboratory finding can signal accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis. Rough endoplasmic reticulum remnants persist when neutrophils mature rapidly. Döhle bodies cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis

Assessment Method

Review neutrophils on peripheral smear.

Positive Finding

Pale blue peripheral cytoplasmic inclusions are present.

Mechanism

Rough endoplasmic reticulum remnants persist when neutrophils mature rapidly.

Clinical Interpretation

The Döhle bodies laboratory finding raises concern for accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis because rough endoplasmic reticulum remnants persist when neutrophils mature rapidly. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Döhle bodies does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Use Döhle bodies to focus evaluation of accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis and the reviewed alternatives: May-Hegglin inclusions, stain artifact, toxic granulation. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Döhle bodies as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

They occur in benign states and can be subtle. Therefore, Döhle bodies cannot confirm or exclude accelerated or stressed granulopoiesis by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • May-Hegglin inclusions

  • stain artifact

  • toxic granulation

False Positive Situations

A false-positive interpretation can result from specimen quality, preparation or staining artifact, and morphologic overlap with May-Hegglin inclusions, stain artifact, toxic granulation.

False Negative Situations

Döhle bodies may be missed when the specimen is delayed, diluted, poorly prepared, altered by treatment or transfusion, or the abnormal cells or material are sparse.
