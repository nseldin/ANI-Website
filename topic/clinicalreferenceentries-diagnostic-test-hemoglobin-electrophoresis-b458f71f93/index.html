Summary

Hemoglobin electrophoresis is a high-yield Hematology/Immunology diagnostic reference. Separates hemoglobin types to evaluate sickle cell disease, thalassemias, and hemoglobin variants.

Quick Answer

Hemoglobin electrophoresis: Separates hemoglobin types to evaluate sickle cell disease, thalassemias, and hemoglobin variants. Nursing focus: prepare correctly, protect safety, trend results with assessment, and escalate red-flag findings.

Result Meanings

  • Label

    Normal pattern

    Meaning

    Expected adult hemoglobin fractions are present without a major variant pattern.

  • Label

    Abnormal pattern

    Meaning

    Hemoglobin S, C, E, F elevation, or thalassemia-like fractions can support sickle cell disease/trait, thalassemia, or other hemoglobin variants.

  • Label

    Clinical significance

    Meaning

    Interpret with CBC indices, smear, iron studies, family history, newborn screen, and symptoms.

Sections

    • What it tells you

    • Separates hemoglobin types to evaluate sickle cell disease, thalassemias, and hemoglobin variants.

    • Before / preparation

    • Verify timing, specimen type, anticoagulant/medication effects, fasting status when relevant, and whether a trend is more important than one isolated value.

    • Priority nursing actions

    • Trend the result with symptoms, vital signs, assessment findings, medications, and baseline history. Escalate critical or rapidly changing values.

    • Concerning results / reportable cues

    • Critical values, sharp trend changes, results that do not fit the client's condition, specimen contamination, or a result that requires immediate treatment.

    • Common NCLEX trap / nuance

    • NCLEX rarely tests a number by itself. Connect the lab to the client, the trend, and the safest nursing action.
