Definition

Pretibial myxedema is an observed and palpated skin sign. It is a clue to thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease. A positive finding means nonpitting indurated plaques with peau-d'orange texture occur over pretibial skin.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Pretibial myxedema means nonpitting indurated plaques with peau-d'orange texture occur over pretibial skin.

Why It Matters

The observed Pretibial myxedema finding can signal thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease. TSH-receptor-driven fibroblasts deposit hydrophilic glycosaminoglycans in the dermis. Pretibial myxedema cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease

Assessment Method

Inspect and palpate the shins and feet.

Positive Finding

Nonpitting indurated plaques with peau-d'orange texture occur over pretibial skin.

Mechanism

TSH-receptor-driven fibroblasts deposit hydrophilic glycosaminoglycans in the dermis.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Pretibial myxedema finding raises concern for thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease because tSH-receptor-driven fibroblasts deposit hydrophilic glycosaminoglycans in the dermis. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Pretibial myxedema does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Pretibial myxedema to focus evaluation of thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease and the reviewed alternatives: cellulitis, venous eczema, lipodermatosclerosis. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Inspect and palpate the shins and feet. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Pretibial myxedema as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Appearance varies and can occur in euthyroid autoimmune disease. Therefore, Pretibial myxedema cannot confirm or exclude thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • cellulitis

  • venous eczema

  • lipodermatosclerosis

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as cellulitis, venous eczema, lipodermatosclerosis.

False Negative Situations

Pretibial myxedema may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of thyroid dermopathy from Graves disease.
