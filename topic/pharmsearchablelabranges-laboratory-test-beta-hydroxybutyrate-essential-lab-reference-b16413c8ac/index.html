Name

Beta-hydroxybutyrate

Range

Reference intervals and point-of-care performance vary by assay, specimen, device, and clinical setting. For adults with suspected DKA, the 2024 consensus uses direct blood beta-hydroxybutyrate at or above 3.0 mmol/L as the ketonemia criterion only together with diabetes or qualifying hyperglycemia and metabolic acidosis. For DKA resolution, it uses plasma beta-hydroxybutyrate below 0.6 mmol/L plus venous pH at or above 7.3 or bicarbonate at or above 18 mmol/L; follow the current adult pathway rather than treating either ketone number as a universal stand-alone diagnosis.

Why

An increased beta-hydroxybutyrate result signals accelerated fat breakdown and ketone production and raises concern for ketoacidosis when metabolic acidosis is also present. DKA is a major cause, including euglycemic DKA with an SGLT2 inhibitor, but alcoholic ketoacidosis, prolonged fasting or starvation, and sustained vomiting can also raise ketones. Direct blood beta-hydroxybutyrate is preferred for DKA assessment because urine nitroprusside testing does not measure this major DKA ketoacid and can underestimate early disease or look worse as beta-hydroxybutyrate converts to acetoacetate during recovery. Promptly assess mental status, breathing pattern, hydration and perfusion, nausea, vomiting or abdominal pain, recent intake and alcohol use, diabetes history, insulin access or omission, SGLT2-inhibitor use, pregnancy, and infection or other triggers. Cause-directed evaluation includes glucose, electrolytes and potassium, bicarbonate, venous pH, creatinine, and osmolality plus trigger testing as ordered. Escalate immediately for altered consciousness, respiratory distress, shock, severe dehydration, or rapid deterioration even when glucose is not markedly elevated. One beta-hydroxybutyrate value does not diagnose DKA or identify its cause; interpret it with the required glucose or diabetes context, acidosis criteria, trend, assay, and full presentation.

High Causes

  • Diabetic ketoacidosis, including SGLT2-inhibitor associated euglycemic DKA

  • Alcoholic ketoacidosis

  • Prolonged fasting or starvation ketosis

  • Sustained vomiting or other carbohydrate-depleted states

Low Causes

  • No substantial ketone-producing state

  • Falling ketonemia during treatment, which indicates DKA resolution only when the paired pH or bicarbonate criterion and clinical condition also improve

Critical Concerns

  • Elevated beta-hydroxybutyrate with low pH or bicarbonate raises concern for ketoacidosis even if glucose is not markedly high.

  • Altered consciousness, respiratory distress, shock, severe dehydration, or rapid deterioration requires immediate escalation.

Nursing Considerations

  • Assess mental status, breathing, hydration and perfusion, vomiting or abdominal pain, intake and alcohol use, diabetes therapy and insulin access, SGLT2-inhibitor use, pregnancy, and likely triggers.

  • Trend direct beta-hydroxybutyrate with glucose, potassium and other electrolytes, bicarbonate, venous pH, creatinine, osmolality, fluid balance, and ordered trigger testing.

Nclex Traps

  • SGLT2 inhibitors can be associated with euglycemic DKA, so a glucose result that is not markedly elevated does not exclude the crisis.

  • Urine ketones do not measure beta-hydroxybutyrate and should not replace direct blood beta-hydroxybutyrate for DKA resolution when the latter is available.
