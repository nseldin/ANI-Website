Definition

Garlic-odor sign is an observed odor clue. It is a clue to selected chemical exposures. A positive finding means a garlic-like odor is present on breath, vomitus, or clothing.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Garlic-odor sign means a garlic-like odor is present on breath, vomitus, or clothing.

Why It Matters

The observed Garlic-odor sign finding can signal selected chemical exposures. Volatile metabolites from arsenic, organophosphorus compounds, or phosphorus can create sulfurous odors. Garlic-odor sign cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • selected chemical exposures

Assessment Method

Note an odor incidentally while using appropriate PPE.

Positive Finding

A garlic-like odor is present on breath, vomitus, or clothing.

Mechanism

Volatile metabolites from arsenic, organophosphorus compounds, or phosphorus can create sulfurous odors.

Clinical Interpretation

The observed Garlic-odor sign finding raises concern for selected chemical exposures because volatile metabolites from arsenic, organophosphorus compounds, or phosphorus can create sulfurous odors. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Garlic-odor sign does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Garlic-odor sign to focus evaluation of selected chemical exposures and the reviewed alternatives: garlic ingestion, selenium exposure, solvent. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: Note an odor incidentally while using appropriate PPE. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Garlic-odor sign as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Diet and many chemicals produce similar odor. Therefore, Garlic-odor sign cannot confirm or exclude selected chemical exposures by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • garlic ingestion

  • selenium exposure

  • solvent

False Positive Situations

A false-positive observation can result from lighting, baseline anatomy, transient physiology, or a mimic such as garlic ingestion, selenium exposure, solvent.

False Negative Situations

Garlic-odor sign may be missed when it is intermittent, subtle, concealed by skin or body habitus, or absent at the observed stage of selected chemical exposures.

Modern Status

Historical nonspecific clue. Garlic-odor sign remains searchable because learners still encounter the term, but it cannot confirm or exclude selected chemical exposures. Apply the reviewed limitation for Garlic-odor sign: Diet and many chemicals produce similar odor.

Safety Precautions

Observe Garlic-odor sign with consent, privacy, adequate exposure, and only the manipulation required by this assessment: Note an odor incidentally while using appropriate PPE. The reviewed precaution for this finding is: Do not intentionally smell contaminated material; protect staff and obtain toxicology guidance.
