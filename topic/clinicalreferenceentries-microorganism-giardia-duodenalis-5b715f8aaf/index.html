Definition

Giardia duodenalis is a clinically important protozoan parasite associated with giardiasis, malabsorptive diarrhea, and postinfectious lactose intolerance. The organism or agent name identifies the cause or association; it is not itself the disease diagnosis, and detection must be interpreted with the specimen, host, and syndrome.

  • Content

    Trophozoites attach to proximal small-intestinal mucosa without routine tissue invasion, disrupting absorption and producing greasy diarrhea, bloating, and weight loss. This mechanism explains the connection between exposure, host vulnerability, tissue effects, and the major syndromes instead of presenting the association as an isolated fact.

    Giardia duodenalis is associated with giardiasis, malabsorptive diarrhea, and postinfectious lactose intolerance. Severity and presentation vary by age, immune status, pregnancy, anatomic site, inoculum, and comorbidity. Urgent escalation is based on the syndrome—such as shock, respiratory compromise, rapidly progressive neurologic findings, severe dehydration, or invasive infection—not merely on a positive organism label.
