Definition

Patellar clonus is a bedside reflex sign. It is a clue to marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability. A positive finding means the patella oscillates rhythmically.

Plain Language

In plain language, a positive Patellar clonus means the patella oscillates rhythmically.

Why It Matters

A positive Patellar clonus response can signal marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability. Quadriceps stretch reflexes cycle because descending inhibition is impaired. Patellar clonus cannot confirm that association by itself; assess it using the method and limits defined for this finding.

Associated Conditions

  • marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability

Assessment Method

With the leg extended and relaxed, push the patella sharply downward and maintain pressure.

Positive Finding

The patella oscillates rhythmically.

Mechanism

Quadriceps stretch reflexes cycle because descending inhibition is impaired.

Clinical Interpretation

A positive Patellar clonus response raises concern for marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability because quadriceps stretch reflexes cycle because descending inhibition is impaired. Compare the response with the reviewed assessment and differentials; Patellar clonus does not establish the diagnosis alone.

Diagnostic Usefulness

Use Patellar clonus to focus evaluation of marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability and the reviewed alternatives: tremor, voluntary quadriceps contraction, knee pain. Follow this card's assessment method and limits: With the leg extended and relaxed, push the patella sharply downward and maintain pressure. Then anticipate or support cause-directed testing selected by the responsible clinician for the suspected condition rather than treating Patellar clonus as a diagnosis.

Evidence Limitations

Technique and knee anatomy limit reproducibility. Therefore, Patellar clonus cannot confirm or exclude marked upper motor neuron hyperexcitability by itself; interpret the result in the population and clinical setting where it was assessed.

Differential Diagnoses

  • tremor

  • voluntary quadriceps contraction

  • knee pain

False Positive Situations

A false-positive maneuver can result from incorrect position or force, pain from tremor, voluntary quadriceps contraction, knee pain, guarding, or calling an incompletely defined response positive.

False Negative Situations

Patellar clonus may be missed with incomplete technique, guarding, early or partial disease, limited cooperation, or a response too subtle for the examiner.

Modern Status

Current but less commonly used pyramidal sign. Interpret Patellar clonus in the assessment context defined for this card because its reviewed limitation is topic-specific: Technique and knee anatomy limit reproducibility.

Safety Precautions

Before assessing Patellar clonus, explain the method, obtain consent, preserve privacy, and use only the technique described here: With the leg extended and relaxed, push the patella sharply downward and maintain pressure. Stop if that technique becomes unsafe. The reviewed precaution for this maneuver is: Avoid testing after knee surgery or with acute patellar injury.
